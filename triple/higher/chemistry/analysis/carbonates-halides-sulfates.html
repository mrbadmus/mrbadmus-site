Tests for carbonates, halides and sulfates

A white salt with no label. Which tests, in which order and with which acid, prove what it is, and which tests would lie to you?

Required practical · identifying ions

Start here

Five salts. Five tubes. Every one said chloride.

A student tested five different white salts for halide ions. She acidified each solution with dilute hydrochloric acid, then added silver nitrate solution. Every tube gave a white precipitate, so she wrote "chloride" five times.

Commit first

What went wrong?

Each test looks for one kind of anion (negative ion). Carbonates fizz with dilute acid, and the carbon dioxide turns limewater milky. Halide ions (chloride, bromide, iodide) give a precipitate with silver nitrate solution in the presence of dilute nitric acid; its colour names the halide. Sulfate ions give a white precipitate with barium chloride solution in the presence of dilute hydrochloric acid.

The acid goes in first: carbonate ions would also give a precipitate with either reagent, and the acid removes them as carbon dioxide.

!

Think again · fizzing

“It fizzed when I added the acid, so it is a carbonate.”

Required practical 7 · chemistry only · the anion tests

Fresh sample. Right acid. Then the reagent.

  1. Dissolve a small amount of each solid in distilled water.
  2. Carbonate: add dilute hydrochloric acid in a tube fitted with a delivery tube, and bubble any gas through limewater.
  3. Halide: add a few drops of dilute nitric acid, then a few drops of silver nitrate solution. Record the colour of any precipitate.
  4. Sulfate: add a few drops of dilute hydrochloric acid, then a few drops of barium chloride solution. Look for a white precipitate.
  5. Use a fresh sample for every test and a clean dropper for every reagent.

Barium chloride is toxic if swallowed. Wash your hands afterwards.

Silver nitrate stains skin and is an irritant. Avoid skin contact.

Dilute acids are irritants. Wear eye protection.

Gas in a stoppered tube can blow the bung out. Use a delivery tube.

Equation · the carbonate test

Learn it

Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂

The fizz is the carbon dioxide. Any carbonate does the same: a salt, water and carbon dioxide.

Ionic equations · strike out the spectators

Most of the ions in a test tube take no part. Ions that are dissolved before the reaction and still dissolved, unchanged, after it are spectator ions. Leave them out and what remains is the ionic equation.

Key fact

Each test has its own acid, the acid always goes in first, and it must never add the ion you are testing for.

Key note · AQA 4.8.3.3–4.8.3.5 · RP7

Tests for carbonates, halides and sulfates

  1. 01Carbonate: acid → CO₂ → limewater milky.
  2. 02Halides: acidify (HNO₃) + AgNO₃ → Cl=white, Br=cream, I=yellow precipitate.
  3. 03Sulfate: acidify (HCl) + BaCl₂ → white BaSO₄ precipitate.
  4. 04Always acidify first to remove carbonate ions.
  5. 05Never acidify with the acid whose ion you are testing for: no HCl in the halide test, no H₂SO₄ in the sulfate test.
  6. 06Naming a salt takes two results: a metal-ion test and an anion test.
  7. 07Ionic: Ag⁺ + X⁻ → AgX; Ba²⁺ + SO₄²⁻ → BaSO₄; CO₃²⁻ + 2H⁺ → H₂O + CO₂.
