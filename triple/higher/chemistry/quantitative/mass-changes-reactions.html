Equation · mass of gas

In an open container the mass of gas that left (or joined) is the difference between the two readings. It is a subtraction, so there is no triangle: one line, both readings in the same unit.

mass of gas = mass before − mass after

If the reading rose (a metal gaining oxygen), swap the order: mass gained = mass after − mass before.

Key fact

Some reactions may appear to involve a change in mass. The explanation is usually a gas: one that escapes from an open container (reading falls) or one from the air that becomes part of the product (reading rises). Sealed, nothing changes.

Key note · AQA 5.3.1.3 · Combined and Triple

Mass changes in reactions

  1. 01Some reactions may appear to involve a change in mass but can usually be explained because a reactant or product is a gas and its mass has not been taken into account.
  2. 02Reading falls: a gas escapes from an open container (marble chips and acid, zinc and acid, heating a carbonate). Gas particles move freely and leave, taking their mass with them.
  3. 03Reading rises: a metal reacts with oxygen from the air and the oxygen becomes part of the solid oxide (2Mg + O2 → 2MgO).
  4. 04Sealed container: nothing enters or leaves, so the reading stays the same. Mass of gas = mass before − mass after, both in the same unit.
  5. 05Higher: predict the mass change from the balanced equation in three steps: n = m ÷ Ar, the molar ratio, then m = n × Mr. 4.8 g Mg gains 3.2 g of oxygen to make 8.0 g MgO.
