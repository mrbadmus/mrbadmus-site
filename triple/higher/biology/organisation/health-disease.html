Health, disease and risk factors

Key knowledge

Health is the state of physical and mental well-being.

Diseases, both communicable and non-communicable, are major causes of ill health.

  • Communicable diseases are caused by pathogens and can spread from one person to another.
  • Non-communicable diseases cannot be passed from one person to another.

Diet, stress and life situations may have a profound effect on both physical and mental health.

  • Defects in the immune system mean a person is more likely to suffer from infectious diseases.
  • Viruses living in cells can be the trigger for cancers.
  • Immune reactions first caused by a pathogen can trigger allergies, such as skin rashes and asthma.
  • Severe physical ill health can lead to depression and other mental illness.

Risk factors are linked to an increased rate of a disease. They can be:

  • aspects of a person's lifestyle, such as smoking
  • substances in the person's body or environment, such as ionising radiation

A causal mechanism has been proven for some risk factors, but not in others.

Many diseases are caused by the interaction of a number of factors.

  • To the person: illness, a lower quality of life, lost earnings, an early death.
  • To the community and the nation: the cost of treatment and care, and fewer people able to work.
  • Globally: these diseases are very common, so the total cost is very large.

Lifestyle factors, including diet, alcohol and smoking, change how common these diseases are locally, nationally and globally. Where more people smoke, more people get lung cancer.

Made-up data: cigarettes smoked a day against lung cancer cases per 100 000 people.

The points rise from left to right: a positive correlation. A correlation shows a link. It does not prove that one causes the other.

A large, randomly chosen sample gives more valid conclusions, because it represents the whole population better.

Made-up data: lung cancer cases per 100 000 people in one year.

  • Never smoked: 15
  • Smoked in the past: 60
  • Smoke now: 150

Each group gets its own bar, with gaps between the bars. The height of each bar is the number of cases.

Reading back: people who smoke now have 150 ÷ 15 = 10 times as many cases as people who never smoked.

Made-up data: the ages at which 200 people were diagnosed with coronary heart disease.

Age is continuous data, so it is grouped into equal class intervals, such as 60 to 70 years. The bars touch, because each interval runs straight into the next. A bar chart of separate groups has gaps instead.

The height of each bar is the number of people in that interval. Most people were diagnosed aged 70 to 80: 70 of the 200.

  • Wrong: A risk factor means you will get the disease.

    Right: A risk factor is linked to an increased rate of the disease. Not everyone with it gets ill.

  • Wrong: A correlation proves a cause.

    Right: A correlation shows a link. A cause is proven only when the causal mechanism is found.

  • Wrong: Health just means not having a disease.

    Right: Health is the state of physical and mental well-being.

Key fact

Health is the state of physical and mental well-being, and diseases, both communicable and non-communicable, are major causes of ill health. Risk factors, such as smoking, diet and alcohol, are linked to an increased rate of a disease; a causal mechanism has been proven for some risk factors, but not in others.

Smoking and lung cancer is a link where the causal mechanism has been found. Tobacco smoke contains carcinogens, which damage the DNA in lung cells.
