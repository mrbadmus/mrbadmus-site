Reflection of Waves

Waves can be reflected at the boundary between two different materials. Waves can also be absorbed or transmitted at the boundary. Reflection sends the wave back; absorption transfers its energy to the material; transmission lets it carry on into the next material. In a ray diagram for reflection, draw the normal at 90° to the surface where the ray meets it: the angle of reflection equals the angle of incidence, both measured from the normal.
⚠️ Common Mistake

Angles are measured from the NORMAL, not from the mirror surface. A wave at a boundary is usually partly reflected, partly absorbed and partly transmitted, not just one of the three.

📌 Key Note

At a boundary between two materials a wave can be reflected, absorbed or transmitted, and often part of it does each. Ray diagram: the normal is at 90° to the surface; angle of incidence = angle of reflection, both measured from the normal.
