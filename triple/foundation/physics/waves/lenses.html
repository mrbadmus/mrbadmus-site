Lenses

Start here

You look at a window across the room through a magnifying glass held at arm's length.

If you had to guess, is the window the right way up or upside down?

Key words

  • convex lens
  • concave lens
  • principal focus
  • focal length

Key knowledge

A lens forms an image by refracting light.

  • A convex lens is thicker in the middle. It brings rays of light together.
  • A concave lens is thinner in the middle. It spreads rays of light apart.

In a convex lens, parallel rays of light are brought to a focus at the principal focus. The distance from the lens to the principal focus is called the focal length.

A concave lens spreads parallel rays apart. They seem to come from a principal focus on the side the light comes from.

The figure shows the symbol for each lens that you use in ray diagrams.

F is the principal focus. 2F is twice the focal length from the lens.

  • Ray 1 leaves the top of the object parallel to the axis. After the lens it passes through the principal focus.
  • Ray 2 leaves the top of the object and passes straight through the centre of the lens without bending.
  • Ray 3, if you need it, passes through F on the object's side. After the lens it travels parallel to the axis.
  • The top of the image is where the rays meet.

Here the object is between F and 2F, so the image is real, inverted and magnified.

  • A real image forms where rays of light really meet. It can be shown on a screen.
  • A virtual image forms where rays only seem to come from. It cannot be shown on a screen.

The image produced by a convex lens can be either real or virtual. With the object inside F, the rays spread apart after the lens. Traced back as dashed lines, they meet on the object's side: this is how a magnifying glass works.

The image produced by a concave lens is always virtual.

It is upright and diminished, on the same side of the lens as the object.

magnification = image height ÷ object height

Magnification is a ratio and so has no units.

Image height and object height should both be measured in either mm or cm.

This equation is on the equation sheet.

  • Wrong: Magnification is measured in cm.

    Right: Magnification is a ratio, so it has no units.

  • Wrong: An image 2.6 cm tall from an object 4.0 mm tall: 2.6 ÷ 4.0.

    Right: Put both heights in the same unit first: 26 mm ÷ 4.0 mm.

  • Wrong: A concave lens can form a real image.

    Right: The image produced by a concave lens is always virtual.

Key fact

A lens forms an image by refracting light. A convex lens brings parallel rays to a focus at the principal focus, and the distance from the lens to the principal focus is the focal length. A convex lens can form a real or a virtual image, a concave lens always forms a virtual image, and magnification = image height ÷ object height.

Use a convex lens to form a sharp image of a lamp on a screen. Measure the image height and the object height, then calculate the magnification.

Repeat with a range of convex lenses.

A convex lens is also called a converging lens. A concave lens is also called a diverging lens.

  • Complete a ray diagram: use a ruler, draw two rays from the top of the object, and put an arrow on each.
  • Compare convex and concave lenses: say what each does to rays, and the kind of image each forms.
