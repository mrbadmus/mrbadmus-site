Mixtures and separation techniques

Stir sugar into water and it seems to vanish. How do you get each substance back out of a mixture, and how do you choose the right method?

Start here

A spoonful of sugar in hot water.

The crystals vanish. The water looks exactly as clear as before, but it now tastes sweet.

If you had to guess, is it still sugar, or has it turned into something new?

Sugar water is a mixture: two or more elements or compounds that are not chemically combined together. Each substance keeps its own chemical properties, so a mixture can be separated by physical processes, which make no new substances. You need to know five: filtration, crystallisation, simple distillation, fractional distillation and chromatography.

Choosing one comes down to two questions. What is different about the substances: does one dissolve while the other does not, or do they boil at different temperatures? And which substance do you want to keep?

Command words in this lesson

Give
The answer only, no reasons: for a mixture, “not chemically combined” is the phrase that scores.
Suggest
Use the information given, such as boiling points, to choose a method you may not have seen used on that mixture.
Explain
Name the property that differs, then say what it lets you collect.
Plan
A method that would work: the steps in order, the apparatus, and what you end up with.

Key fact

The substances in a mixture are not chemically combined, so each keeps its own properties. Separation uses a difference in a physical property, such as solubility or boiling point, and no new substance is made.
