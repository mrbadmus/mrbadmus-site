Mixtures and separation techniques

  • mixture

Key knowledge

A mixture consists of two or more elements or compounds not chemically combined together.

The chemical properties of each substance in the mixture are unchanged.

Mixtures can be separated by physical processes: filtration, crystallisation, simple distillation, fractional distillation and chromatography. These do not involve chemical reactions, and no new substances are made.

The filter paper holds back the insoluble solid, called the residue. The liquid that runs through is the filtrate.

A dissolved solid passes through the paper with the water.

Heat the solution in an evaporating basin so that some of the water evaporates. Leave it to cool, and crystals form. Filter off the crystals and dry them.

Heat the solution. The water boils, and its vapour is cooled in the condenser. It condenses back to a liquid, the distillate. The dissolved salt stays in the flask.

This separates liquids that mix and have different boiling points.

The vapour rises up the fractionating column. The liquid with the lowest boiling point reaches the top first and is collected first. Ethanol boils at 78 °C and water at 100 °C, so ethanol is collected first.

Chromatography separates dissolved substances, such as the dyes in ink. Each substance moves a different distance up the paper, so they separate into spots. Rf = distance moved by substance ÷ distance moved by solvent. In the diagram, Rf = 3.6 ÷ 8.0 = 0.45.

To get pure, dry copper sulfate crystals from copper sulfate mixed with sand:

  • 1. Add water and stir, so the copper sulfate dissolves.
  • 2. Filter. The sand stays on the paper as the residue.
  • 3. Heat the filtrate to evaporate some of the water, then leave it to cool so crystals form.
  • 4. Filter off the crystals and dry them between filter papers.

  • Wrong: A fine enough filter paper catches dissolved salt.

    Right: Dissolved substances pass through any filter paper. Crystallise to get the salt back.

  • Wrong: Evaporating salt water collects the water.

    Right: The water escapes into the air. Distil it to collect the water.

  • Wrong: Separating a mixture makes new substances.

    Right: Physical processes make no new substances. Each substance comes back unchanged.

Key fact

A mixture is two or more elements or compounds that are not chemically combined, so each substance keeps its own chemical properties. Physical processes, such as filtration, crystallisation, distillation and chromatography, separate a mixture without making any new substances.

  • The thermometer bulb sits level with the side arm, so it reads the temperature of the vapour that is being collected.
  • Cold water goes into the condenser at the bottom and out at the top, so the condenser stays full of cold water.
  • Anti-bumping granules in the flask make the liquid boil smoothly.

Boiling a solution until it is dry can make it spit, and leaves a powder instead of crystals. Evaporate only some of the water, then let it cool.
