Atoms, elements and compounds

Every substance is atoms. So what makes one substance an element, another a compound and a third a mixture, and how can you tell from a drawing of its particles?

Start here

A metal that burns, a gas that poisons, a crystal you eat.

Sodium is a soft metal that reacts violently with water. Chlorine is a toxic yellow-green gas. When the two react, the product is sodium chloride: table salt, safe to sprinkle on food.

Commit first

Why is salt safe to eat when sodium and chlorine are not?

All substances are made of atoms. An atom is the smallest part of an element that can exist. An element contains only one type of atom. There are about 100 elements (118 are known today), each with a symbol in the periodic table: C is carbon, Fe is iron, Na is sodium.

A compound forms when two or more elements react and become chemically combined in fixed proportions. It is a new substance with different properties, and only a chemical reaction can separate it into its elements. A mixture is two or more elements or compounds not chemically combined: each keeps its chemical properties, the proportions can vary, and physical processes such as filtration or distillation separate it.

Every compound has a formula. The small number after a symbol counts the atoms of that element only: H₂SO₄ has 2 hydrogen, 1 sulfur and 4 oxygen atoms. A symbol equation shows reactants turning into products. Atoms are not created or destroyed in a reaction, only rearranged, so the equation must be balanced: the same number of each type of atom on both sides. Written as H₂ + O₂ → H₂O, hydrogen + oxygen → water is not balanced: 2 O on the left, 1 on the right.

Key fact

Reading a particle diagram: more than one kind of particle means a mixture. One kind, with one type of atom, means an element. One kind, with different atoms combined in the same ratio in every particle, means a compound.

In the mixture boxes the substances slide apart to show they are not chemically combined; a real separation needs a physical process such as filtration or distillation.
