Relative formula mass

Triple Science · Foundation tier
  • Combined Science · Foundation tier
  • Combined Science · Higher tier
  • Triple Science · Higher tier

Start here

A water molecule, H2O, has three atoms. An oxygen molecule, O2, has two.

If you had to guess, which has the bigger relative formula mass?

Key words

  • relative atomic mass (Ar)
  • relative formula mass (Mr)
  • percentage by mass

Key knowledge

Every element has a relative atomic mass, Ar. You read it from the periodic table you are given in the exam.

  • H = 1
  • C = 12
  • N = 14
  • O = 16
  • Na = 23
  • Mg = 24
  • Cl = 35.5
  • Ca = 40

The relative formula mass (Mr) of a compound is the sum of the relative atomic masses of the atoms in the numbers shown in the formula.

CO2 has 1 C and 2 O: Mr = 12 + (2 × 16) = 44

Mr has no unit.

A number after a bracket multiplies everything inside the bracket.

Ca(OH)2 has 1 Ca, 2 O and 2 H.

Mr = 40 + (2 × 16) + (2 × 1) = 74

In a balanced chemical equation, the sum of the relative formula masses of the reactants in the quantities shown equals the sum of the relative formula masses of the products in the quantities shown.

2Mg + O2 → 2MgO

Reactants: (2 × 24) + 32 = 80. Products: 2 × 40 = 80.

This is conservation of mass: the same atoms are on both sides.

percentage by mass of an element = (Ar × number of atoms of that element) ÷ Mr × 100

Oxygen in CO2: Mr = 44, and the two O atoms are 2 × 16 = 32.

32 ÷ 44 × 100 = 72.7%

  • Wrong: Ca(OH)2 has 1 O and 1 H.

    Right: The 2 after the bracket doubles everything inside: 2 O and 2 H.

  • Wrong: The Mr of MgO is 80, because the equation says 2MgO.

    Right: The Mr of MgO is 40. The 2 means two of them: 2 × 40 = 80 in the equation.

  • Wrong: Oxygen in CO2 is 16 ÷ 44 × 100.

    Right: Count every atom of the element. CO2 has two O, so use 2 × 16 = 32.

Key fact

The relative formula mass (Mr) of a compound is the sum of the relative atomic masses of the atoms in the numbers shown in the formula. In a balanced equation, the total Mr of the reactants equals the total Mr of the products, in the quantities shown.

  • Calculate: write each atom's Ar times how many there are, then add.
  • Explain: say which atoms are counted and why the totals match.
