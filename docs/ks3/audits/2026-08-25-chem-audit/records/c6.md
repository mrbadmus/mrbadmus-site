# C6 — Acids and alkalis — audit record

Auditor: unit agent C6 · 25 Aug 2026 · live pages vs committed build, both personas per lesson.

## Pre-audit deep think

Written before opening any page. Seven lessons: acids-and-alkalis, the-ph-scale-and-indicators, neutralisation, acid-plus-metal, acids-and-carbonates, making-a-pure-dry-salt, catalysts.

### Misconceptions this topic classically breeds

1. **"Acid = dangerous, alkali = safe."** Students meet "acid" through cartoons (burns through floors). They rarely know strong alkalis (oven cleaner, drain cleaner) are at least as corrosive. A lesson that only shows acid hazards plants this. Watch for hazard symmetry.
2. **"Concentrated = strong."** At KS3 we can't do the strong/weak dissociation story, but a lesson can still avoid entrenching the conflation. Watch for "strong acid" used loosely to mean concentrated, and for a pH simulation where "more acid" is described as "stronger".
3. **"Neutralisation always gives pH 7" / "neutral means water only".** If you add the wrong amounts you overshoot; also neutralisation makes a SALT + water, not "nothing". Watch a neutralisation sim: does adding excess alkali keep the meter at 7 (wrong model) or overshoot past 7 (honest)?
4. **"pH measures how much acid there is"** rather than acidity/alkalinity of the solution; also "pH scale stops at 1 and 14" as hard walls, and the idea it's linear (each step ×10 is KS4-adjacent — absence is fine, but claiming pH 1 is "twice as acidic" as pH 2 would be wrong).
5. **Universal indicator vs litmus blur** — "indicator turns red in acid" as one undifferentiated fact. Litmus gives red/blue only; UI gives the spectrum. Diagrams that show litmus paper with an orange colour, or UI described as "the indicator", breed the blur.
6. **"The bubbles in acid + metal are made of air"** or of CO2. Acid + metal gives HYDROGEN; acid + carbonate gives CARBON DIOXIDE. If either lesson's sim just draws generic bubbles with no gas-test payoff (squeaky pop vs limewater), the two reactions merge in memory.
7. **"The metal/carbonate dissolves"** rather than reacts. "Dissolves" implies you could evaporate the acid and get the metal back. Watch every use of "dissolve" in lessons 4–6 — in lesson 6 (making a salt) "excess copper oxide" genuinely is filtered off, and the copper sulfate crystals come FROM the reaction, not from dissolved metal.
8. **Mass/matter not conserved** — gas "disappears", or the salt "appears". Watch for wording that lets atoms vanish.
9. **"Salt = table salt".** In this unit "salt" is a class of compounds. If lesson 6 never disarms the everyday meaning, students hear "making a pure dry salt" as making NaCl for chips.
10. **Naming pattern errors**: hydrochloric → chlorides, sulfuric → sulfates, nitric → nitrates. Easy to state, easy for a page to typo ("sulphate", "hydrochloride"). Also "sulphur/sulphuric" vs the required UK-AQA "sulfur/sulfuric".
11. **Catalysts (lesson 7)**: the classic wrongs — "catalysts are used up", "catalysts make more product", "catalysts take part in nothing" (they do take part, they're just regenerated — at KS3 the honest line is "not used up at the end"). Also catalyst ≠ enzyme confusion, and "speeds up" morphing into "makes the reaction happen that otherwise couldn't". Odd placement: catalysts in an acids unit — presumably via decomposition of hydrogen peroxide or acid+metal rate. Check the lesson justifies its slot and covers NC ground (KS3 NC: "catalysts speed up reactions but are unchanged and not used up").

### Models hard to render honestly

- A pH meter/indicator sim where the colour is a lookup from a slider: fine, but if the slider is labelled "amount of acid" mapping linearly to pH it teaches a wrong quantitative model.
- Neutralisation animation: if H+ + OH- particles are shown at KS3 that's over-pitch; if instead "acid blob + alkali blob = water" it risks "cancel out into nothing". The honest KS3 render: mixing, indicator colour change, and a salt left on evaporation.
- Bubbles in sims: bubble = gas from the reaction, must stop when metal/carbonate or acid runs out. A sim that bubbles forever teaches infinite reactant.
- Crystallisation: if the sim shows boiling to dryness giving nice crystals, that's wrong practice (spitting, decomposed/powdery product) — correct model is evaporate to point of crystallisation then leave to cool.

### Words that trip an 11-year-old

corrosive vs irritant (and "dilute" as verb/adjective), alkali vs alkaline vs base (KS3 usually ducks "base" — if "base" appears it must be introduced), neutral vs neutralise, indicator, universal, concentration, excess, filtrate/residue, saturated, soluble/insoluble, effervescence (if used, must be glossed as fizzing), "salt" (see above), catalyst/catalyse. Also symbol-adjacent traps: CO2 vs CO, H2 vs H (protocol: flat formulae are ruled fine — don't report).

### Where a sim could work perfectly yet teach the wrong model

- pH slider that lets pure water be reached by "diluting acid forever" (dilution asymptotes toward 7, never crosses — if the sim crosses to 8+ by dilution, wrong).
- Neutralisation that snaps to 7 regardless of volume added (misconception 3).
- Acid+metal sim where ALL metals react (copper should NOT fizz in dilute acid — if a sim lets copper + HCl bubble, that's an S1).
- Catalyst sim where the catalyst bar visibly depletes, or where product amount increases with catalyst.
- Indicator sim where universal indicator in a strong acid shows a colour keyed to "red = acid" but the same red for pH 1 and pH 6.

### Safety flags to expect (flag for Mide, never rewrite)

Eye protection with all acids/alkalis; corrosive vs irritant at the dilutions students actually use; heating acids; squeaky pop test scale; crystallising dish over a beaker of water not direct boiling; hydrogen peroxide + catalyst (if used in L7). Any practical description missing goggles wording gets flagged for sign-off.

### Fairness predictions for the ladder

Naming-the-salt questions require the acid→salt table to be ON the page. Gas-test questions require the test stated. Any question about "bases" fails fairness if the lesson only taught "alkalis". Catalyst questions must not require KS4 activation-energy language.
