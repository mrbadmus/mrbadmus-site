## Pre-audit deep think

Written BEFORE opening any page.

**Misconceptions this topic classically breeds**

1. **"Cold" as a substance.** Endothermic work invites "the reaction gives out
   cold" / "cold flows into your hand". Honest model: energy is taken IN from
   the surroundings, so the surroundings cool. Any wording like "makes cold"
   or a drawing with blue "cold arrows" flowing outward is an S1.
2. **Heat vs temperature conflation.** Temperature is not energy. A lesson
   that uses "heat" and "temperature" interchangeably ("the reaction gets
   hotter, so it has more heat") plants the KS4-costing error. Watch every
   sentence in lesson 04 especially — it is literally about the difference
   between measuring a temperature change and inferring an energy transfer.
3. **Energy created/destroyed.** Exothermic "makes energy"; endothermic "uses
   up energy". Conservation must survive every sentence: energy is
   *transferred to/from the surroundings*, never produced or consumed.
4. **System/surroundings boundary.** In a solution reaction the thermometer
   sits IN the mixture; the water is part of the surroundings receiving the
   energy. Hard to render honestly — a sim showing the thermometer reading
   rising while arrows point *into* the beaker labelled "energy in" would
   teach exothermic backwards. Test every arrow against the direction of
   transfer, not against whether the animation runs.
5. **Changes of state — the KS3 killer set:**
   - Particles themselves melting/expanding/softening (particles are
     unchanged; the *arrangement and movement* change).
   - "Bonds break when ice melts" — at KS3 the honest wording is forces of
     attraction *between* particles are weakened/overcome, NOT chemical bonds
     breaking (that phrasing collides with C5/C6 bonding language).
   - Temperature keeps rising while a substance melts/boils. A heating-curve
     drawing or sim that has no flat plateau at the change of state — or worse,
     a sloped one — is an S1.
   - Particles stop dead when frozen (they still vibrate).
   - Melting vs dissolving blur (ruled a classic; C7 lesson 01 borders it).
6. **Condensing/freezing release energy** — students accept energy-in for
   melting but rarely meet the reverse. If lesson 01 only does the "energy in"
   half, that's a curriculum/pitch gap.
7. **Exothermic = fire.** If the only exothermic examples are combustion,
   students bind "exothermic" to flames. Need neutralisation/oxidation
   (hand-warmers, self-heating cans) too. Conversely: ignition energy needed
   to *start* burning ≠ the reaction being endothermic.
8. **Sign/direction of ΔT in lesson 04.** "Temperature change = final −
   start" going negative for endothermic — is a negative number handled
   honestly, or fudged? Year 7s meet negatives shakily; a scaffold step is
   needed, not avoidance.

**Models hard to render honestly**

- A particle-view state-change animation: particle SIZE must stay constant,
  spacing in liquid ≈ solid (NOT double), gas spacing large; motion never
  stops at solid. Sims fail here while "working perfectly".
- An energy-transfer diagram for exo/endo: arrows must cross a
  system/surroundings boundary in the correct direction, and the thermometer
  must live in the surroundings side of the story.
- A heating curve with plateaus — and the honest caveat that the plateau is
  where energy goes into weakening attractions, not raising temperature.

**Words that trip an 11-year-old**

exothermic/endothermic (which is which — is a memory hook given? "exo = exit"),
surroundings, transferred, thermal, conserved, latent (should NOT appear at
KS3), sublimation (fine if defined), precise/accurate (lesson 04 — these are
different words and KS3 uses them loosely at its peril), "reproducible",
"anomalous". Also °C spoken as "degrees Celsius" consistently.

**Where a simulation could teach the WRONG model while working perfectly**

- Exothermic sim: temperature slider/readout rises — but if nothing shows
  energy LEAVING the chemicals into the water, students read "the reaction
  heats up" as the reaction *gaining* energy. Direction unshown = direction
  wrong.
- Endothermic sim: beaker turns blue/icy — colour-coding cold as a blue
  substance radiating outward.
- State-change sim: heating slider makes particles spread instantly and
  uniformly (solid→gas with no liquid), or particles grow, or vibration stops
  at 0 °C.
- Measuring sim: thermometer reads instantly and exactly — hides why we wait
  for the max/min temperature, why we stir, why polystyrene cups.

**Pitch traps**: Q=mcΔT, bond-energy calculations, "enthalpy", ΔH, energy
level diagrams with activation energy are all KS4 — flag if present. Pitched
too low: only "hot things feel hot" with no particle model would bore.

**Safety flags to expect**: lesson 04 is a practical (acids? burning? hot
water?) — safety wording is FLAG-for-Mide territory, never rewritten by me.
