Auditor: unit agent for C8, 25 Aug 2026. Live URLs `https://mrbadmus.com/ks3/chemistry/the-periodic-table/<lesson>`; local committed build `mrbadmus_site/ks3/chemistry/the-periodic-table/`. Seven lessons audited in order.

## Pre-audit deep think

Written before opening any page.

### Misconceptions this topic classically breeds

1. **"Metals are hard/shiny/heavy things" — property lists as identity.** Students classify by everyday feel: mercury (liquid) and sodium (soft, dull when tarnished) break the naive list; carbon as graphite conducts and is shiny-ish yet is a non-metal. If lesson 1 teaches metals as a fixed checklist of properties without exceptions, it plants "every metal is hard and strong" and "every non-metal is a dull gas". Watch for whether the lesson handles exceptions honestly (mercury, graphite, iodine's shine) or dodges them.
2. **"The periodic table is a list of substances", not elements.** Year 7s conflate elements with everyday materials — "steel is on the periodic table", "water is an element". Any lesson that says "everything is made of elements in the table" without distinguishing element/compound/mixture invites it.
3. **Mendeleev myths.** Common textbook slop: (a) "Mendeleev invented the periodic table from nothing" — Newlands/Döbereiner erased; acceptable at KS3 to focus on Mendeleev, but not to say nobody else tried; (b) "he arranged by atomic number" — FALSE, he arranged by atomic weight/mass and swapped some (Te/I) on properties; atomic number ordering is Moseley, later. A KS3 lesson that retro-fits atomic number onto Mendeleev is an S1. (c) "gaps predicted new elements" is right — but check the predicted elements named (eka-silicon → germanium, eka-aluminium → gallium) are correctly matched.
4. **Groups/periods swapped.** Columns=groups, rows=periods is arbitrary to a child and constantly reversed. Also "group number = number of shells" (wrong; that's period). Watch every diagram: does the highlighted band in a "group" figure actually run vertically?
5. **"Reactivity always increases down a group."** True for Group 1, REVERSED for Group 7. If the alkali-metals lesson stamps "down = more reactive" as a periodic-table law and the halogens lesson doesn't explicitly counter it, students carry the wrong generalisation. The two lessons must talk to each other.
6. **Electron-shell over-reach.** KS3 NC does not require electronic structure; "why groups exist" (lesson 6) is the danger zone: explaining group behaviour via outer electrons is fine as a taster but can slide into KS4 language (ions, oxidation, "wants to lose an electron" anthropomorphism — "atoms WANT full shells" is itself a notorious misconception/teleology). Watch for "atoms want/like/are happy".
7. **Group 0 naming.** "Noble gases are inert because they have 8 outer electrons" — helium has 2; if a diagram shows helium with 8, that's an S1. Also "Group 8" vs "Group 0" inconsistency.
8. **Halogen states/colours.** Classic error set: chlorine green-yellow gas, bromine red-brown LIQUID, iodine grey-black SOLID that sublimes to purple vapour — often mangled ("iodine is a purple liquid"). Also "halogens are all poisonous gases".
9. **Alkali metal demos.** Sodium-in-water: fizzing is HYDROGEN, not oxygen or "air"; the metal is not "dissolving", it's reacting; lithium/sodium/potassium order and flame colours (Li crimson, Na yellow/orange, K lilac) get shuffled. Safety wording matters here — a practical that reads like an invitation to try it is a sign-off flag.
10. **Oxides lesson (7).** The load-bearing pattern: metal oxides basic/alkaline, non-metal oxides acidic. Classic errors: "metal oxides are alkalis" (only soluble ones make alkaline solutions — most metal oxides are insoluble bases); "all non-metal oxides are acidic" (CO and H2O aren't); litmus/universal-indicator colour mix-ups; "amphoteric" is KS4+, shouldn't appear. Also state that oxygen is FROM THE AIR in combustion, and mass increase on burning magnesium — mass-conservation link.

### Models hard to render honestly

- **A miniature periodic table graphic.** If the page draws a simplified table, does the layout have the right shape (Group 1 far left with H ambiguous, transition block, Group 0 far right)? Hydrogen placed in Group 1 without comment implies H is an alkali metal. A drawn table with the wrong number of columns/wrong element order is an S1 that a green gate never catches.
- **Reactivity simulations.** A sim where "bigger fizz = further down group 1" works perfectly and teaches rightly; but if the sim shows the metal sinking (they float), or bubbles labelled "oxygen", or potassium NOT igniting while text says it does — model mismatch.
- **Shell diagrams** (lesson 6): electron dots must count correctly (Li 2,1; Na 2,8,1; Cl 2,8,7; He 2; Ne 2,8). Any miscount is S1.
- **Displacement/colour chemistry** in halogens at KS3 — if present, must keep colours right.

### Words that trip an 11-year-old

"Malleable", "ductile", "sonorous", "brittle", "lustrous" — property words needing plain-English glosses at first use. "Tarnish", "dense/density" (Year 7 may not have met density formally), "inert", "sublime", "corrosive vs irritant", "alkali vs alkaline vs alkali metal" (three near-identical words, genuinely confusing), "period" (already means a school lesson and menstruation — needs a clean definition sentence), "group 0 vs zero as nothing". "Atomic number" arrives here before atomic structure — how is it defined without protons? If protons are invoked, is that pitched as a taster or assumed?

### Where a simulation could teach the WRONG model while working perfectly

- A sortable metal/non-metal classifier that accepts only stereotype answers (marks graphite non-conducting, marks mercury "not a metal because liquid").
- A group-1 water sim whose vigour scale tops out at sodium, implying potassium ~ sodium.
- A periodic-table explorer where clicking a period highlights a column (axis swap) — works, animates, teaches backwards.
- A trend slider that interpolates reactivity linearly and shows Group 7 increasing downward.
- An oxides pH sim where every metal oxide turns universal indicator purple (teaching "metal oxide = strong alkali") rather than distinguishing soluble/insoluble.

### Curriculum slot check (KS3 NC chemistry — Periodic Table strand)

NC requires: the varying physical/chemical properties of elements; principles underpinning Mendeleev's table; the modern table as ordered by atomic number and its use to predict properties; metals/non-metals; group patterns for Group 1, Group 7, Group 0 (properties of metals and non-metals; how patterns in reactions can be predicted with reference to the periodic table). This unit's 7 lessons map plausibly onto all of it. KS3.C.PT.06 is a KNOWN ruled gap — not to be rediscovered. Watch instead for: is "using the table to PREDICT properties" actually exercised anywhere (a predict-then-check activity), or only stated?

### Cross-lesson consistency to check

- Reactivity direction stated in L4 vs L5 (must be opposite and explicitly contrasted).
- "Group 0" naming consistent across L3, L6.
- Atomic number: L2 (Mendeleev used MASS) vs L3 (modern table uses atomic number) — the handover between the two must be explicit or students conflate them.
- Property vocabulary introduced in L1 reused consistently in L7.
