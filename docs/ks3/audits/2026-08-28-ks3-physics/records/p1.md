## Pre-audit deep think

Written in full BEFORE opening a single page, from the lesson slugs alone.

P1 is the unit the brief singles out. Energy is the one topic in school physics
where the *vocabulary itself* is the physics: the 2014 National Curriculum and
the Institute of Physics' energy guidance deliberately replaced the primary
"types/forms of energy" model with **stores and transfers**, and AQA's GCSE
mark schemes police the difference. A KS3 energy unit that teaches the old
model does not merely use dated words — it installs a mental model that KS4
then has to demolish, in the one topic where demolition is hardest because the
old model *works* for answering easy questions. So the central question of this
audit is not "is anything factually false" but "does this unit hold one model,
in every sentence, on every page, in every caption and every feedback line".

### The model the unit must hold

- **Stores** (things a system HAS, at a moment): kinetic; gravitational
  (gravitational potential); elastic (elastic potential); thermal (internal);
  chemical; magnetic; electrostatic; nuclear. Eight is the full list; a KS3
  unit may honestly teach a subset, but it must not present a *different*
  list as complete.
- **Transfers / pathways** (how energy moves BETWEEN stores, over an
  interval): mechanically (a force doing work); electrically (a current doing
  work); by heating (a temperature difference); by radiation (light, sound and
  other waves).
- **Light, sound, electrical and heat are NOT stores.** They are pathways or
  processes. "Light energy", "sound energy", "heat energy", "electrical
  energy (store)" and "movement energy" are the five phrases that mark the old
  model.
- **Heat is a verb, not a noun.** You heat something; there is no "heat" in an
  object. What is in the object is a thermal (internal) store. "Heat energy",
  "the heat in the water", "heat rises", "heat rays" are all the caloric
  model wearing modern clothes.
- **Energy is never converted, changed into, turned into, or used up.** It is
  *transferred* from one store to another. "Converted" is the arrow-chain
  model's verb and it smuggles the forms model back in.
- **Energy is never lost.** It is *dissipated* — transferred to the thermal
  store of the surroundings, where it is spread too thinly to be useful. A
  unit that says "conservation of energy" on page 3 and "energy is lost as
  heat" on page 7 has contradicted itself in a way a bright child WILL notice
  and a weak child will resolve by deciding physics is arbitrary.

### The single sentence most often taught wrong in British classrooms

> **"The energy is converted into heat energy and lost."**

Every one of its three clauses is wrong under the model the same teacher
taught ten minutes earlier: *converted* (should be transferred), *heat energy*
(there is no such store; heating is a pathway, thermal is the store), *lost*
(dissipated — conservation forbids loss). It survives because it is short, it
sounds explanatory, and it lets a student answer a question without
understanding anything. Runners-up, all of which I will hunt by name:

- "Heat rises." (Hot, less dense *fluid* rises. Heat is not a thing and does
  not go anywhere on its own.)
- "Metal feels colder than wood, so it is colder." (Rate of transfer, not
  temperature — and the classic is that they are at the SAME temperature.)
- "A jumper keeps the cold out." / "Insulation keeps heat in." (An insulator
  reduces the RATE of transfer; nothing keeps cold out because cold is not a
  substance that comes in.)
- "A lever multiplies your energy / gives you extra force for free."
  (It multiplies FORCE and divides DISTANCE; the energy in cannot be beaten.)
- "Radiation is dangerous / radiation is nuclear."
- "The two objects will end up at the temperature half way between."
  (Only if `m × c` is equal on both sides.)

### Misconceptions a real KS3 class arrives with

1. **Energy is a fuel-like substance that runs out.** "The battery has no
   energy left." Directly contradicts conservation; the unit's lesson 3 must
   confront it explicitly or the conservation lesson is words only.
2. **Energy = activity, effort or liveliness.** A stationary compressed spring
   or a raised brick "has no energy" because nothing is happening. This is the
   misconception the *stores* language exists to kill, and the reason
   "before and after" snapshots beat arrow chains.
3. **The primary forms list, memorised.** Most Year 7s arrive able to recite
   nine "types". If lesson 1 opens by asking "what types of energy do you
   know?" it re-installs the model before correcting it.
4. **Gravitational store belongs to the object.** It belongs to the
   object–Earth *system*; at KS3 "the gravity store of the ball and the Earth
   together" is a mouthful, so units usually say "the ball's gravity store" —
   acceptable shorthand, but it must not later be used to argue the ball
   *contains* something.
5. **Caloric.** Heat as a fluid that flows in and out; **cold as a substance**
   that comes in when you open a door. Watch for "lets the cold in", "the cold
   gets through", "keeps the cold out".
6. **Temperature IS energy.** Two objects at the same temperature contain the
   same energy; a bath at 40 °C has less energy than a cup at 90 °C. This is
   the thermal-equilibrium lesson's ground and, per the brief, P11's too —
   the two units must not disagree.
7. **Metals are intrinsically cold.** The touch test.
8. **Insulators generate warmth.** A coat warms you; wrapping ice in a coat
   melts it faster. The honest demonstration (ice in a coat lasts LONGER) is
   the single best KS3 thermal demo and its absence would be a real gap.
9. **Conduction happens because metal is hard/dense/solid** — rather than
   vibration passed particle-to-particle, plus (in metals) delocalised free
   electrons that carry it far faster. A conduction lesson with no particle
   mechanism is hand-waving even if every sentence in it is true.
10. **Radiation = nuclear, or = "heat rays".** Also the reverse error:
    radiation confused with convection (both are "hot air moving" to a child).
    The discriminator that must be present: radiation needs **no medium** —
    it crosses a vacuum, which is how the Sun reaches us; conduction and
    convection cannot.
11. **Convection: "hot air rises".** The honest version is density: the warmed
    fluid expands, becomes less dense, and is *pushed up* by the denser cold
    fluid sinking around it.
12. **Machines create energy / make work smaller.** They trade force against
    distance; work in ≥ work out, always, and the difference goes to the
    thermal store by friction. Efficiency >100% and perpetual motion.
13. **"Energy is conserved, except the bit that's lost."** The unit will say
    both if it is not disciplined.

### Where AQA's language differs from everyday language

Every one of these is a word the unit will use and a Year 7 already "knows":

| word | everyday meaning a child brings | physics meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **energy** | liveliness, fuel, electricity | a conserved accounting quantity, in joules |
| **store** | a shop | a way of counting how much energy a system has |
| **transfer** | move house, move schools | energy moving between stores |
| **heat** | a noun: the hot stuff | a verb: a process, a pathway |
| **work** | a job, homework, effort | force × distance; a measure of energy transferred |
| **power** | strength; the mains | rate of transfer, joules per second, watts |
| **conductor** | orchestra, bus, lightning rod | material that transfers thermally/electrically well |
| **insulator** | loft insulation only | material that transfers poorly |
| **radiation** | nuclear, dangerous | energy carried by EM waves |
| **efficient** | quick, good at something | useful out ÷ total in |
| **machine** | something with a motor | any device that changes a force |
| **system** | a set-up, a scheme | the chosen thing you are accounting for |

A KS3 unit that does not *warn* about at least **work**, **power**, **heat**
and **radiation** is leaving the child's everyday meaning in place under a new
label. I will check each at first use.

### What diagrams of this topic usually get wrong

- **Arrow chains** ("chemical energy → kinetic energy → heat energy") — the
  forms model as a picture. The correct KS3 drawing is a **before/after pair
  of store bars**, not a chain.
- **Sankey diagrams** whose arrow widths are not to scale, or whose out-total
  does not equal the in-total, or which label branches as "types".
- **Conduction** drawn as a red gradient creeping along a bar with no
  particles, or as "heat" drawn as an object moving. The honest drawing shows
  particles vibrating harder at the hot end and jostling neighbours, plus free
  electrons in a metal.
- **Radiation** drawn as squiggly arrows labelled "heat rays" leaving a fire,
  with no indication that they cross a vacuum and no mention of infrared.
- **Convection** drawn as a tidy loop with an arrow labelled "hot air rises"
  and no density argument.
- **Vacuum flask cutaways** with the wrong feature against the wrong
  mechanism — the classic error being "the vacuum stops radiation" (it does
  not; the vacuum stops conduction and convection, the silvering reduces
  radiation, the stopper stops evaporation and convection).
- **Levers** drawn with effort and load arrows the same length, no pivot
  distances marked, and no indication that the effort moves further.
- **Thermal equilibrium** drawn as both bodies meeting at the midpoint
  temperature regardless of mass.
- Anything drawn "to scale" that is not (chemistry's SYS-6, checked here).

### Where a simulation could behave perfectly and teach the wrong model

- A **store bar chart** that lets the total change between "before" and
  "after" — a conservation sim that does not conserve.
- A **conduction rod** whose colour front advances at a speed set by a slider
  rather than by anything particle-like: cosmetically perfect, causally empty.
  Worse: one where the metal is fast and the wood is slow with no mechanism
  offered, so the child concludes "metal is magic".
- A **radiation sim** where the receiver warms only when there is air between,
  or where black surfaces absorb but are never shown to also *emit* better.
- An **insulation bench** with a fixed caption ("the lagged beaker stays
  warmest") that is false at some reachable setting — chemistry's SYS-5, which
  hit five units there.
- A **lever/pulley bench** showing the force falling with no matching rise in
  distance, or reporting an efficiency at or above 100%.
- A **thermal-equilibrium bench** that always meets in the middle.
- Any bench with a **"lost" readout** — a number labelled lost in a unit whose
  third lesson is conservation.

### Unit-structure risks specific to THIS slot (from the slugs alone)

- **Convection appears in no lesson title.** Conduction, radiation and
  insulation are there; convection is not. The KS3 programme of study names
  "energy transfer by conduction, convection and radiation" together. If
  convection is not taught inside another P1 lesson (most plausibly
  `insulation` or `heating-and-thermal-equilibrium`) or elsewhere in the
  physics course, that is a genuine curriculum-coverage finding, and it also
  makes the radiation lesson harder to teach honestly — radiation is normally
  defined *against* the other two. **First thing I will check.**
- **`simple-machines` sits inside an energy unit.** Defensible — it is the
  cleanest demonstration that energy cannot be beaten — but it needs
  `work = force × distance`. If nothing in P1 defines work, the lesson can
  only assert the trade-off. Prerequisite check required.
- **Does P1 quantify anything?** The NC expects joules, comparisons of energy
  values, power ratings and fuel/domestic-bill contexts. Eight lessons with
  no number in joules would be a pitch finding. The brief specifically asks
  whether **insulation quantifies anything, and honestly** — payback times and
  savings figures are the classic place a KS3 page invents a number.
- **`heating-and-thermal-equilibrium` vs P11.** Temperature/thermal-store
  ground is shared. If the two units define temperature differently, or one
  says "heat energy" and the other does not, that is an S1-grade
  inconsistency by the brief's own terms.
- **Ordering:** stores → before/after → conservation → heating → conduction →
  radiation → insulation → machines is a sound sequence. Risk points: does
  lesson 1 assume "system"? Does lesson 2's before/after need conservation
  (taught after it)? Does `insulation` depend on convection (possibly never
  taught)?

### The auditor's summary

**P1 holds the AQA stores-and-transfers model better than any unit I expected to
find, and its worst failures are in its numbers rather than its words.** The
phrase "heat energy" does not occur once in eight lessons; "types of energy" and
"forms of energy" do not occur; every appearance of "light energy", "sound
energy" or "electrical energy" is inside a sentence the page is in the act of
demolishing; and every bare noun-*heat* is either a quoted misconception, the
caloric history, or a fixed term of art. Lessons 2, 3, 6 and 7 are, in their
prose, as good as school physics writing gets — the second-law stretch on L2,
the Pauli/neutrino stretch on L3, Olbers' paradox on L6 and the whole-house
close on L7 are all correct, all pitched right, and none of them condescends.

The three findings that matter most are all cases of an instrument contradicting
the lesson wrapped around it:

1. **P1-13** — the two-quantities bench shows a spark at 1500 °C holding two and
   a half times the energy of a 100 kg bath at 40 °C, which is the negation of
   the hook, the think-again, the rung-2 feedback and the bench's own closing
   sentence, all of which are on the same page.
2. **P1-14** — the same lesson defines temperature as "average speed" while
   P11, which these students meet a term *earlier*, defines it as "average
   kinetic energy" and calls the quantity "internal energy" rather than "thermal
   store". Two names and two definitions for one idea, with no bridge in either
   direction from the later lesson.
3. **P1-25 with P1-24** — the simple-machines lesson teaches E = F × d and then
   prints a table whose own rows fail it, alongside a sentence saying the output
   column exceeds the input column on the page that exists to forbid exactly
   that.

None of the twenty-eight needs a rebuild. Eighteen are one-sentence or
one-constant edits; P1-13 and P1-25 need a constant set and a small engine
change each; P1-10 is a Design brief; P1-26 is a shared-drawer geometry fix that
should be taken once for P1 and P2 together. Fix those and this unit is the one
the rest of the physics estate should be measured against.
