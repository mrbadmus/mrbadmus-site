### The three worth a paragraph

**P8 — "SUMMER TERM" was not welded. It was computed, and computed wrongly.**
The brief says these names are authored as literals. They were not; that had
already been fixed. The term came out of a function that looked at the calendar
**month and nothing else** — right for eleven months a year, and wrong for the
one that matters. Today is 21 August, month 8, so it fell through to Summer —
while the class it was labelling belongs to **2026-27, a year that has not
started**, whose first week is Autumn. It now asks the academic year, anchored
on that year's own start date, so the holidays read as Autumn Week 1 exactly as
you ruled. Proved on twelve dated cases including both sides of every boundary
and the midnight-on-1-September edge.

**P9 — the spine's "now" was three fours, not one.** The brief names the dot.
It did not name `trough`, which shades weeks 1-4 as **already elapsed** — so a
class in week 1 drew three weeks of school that have not happened in the "done"
tone, directly under the wrong dot. Found by reading the whole block instead of
the line the brief pointed at.

**P2 — the counter was already right, and the brief's premise is half wrong.**
"01/02" was **correct**: one covered lesson gives two recall rungs, and the
round is already `min(6, pool)`. Nothing about the round needed changing, and it
returns to six by itself when the Physics and C4+ banks land — which is the
ruling's own test. What was wrong was the page *announcing* six in four places,
every one of which spells the number as a **word**, which is why no search for
a digit had ever found them. And "STREAK BROKEN" greeted a student who had not
answered anything: zero meant both "no streak" and "a broken one". A first wrong
answer is not a broken streak either — nothing was built, so nothing broke.
