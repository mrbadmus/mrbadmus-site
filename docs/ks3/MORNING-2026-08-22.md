## What a student can do this morning that they could not yesterday

**They can do their homework, and it is safe from the first question.**

Yesterday the assignment page could not open at all on this week's real work, and the button
at the end of it wrote a made-up date and sent nothing anywhere. Both pages were previews.

As of this morning `student/class.html` and `student/assignment.html` are **live on
mrbadmus.com**, wired to real data, and the model underneath them is the one you ruled:

- **Every answer saves the moment it is given.** Not at the end, not in the browser. The
  moment a student confirms question one, a row exists with their answer in it.
- **Progress is visible all week** — "3 of 4 answered", and the same as a percentage — to the
  student, and now to you.
- **The button says Complete, and it does not save anything**, because saving already happened.
  It marks the work finished and stamps the *server's* clock.
- **Nothing is locked by the deadline.** A student can still answer and still complete after
  Thursday; it is simply recorded as late. And a student who answered everything but never
  pressed Complete is *unfinished*, not late.
- **A second go is kept, and the better one counts.**

I drove all of it as a real student, in a browser, against production. The strongest single
piece of evidence is one row:

```
attempt 1   started_at  12:55:44      ← written by the FIRST ANSWER
            completed_at 13:01:06     ← written by the button, six minutes later
            is_late false   score 1/4   4 answers
```

Those two timestamps being six minutes apart is the whole ruling working. Under the old page
there was no first timestamp at all, because nothing was written until the end — and then it
was written wrong.
