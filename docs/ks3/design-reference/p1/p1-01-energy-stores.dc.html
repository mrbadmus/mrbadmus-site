Energy stores

Roll a ball across a carpet and it stops. The energy it had is gone. Except that nothing in physics is ever gone — so where is it?

Draft — not yet science-reviewed.

Start here

The ball stops. The energy does not.

A ball rolls across a carpet, slows, and stops. Before, it was moving and had energy. After, it is still. Nothing hit it and nothing took it away.

Commit to where the energy went.

    •

Into the carpet and the ball, as a very small rise in temperature — a few thousandths of a degree, spread across a wide area, far too little to feel but exactly enough to account for every joule. A sensitive enough thermometer would find it. "Used up" is what it looks like; "moved somewhere hard to notice" is what happened.

Energy is not a substance and it is not a fuel. It is a number — a quantity you can calculate for a situation, which comes out the same before and after no matter what happened in between. To keep track of that number you need somewhere to write it down, and the places you write it down are called stores. This lesson is about learning the list, and about one distinction that trips up almost everyone.

Key fact

Energy is never used up or created. It moves between stores, and the total is always the same before and after.

!

Think again

“A torch turns electrical energy into light energy and sound energy.”

Every word of that is in common use and two of them are not stores at all. Sort each one before you read on.

{{ card.name }}

{{ card.note }}

A store is somewhere energy sits and can be counted while nothing is happening. Pause the world and a stretched spring still has its energy; a hot cup still has its energy. A pathway is a way energy travels from one store to another. Pause the world and there is no light in transit to count — light is a journey, not a destination.

So the torch does not turn electrical energy into light energy. It empties a chemical store in the battery and fills a thermal store in the room, and the light and the electric current are the two routes by which it happens. Saying it properly takes longer, and it is the difference between describing what happened and just naming things.

“Energy gets used up when you use it.”

Nothing is used up. Every joule that goes into a torch is still there afterwards — it has simply ended in the thermal store of the bulb, the air and the room, spread so thinly across so many particles that no useful transfer can be got out of it again. That is what people mean by wasted energy: not destroyed, dissipated. The distinction matters because it is the difference between a quantity that is conserved and one that is not, and only one of those lets you predict anything.

Key fact

Energy does not come in types and is never a substance. It sits in stores — kinetic, gravitational, chemical, thermal, elastic — and a transfer moves it between them. Light and sound are pathways along which energy travels, not stores it can sit in.

Key note

Eight stores: kinetic, gravitational, elastic, thermal, chemical, magnetic, electrostatic, nuclear. Light, sound, electricity and heating are pathways — routes between stores, not places energy sits.

Going further

Nobody has ever seen energy. There is no instrument that detects it, no bottle you could keep some in, and no experiment that shows it directly — you only ever measure masses, speeds, temperatures and heights, and then calculate. What makes it worth having is a single strange fact: whatever situation you pick, however complicated, if you add up that calculated number before and after, you get the same answer. Nobody knows why the universe should have a quantity like that, and the physicist Richard Feynman was blunt about it — energy is not a mechanism or a substance, it is a number that happens not to change. That is all it is, and it is one of the most useful things ever discovered.

Ask about this lesson

The store audit is a teaching model. The eight stores listed are the set used at KS3; a fuller treatment describes magnetic and electrostatic energy as belonging to fields rather than to objects, and nuclear energy as a difference in mass. Each scenario names the store an object’s energy is chiefly in and ignores the small amounts held in every other store at the same moment — a raised ball has a thermal store too, and it is not what the question is about. Light and sound are treated throughout as pathways rather than stores, which is the convention this course follows.
