Types of reaction · Classify

Which reaction is this?

Four types, and nobody labels them for you. What do you look at first — and what do you do with a reaction that fits none of them?

Draft — not yet science-reviewed.

Start here

Four reaction types. One of them gets heavier, one gets lighter, one glows and one changes colour in a beaker.

You have met combustion, thermal decomposition, oxidation and displacement. In an exam nobody tells you which one you are looking at — you are given what happened and expected to name it.

Which single question sorts them fastest?

    •

How many reactants there are, and what they are. One reactant means decomposition — nothing else does that. Two, one of which is oxygen means oxidation, and combustion if it burns. A metal and a compound of another metal means displacement. Count first, name second.

The four types are not four unrelated facts. Combustion is a special case of oxidation — the fast, flaming kind. Decomposition is the only one that runs a compound backwards. Displacement is the only one where a metal has a rival.

So the useful test is structural, not visual. Look at the equation, not the drama.

Reference · the decision rule

Four questions, in this order

  1. Is there only one reactant? Then it is thermal decomposition, if heat did it.
  2. Is one reactant a metal and the other a compound containing a different metal? Then it is displacement.
  3. Is one reactant oxygen, and did it burn with a flame? Then it is combustion.
  4. Is one reactant oxygen, with no flame? Then it is oxidation — and note that the answer to 3 is also oxidation, just a more specific name for it.

Mass is the tie-breaker when you are unsure. Gaining oxygen makes the product heavier. Losing a gas makes what is left lighter. Both of those are measurable, and neither depends on what the reaction looked like.

Key fact

Name a reaction from its reactants, not from its appearance. One reactant means decomposition; a metal and another metal's compound means displacement; oxygen means oxidation, and combustion if it burns.

In your words

Write the rule you would give someone who missed this unit

One sentence per type, and each one has to say what you would look at rather than what it looks like. Nobody marks this. Compare it with the version underneath when you are done.

  • Thermal decomposition: one reactant, two or more products, heat needed all the way through, mass left behind goes down.
  • Combustion: a fuel and oxygen, a flame, energy given out, and carbon dioxide and water if the fuel contains carbon and hydrogen.
  • Oxidation: something gains oxygen and the product is heavier. Combustion is the fast version of this.
  • Displacement: a metal and a compound of a less reactive metal, and the less reactive one comes out as a solid.

If yours says the same things in worse English, yours is the more useful one — you will remember it.

Think again

“A reaction can only be one of the four types, so if it is oxidation it cannot be combustion.”

The four have four different names, which certainly suggests four separate boxes. Commit before you read on.

    •

Burning magnesium is combustion and oxidation, and both answers are right. The names are not four boxes side by side: combustion sits inside oxidation, as the case where the reaction is fast enough to burn. Naming it combustion is more specific and therefore usually the better answer — it says more.

The two that genuinely cannot overlap are decomposition and the rest, because decomposition has one reactant and the others have two. So if you are ever torn between combustion and oxidation, you are not confused — you have noticed something true. Give the more specific name, and say why the other also applies.

Key note

Name a reaction from its reactants. One reactant broken apart by heat is thermal decomposition. A metal plus a compound of a less reactive metal is displacement. Oxygen as a reactant is oxidation, and combustion when it burns with a flame — so combustion sits inside oxidation rather than beside it. Mass is the tie-breaker: gaining oxygen makes it heavier, losing a gas makes it lighter.

Going further

There are more reaction types than four, and the two you have already met without a label are worth naming. Marble chips in acid is neutralisation, which is the whole of the next unit. Iron and sulfur heated together to make iron sulfide is synthesis — two elements joining to make one compound, the exact reverse of decomposition. Chemists also cut the same set a different way: exothermic reactions give out energy, endothermic ones take it in, which puts combustion and displacement on one side and thermal decomposition on the other.

None of these classifications is a fact about nature; they are all ways of grouping reactions so that a prediction becomes possible. That is what a type is for. Knowing a reaction is a displacement lets you predict the products from a reactivity order you can look up, and knowing one is a combustion lets you predict carbon dioxide and water without being told. A name that does not let you predict anything would not be worth learning.
