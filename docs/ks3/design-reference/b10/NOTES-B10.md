## 1. New instruments

### 1.1 `variation-plotter` — flagship of `b10-01`
Six characteristics; the student **predicts the shape before plotting**, then
gets the graph, a shape explanation and a separate cause line. The bar gap is
computed from the data type — touching for continuous, spaced for discontinuous
— so the graph convention is taught by the rendering rather than asserted.

### 1.2 `zoom-bench` — flagship of `b10-02`
Six nested levels from a person to a base pair, with a scale figure on each,
plus a four-question *say it back* panel. The scale column is the argument: it
drops by roughly a thousandfold per step and nothing new is introduced.

### 1.3 `model-builder` — flagship of `b10-03`
Three decisions (strands, base position, pairing rule) tested against four real
pieces of 1952 evidence. Exactly one of the twelve combinations passes all four,
and the failing tests each name what rules the model out. **The instrument
demonstrates elimination rather than revelation**, which is the lesson's point
about how the structure was actually reached. It opens preset to Pauling's
wrong triple helix.

### 1.4 `pea-cross` — flagship of `b10-04`
Two parent genotypes (PP / Pp / pp), grow one seed or a hundred, running tally
and ratio. Uses `Math.random()` per gamete, so the 3:1 emerges only over
numbers — which is why the hundred-seed button exists and why Mendel counted
thousands.

### 1.5 `species-cases` — flagship of `b10-05`
Seven pairs, three verdicts including **"the test does not settle it"**. Three
cases genuinely resolve to that third option (bacteria, dandelions, ring
species), so the instrument teaches that a definition can have a boundary as
well as a rule. Bench marking follows the house rule.

## 2. Science flags — numbered for review

1. **The sixty-student data set** (b10-01) is invented, shaped to look real.
   Blood group frequencies are approximate UK values. Confirm invented class
   data is acceptable, or supply a real set.
2. **Eye colour as three categories** (b10-01). A simplification, and the legal
   line says so — real eye colour grades and involves several genes. **Confirm**,
   since it is the one characteristic on the bench that arguably belongs on the
   other side of the continuous/discontinuous line.
3. **Tongue rolling** (b10-01) is described as "largely genetic" with the
   single-gene story called too simple. Confirm — many textbooks still teach it
   as a clean single-gene example.
4. **Dutch height, ~1.84 m and a twenty-centimetre rise since 1860** (b10-01
   *Going further*). Widely reported. Confirm the figures.
5. **"Two metres of DNA per cell", nucleus ~0.006 mm** (b10-02). Standard
   figures, rounded. Confirm.
6. **"Around twenty thousand genes"** and the Human Genome Project surprise
   (b10-02 *Going further*), plus the chromosome-count comparisons (chimp 48,
   potato 48, dog 78, a fern over a thousand). Confirm.
7. **"One gene, one characteristic"** is used as the working model in b10-02 and
   the legal line flags it as a deliberate simplification. Confirm that framing.
8. **The four evidence cards in b10-03** — Photo 51, Franklin's water
   measurements, Chargaff's ratios, Pauling's triple helix. All real and all
   correctly attributed. Confirm the Pauling card is wanted: it teaches that a
   wrong model is useful evidence, which is unusual and I think valuable.
9. **The credit note** (b10-03, in a `ks3-support` layer): Gosling took Photo
   51 in Franklin's lab; Wilkins showed it to Watson without her knowledge; her
   unpublished report reached Crick via a research council committee; the 1953
   paper said only "stimulated by" unpublished King's results; Franklin died in
   1958 aged 37; the Nobel was 1962 and cannot be posthumous. **This is the
   most reviewable passage in B10.** It is deliberately placed in the support
   layer rather than the main flow, states that historians still disagree about
   interpretation, and does not name a villain. Confirm the treatment and the
   placement.
10. **Rung 4 of b10-03** asks students to write a fairer acknowledgement. The
    criteria explicitly reward *not* treating it as heroes and villains.
    **Confirm you want a values judgement as a marked rung** — I think it is the
    right way to meet the statutory "part played by" clause, and it is arguable.
11. **Miescher 1869, and Avery, MacLeod and McCarty 1944** (b10-03 think-again).
    Correct. Confirm the depth.
12. **Meselson and Stahl, five years later** (b10-03 *Going further*), and the
    paraphrase of the paper's closing sentence. The sentence is **paraphrased,
    not quoted**, deliberately. Confirm that is preferred to a direct quotation.
13. **The pea genetics in b10-04** uses P/p, clean dominance and a 3:1
    expectation. Dominant and recessive are not named as terms — the lesson says
    "overrides" and "hidden". **Confirm you want the vocabulary held back to
    GCSE**, which is what the programme of study implies but does not state.
14. **Mendel: ~28,000 plants, 1856–1863, published 1866, rediscovered 1900,
    died 1884** (b10-04 *Going further*). Confirm the figures.
15. **Horse 64, donkey 62, mule 63 chromosomes** (b10-05), and "almost always
    infertile" with fertile female mules noted in the legal line. Confirm.
16. **The pipistrelle cryptic-species case** (b10-05, and the think-again).
    Real — the split was recognised in the 1990s on echolocation frequency.
    Confirm.
17. **Ring species** (b10-05 *Going further*), including the statement that the
    gull case is messier than the textbook version. Confirm you want the caveat;
    it is the same editorial choice as B9 flag 6.
18. **Liger fertility** (b10-05 bench): males infertile, a few females fertile.
    Confirm.
19. **No diagrams.** A chromosome-to-DNA nesting figure and Photo 51 itself are
    the obvious candidates, and neither is in the diagram manifest. **Photo 51
    is a specific historical image with rights attached** — if it is wanted,
    that is a licensing decision, not just a commissioning one.
