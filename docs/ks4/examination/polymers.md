# Examination — Polymers (polymers) — AQA 8464 5.2.2.5 / 8462 4.2.2.5 (Triple block: 8462 4.7.3.1, 4.10.3.3)
Verdict: CHANGES REQUIRED
Examiner: Opus, 25 Sep 2026. Spec sources fetched: AQA-8462-SP-2016.PDF and AQA-8464-SP-2016.PDF (filestore.aqa.org.uk, both "Version 1.1 04 October 2019"), read as text. Sections quoted: 8462 4.2.2.5, 4.2.2.4, 4.2.1.4 (via 8464 5.2.1.4), 4.7.3.1, 4.7.3.2, 4.10.3.3; 8464 5.2.1.4, 5.2.2.5, 5.7.1.4.

Spec statements relied on (verbatim):
- 8464 5.2.2.5 / 8462 4.2.2.5: "Polymers have very large molecules. The atoms in the polymer molecules are linked to other atoms by strong covalent bonds. The intermolecular forces between polymer molecules are relatively strong and so these substances are solids at room temperature. Students should be able to recognise polymers from diagrams showing their bonding and structure."
- 8464 5.2.1.4: "Some covalently bonded substances have very large molecules, such as polymers." … "Polymers can be represented in the form: [repeat unit in brackets] where n is a large number." … "represent the covalent bonds … in the repeating units of polymers … using a line to represent a single bond". (So brackets-and-n and "repeating unit" are BASE.)
- 8462 4.7.3 heading "Synthetic and naturally occurring polymers (chemistry only)"; 4.7.3.1: "Alkenes can be used to make polymers such as poly(ethene) and poly(propene) by addition polymerisation. In addition polymerisation reactions, many small molecules (monomers) join together to form very large molecules (polymers)." … "In addition polymers the repeating unit has the same atoms as the monomer because no other molecule is formed in the reaction." … "recognise addition polymers and monomers from diagrams … and from the presence of the functional group C=C in the monomers … relate the repeating unit to the monomer."
- 8462 4.7.3.2 "Condensation polymerisation (HT only)" (inside the chemistry-only 4.7.3).
- 8462 4.10.3 "Using materials (chemistry only)"; 4.10.3.3 "Ceramics, polymers and composites": "Thermosoftening polymers melt when they are heated. Thermosetting polymers do not melt when they are heated. … explain the difference between thermosoftening and thermosetting polymers in terms of their structures."
- 8464 5.7.1.4: "Alkenes are used to produce polymers …" "(Students do not need to know the formulae or names of individual alkenes.)" The word "monomer" does not occur anywhere in 8464 (text search of the fetched PDF).
- 8462 4.10.4 is "The Haber process and the use of NPK fertilisers" — so NOTES §8's "4.10.4.3" for thermosoftening/thermosetting is a wrong section number (correct: 4.10.3.3).
