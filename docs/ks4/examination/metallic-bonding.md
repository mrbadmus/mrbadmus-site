# Examination — Metallic bonding (metallic-bonding) — AQA 8462 4.2.1.5 / 8464 5.2.1.5
Verdict: CHANGES REQUIRED
Examiner: Opus, 25 Sep 2026. Spec sources fetched: AQA-8462-SP-2016.PDF (v1.1, 04 Oct 2019) and AQA-8464-SP-2016.PDF (v1.1, 04 Oct 2019) from filestore.aqa.org.uk, read as text (4.2.1.1, 4.2.1.5, 4.2.2.7, 4.2.2.8; 8464 5.2.1.5, 5.2.2.7, 5.2.2.8 are word-identical). Also the 8462 subject-content web page (summary only). Mark-scheme conventions cited from memory of AQA 8462/8464 papers 1F/1H 2018–2024.

Spec statements relied on (verbatim, 8462 = 8464):
- 4.2.1.1: "For metallic bonding the particles are atoms which share delocalised electrons … Metallic bonding occurs in metallic elements and alloys. Students should be able to explain chemical bonding in terms of electrostatic forces and the transfer or sharing of electrons."
- 4.2.1.5: "Metals consist of giant structures of atoms arranged in a regular pattern. The electrons in the outer shell of metal atoms are delocalised and so are free to move through the whole structure. The sharing of delocalised electrons gives rise to strong metallic bonds." (no HT marker, not "chemistry only")
- 4.2.2.7: "Metals have giant structures of atoms with strong metallic bonding. This means that most metals have high melting and boiling points. In pure metals, atoms are arranged in layers, which allows metals to be bent and shaped. Pure metals are too soft for many uses and so are mixed with other metals to make alloys which are harder." + "explain why alloys are harder than pure metals in terms of distortion of the layers of atoms" (base, both tiers, both pathways)
- 4.2.2.8: "Metals are good conductors of electricity because the delocalised electrons in the metal carry electrical charge through the metal. Metals are good conductors of thermal energy because energy is transferred by the delocalised electrons." (base)

Scope ruling: the page teaches conduction, malleability/ductility and high melting point (4.2.2.7/4.2.2.8) as *applications of the 4.2.1.5 model*. That is correct and exam-realistic: AQA routinely asks "use the model of metallic bonding to explain…" in the same item as the structure. No change. Alloy hardness (4.2.2.7) is correctly absent from the taught body (flag 19).
