Spec statements relied on (8462 4.2.1.1 = 8464 5.2.1.1, identical text, no HT marker, not "chemistry only"):
> "There are three types of strong chemical bonds: ionic, covalent and metallic. For ionic bonding the particles are oppositely charged ions. For covalent bonding the particles are atoms which share pairs of electrons. For metallic bonding the particles are atoms which share delocalised electrons. Ionic bonding occurs in compounds formed from metals combined with non-metals. Covalent bonding occurs in most non-metallic elements and in compounds of non-metals. Metallic bonding occurs in metallic elements and alloys. Students should be able to explain chemical bonding in terms of electrostatic forces and the transfer or sharing of electrons."

Also used: 4.2.1.2/5.2.1.2 (metal atoms lose electrons → positive ions; Groups 1, 2, 6, 7 charges from group number), 4.2.1.3/5.2.1.3 ("strong electrostatic forces of attraction between oppositely charged ions"), 4.2.1.4/5.2.1.4 (small molecules, polymers, giant covalent e.g. diamond and silicon dioxide; the eight molecules), 4.2.1.5/5.2.1.5 (delocalised electrons "free to move through the whole structure"), 4.2.2.3–4.2.2.4 (ionic conduct when melted/dissolved; small molecules: weak intermolecular forces overcome, not the covalent bonds), 4.2.2.7/5.2.2.7 ("most metals have high melting and boiling points"), 4.2.2.8/5.2.2.8 (delocalised electrons carry charge).
