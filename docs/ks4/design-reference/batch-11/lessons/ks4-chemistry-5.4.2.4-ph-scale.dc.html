The scale

Below 7, 7, above 7.

Acids produce hydrogen ions (H+) in aqueous solution; aqueous solutions of alkalis contain hydroxide ions (OH−). The pH scale, from 0 to 14, measures how acidic or alkaline a solution is. Acids have pH values less than 7; alkalis have pH values greater than 7; pH 7 is neutral. The lower the pH, the more acidic. Universal indicator gives an approximate pH by colour; a pH probe gives a number.

Neutralisation

pH climbs through 7.

Add alkali to an acid and the pH rises: slowly at first, then fast through 7, then levelling off above it. The curve is what a probe records. The reaction between the ions is the same whatever acid and alkali you use.

Higher · each pH unit is ten times

Ten, a hundred, a thousand.

As the pH decreases by one unit, the hydrogen ion concentration of the solution increases by a factor of 10. So a difference of n whole units is a factor of 10n.

factor = 10(difference in pH)

Worked

pH 1 compared with pH 3. Difference 3 − 1 = 2 units. Factor = 102 = 10 × 10 = 100. The pH 1 solution has 100 times the hydrogen ion concentration.

Key fact

Acids: pH below 7, hydrogen ions. Alkalis: pH above 7, hydroxide ions. Neutral: 7. Neutralisation: H+ + OH− → H2O. Higher: one pH unit is a factor of 10 in H+ concentration.
