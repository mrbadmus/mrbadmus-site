Relative atomic mass, atomic number and isotopes

Two numbers label an atom. Isotopes share one of them. Relative atomic mass is the weighted mean of their mass numbers, and you can calculate it from percentage abundances.

Isotopes

Same protons, different neutrons.

Atoms of the same element can have different numbers of neutrons. These atoms are called isotopes of that element. Same atomic number, so the same element; different mass number.

Equation · relative atomic mass

The relative atomic mass of an element is an average value that takes account of the abundance of the isotopes of the element. It is a weighted sum, not a triangle: one line, with the whole top bracketed.

Ar = [ Σ (% abundance × mass number) ] ÷ 100

Multiply each isotope’s abundance by its mass number, add them all, then divide the whole total by 100. Without the outer bracket, only the last product is divided.

{{ mixLine }} Real chlorine is about 75% chlorine-35 and 25% chlorine-37, so Ar = 35.5. Many Ar values are whole numbers (C 12, O 16, Na 23); chlorine’s is often the first one pupils meet that is not.
