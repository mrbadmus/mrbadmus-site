The mole

One number, any substance.

Chemical amounts are measured in moles (mol). The mass of one mole of a substance in grams is numerically equal to its relative formula mass. One mole of any substance contains the same number of particles: 6.02 × 1023, the Avogadro constant, NA. The particles can be atoms, molecules, ions, electrons or formula units.

Standard form, before you need it

6.02 × 1023 means 6.02 with the decimal point moved 23 places to the right: 602 followed by 21 zeros. To multiply by a number, multiply the front part: 2 × 6.02 × 1023 = 12.04 × 1023 = 1.204 × 1024. To divide two numbers with the same power of ten, divide the front parts: 3.01 × 1023 ÷ 6.02 × 1023 = 0.5.

Key fact

n = m ÷ Mr, with m in grams. Particles = n × 6.02 × 1023. One mole of any substance holds the same number of particles; its mass in grams is its Mr.
