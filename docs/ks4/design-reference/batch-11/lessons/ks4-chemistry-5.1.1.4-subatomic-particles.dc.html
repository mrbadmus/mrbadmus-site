Size and scale

Very small, and mostly empty.

Atoms are very small: a radius of about 0.1 nm, which is 1 × 10−10 m. The radius of a nucleus is less than 1/10 000 of that of the atom, about 1 × 10−14 m. Almost all the mass is in the nucleus.

Counting in atoms and ions

Two numbers, two subtractions.

The nuclear symbol gives the mass number (top) and the atomic number (bottom). The atomic number is the number of protons. Ions form when atoms lose or gain electrons: the number of protons never changes. Worked case:

2713Al3+

protons = atomic number = 13

neutrons = mass number − atomic number = 27 − 13 = 14

electrons = protons − charge = 13 − (+3) = 10

A 3+ charge means three electrons were lost. A negative ion gained electrons, so subtracting a negative charge adds them: for Cl−, 17 − (−1) = 18.

Higher · writing the electron transfer

Half equations.

On Higher you write the loss or gain of electrons as a half equation. Metals lose electrons; non-metals gain them. Chlorine and oxygen are diatomic, so their half equations start from Cl2 and O2.

Na Na+ + e−

Mg Mg2+ + 2e−

Cl2 + 2e− 2Cl−

O2 + 4e− 2O2−

Key fact

Proton +1, neutron 0, electron −1. Protons and neutrons have relative mass 1; the electron’s is very small. Protons = atomic number; neutrons = mass number − atomic number; electrons in an ion = protons − charge.
