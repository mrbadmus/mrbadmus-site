The process of electrolysis

Melt or dissolve an ionic compound and its ions can move. Pass a direct current and the ions travel to the oppositely charged electrodes and are discharged as elements.

The words

Electrolyte, cathode, anode.

When an ionic compound is melted or dissolved in water, the ions are free to move about within the liquid or solution. These liquids and solutions are able to conduct electricity and are called electrolytes. In the liquid the charge is carried by the moving ions; in the wires it is carried by electrons. The electrodes are usually inert (graphite or platinum) so they do not react. Tap each.

Higher · what happens at the electrode

Gain at the cathode, loss at the anode.

At the cathode, positively charged ions gain electrons, so the reactions are reductions. At the anode, negatively charged ions lose electrons, so the reactions are oxidations. The electrons that the anions give up flow round the external circuit to the cathode, where the cations collect them.

cathode (−) · Pb2+ + 2e− Pb gains electrons: reduction

anode (+) · 2Br− Br2 + 2e− loses electrons: oxidation

Key fact

Positively charged ions move to the negative electrode (the cathode); negatively charged ions move to the positive electrode (the anode). Ions are discharged at the electrodes producing elements.
