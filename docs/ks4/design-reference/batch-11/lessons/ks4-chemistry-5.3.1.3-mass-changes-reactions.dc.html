Equation · mass of gas

In an open container the mass of gas that left (or joined) is the difference between the two readings. It is a subtraction, so there is no triangle: one line, both readings in the same unit.

mass of gas = mass before − mass after

If the reading rose (a metal gaining oxygen), swap the order: mass gained = mass after − mass before.

Higher · equation · moles from mass

Predicting the change from the equation.

On Higher you can predict how much mass a metal gains when it burns, from the balanced equation. The tool is the mole: n = m ÷ Mr. Moles have their own lesson, Moles; here is the triangle you need. Tap the quantity you want to find.

Three steps, each one taught on its own first: moles of the metal (n = m ÷ Ar), the ratio from the equation, then mass of oxygen (m = n × Mr). Mass-ratio working (48 g : 80 g) appears only as a check.

Key fact

Some reactions may appear to involve a change in mass. The explanation is always a gas: one that escapes from an open container (reading falls) or one from the air that becomes part of the product (reading rises). Sealed, nothing changes.
