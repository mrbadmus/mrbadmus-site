Which salt?

The acid names the salt.

Acids are neutralised by metal oxides and metal hydroxides (bases; soluble hydroxides are alkalis) to produce salts and water, and by metal carbonates to produce salts, water and carbon dioxide. Hydrochloric acid produces chlorides, nitric acid produces nitrates, sulfuric acid produces sulfates. The metal comes from the base.

acid + metal oxide salt + water

acid + metal carbonate salt + water + carbon dioxide

CuO + H2SO4 CuSO4 + H2O

Neutralisation · the ionic equation

Acids produce hydrogen ions, H+, in aqueous solution; alkalis produce hydroxide ions, OH−. In neutralisation between an acid and an alkali, hydrogen ions react with hydroxide ions to produce water. The other ions (Na+, Cl−) are unchanged and stay in solution as the salt.

H+(aq) + OH−(aq) H2O(l)

Triple · when both reactants dissolve

Titration.

Sodium chloride from sodium hydroxide and hydrochloric acid cannot use the excess-solid method: there is no solid to filter off. Instead the volumes of acid and alkali that react with each other are measured by titration using a suitable indicator, then the reaction is repeated with those volumes and no indicator, and the solution crystallised. The method and the calculation are in Titrations.

Key fact

Warm the acid, add the insoluble solid until no more reacts, filter off the excess, heat on a water bath until crystals start to form, leave to cool, pat dry. Excess solid means no acid is left in the salt.
