Three types

Structural, behavioural, functional.

Organisms have features (adaptations) that enable them to survive in the conditions in which they normally live. These adaptations may be structural, behavioural or functional. Tap each.

Adaptations are not chosen. Individuals with a feature that suits the environment are more likely to survive and breed, so over many generations the feature becomes common: natural selection.

Heat, and the extremes

Ears, and vents.

Heat is lost through the surface. A large animal with small ears and short limbs has a small surface area compared with its volume, so it keeps heat in. A small animal with big ears has a large surface area compared with its volume, so it loses heat. The calculation is in Transport in cells; here you use the idea.

Some organisms live in environments that are very extreme, such as at high temperature, pressure, or salt concentration. These organisms are called extremophiles. Bacteria living in deep sea vents are extremophiles.

Key fact

Adaptations may be structural (body parts), behavioural (what the organism does) or functional (processes inside the body). Extremophiles live in very extreme environments, such as bacteria in deep sea vents.
