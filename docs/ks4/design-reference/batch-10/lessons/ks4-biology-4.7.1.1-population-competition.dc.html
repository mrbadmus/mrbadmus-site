Competition

What they fight over.

Plants in a community or habitat often compete with each other for light and space, and for water and mineral ions from the soil. Animals often compete with each other for food, mates and territory. Tap each.

{{ cFig }}

Competition is fiercest between members of the same species, because their needs are exactly the same (competition within a species). It also happens between species: grey squirrels outcompete red squirrels for food and nesting sites (competition between species).

A hedgerow holds hawthorn bushes, blackbirds and robins. Suggest what the blackbirds and robins compete for.

What limits a population

Why numbers stop rising.

A population cannot grow for ever. Its size is limited by the food, water and space available, and by predators and disease. Tap each.

Key fact

Plants compete for light and space, and for water and mineral ions from the soil. Animals compete for food, mates and territory. Predator numbers follow prey numbers, with a lag.
