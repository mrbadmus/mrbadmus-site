What it shows

Total mass at each level.

Pyramids of biomass can be constructed to represent the relative amount of biomass in each level of a food chain. Trophic level 1 is at the bottom of the pyramid. The width of each bar is proportional to the biomass at that level, measured in g/m² or kg/m².

Drawing to scale · a taught step

Choose a scale that lets the widest bar fit the paper, then divide every value by it. This is a drawing scale, not a spec equation, so there is no triangle.

bar width = biomass ÷ scale value

Worked example, the grassland above, scale 1 cm = 40 g/m²:

grass: 400 ÷ 40 = 10 cm
grasshoppers: 80 ÷ 40 = 2 cm
frogs: 16 ÷ 40 = 0.4 cm

If a value is in kg/m², convert it to the scale’s unit first: 1 kg = 1000 g, so 0.8 kg/m² = 800 g/m². Draw each bar centred on the one below, label every level with its organism, and write the scale on the drawing.

Key fact

A pyramid of biomass shows the total mass of living material at each trophic level, level 1 at the bottom. Bar width = biomass ÷ scale value; convert kg/m² to g/m² first and write the scale on the drawing.
