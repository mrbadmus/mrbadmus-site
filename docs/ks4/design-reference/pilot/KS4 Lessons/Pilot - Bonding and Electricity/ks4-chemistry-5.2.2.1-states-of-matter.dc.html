Start here · an exam scenario

A tube of white wax in a hot-water bath.

A student puts a boiling tube of solid stearic acid, a waxy substance, into a beaker of water at 90 °C. She stirs the wax with the thermometer and reads the temperature every 30 seconds for ten minutes. The water bath never stops heating the tube.

In a solid, particles are packed in a regular arrangement and vibrate about fixed positions. In a liquid, they are still close together but randomly arranged, and they move past each other. In a gas, they are far apart and move quickly in all directions. Melting and boiling need energy, because the forces between the particles have to be overcome. The stronger those forces, the more energy it takes, and the higher the melting or boiling point. Run the investigation and watch the energy go.

Limits of the model · AQA 5.2.2.1 (HT only)

The particle diagrams you have used draw particles as small, solid, inelastic spheres with nothing between them. Real particles are not solid balls, they are not all spheres, and there are forces between them. The simple model cannot say why one substance melts at 69 °C and another at 801 °C.

Key fact

While a pure substance melts or boils its temperature stays constant: the energy goes into overcoming the forces between particles. It is a physical change; the substance is unchanged.
