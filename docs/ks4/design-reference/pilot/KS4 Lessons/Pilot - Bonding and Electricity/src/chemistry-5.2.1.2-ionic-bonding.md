## summary

Describe how ionic bonds form by electron transfer between metals and non-metals.

## common_mistake

Students often say the ionic bond IS the transfer of electrons from the metal to the non-metal. That is not the bond: the transfer is a one-off event that only creates the ions. The ionic bond itself is the strong electrostatic attraction between the oppositely charged ions produced. Remember too that these charges must balance, so every ionic compound has an overall charge of zero.

## examiner_tip

Describing how an ionic bond forms, don't stop at "electrons transfer" — that only makes the ions. The bond is the strong electrostatic attraction between the oppositely charged ions. Then check the charges balance to an overall zero.

## key_note

Ionic bonding: metal loses electrons → positive ion. Non-metal gains electrons → negative ion. Opposite charges attract → ionic bond. Both achieve full outer shells. Group number tells you charge: Group 1 → +1, Group 7 → −1 etc. Overall compound charge = zero.
