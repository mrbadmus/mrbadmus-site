Full outer shells

Stable atoms, so unreactive and single.

The noble gases are unreactive and do not easily form molecules, because their atoms have stable arrangements of electrons. They have eight electrons in their outer shell, except helium, which has only two. So they exist as single atoms: they are monatomic. Pick an atom.

Why it rises

Going down the group, the atoms get bigger and their relative atomic mass increases. The forces between the atoms get stronger, so more energy is needed to overcome them: the boiling point rises. Boiling separates the atoms; it does not break anything inside them. Density also increases down the group.

Key fact

Noble gases are unreactive because their atoms have stable arrangements of electrons. Their boiling points increase with increasing relative atomic mass, going down the group.
