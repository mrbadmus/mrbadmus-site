One peat bog · two futures

Peat is stored carbon and a habitat.

A peat bog is waterlogged, so there is not enough oxygen for decomposers. Dead plants only partly decay, and over thousands of years they build up as peat. Peat stores a large amount of carbon, and the bog is home to specialised plants, animals and microorganisms.

Peat is dug up mainly to sell as compost, for gardens and for commercial food production. It can also be burned as a fuel. Why extra carbon dioxide matters is in Global warming.

The conflict

Cheap compost, or a bog kept wet?

For digging peat

Peat compost is cheap and available. It helps farmers and growers increase food production.

For conserving the bog

The bog stays a habitat, so its biodiversity is kept. The carbon stays locked in the peat, so less carbon dioxide is released.

Peat-free compost helps protect peat bogs: less demand for peat means fewer bogs are dug.

Key fact

Destroying peat bogs reduces a habitat, so biodiversity falls, and the decay or burning of the peat releases carbon dioxide.
