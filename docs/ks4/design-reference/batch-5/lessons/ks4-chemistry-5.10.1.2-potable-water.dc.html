Potable, not pure

Safe to drink still has things dissolved in it.

Potable water is water that is safe to drink. It is not pure in the chemical sense, because it contains dissolved substances. Pure water is water only. In the UK, rain provides fresh water, with low levels of dissolved substances, that collects in the ground and in lakes and rivers. Tap each step to see why it is done.

Salty water: desalination

Where fresh water is scarce, sea water can be made potable by distillation or by reverse osmosis, which uses membranes. Dissolved salt passes straight through a filter, so filter beds can’t remove it. Both methods need large amounts of energy.

Waste water · sewage treatment

Two streams, two kinds of bacteria.

Sewage and agricultural waste water must have organic matter and harmful microbes removed before it is put back into the environment. Industrial waste water may also need harmful chemicals removed.

{{ sewageFig }}

Ground water is usually the easiest to make potable: filter and sterilise. Waste water needs many more stages. Salt water needs desalination, which uses large amounts of energy.

Key fact

Potable water is safe to drink but not pure: it contains dissolved substances. Most UK potable water comes from fresh water passed through filter beds and sterilised.
