The definition · outer electrons

Metals react to form positive ions. Non-metals do not.

A metal atom has 1, 2 or 3 outer electrons. When it reacts it loses them and forms a positive ion. A non-metal atom has 4 to 7 outer electrons, so it gains or shares electrons instead, or it has a full outer shell (Group 0). The number of outer electrons is the group number, so it also sets where the element sits. Pick an element.

Two exceptions to know

Most non-metals are small molecules with weak forces between the molecules, so they have low melting and boiling points. Carbon (diamond and graphite) and silicon are giant structures with very high melting points. Graphite and graphene, forms of carbon, conduct electricity because one electron from each carbon atom is delocalised. Why is taught in the bonding lessons.

Key fact

Elements that react to form positive ions are metals. Elements that do not form positive ions are non-metals.
