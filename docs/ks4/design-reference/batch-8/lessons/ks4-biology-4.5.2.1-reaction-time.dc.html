The ruler drop.

Reaction time is the time between a stimulus and your response. A partner holds a ruler vertically with 0 cm level with the top of your thumb and finger. Without warning, they drop it; you catch it. Read the distance at the top of your thumb. The longer the distance, the longer the reaction time. Test one factor: distraction, by doing mental arithmetic while you catch.

Typical reaction times range from 0.2 s to 0.9 s; for a simple stimulus like this, about 0.2–0.3 s. Alcohol, tiredness and distraction slow reactions; caffeine is a stimulant and speeds up the nervous system. The pathway itself: The nervous system.

Method · mean of repeats

mean = total of the repeats ÷ number of repeats

Written out

Measurements are affected by random error: results vary in unpredictable ways. Repeating and reporting a mean reduces the effect. Put every repeat in the same unit first: ms ÷ 1000 gives s, so 240 ms = 0.24 s. A distance in m or mm must go into cm before you use the table: m × 100, mm ÷ 10.
