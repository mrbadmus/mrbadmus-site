Blood glucose and diabetes

A meal pushes your blood glucose up and insulin brings it back. Break the loop two different ways and see what diabetes does.

Control

After a meal.

Blood glucose concentration is monitored and controlled by the pancreas. If it is too high, the pancreas produces the hormone insulin, which causes glucose to move from the blood into the cells. In liver and muscle cells, excess glucose is converted to glycogen for storage. Step through a meal, then break the loop.

Higher

Too low: glucagon

If blood glucose concentration is too low, the pancreas produces the hormone glucagon, which causes glycogen to be converted into glucose and released into the blood. Glucagon and insulin interact in a negative feedback cycle to control blood glucose.

Key fact

Too much glucose: the pancreas releases insulin, glucose moves into cells, and liver and muscle cells store the excess as glycogen. Type 1: too little insulin. Type 2: cells don’t respond.
