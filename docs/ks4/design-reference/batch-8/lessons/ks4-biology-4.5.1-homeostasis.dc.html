Homeostasis

Keeping conditions steady.

Homeostasis is the regulation of the internal conditions of a cell or organism to maintain optimum conditions for function, in response to internal and external changes. It matters for enzyme action and all cell functions. Tap each condition it controls in the human body.

These automatic control systems may involve nervous responses or chemical responses. Nerves: The nervous system. Hormones: The endocrine system.

Control system

Receptor, coordination centre, effector.

Every control system has three parts. Receptor cells detect stimuli. Coordination centres, such as the brain, spinal cord and pancreas, receive and process the information. Effectors, muscles or glands, bring about responses which restore optimum levels. Step a temperature rise through, then break one part.

Higher

Negative feedback

This kind of control is called negative feedback: the response opposes the change. If the level rises, the response brings it down; if it falls, the response brings it up. The level moves a little either side of the optimum and is brought back each time.

Key fact

Homeostasis keeps internal conditions at the optimum. A receptor detects a change, a coordination centre processes it, and an effector responds.
