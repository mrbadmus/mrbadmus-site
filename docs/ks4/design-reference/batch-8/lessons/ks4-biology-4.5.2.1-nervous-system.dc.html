The nervous system

Receptor to effector.

The nervous system lets you react to your surroundings and coordinate your behaviour. Information from receptors passes along neurones as electrical impulses to the central nervous system (CNS): the brain and spinal cord. The CNS coordinates the response of effectors: muscles contracting or glands secreting hormones. Tap each type of neurone.

Synapses

Across the gap.

Neurones are not joined end to end. Where two meet there is a tiny gap, a synapse. An electrical impulse can’t cross it: a chemical carries the signal across. Step through.

Nerve impulses travel along neurones; hormones travel in the blood and are slower: The endocrine system. Some drugs and alcohol slow the nervous system and increase reaction time: Reaction time.

Key fact

Electrical impulses travel along neurones: sensory to the CNS, relay within it, motor to the effector. A chemical crosses each synapse.
