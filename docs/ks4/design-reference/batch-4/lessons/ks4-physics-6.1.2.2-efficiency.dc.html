Efficiency has no unit. As a decimal it is between 0 and 1. Multiply by 100 for a percentage.

Where the waste goes

Wasted energy is not destroyed.

Wasted energy is dissipated: it spreads out into the surroundings and is stored in less useful ways, mostly as a slight warming of the surroundings.

Lubrication

Oil between moving parts reduces friction, so less energy is transferred to the thermal store of the parts and dissipated.

Thermal insulation

Insulating a hot device or a building reduces the energy transferred to the surroundings.

Higher · ways to increase efficiency

{{ route }}
  • Lubricate moving parts: less friction, less energy wasted as thermal energy.
  • Streamline vehicles: less air resistance, so less energy is transferred to the air.
  • Insulate hot objects: less energy is transferred to the surroundings.
  • Swap the component: an LED transfers a bigger share of its input to light than a filament lamp.
  • Regenerative braking: a hybrid or electric car stores some of its kinetic energy in the battery instead of wasting it as thermal energy in the brakes.

For each way, say what is reduced and where that energy would have gone.

Key fact

Efficiency = useful output ÷ total input. It has no unit and is never more than 1. Wasted energy = total input − useful output.
