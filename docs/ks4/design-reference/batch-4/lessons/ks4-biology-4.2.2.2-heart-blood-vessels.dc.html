The heart · a double circulation

Follow one red blood cell round the circuit.

The right side of the heart pumps blood to the lungs. The left side pumps it to the rest of the body. So blood passes through the heart twice on every complete circuit: a double circulatory system.

Thicker on the left

Both ventricles pump the same volume of blood. The left ventricle has the thicker, more muscular wall because it pumps blood all round the body, at higher pressure. The right ventricle only pumps to the lungs.

Valves

Valves between the atria and ventricles, and where the arteries leave the ventricles, stop blood flowing backwards. You don't need their names.

Coronary arteries

The heart muscle can't use the blood passing through its chambers. Coronary arteries branch off the aorta and supply the heart muscle with oxygenated blood.

Pacemakers

A group of cells in the right atrium acts as a natural pacemaker and controls the resting heart rate. An artificial pacemaker is an electrical device that corrects an irregular heart rate.

Blood vessels · structure and function

Arteries away, veins back, capillaries in between.

{{ vesselFig }}

Artery

  • Carries blood away from the heart, at high pressure.
  • Thick muscular wall, with elastic fibres that stretch and recoil with each beat.
  • Narrow lumen. No valves along its length: the pressure keeps blood moving one way.

Vein

  • Carries blood back to the heart, at low pressure.
  • Thinner wall and a wide lumen.
  • Valves stop the low-pressure blood flowing backwards.

Capillary

  • Links arteries to veins, in a dense network through every tissue.
  • Wall one cell thick: a short diffusion distance.
  • Oxygen and glucose diffuse out to the cells; carbon dioxide and waste diffuse in.

Pressure falls all the way round: highest in the arteries, lower in the capillaries, lowest in the veins.

The lungs · gas exchange

Trachea, bronchi, alveoli, capillaries.

{{ lungFig }}

Air passes down the trachea, into the two bronchi, and on to millions of tiny air sacs, the alveoli. Each alveolus is wrapped in a network of capillaries.

Oxygen diffuses from the air in the alveoli into the blood. Carbon dioxide diffuses from the blood into the alveoli, and is breathed out.

  • Very large surface area from so many alveoli.
  • Thin walls, so a short diffusion distance.
  • A dense capillary network and good blood supply, which keeps the concentration gradient steep.

Key fact

Blood passes through the heart twice on each circuit. Arteries carry it away from the heart, veins carry it back, and capillaries are where substances are exchanged.
