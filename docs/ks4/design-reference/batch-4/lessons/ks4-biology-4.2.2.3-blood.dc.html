Four components · structure and job

Plasma carries; the cells do the rest.

{{ rbcFig }}

Red blood cells

Carry oxygen from the lungs to the tissues. Haemoglobin binds oxygen in the lungs and releases it in the tissues.

  • Biconcave shape: a large surface area for oxygen to diffuse in and out.
  • No nucleus: more room for haemoglobin.
  • Packed with haemoglobin.

White blood cells

Larger than red cells, and each has a nucleus. They defend against pathogens in three ways:

  • Phagocytosis: phagocytes engulf pathogens and digest them.
  • Antibody production: lymphocytes make antibodies that target a particular pathogen.
  • Antitoxin production: antitoxins neutralise the toxins released by bacteria.

Platelets

Small fragments of cells, with no nucleus. They help the blood to clot at a wound, which stops bleeding and keeps pathogens out.

Plasma

The pale yellow liquid the cells are carried in. It transports dissolved glucose and amino acids, carbon dioxide, urea, hormones and antibodies.

Key fact

Blood is a tissue of plasma, with red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets suspended in it. Red cells carry oxygen, white cells defend against pathogens, platelets help clotting, and plasma transports dissolved substances.
