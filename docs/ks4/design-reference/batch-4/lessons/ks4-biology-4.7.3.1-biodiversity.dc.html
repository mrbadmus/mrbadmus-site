What biodiversity means

The variety of all the different species.

Biodiversity is the variety of all the different species of organisms on Earth, or within an ecosystem.

Every kind of organism counts: plants, animals, fungi and microorganisms. A field with a thousand rabbits and nothing else has very low biodiversity. A hedgerow with hundreds of different species has high biodiversity.

Stability

A great biodiversity ensures the stability of ecosystems by reducing the dependence of one species on another for food, shelter and the maintenance of the physical environment.

People depend on it

The future of the human species on Earth relies on us maintaining a good level of biodiversity.

In the species-rich web, most consumers have more than one food, so they depend less on any one species. Losing one species does less damage: the community is more stable.

Human activity

Many human activities are reducing biodiversity.

Waste

Pollution of land, water and air kills plants and animals.

Deforestation

Clearing forest destroys the habitats of the many species that live there.

Global warming

Changing climate changes habitats faster than some species can cope with.

Only recently have measures been taken to try to stop this reduction. Each of these, and the measures, has its own lesson.

Key fact

Biodiversity is the variety of all the different species in an ecosystem. High biodiversity makes an ecosystem stable, because species depend less on any one other species for food, shelter and the physical environment.
