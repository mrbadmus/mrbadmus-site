The spectrum and its uses

One family of waves, many jobs.

All electromagnetic waves travel at the same speed through a vacuum. They differ in wavelength and frequency, and that is what makes each type suited to different uses.

{{ spectrumFig }}
Radio waves
Television and radio.
Microwaves
Satellite communications; cooking food.
Infrared
Electrical heaters; cooking food; infrared cameras.
Visible light
Fibre optic communications.
Ultraviolet
Energy efficient lamps; sun tanning.
X-rays and gamma rays
Medical imaging and treatments.

Some uses carry risks. Ultraviolet can make skin age prematurely and increases the risk of skin cancer, so sun tanning has a cost. X-rays and gamma rays are ionising radiation, which can cause the mutation of genes and cancer, so exposure is kept as low as possible.

Each wave is chosen for how it behaves.

{{ xrayFig }}
{{ satFig }}
  • Microwaves for satellites: they pass through the atmosphere to and from the satellite.
  • Microwaves for cooking: they are absorbed by water molecules in the food, transferring energy to its thermal store. They are absorbed mostly in the outer layers; the inside heats by conduction.
  • Infrared for heaters and cameras: it is absorbed by surfaces, transferring energy to their thermal store; warm objects emit it, so a camera can detect them in the dark.
  • Ultraviolet in energy efficient lamps: a coating inside the lamp absorbs the ultraviolet and gives out visible light.
  • X-rays for imaging: they pass through soft tissue but are absorbed by bone, so bone shows up on the image.
  • Gamma rays for treatment: a focused beam carries enough energy to kill cancer cells.
