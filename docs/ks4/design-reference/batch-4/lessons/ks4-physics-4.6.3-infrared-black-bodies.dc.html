Temperature and emission

Turn up the temperature. Watch the curve.

All bodies, no matter what temperature, emit and absorb infrared radiation. The hotter the body, the more infrared radiation it radiates in a given time. The intensity and wavelength distribution of the emission both depend on the temperature.

Perfect black bodies

The best absorber is the best emitter.

{{ cavityFig }}

A perfect black body absorbs all of the radiation that falls on it. It does not reflect or transmit any. Because a good absorber is also a good emitter, a perfect black body is the best possible emitter.

A hollow box with a tiny hole behaves almost like one: radiation that gets in bounces around inside until it is absorbed.

Real surfaces, from the infrared practical: matt black is the best emitter and absorber; light, shiny surfaces reflect infrared and are the poorest emitters and absorbers.

Same rate both ways

Its temperature stays constant. It is still absorbing and emitting.

{{ earthFig }}

The temperature of the Earth depends on many factors, including the rates of absorption and emission of radiation, and the reflection of radiation into space.

Some of the Sun's radiation is reflected straight back into space. The rest is absorbed and warms the Earth, which emits infrared. Some of that infrared is absorbed by gases in the atmosphere and emitted back towards the surface. If the Earth absorbs radiation faster than it loses it, its temperature rises until the two rates match again.

Key fact

All bodies emit and absorb infrared. The hotter the body, the more it radiates in a given time, with the distribution shifted to shorter wavelengths. A perfect black body absorbs all radiation and is the best possible emitter.
