What happens next?

{{ dropNote }}
Evaporation
Energy from the Sun turns liquid water from seas, lakes and soil into water vapour.
Transpiration
Plants lose water vapour from their leaves, through the stomata.
Condensation
Rising vapour cools and turns into tiny liquid droplets. Clouds are made of these droplets.
Precipitation
Droplets join into larger drops and fall as rain, snow, sleet or hail.
Runoff and groundwater
Water flows over the land into rivers, or soaks into the ground, and drains back to the sea.

Why it matters · fresh water

The sea is salty. The rain is not.

When sea water evaporates, only the water turns to vapour. The dissolved salts are left behind in the sea. So the vapour, and the rain or snow it becomes, is fresh water.

That fresh water lands on the land, where plants and animals can use it, before it drains back into the sea. This is why the water cycle matters to living things on land.

Plants

Take in water from the soil through their roots, carry it up to the leaves, and lose water vapour by transpiration.

Animals

Take in water by drinking and in food. Return it in urine and faeces, in sweat, and as water vapour in the air they breathe out.

Key fact

The water cycle provides fresh water for plants and animals on land before it drains into the seas. Water is continuously evaporated and precipitated.
