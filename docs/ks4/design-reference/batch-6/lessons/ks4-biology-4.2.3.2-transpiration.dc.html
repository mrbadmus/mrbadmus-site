Stomata and guard cells

Water leaves through the stomata.

Water evaporates from the leaves and leaves as water vapour, mostly through the stomata. More water is drawn up the xylem from the roots to replace it: this is the transpiration stream. Each stoma is a pore between two guard cells, which control gas exchange and water loss. Open and close it.

Four factors · the potometer

Change the conditions, watch the bubble.

A potometer measures how fast a leafy shoot takes up water. As the shoot takes water in, an air bubble moves along the capillary tube. The further it moves in one minute, the faster the water uptake. Change one condition at a time.

Key fact

Transpiration is the loss of water vapour from the leaves, mostly through the stomata. It is faster when it is warmer, brighter, drier and windier.
