What does the acid meet?

The four reactions

The salt comes from the metal and the acid.

Hydrochloric acid makes chlorides, sulfuric acid makes sulfates, nitric acid makes nitrates. The reactions with oxides, hydroxides and carbonates are all neutralisation. Only metals above hydrogen in the reactivity series react with dilute acids: copper doesn’t.

Formulae of salts

Balance the charges.

A salt has no overall charge. Take enough of each ion for the charges to cancel. Mg2+ needs two Cl−, so magnesium chloride is MgCl2. Two Na+ balance one SO42−, so sodium sulfate is Na2SO4. Now you.

Acid + metal is redox

The metal loses electrons; the hydrogen ions gain them.

Oxidation is loss of electrons; reduction is gain of electrons. In the ionic equation, the chloride or sulfate ions don’t change: they are spectator ions, so they are left out. Tap each half to see which species is oxidised and which is reduced.

Mg + 2H+ Mg2+ + H2

Zinc and iron react the same way: Zn Zn2+ + 2e−; Fe Fe2+ + 2e−.

Key fact

Acid + metal gives a salt and hydrogen. Acid + metal oxide or hydroxide gives a salt and water. Acid + carbonate gives a salt, water and carbon dioxide.
