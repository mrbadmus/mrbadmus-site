Step down the group

One outer electron. Each step down, it is further out.

The alkali metals all have one electron in their outer shell. They are soft enough to cut with a knife, have low melting points, and lithium, sodium and potassium all float on water. They react with water to make a metal hydroxide and hydrogen. Before each metal goes in, predict.

The solution left behind is alkaline: universal indicator turns purple. That is where the name comes from.

Oxygen and chlorine

Not only water.

A freshly cut surface is shiny, then quickly goes dull as an oxide forms. Heated in chlorine, the metals react vigorously to form white solid chlorides. Both reactions get more vigorous down the group. Tap a reaction.

Why reactivity increases

Further away, weaker pull, easier to lose.

Going down the group, the atoms have more shells, so the outer electron is further from the nucleus. The attraction between the nucleus and the outer electron is weaker, so the electron is lost more easily, forming a +1 ion. That makes the metal more reactive.
