Binary fission

One becomes two, every division.

Bacteria multiply by simple cell division, called binary fission, as often as once every 20 minutes if they have enough nutrients and a suitable temperature. They can be grown in a nutrient broth solution or as colonies on an agar gel plate. Each division doubles the number. Press Divide and watch the count.

Biology required practical 2

Which antibiotic works best?

Investigate the effect of antiseptics or antibiotics on bacterial growth using agar plates and measuring zones of inhibition. Spread bacteria evenly over the agar. Add paper discs soaked in each antibiotic, and a control disc soaked in sterile water. Use the same disc size, concentration and incubation time for a fair test. Incubate at 25 °C. Tap a disc to measure its clear zone.

Equation · area of a clear zone

You measure the diameter, but the equation needs the radius. Halve it first, as its own line:

r = diameter ÷ 2

Keep every length in mm, so the area comes out in mm2: cm × 10 gives mm. π is a fixed number, about 3.14. Tap the quantity you want to find.

Two steps · how many bacteria after a time?

Step 1 finds how many times the bacteria divide, using the triangle below. Time and mean division time must be in the same unit: hours × 60 gives minutes. Step 2 doubles the starting number once for each division, one line per division, just as you did with the Divide button.

bacteria = starting number × 2 for each division

Give the answer in standard form

Move the decimal point until one digit is in front of it, and count the places moved: 512 = 5.12 × 102. Bigger populations: 3 200 000 = 3.2 × 106.

Key fact
