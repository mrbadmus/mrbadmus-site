Birth and balance

Gravity in, fusion energy out.

A star forms from a cloud of dust and gas called a nebula. Gravity pulls the dust and gas together to form a protostar, which gets hotter. When it is hot enough, hydrogen nuclei fuse to form helium, releasing energy: the star is born. On the main sequence the star is stable, because the inward force of gravity is balanced by the outward pressure from fusion energy. Try each force on its own.

Where the elements come from

Fusion makes new elements.

Fusion processes in stars produce all of the naturally occurring elements. Elements up to iron are formed by fusion in stars. Elements heavier than iron are produced in a supernova, and the explosion of a massive star spreads the elements throughout the universe. Tap an element to see where it was made.

Key fact

A main-sequence star is stable because gravity balances the outward pressure from fusion energy. Its mass decides what it becomes: a white dwarf, or a supernova then a neutron star or black hole.
