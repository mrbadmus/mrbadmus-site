How a vaccine works

Ready before it arrives.

A vaccine contains small quantities of dead or inactive forms of a pathogen. These stimulate white blood cells to produce antibodies. If the same pathogen re-enters the body, the white blood cells respond quickly to produce the correct antibodies, preventing infection. Compare the two people.

Antibodies are specific: a vaccine protects against one pathogen. The flu virus changes, so a new flu vaccine is made each year.

Vaccinating a population

Break the chain.

If a large proportion of the population is immune to a pathogen, the spread of the pathogen is very much reduced. Set how many people are immune, then watch one infected person arrive.
