## 2. What is in each column

| column | value |
|---|---|
| `school_id` | subselect on `schools.name = 'Rainford High School'` — never a uuid; test and prod ids differ |
| `key_stage` | `'KS4'` on all three years, Year 9 included |
| `year_group` | 9, 10 or 11 |

| `pathway` | **NULL on every row** — see §3 |
| `subject_id` | subselect on `subjects.name` |
| `academic_week` | 1..n, renumbered densely within each (year, subject, tier) block |
| `topic` | Rainford's unit banner, verbatim ("Unit 1 Cell biology") |
| `subtopic` | the platform slug, or NULL where nothing honestly corresponds |
| `notes` | `L<Rainford's lesson number> · <verbatim title>`, plus the reason when subtopic is NULL |

### academic_week is renumbered, per tier

Rainford's own lesson numbers run **1..120 continuously across all three
years**. They are not weeks and they do not restart, so they are dropped from
`academic_week` and preserved in `notes` instead.

The renumbering block is (year, subject, **tier**), not just (year, subject).
A Foundation class does not teach the "[HT ONLY]" lessons, so its tenth taught
lesson is a different lesson from Higher's tenth. Dense per-tier numbering says
that; shared numbering with holes on the Foundation side would claim a
Foundation class sits through a 45th lesson it never sees.

Longest block: **Year 11 Biology Higher, 55**. Migration `20260906230500`
widened this table's KS4 ceiling to 60 for exactly this, and the generator
asserts against it.

## 3. tier and pathway — the one modelling decision

**tier is read off the column. pathway is NULL on every row, deliberately.**

Rainford's sheet does **not** distinguish Combined from Triple as a sequence.
What it does is mark individual *lessons*: a Triple-only lesson carries a
`(BIO)`, `(CHEM)` or `(PHYS)` prefix — 74 lesson rows do — and everything else
is taught to everybody. There is one sequence per tier; a Triple class takes it
whole, a Combined class skips the marked lessons.

Three representations were weighed:

| | | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)** | `pathway` NULL. One sequence, both pathways, as Rainford wrote it. | **chosen** |
| (b) | Emit each block twice, combined and triple, deleting the marked lessons from the combined copy. | rejected — **invention** |
| (c) | `pathway='triple'` on marked rows, `'combined'` on the rest. | rejected — **false** |

**(b) is invention.** Deleting 74 lessons renumbers every week after each
deletion, so the "Combined sequence" it produced would be a week order Rainford
has never written down and no Rainford teacher would recognise. A wrong mapping
is worse than a gap, and a fabricated week order is a wrong mapping of the
whole document.

**(c) is the same lie in smaller print.** It would say a Combined class does
not teach "Group 1" because the row above it happened to be Triple-only, and
would leave two interleaved sequences each counting the other's lessons as
weeks.

**(a) loses nothing.** The Triple marking survives in two independent places:
verbatim in `notes` (the prefix is kept byte-for-byte) and structurally in
`ks4_data.classify()` for every mapped row. Either recovers it.

### Verifying the prefix reading

The brief asked whether `(BIO)`/`(CHEM)`/`(PHYS)` really marks Triple-only
content. `check_triple_prefix()` measures it on every run, against the site's
own `triple_only` flag:

- **66 mapped entries carry a prefix; 58 map to a subtopic the site
  independently flags `triple_only`** (88%).
- 7 unprefixed entries map to a `triple_only` subtopic.

**Conclusion: the reading is correct, and the two are not substitutes for each
other.** Every disagreement is explained by granularity — Rainford marks a
*lesson*, the platform flags a *page*, and one page often carries both shared
and Triple-only material.

Prefixed, but the page is not flagged Triple (8):

| entry | maps to | why |
|---|---|---|
| `(PHYS) Pressure and volume` | `particle-motion-pressure` | p₁V₁ = p₂V₂ is Physics-only; the page also carries the Combined particle-motion content |
| `(PHYS) Reflection RP` | `properties-em-waves-1` | the reflection RP is Physics-only; the page is not |
| `(BIO)  Decomposition`, `(BIO) Anaerobic decay & biogas`, `(BIO) Decay RP`, `(BIO) Decay RP 2` | `decomposition` | rate of decay and biogas are Biology-only; decomposition itself is not |
| `(PHYS) Braking forces` | `stopping-distance-braking` | the Physics-only extension of a shared page |
| `(PHYS) Changing momentum` | `momentum` | F = Δp/Δt is Physics-only HT |

Unprefixed, but the page **is** flagged Triple (7): `Thermal conductivity`,
`Thermal insulators investigation`, `Infrared RP`, `Resolving forces`,
`Parallelogram of forces (HT Only)`, `Meisosis`, `Classification`.

⚠️ **For Mide:** the last two are worth a second look — `meiosis` and
`classification-living-organisms` are flagged `triple_only` on the platform,
but AQA Combined Science Trilogy does teach both, and Rainford teaches them
unprefixed to everybody. That is a question about
`generate_site_v5.PATHWAY_TOPIC_MAP`, not about this mapping, so nothing was
changed. Flagged, not acted on.

## 4. How the mapping was made

Two rules, applied in order, and a refusal.

**R1 — a verbatim or near-verbatim platform title wins.**
"Metabolism" → `metabolism`. "(CHEM) Atom Economy" → `atom-economy`.

**R2 — a lesson covering two platform pages maps to the FIRST-NAMED.**
"Land use & deforestation" → `land-use`; `deforestation` is recorded here, not
mapped. Mapping such a lesson to one page is partial; mapping it to neither
would leave the teacher with nothing at all.

**The refusal — nothing is guessed.** A lesson with no honest platform
equivalent carries `absent(...)` or `ambiguous(...)` in `MAP`, becomes a row
with `subtopic = NULL`, and is named at the foot of the seed SQL and in §6.
It is still a row: a test lesson is a real week of a real timetable, and
dropping it would shift every lesson after it.

### Two deliberate deviations from R2

- **`Speed, distance time graphs` → `distance-time-graphs`**, not the
  first-named `distance-speed-velocity`. The very next lesson is `Velocity`,
  which *is* `distance-speed-velocity`; obeying R2 would point both at one
  page and orphan the graphs page.
- **`(PHYS) Circular Motion` → `motion-in-a-circle`.** It sits in Rainford's
  *Unit 8 Space physics*, where the unit context argues for
  `gravity-stable-orbits`. R1 won on the title, and `motion-in-a-circle`'s own
  summary — "why circular motion involves constant speed but changing
  velocity, and the centripetal force" — is what that lesson teaches.
  **Flagged for Mide:** if he reads it as an orbits lesson, one line of `MAP`
  changes it.

## 8. Two things found in the spreadsheet itself

**A stray space becomes a phantom lesson.** Row 123, the Chemistry Foundation
column, holds a single space and nothing else — no lesson number, no unit, no
Higher text beside it. A naive read counts it and reports Year 11 Chemistry at
45 lessons. `_cell()` normalises a whitespace-only cell to None, so **Year 11
Chemistry is 44 lessons**, which is what Rainford teaches.

**A unit banner sits two lessons early.** In the sheet, the
`Unit 6 Inheritance, variation & evolution` banner is written on the L103 row —
`Growth of seedling RP2`, a plant-hormones practical — so L103, the Homeostasis
test (L104) and its review (L105) all forward-fill to Unit 6 rather than Unit 5.
That is reproduced faithfully rather than corrected: it is Rainford's document,
and silently moving a banner is the kind of "helpful" edit that makes a seed
disagree with the source it claims to come from. Worth one line back to
Rainford.

## 9. What was applied, and where

**TEST only** (`qeppkiswvclkkwbxmlok`). Production untouched. No
`supabase db push`.

670 rows in `public.scheme_of_work_overrides`, all `key_stage='KS4'`, all
Rainford's `school_id`, all `pathway` NULL. Every block's `max(academic_week)`
equals its row count — dense numbering, no gaps — and the largest is 55,
inside the ceiling of 60.

The applied rows were checked against the generator's own output by
independent MD5 over every emitted column, ordered identically in Python and in
SQL: **`fc194b16317d8ec1b40a0c97794b0845` both sides.** The database holds
exactly what `rainford_sow.py` emits, column for column.

### KS3 was not touched by this work

| | baseline | after |

| `scheme_of_work_entries` KS3 | 185 · `42b19ee6…` | **185 · `42b19ee6…` unchanged** |

⚠️ **The KS3 override count moved from 0 to 162, and it was not this work.**
Those rows are Rainford Years 7, 8 and 9, written between 21:59 and 22:01 UTC
on 6 September, interleaved with these KS4 writes — the concurrent agent
handling Rainford's KS3 sheet. This generator's `delete` is scoped
`key_stage = 'KS4' and school_id = <Rainford>`, and every insert writes a
literal `'KS4'`, so it cannot reach a KS3 row. Reported, not fixed.

**One thing for Mide to be aware of:** Rainford's Year 9 now appears in the
overrides table under **both** key stages — 22 KS3 rows and 112 KS4 rows. That
is a legitimate consequence of Rainford beginning KS4 in Year 9 while the KS3
sheet still lists a Year 9, and the database allows it (`key_stage` is in the
unique key). Whether a Year 9 class should see both is a product question.
