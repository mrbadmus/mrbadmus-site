Spec statements (verbatim, 4.10.4.2): "Compounds of nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium are used as fertilisers to improve agricultural productivity. NPK fertilisers contain compounds of all three elements. Industrial production of NPK fertilisers can be achieved using a variety of raw materials in several integrated processes. NPK fertilisers are formulations of various salts containing appropriate percentages of the elements. Ammonia can be used to manufacture ammonium salts and nitric acid. Potassium chloride, potassium sulfate and phosphate rock are obtained by mining, but phosphate rock cannot be used directly as a fertiliser. Phosphate rock is treated with nitric acid or sulfuric acid to produce soluble salts that can be used as fertilisers." Students should be able to: "recall the names of the salts produced when phosphate rock is treated with nitric acid, sulfuric acid and phosphoric acid"; "compare the industrial production of fertilisers with laboratory preparations of the same compounds, given appropriate information." Skills: "AT 4 Prepare an ammonium salt." (an apparatus-and-technique opportunity, not a required practical).

## 5. For the lesson author
**Misconceptions seen in AQA marking:** "NPK means nitrogen, phosphate, potash" (elements are N, P, K); "phosphate rock is spread straight on fields"; "potassium compounds are made by the Haber process"; "ammonia itself is the fertiliser in the bag"; the three phosphate-rock products confused; lab vs industry answered with "the lab is safer" and no chemistry.
**Typical questions** ⚑ examiner-drafted
- *Name the salts produced when phosphate rock is treated with (a) nitric acid (b) sulfuric acid (c) phosphoric acid. [3]* — (a) phosphoric acid and calcium nitrate; (b) single superphosphate / calcium phosphate and calcium sulfate; (c) triple superphosphate / calcium phosphate.
- *Calculate the percentage by mass of nitrogen in ammonium nitrate, NH₄NO₃. Ar N 14, H 1, O 16. [2]* — Mr = 80 (1); 35 % (1).
- *Compare making ammonium sulfate in the lab with making it industrially. [4]* — lab: dilute solutions, small scale, batch, titration then crystallise by evaporating; industry: concentrated, large scale, continuous, raw materials made on site (ammonia, sulfuric acid), heat from the reaction used to evaporate water.

## 6. Verdict
SOURCE HAS ERRORS. The theory's "ACID RAIN" heading is wrong. Two spec-core requirements are missing and are written into the source: the three phosphate-rock salts, and lab vs industrial preparation. Theory 3 and q2 (eutrophication) are off-spec. The `higher` field is either off-spec (Ostwald equations) or base (% by mass); nothing in 4.10.4.2 is HT. q1 is good on both routes. Nothing for Mide.
