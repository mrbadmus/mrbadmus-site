Spec statements (verbatim, 8464 = 8462): "Chromatography can be used to separate mixtures and can give information to help identify substances. Chromatography involves a stationary phase and a mobile phase. Separation depends on the distribution of substances between the phases. The ratio of the distance moved by a compound (centre of spot from origin) to the distance moved by the solvent can be expressed as its Rf value: Rf = distance moved by substance / distance moved by solvent. Different compounds have different Rf values in different solvents, which can be used to help identify the compounds. The compounds in a mixture may separate into different spots depending on the solvent but a pure compound will produce a single spot in all solvents. Students should be able to: explain how paper chromatography separates mixtures; suggest how chromatographic methods can be used for distinguishing pure substances from impure substances; interpret chromatograms and determine Rf values from chromatograms; provide answers to an appropriate number of significant figures." (MS 1a, 1c, 1d, 2a)

## 5. For the lesson author
**Misconceptions seen in AQA marking:** measuring to the top of the spot, or from the paper's edge, instead of from the origin to the centre of the spot; dividing the wrong way; an ink baseline; solvent above the baseline; "one spot proves pure" from one solvent only; Rf given with a unit; too many significant figures.

**Command words:** Calculate, Determine (from the chromatogram), Explain why (pencil / solvent below line / lid), Suggest (how to show it is pure), Compare.

**Typical questions** ⚑ examiner-drafted
- *Give one reason the start line is drawn in pencil. [1]* — pencil/graphite will not dissolve in the solvent or move up the paper.
- *Spot X moved 3.4 cm; the solvent moved 8.0 cm. Calculate the Rf value of X. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. [2]* — 3.4 ÷ 8.0 (1); = 0.43 (1).
- *Explain how paper chromatography separates the dyes in an ink. [3]* — the solvent (mobile phase) moves up the paper (stationary phase) (1); dyes are distributed differently between the phases / have different solubilities and attraction to the paper (1); so they travel different distances (1).
- 6-mark RP12/RP6 method: baseline in pencil; spots of the unknown and the references; solvent below the line; lid; remove before the front reaches the top; mark the front; measure; calculate Rf; compare.

**Required practical:** Combined **RP12** (8464 5.8.1.3); Chemistry **RP6** (8462 4.8.1.3). On all four routes.

**Equations:** Rf = distance moved by substance ÷ distance moved by solvent — **Learn it** on every route (no chemistry equation sheet exists).
