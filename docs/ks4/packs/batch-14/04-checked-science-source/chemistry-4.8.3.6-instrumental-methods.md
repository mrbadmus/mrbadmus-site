## higher

Explain FES in terms of electron excitation and characteristic wavelength emission. Interpret emission spectra quantitatively (intensity ∝ concentration). Describe how other instrumental techniques work: mass spectrometry (relative molecular mass, fragmentation pattern), infrared spectroscopy (absorption at characteristic frequencies identifies functional groups), gas chromatography (separation by boiling point in carrier gas, retention time for identification).

## common_mistake

Flame emission spectroscopy gives QUANTITATIVE results (concentration) — not just qualitative (presence/absence) like the visual flame test. The intensity of the emission line is proportional to concentration. Higher intensity = more of that element present.

## key_note

Instrumental analysis: more sensitive, accurate, fast, objective than chemical tests. Flame emission spectroscopy: atomise sample in flame → electrons excited → emit light at characteristic wavelengths → spectrum identifies element and intensity gives concentration. Applications: water quality, clinical chemistry, environmental monitoring.
