What the frozen data lacks: LD and HD poly(ethene) are made from the **same monomer, ethene**, under **different reaction conditions** (LDPE: very high pressure, about 200 °C, a trace of oxygen; HDPE: low pressure, lower temperature, a catalyst). The different conditions give branched vs unbranched chains. AQA credits "different conditions / temperature / pressure / catalyst" and needs no values. A quantitative comparison from a data table (density, strength, maximum working temperature, cost) is also absent.

Spec term: AQA says **thermosoftening**, not "thermoplastic". Structure: no cross-links, only weak intermolecular forces between chains, overcome on heating.

## common_mistake

Borosilicate glass has a HIGHER melting point than soda-lime glass — that's why it's used in laboratory equipment. Thermosetting polymers have CROSS-LINKS — they don't melt. Thermoplastic polymers have NO cross-links — they can be melted and recycled. Composites are SUPERIOR to either component alone in at least one property.

## key_note

Ceramics: soda-lime glass (sand + Na₂CO₃ + CaCO₃), borosilicate (higher melting point), clay ceramics (fired in kiln). Polymers: LDPE (branched, flexible), HDPE (unbranched, rigid), thermosetting (cross-links, don't melt), thermoplastic (no cross-links, recyclable). Composites: matrix + reinforcement — CFRP, fibreglass, reinforced concrete.
