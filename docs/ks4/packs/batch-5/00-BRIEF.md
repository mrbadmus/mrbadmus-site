4. **Practice is the same size in every lesson** — same number of questions at each rung of the end practice and the exam ladder. Set the number once (at least the content-standards floor) and keep it across the batch.

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** Test an unknown element side by side with a known metal (conduct, bend, oxide in water + indicator), predict before each test, then place it on the table and link the result to its outer electrons.

**Route layers:**

**Required practical:** none.

**Calculations:** none.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.

**Misconceptions to confront:**

- "Metals gain electrons when they react." → Metals react to form positive ions: they lose electrons (5.1.2.3).
- "Metals are on the left, so the middle ones must be non-metals." → Most elements are metals; metals are left and towards the bottom (5.1.2.3).

### 5. The Structure of an Atom
`structure-of-atom` · Physics / atomic-structure · AQA 8464 6.4.1.1; 8463 4.4.1.1 (supporting 6.4.1.2 / 4.4.1.2) · true routes CF CH TF TH

**Family:** MODEL — the nuclear model (tiny dense positive nucleus, electrons at energy levels) explains mass, charge and empty space.

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** A to-scale zoom from atom (1 × 10⁻¹⁰ m) down to nucleus (less than 1/10 000 of it), where the pupil places protons, neutrons and electrons and watches an electron jump up on absorbing light and drop back emitting it.

**Route layers:** none — all base.

**Required practical:** none.

**Calculations:** None. (Standard form reading only — 1 × 10⁻¹⁰ m vs 1 × 10⁻¹⁴ m, MS 1b; scale comparison "nucleus is 10 000 times smaller". No equation.)

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "Electrons are in the nucleus." — the nucleus is protons and neutrons; electrons are at energy levels around it.
- "Electrons make up a lot of the mass." — most of the mass is in the nucleus; an electron's relative mass is very small.
- "An atom is solid all the way through." — the nucleus is less than 1/10 000 of the atom's radius; the rest is empty space.
- "Atoms are charged because they contain charged particles." — electrons = protons, so no overall charge.
- "Electrons move up a level by themselves." — they move up only on absorbing EM radiation, and down by emitting it.

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** A hand on a desk doesn't go through it — yet an atom is almost entirely empty space.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
