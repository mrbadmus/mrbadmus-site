## 5. For the lesson author
**Misconceptions seen in AQA marking**
- "Sustainable means not using resources / stopping industry."
- "Recyclable means renewable" (aluminium cans are recyclable; aluminium ore is still finite).
- "Synthetic products are always less sustainable than natural ones."
- "Finite resources will run out tomorrow" — no sense of scale; orders of magnitude settle it.
- Given a table of resources, pupils classify by familiarity rather than by the information given (the spec says "given appropriate information").

**Command words**: State, Give, Distinguish, Suggest, Use the data/Figure, Evaluate (only with data).

**Typical questions** ⚑ examiner-drafted
- *What is meant by sustainable development? [1]* — development that meets the needs of current generations without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.
- *Give one example of a natural product that has been replaced by a synthetic product. [1]* — e.g. wool replaced by polyester / natural rubber by synthetic rubber.
- *Table: resource, time to replace. Which resources are finite? [2]* — read from the table.
- *Known reserves 8 × 10⁹ t; use 4 × 10⁷ t per year. Estimate how many years the reserves will last. [2]* — 8 × 10⁹ ÷ 4 × 10⁷ (1) = 200 years (1).

**Required practical**: none.

**Equations**: none.
