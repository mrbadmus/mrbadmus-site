Spec statements (verbatim, 8463 = 8464): "If a change of state happens: The energy needed for a substance to change state is called latent heat. When a change of state occurs, the energy supplied changes the energy stored (internal energy) but not the temperature. The specific latent heat of a substance is the amount of energy required to change the state of one kilogram of the substance with no change in temperature." E = m L — "Students should be able to apply this equation, which is given on the Physics equation sheet, to calculate the energy change involved in a change of state." Units J, kg, J/kg. "Specific latent heat of fusion – change of state from solid to liquid. Specific latent heat of vaporisation – change of state from liquid to vapour." "Students should be able to interpret heating and cooling graphs that include changes of state. Students should be able to distinguish between specific heat capacity and specific latent heat." AT 5: "Perform an experiment to measure the latent heat of fusion of water" (a skills opportunity, **not** a required practical).

## 5. For the lesson author
**Misconceptions seen in AQA marking**
- "Temperature rises while it melts/boils" — or the flat section read as "no energy is being supplied".
- Using E = mcΔθ for a change of state (or E = mL across a temperature change); in two-stage questions (heat to 100 °C, then boil), doing only one stage.
- L quoted in kJ/kg but substituted as J/kg without converting (×1000 lost); mass in grams.
- "Energy is used to increase kinetic energy" during melting (it increases potential energy / internal energy without changing average KE).
- Confusing boiling with evaporation, and freezing point with melting point (they are the same temperature).
- Reading a cooling curve: the freezing plateau labelled as "melting".

**Command words**: Calculate, Determine (from a graph), Describe (what happens to the particles), Explain (why temperature stays constant), Compare (SHC and SLH), Sketch (a heating curve).

**Typical questions** ⚑ examiner-drafted
- *Define specific latent heat. [1]* — the energy needed to change the state of 1 kg of a substance with no change in temperature (1).
- *Calculate the energy needed to melt 250 g of ice at 0 °C. Specific latent heat of fusion of ice = 334 kJ/kg. [3]* — m = 0.25 kg and L = 334,000 J/kg (1); E = 0.25 × 334,000 (1); = 83,500 J (1). (Answer 83.5 kJ also credited.)
- *The graph shows the temperature of a substance as it is heated. Explain why the temperature is constant between B and C. [2]* — the substance is changing state (1); the energy supplied increases the internal (potential) energy / breaks bonds between particles rather than increasing kinetic energy (1).
- *Calculate the total energy to turn 0.20 kg of water at 20 °C into steam at 100 °C. c = 4200 J/kg °C, Lv = 2.26 × 10⁶ J/kg. [4]* — ΔE = 0.20 × 4200 × 80 = 67,200 J (1); E = 0.20 × 2,260,000 = 452,000 J (1); total = 67,200 + 452,000 (1); = 519,200 J (≈ 5.2 × 10⁵ J) (1).
- *Describe a method to determine the specific latent heat of fusion of ice. [6]* (AT 5 skills question, not an RP — set occasionally.) Levels: **L3 (5–6)** coherent method: immersion heater in a funnel of crushed ice over a beaker; a control funnel with no heater (to account for melting by the room); measure energy with a joulemeter (or V, I, t); collect and weigh the water melted by the heater (mass from heated funnel minus control) over the same time; L = E ÷ m; sources of error. **L2 (3–4)** a workable method missing the control or how energy is measured. **L1 (1–2)** relevant steps only. ⚑

**Required practical**: none. 6.3.2.3 carries an AT 5 opportunity only ("Perform an experiment to measure the latent heat of fusion of water"). Do not badge the page "Required practical".

**Equations**: E = m L — **on the sheet** (8463 Appendix A sheet list eq 9; printed on both June 2026 sheets as "thermal energy for a change of state = mass × specific latent heat"). Not on the recall list. Base, both tiers and pathways.
