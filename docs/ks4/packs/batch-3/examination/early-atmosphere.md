Spec statements (verbatim; 8464 and 8462 are identical):
- **5.9.1.2:** "Theories about what was in the Earth's early atmosphere and how the atmosphere was formed have changed and developed over time. Evidence for the early atmosphere is limited because of the time scale of 4.6 billion years. One theory suggests that during the first billion years of the Earth's existence there was intense volcanic activity that released gases that formed the early atmosphere and water vapour that condensed to form the oceans. At the start of this period the Earth's atmosphere may have been like the atmospheres of Mars and Venus today, consisting of mainly carbon dioxide with little or no oxygen gas. Volcanoes also produced nitrogen which gradually built up in the atmosphere and there may have been small proportions of methane and ammonia. When the oceans formed carbon dioxide dissolved in the water and carbonates were precipitated producing sediments, reducing the amount of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere. No knowledge of other theories is required. Students should be able to, given appropriate information, interpret evidence and evaluate different theories about the Earth's early atmosphere."
- **5.9.1.3:** "Algae and plants produced the oxygen that is now in the atmosphere by photosynthesis … Algae first produced oxygen about 2.7 billion years ago and soon after this oxygen appeared in the atmosphere. Over the next billion years plants evolved and the percentage of oxygen gradually increased to a level that enabled animals to evolve."
- **5.9.1.4:** "Algae and plants decreased the percentage of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere by photosynthesis. Carbon dioxide was also decreased by the formation of sedimentary rocks and fossil fuels that contain carbon. Students should be able to: describe the main changes in the atmosphere over time and some of the likely causes of these changes; describe and explain the formation of deposits of limestone, coal, crude oil and natural gas."

## 5. For the lesson author

**What this lesson owns:** the one AQA theory (volcanoes → CO₂-rich, O₂-poor atmosphere; oceans), how O₂ increased (algae, then plants, ≈ 2.7 bn years; enabled animals), how CO₂ decreased (dissolving, precipitated carbonates, photosynthesis, sedimentary rocks, fossil fuels), the formation of limestone, coal, crude oil and natural gas, and evaluating evidence for the theory. It does not own today's percentages (5.9.1.1) or greenhouse gases (5.9.2).

**The fossil-fuel and limestone formation the pack lacks** (author must write it; base; spec 5.9.1.4)
- **Limestone:** shells and skeletons of marine organisms (calcium carbonate) settle as sediment and are compressed over millions of years into sedimentary rock. Also carbonates precipitated from the oceans.
- **Coal:** from plant material (trees and ferns) that did not fully decay, buried and compressed (heat and pressure) over millions of years.
- **Crude oil and natural gas:** from plankton / tiny marine organisms buried in mud on the sea bed and compressed over millions of years, without oxygen.
- **The point for this lesson:** each of these locks carbon from atmospheric CO₂ away in rock.

**Misconceptions seen in AQA marking**
- "Volcanoes released oxygen."
- "Oxygen came from the oceans / from water splitting."
- "Plants were around 4 billion years ago."
- Confusing early-atmosphere CO₂ loss with today's greenhouse effect.
- Fossil fuels "made from dinosaurs". Coal and oil swapped (coal from plants on land, oil from sea plankton).
- "Nitrogen came from photosynthesis" or "from the ocean".
- Writing the respiration equation instead of photosynthesis.
- Treating the theory as fact. "Evaluate" answers that say only "no one was there" with no reference to the type of evidence (rock and fossil records, other planets' atmospheres).

**Command words:** Describe, Explain, Give, Suggest, Evaluate, Compare.

**Typical questions** ⚑ examiner-drafted
- ⚑ 2 marks, Give, **base**: *"Give two ways the amount of carbon dioxide in the early atmosphere decreased."* — any two of:
  - dissolved in the oceans;
  - photosynthesis by algae/plants;
  - formed carbonates / sedimentary rocks (limestone);
  - locked in fossil fuels.
- ⚑ 3 marks, Describe, **base**: *"Describe how crude oil was formed."*
  - (1) From remains of plankton / tiny sea organisms.
  - (1) Buried in mud / sediment (without oxygen).
  - (1) Compressed (heat and pressure) over millions of years.
- ⚑ 2 marks, Explain, **base**: *"Explain why there is so much nitrogen in today's atmosphere."*
  - (1) Nitrogen was released by volcanoes.
  - (1) It is unreactive, so it was not removed (and it built up as other gases were removed).
- ⚑ 4 marks, Evaluate, **base**: *"Scientists think the early atmosphere was like the atmosphere of Mars today. Evaluate this idea."* Accept any four of:
  - (+) Mars's atmosphere is mainly CO₂ with little O₂, matching the theory.
  - (+) Mars formed at a similar time and its volcanoes released similar gases.
  - (−) Mars has no life or oceans to remove CO₂, so it may not show what happened on Earth.
  - (−) There is no direct evidence because of the 4.6 bn-year time scale.
  - (Conclusion) A judgement is reached.
- ⚑ 6 marks, Describe, **base**, the classic: *"Describe how the Earth's atmosphere changed from the early atmosphere to the atmosphere today."*
  - **Level 3 (5–6):** a sequenced account that covers:
    - volcanic origin (CO₂, H₂O, N₂, little O₂);
    - condensation into oceans, with CO₂ dissolving and carbonates forming;
    - algae, then plants, photosynthesising from ≈ 2.7 bn years, so O₂ rises and CO₂ falls;
    - CO₂ locked in sedimentary rocks and fossil fuels;
    - N₂ building up to ≈ 80 %.
  - **Level 2 (3–4):** several correct changes with causes, but the sequence is incomplete.
  - **Level 1 (1–2):** isolated facts.

**Required practical:** none. The spec suggests an opportunity to show "aquatic plants produce oxygen in daylight". That is a demonstration, not an RP.

**Equations to learn** (no chemistry equation sheet)
- Photosynthesis: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen; 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂. **Base.** No calculation, no CFIFA block.
