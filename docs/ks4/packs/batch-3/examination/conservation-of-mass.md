Spec statement (verbatim, identical in both): "The law of conservation of mass states that no atoms are lost or made during a chemical reaction so the mass of the products equals the mass of the reactants. This means that chemical reactions can be represented by symbol equations which are balanced in terms of the numbers of atoms of each element involved on both sides of the equation. Students should understand the use of the multipliers in equations in normal script before a formula and in subscript within a formula."

## 5. For the lesson author

**What this lesson owns:** the law (atoms not made or lost), balancing with coefficients (multipliers vs subscripts), word → symbol equations with state symbols, and finding a missing mass by conservation. It does **not** own: apparent mass changes with gases (`mass-changes-reactions`), moles and reacting masses (HT, `using-moles-calculations`), or half/ionic equations (`half-equations`).

**Misconceptions seen in AQA marking**
- Changing subscripts to balance: H₂O → H₂O₂, or O₂ → O.
- Putting a coefficient in the middle of a formula: H2₂O.
- A coefficient read as multiplying only the first atom. 2H₂O is 4 H and 2 O, not 4 H and 1 O. 2Ca(OH)₂ is 2 Ca, 4 O and 4 H, not 2 Ca, 2 O and 2 H.
- Diatomic elements written as single atoms: O, H, Cl.
- "Mass is lost when something burns" or "mass is gained": the gas is forgotten. Correct it here in one line; the full treatment is in 5.3.1.3.
- Coefficients read as grams.
- Fractions or decimals left as coefficients at GCSE (½O₂ is accepted in some mark schemes, but whole numbers are expected).
- State symbols: (aq) for a pure liquid; (l) for a solution.

**Command words:** Balance, Complete, Write (a balanced symbol equation), Give, Explain, Calculate.

**Typical questions** ⚑ examiner-drafted
- ⚑ 1 mark, Balance, **base**: *"Balance the equation: __Na + __Cl₂ → __NaCl."* — 2, (1), 2.
- ⚑ 2 marks, Complete, **base**: *"Complete and balance: __Al + __O₂ → __Al₂O₃."*
  - (1) Correct formulae (given here).
  - (1) 4, 3, 2.
  - If formulae must be written: (1) formulae, (1) balancing.
- ⚑ 2 marks, Calculate, **base**: *"10.0 g of calcium carbonate decomposes completely. 5.6 g of calcium oxide is formed. Calculate the mass of carbon dioxide produced."*
  - (1) 10.0 − 5.6.
  - (1) 4.4 g.
- ⚑ 2 marks, Explain, **base**: *"Explain why the total mass in a sealed flask does not change during a reaction."*
  - (1) Atoms are not made or destroyed / no atoms are lost or gained.
  - (1) Atoms are only rearranged, and the flask is sealed, so no substance enters or leaves.
- ⚑ 3 marks, Write, **base**: *"Write a balanced symbol equation, with state symbols, for the reaction of magnesium with hydrochloric acid."*
  - (1) Formulae Mg, HCl, MgCl₂, H₂.
  - (1) Balanced with 2HCl.
  - (1) State symbols (s), (aq), (aq), (g).
- A 6-mark extended response is not typical for this content alone. Rung 4 should be a written explanation chained with a calculation: for example, a 4-mark task to explain why mass is conserved and use it to find a missing mass, with marking points.

**Required practical:** none.

**Equations to learn** (no chemistry equation sheet)
- Total mass of reactants = total mass of products. **Base.**
- No formula calculation; no CFIFA block required. If the author includes a missing-mass calculation with a unit change (kg → g), give the CFIFA Convert step there.
