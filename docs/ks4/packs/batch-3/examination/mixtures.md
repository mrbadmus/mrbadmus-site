Spec statement (verbatim, identical in both): "A mixture consists of two or more elements or compounds not chemically combined together. The chemical properties of each substance in the mixture are unchanged. Mixtures can be separated by physical processes such as filtration, crystallisation, simple distillation, fractional distillation and chromatography. These physical processes do not involve chemical reactions and no new substances are made. Students should be able to: describe, explain and give examples of the specified processes of separation; suggest suitable separation and purification techniques for mixtures when given appropriate information." Skills: WS 2.2, 2.3; AT 4 "Safe use of a range of equipment to separate chemical mixtures."

**What this lesson owns, against the overlaps.**
- **Owns:**
  - the definition of a mixture, and that each substance keeps its chemical properties;
  - that separation is physical, with no new substances;
  - the five named processes: what each separates, how it works in outline, and an example of each;
  - **choosing** a technique (or a sequence: dissolve → filter → crystallise) from given information.
- **Does not own:**
  - chromatography's mechanism (phases), Rf as a skill, or the chromatography RP (`chromatography`);
  - the crude-oil column and fractions (`fractional-distillation`);
  - potable-water treatment and its RP (`potable-water`);
  - pure vs impure / melting points (`pure-substances`).
- **Rf here:** it may appear as a preview, but it is not the lesson's calculation. The pack's Rf FIFA and q2 are best left to `chromatography`, or used here only as a one-item preview (author's choice).

## 5. For the lesson author

**Misconceptions seen in AQA marking**
- "Filter the salt out of salt water."
- "Evaporate to get pure water": water is lost to the air. Pure water needs distillation, with a condenser.
- Crystallisation described as "heat until all the water has gone" (that is evaporating to dryness), or as "cooling the water into crystals" (freezing).
- Residue and filtrate swapped.
- In distillation, the thermometer bulb placed in the liquid rather than at the side-arm. Water in the condenser entering at the top.
- In fractional distillation, "the heaviest/biggest molecules rise to the top"; or the lab apparatus described as "collected at different heights".
- "Mixtures can be separated by chemical reactions", or "separation makes new substances".
- Mixture vs compound: "a mixture has fixed proportions".

**Command words:** Describe, Explain, Suggest, Give, Name, Plan (a method), Complete (a diagram).

**Typical questions** ⚑ examiner-drafted
- ⚑ 1 mark, Give, **base**: *"Give the meaning of the term mixture."* — two or more elements or compounds not chemically combined (together). Do not accept "two substances mixed".
- ⚑ 1 mark, Suggest, **base**: *"Suggest the best method to separate ethanol (boiling point 78 °C) from water (100 °C)."* — fractional distillation. Do not accept simple distillation: the boiling points are close.
- ⚑ 4 marks, Describe, **base**: *"Describe how to obtain pure, dry crystals of copper sulfate from a mixture of copper sulfate and sand."*
  - (1) Add water and stir/warm, so the copper sulfate dissolves.
  - (1) Filter to remove the sand (residue).
  - (1) Heat the filtrate to evaporate some of the water, then leave it to cool/crystallise.
  - (1) Filter off the crystals and dry them between filter papers or in a warm oven.
- ⚑ 6 marks, Plan, **base**: *"Rock salt is a mixture of sodium chloride and sand. Plan a method to obtain a sample of pure water and a sample of pure sodium chloride from rock salt."*
  - **Level 3 (5–6):** a logical sequence: dissolve, filter; split the filtrate; distil one portion with a condenser to collect water; crystallise the other. Includes at least one safety or quality point.
  - **Level 2 (3–4):** most steps, with an omission or a flawed step (for example evaporating to collect the water).
  - **Level 1 (1–2):** isolated techniques named.
- ⚑ 2 marks, Explain, **base**: *"Explain why fractional distillation, and not simple distillation, is used to separate a mixture of liquids."*
  - (1) The liquids have boiling points that are close together.
  - (1) The column lets each liquid condense and re-evaporate, so the one with the lowest boiling point reaches the top first and is collected first.

**Required practical:** none owned by this lesson. Chromatography is **8464 RP12 / 8462 RP6** and is owned by `chromatography` (5.8.1.3). The method, variables and risks go there, not here. The crystallisation steps above are also the end of the making-salts RP (8464 RP8 / 8462 RP1), owned elsewhere.

**Equations to learn** (no chemistry equation sheet)
- None owned by this lesson. If it previews Rf: Rf = distance moved by substance ÷ distance moved by solvent. **Base** (5.8.1.3).

**CFIFA:** none required. This is a CLASSIFY lesson. If Rf is previewed, the FIFA takes C = "nothing to convert". A wrong-unit Rf example (spot in mm, front in cm) belongs to `chromatography`.
