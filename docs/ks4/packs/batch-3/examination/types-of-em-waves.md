Spec statements (verbatim, 8463 = 8464): "Electromagnetic waves are transverse waves that transfer energy from the source of the waves to an absorber. Electromagnetic waves form a continuous spectrum and all types of electromagnetic wave travel at the same velocity through a vacuum (space) or air. The waves that form the electromagnetic spectrum are grouped in terms of their wavelength and their frequency. Going from long to short wavelength (or from low to high frequency) the groups are: radio, microwave, infrared, visible light (red to violet), ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays. Our eyes only detect visible light and so detect a limited range of electromagnetic waves. Students should be able to give examples that illustrate the transfer of energy by electromagnetic waves."
6.6.2.3 (identical in both): "(HT only) Radio waves can be produced by oscillations in electrical circuits. … Changes in atoms and the nuclei of atoms can result in electromagnetic waves being generated or absorbed over a wide frequency range. Gamma rays originate from changes in the nucleus of an atom."

## 5. For the lesson author
**Misconceptions seen in AQA marking**
- Spectrum order wrong — especially microwave/infrared swapped, or X-ray/gamma; order given by frequency when the question asked for wavelength (or the reverse).
- "Higher frequency waves travel faster"; "radio waves are sound waves"; "EM waves need a medium".
- "Light is not an EM wave" / "visible light is the whole spectrum".
- "Gamma and X-rays are the same thing" — distinguish by origin (nucleus vs not).
- "Longitudinal" for EM waves.
- Saying EM waves "carry particles" or "carry matter" — they transfer energy.

**Command words**: Name, Give (the order), Complete (the spectrum diagram — a frequent 2–3-mark item), Describe, Give an example, Calculate (with v = fλ, given c in the question).

**Typical questions** ⚑ examiner-drafted
- *Complete the electromagnetic spectrum (four boxes blank). [2]* — all four correct = 2; two or three correct = 1.
- *Give two properties that all electromagnetic waves share. [2]* — any two: transverse (1); travel at the same speed in a vacuum/air (1); transfer energy (1); can travel through a vacuum (1).
- *Give one example of energy being transferred by electromagnetic waves. [1]* — e.g. the Sun warms the Earth by infrared; microwaves heat food; radio waves transfer energy to an aerial.
- *A radio wave has frequency 9.0 × 10⁷ Hz. Its speed is 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s. Calculate its wavelength. [3]* — λ = v ÷ f (1); = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 9.0 × 10⁷ (1); = 3.3 m (1).
- *Explain why humans can see only part of the electromagnetic spectrum. [2]* — our eyes only detect (respond to) visible light (1); so we detect a limited range of wavelengths/frequencies (1).
- No 6-markers arise from 6.6.2.1 alone; EM 6-markers come from uses/hazards (6.6.2.3–6.6.2.4, HT explanations).

**Required practical**: none at 6.6.2.1 (RP21 Combined / RP10 Triple, infrared absorption/emission, sits at 6.6.2.2).

**Equations**: v = f λ — supporting, from 6.6.1.2; **learn it** per the recall list (8463 Appendix A eq 16) and **printed on both June 2026 sheets**. No equation is stated in 6.6.2.1 itself.
