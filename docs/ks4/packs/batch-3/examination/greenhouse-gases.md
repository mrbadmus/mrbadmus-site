Spec statements (verbatim; 8464 and 8462 identical):
- **5.9.2.1:** "Greenhouse gases in the atmosphere maintain temperatures on Earth high enough to support life. Water vapour, carbon dioxide and methane are greenhouse gases. Students should be able to describe the greenhouse effect in terms of the interaction of short and long wavelength radiation with matter."
- **5.9.2.2:** "Some human activities increase the amounts of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere. These include: carbon dioxide; methane. Students should be able to recall two human activities that increase the amounts of each of the greenhouse gases carbon dioxide and methane. Based on peer-reviewed evidence, many scientists believe that human activities will cause the temperature of the Earth's atmosphere to increase at the surface and that this will result in global climate change. However, it is difficult to model such complex systems as global climate change. This leads to simplified models, speculation and opinions presented in the media that may be based on only parts of the evidence and which may be biased. Students should be able to: evaluate the quality of evidence in a report about global climate change given appropriate information; describe uncertainties in the evidence base; recognise the importance of peer review of results and of communicating results to a wide range of audiences."
- **5.9.2.3:** "An increase in average global temperature is a major cause of climate change. There are several potential effects of global climate change. Students should be able to: describe briefly four potential effects of global climate change; discuss the scale, risk and environmental implications of global climate change."
- **5.9.2.4:** "The carbon footprint is the total amount of carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases emitted over the full life cycle of a product, service or event. The carbon footprint can be reduced by reducing emissions of carbon dioxide and methane. Students should be able to: describe actions to reduce emissions of carbon dioxide and methane; give reasons why actions may be limited."

## 5. For the lesson author

**What this lesson owns:** all four of 5.9.2.1–4: mechanism, human activities, peer review and evidence evaluation, effects, carbon footprint, and why reduction is limited. It does not own pollutants (CO, SO₂, NOₓ, particulates → `atmospheric-pollutants`) or the ozone layer (not in spec).

**Why actions may be limited** (author must write; base; the usual credited reasons)
- **Scientific disagreement or uncertainty:** some people doubt the evidence or the causes.
- **Economic considerations:** cost; countries or companies do not want to harm their economy or growth.
- **Lack of public information or education.**
- **Lifestyle:** people are reluctant to change.
- **International cooperation is difficult:** countries cannot agree, so one country's action is undone by another's.
- **Technology:** some alternatives are not yet available or not affordable (for example CCS at scale). EVs only help with low-carbon electricity.

**Peer review and evidence** (author must write; base)
- Peer review = other scientists check the work before it is published, so the evidence is more reliable.
- Climate is a complex system, so models are simplified and predictions are uncertain.
- Media reports may use only part of the evidence and may be biased.
- Evaluating a report: who wrote or paid for it; sample size, time span and range of data; whether it was peer reviewed; whether it confuses a correlation with a cause.

**Misconceptions seen in AQA marking**
- **The ozone layer confused with the greenhouse effect:** "greenhouse gases make a hole", "UV gets trapped", "CFCs cause global warming".
- **The greenhouse effect is bad / unnatural:** this misses that it is necessary for life.
- **"Greenhouse gases reflect heat back to Earth":** the creditable verb is **absorb** (then re-emit/radiate).
- **Wavelengths swapped:** saying the Sun's radiation is long wavelength and the Earth's is short.
- **Pollutant ≠ greenhouse gas:** naming SO₂, CO or particulates as greenhouse gases.
- **Climate change = only "it gets hotter":** the four-effects answer needs four *different* effects (sea level, storms, rainfall, food production, species distribution).
- **Carbon footprint = "CO₂ from a car":** this misses the full life cycle and the other gases.

**Command words:** Describe, Explain, Give, Suggest, Evaluate, Discuss.

**Typical questions** ⚑ examiner-drafted
- ⚑ 2 marks, Give, **base**: *"Give two human activities that increase the amount of methane in the atmosphere."* — livestock / cattle farming (1); landfill sites / decomposition of rubbish (1). Also accept rice paddy fields; gas leaks.
- ⚑ 4 marks, Describe, **base**: *"Describe how greenhouse gases keep the Earth warm."*
  - (1) Short-wavelength radiation from the Sun passes through the atmosphere.
  - (1) The Earth's surface absorbs it and emits long-wavelength (infrared) radiation.
  - (1) Greenhouse gases absorb the long-wavelength radiation.
  - (1) They re-emit/radiate it in all directions, including back to the Earth, so the Earth's temperature is higher.
- ⚑ 4 marks, Describe, **base**: *"Describe four potential effects of global climate change."* — four of:
  - sea level rise / flooding / coastal erosion;
  - more frequent or severe storms;
  - changes in the amount, timing or distribution of rainfall;
  - temperature and water stress for humans and wildlife;
  - changes in food-producing capacity;
  - changes in distribution of wildlife species.
  - Do not credit "it gets hotter" alone. Do not credit ozone damage.
- ⚑ 2 marks, Give, **base**: *"Give two reasons why actions to reduce carbon footprints may be limited."* — any two from the list above.
- ⚑ 6 marks, Evaluate, **base**: *"A newspaper report claims that one cold winter proves global warming is not happening. Evaluate the claim."* (Data: 50-year global temperature record and CO₂ record.)
  - **Level 3 (5–6):** distinguishes weather from climate and a single event from a long-term trend; uses the data (rising trend with year-to-year variation); considers source and bias, and peer review; reaches a justified conclusion.
  - **Level 2 (3–4):** uses the data or the evidence-quality points, but not both.
  - **Level 1 (1–2):** an unsupported opinion with isolated facts.
