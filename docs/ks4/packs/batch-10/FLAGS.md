## 1. Ecosystems (`ecosystems`)

# Flags — ecosystems (examiner, 2 Oct 2026)

**ECO-F1** · ROUTE · `higher` (served CH TH only): "Students should be able to explain how changes to abiotic and biotic factors affect communities, using data as evidence. Stable communities have balanced interactions … The concept of the ecosystem as a self-sustaining unit with inputs (sunlight, inorganic nutrients) and outputs (heat, waste) is important for understanding sustainability." · (a) The first two sentences are not HT: explaining a change in a factor from data is the base skill on every route, so CF and TF pupils currently lose it. (b) The third sentence is in neither spec, and is misleading: mineral nutrients are recycled within an ecosystem, not taken in and sent out as "waste". · "Students should be able to explain how a change in an abiotic factor would affect a given community given appropriate data or context." (4.7.1.2; same for biotic, 4.7.1.3); "All materials in the living world are recycled to provide the building blocks for future organisms." (4.7.2.2) · 8464/8461 4.7.1.1–4.7.1.3, 4.7.2.2 · Teach the data skill on all four routes; badge nothing Higher. Drop the inputs/outputs sentence.

**ECO-F2** · IMPRECISE · th3 "ARTIFICIAL ECOSYSTEMS created by humans include: farmland (agricultural fields), fish farms, ornamental gardens, nature reserves." and "LEVEL OF PRODUCTIVITY (how much energy is fixed by plants)." · Nature reserves protect natural or semi-natural habitat; they are a measure to maintain biodiversity, not an artificial ecosystem. "Productivity" is in neither spec. · Biodiversity is "the variety of all the different species of organisms on earth, or within an ecosystem." · 8464/8461 4.7.3.1, 4.7.3.6 · Drop nature reserves from the artificial list and drop productivity. Keep farmland/monoculture (q3 depends on it).

**ECO-F3** · IMPRECISE · th2 "A STABLE COMMUNITY is one where populations remain roughly constant over time — because the various checks and balances (predation, competition, disease) keep each population within its range." · Leaves out the environmental (abiotic) factors, which the spec definition names. · "A stable community is one where all the species and environmental factors are in balance so that population sizes remain fairly constant." · 8464/8461 4.7.1.1 · Use the spec sentence. **ECO-F4** · IMPRECISE (minor) · q2 wx1: "Ecosystems rarely collapse immediately — they often shift to a new, less diverse stable state. Science is correct. · — · — · Usable verbatim on all routes; no change to frozen text.

**ECO-F5** · GAP (minor) · Competition is absent from this file. · Spec 4.7.1.1 asks for "the importance of interdependence and competition in a community" and "suggest the factors for which organisms are competing in a given habitat". # Flags — adaptations (examiner, 2 Oct 2026)

**AD-F2** · IMPRECISE (minor) · key_note "Cold adaptations: small surface area, insulation." · It is the surface area **to volume ratio** that matters; a large animal has a large surface area but a small SA:V. th2 has it right. · "small ears and limbs … reduces surface area to volume ratio, minimising heat loss" (th2). · 8464/8461 4.1.3.1; 4.7.1.4 · Re-cut as "small surface area to volume ratio".

**AD-F3** · IMPRECISE (minor) · th2 "Bioluminescence — FUNCTIONAL"; th2 "Hibernation — BEHAVIOURAL"; th3 "Speed and agility — STRUCTURAL/FUNCTIONAL". · Each sits across two types: bioluminescence needs a light organ (structural) and a chemical reaction (functional); hibernation is a behaviour with large functional changes.
