## higher

Write balanced ionic equations for reactions of transition metals and their compounds. Explain variable oxidation states in terms of d-electron configuration — transition metals can lose different numbers of electrons from the 3d and 4s subshells. Calculate the oxidation state of a transition metal from a compound formula. Evaluate the use of specific transition metals as catalysts with reference to their variable oxidation states.

## common_mistake

Transition metals have HIGH melting points, HIGH density and are HARD — the opposite of Group 1. Group 1 are soft, low melting point, low density and very reactive. Don't confuse their properties. Mercury is the exception among transition metals — it is a liquid at room temperature.

## key_note

Transition metals vs Group 1: higher melting point, higher density, harder, much less reactive. Typical properties: coloured compounds, catalytic activity, variable oxidation states, high strength. Key examples: iron (Haber process catalyst, steel), copper (wiring), nickel (hydrogenation), platinum (catalytic converters).
