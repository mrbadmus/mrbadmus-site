Define the mole, use the Avogadro constant and calculate moles from mass and Mr.

## common_mistake

Make sure mass is in GRAMS before dividing by Mr. If given mass in kg, multiply by 1000 first. Also: 1 mole of H₂O contains 6.02 × 10²³ MOLECULES — but 3 × 6.02 × 10²³ ATOMS (2 H + 1 O per molecule). Always be clear about what particles are being counted.

## key_note

1 mole = 6.02 × 10²³ particles (Avogadro constant). n = m ÷ Mr. The molar mass = Mr in g/mol. % mass = (Ar × atoms ÷ Mr) × 100.

## fifas — CFIFA form (Convert step added; the four FIFA steps below are verbatim)

**Question:** Calculate the number of moles in 27 g of aluminium (Ar = 27).

**Convert:** [NEW — examiner-corrected] Nothing to convert — the mass is already in grams, and n = m ÷ Mr needs the mass in grams (Ar has no unit).

**F:** moles = mass ÷ Mr

**I:** moles = 27 ÷ 27

**F:** moles = 1

**A:** 1 mol of aluminium
