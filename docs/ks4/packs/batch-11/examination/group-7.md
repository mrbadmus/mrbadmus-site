## 5. For the lesson author
**Misconceptions seen in AQA marking**
- "Reactivity increases down Group 7, like Group 1."
- "Fluorine is more reactive because it has fewer electrons / more protons" instead of the outer shell being closer to the nucleus.
- Explaining the halogen trend in terms of **losing** electrons, i.e. Group 1 reasoning carried across.
- "Bromine displaces chlorine" (the order reversed); "iodine displaces chloride".
- Explaining the boiling-point trend by covalent bonds breaking instead of intermolecular forces.
- Writing halogens as single atoms (Cl instead of Cl₂) in equations.

**Command words**: Explain why reactivity decreases, Predict (astatine; a displacement), Describe what you would see, Complete the equation, (HT) Write an ionic equation / identify what is oxidised.

**Typical questions** ⚑ examiner-drafted
- *Explain why chlorine is more reactive than bromine. [3]*: chlorine atom smaller / fewer shells (1); outer shell closer to the nucleus (1); stronger attraction for an electron to be gained (1).
- *Chlorine water is added to potassium iodide solution. Describe what you would see and explain why. [2]*: solution turns brown (1); chlorine is more reactive than iodine, so it displaces iodine (1).
- *Predict the state of astatine at room temperature. Give a reason. [2]*: solid (1); melting/boiling points increase down the group (1).
- (HT) *Write the ionic equation for chlorine reacting with bromide ions. [2]*: Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂ (1 species, 1 balance).

**Required practical**: none.

**Equations**: three balanced symbol equations (displacement). The HT layer adds the ionic form. No formula triangles.
