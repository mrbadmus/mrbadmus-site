## 5. For the lesson author
**Misconceptions seen in AQA marking**
- "Transition metals are more reactive because they are harder."
- Properties listed without a comparison word. AQA wants "higher melting point **than** Group 1".
- "Transition metals react vigorously with water", i.e. Group 1 behaviour carried across.
- Iron named as a catalyst for the wrong process, or "platinum in the Haber process".
- Naming the property with no example. The spec asks for exemplification by Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, Cu or their compounds.
- "All metal compounds are coloured." Group 1 compounds are white.

**Command words**: Compare (with Group 1), Describe two differences, Give one property / one use, Suggest why… is used as a catalyst.

**Typical questions** ⚑ examiner-drafted
- *Compare the properties of iron with those of sodium. [4]*: iron higher melting point (1); higher density (1); harder / stronger (1); less reactive with water/oxygen (1).
- *Give two properties of transition metal compounds that are not shown by Group 1 compounds. [2]*: coloured (1); useful as catalysts / ions with different charges (1).
- *Iron forms two chlorides, FeCl₂ and FeCl₃. What does this show about iron ions? [1]*: iron forms ions with different charges (Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺).

**Required practical**: none. **Equations**: none.
