Spec statements (verbatim, 8461 4.5.2.3):
- "Students should be able to relate the structures of the eye to their functions. This includes: • accommodation to focus on near or distant objects • adaptation to dim light."
- "The eye is a sense organ containing receptors sensitive to light intensity and colour."
- "Students should be able to identify the following structures on a diagram of the eye and explain how their structure is related to their function: • retina • optic nerve • sclera • cornea • iris • ciliary muscles • suspensory ligaments."
- "Accommodation is the process of changing the shape of the lens to focus on near or distant objects. To focus on a near object: • the ciliary muscles contract • the suspensory ligaments loosen • the lens is then thicker and refracts light rays strongly. To focus on a distant object: • the ciliary muscles relax • the suspensory ligaments are pulled tight • the lens is then pulled thin and only slightly refracts light rays."

## 5. For the lesson author
**Misconceptions seen in AQA marking**
- Near/far accommodation reversed (the commonest error on this topic).
- "The suspensory ligaments contract" — ligaments are not muscles; they loosen or are pulled tight.
- "The lens does all the focusing" — the cornea does most of the refraction; the lens fine-tunes.
- "The pupil is a muscle" / "the pupil contracts" — the iris muscles contract; the pupil is a hole.
- "The retina sends light to the brain" — receptors produce impulses; the optic nerve carries impulses.

**Command words**: Identify / Label, Describe, Explain how the structure is related to its function, Explain (accommodation).

**Typical questions** ⚑ examiner-drafted
- *Label the cornea, iris, retina, sclera and optic nerve on the diagram. [5]*
- *Describe how the eye focuses on a near object. [3]* — ciliary muscles contract (1); suspensory ligaments loosen (1); lens becomes thicker / refracts light more strongly (1).
- *Explain why the pupil gets smaller in bright light. [2]* — circular muscles of iris contract (1); less light enters, protecting the retina (1).
