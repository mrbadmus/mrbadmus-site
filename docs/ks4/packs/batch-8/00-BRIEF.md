## The four lesson rules (Mide, 2 Oct 2026) — apply to every lesson here

1. **Start here is a two-option guess**, framed as a guess, answerable from everyday experience on every route; the reveal is encouraging either way and leads straight into the teaching.
2. **Equations are formula triangles you can cover** — in the equation block, the equation-sheet panel and CFIFA's Formula step. A square or a ½ gets its own extra line (square-root, ×2).
3. **Teach every step before you test it.** A calculation that chains two equations gets its own "Step 1 … Step 2 …" worked example before any pupil does one; CFIFA on each step.
4. **Practice is the same size in every lesson** — same number of questions at each rung of the end practice and the exam ladder. Set the number once (at least the content-standards floor) and keep it across the batch.

Each lesson below says which calculations chain, which equations need triangles, and how many verbatim quiz items are usable; the rest of each bank is yours to write to the fixed size.

### 1. Homeostasis
`homeostasis` · Biology / homeostasis · AQA 8464 4.5.1; 8461 4.5.1 · CF CH TF TH

**Family:** SYSTEM. A receptor, a coordination centre and an effector work together; the lesson is "what happens to the level when one part fails?" (BATCH-PLAN's suggestion kept).

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** Push a variable (temperature, glucose, water) off its normal level; the pupil predicts which component reacts next and watches the level come back.

**Route layers:**
- Higher: the name "negative feedback" and interpreting feedback-loop diagrams. 8464 4.5.3.6 (HT only) / 8461 4.5.3.7 (HT only); glucagon raising low glucose, 8464/8461 4.5.3.2 (HT only). Base says "the response restores the optimum level" (F1).
- No triple layer. Shivering and sweating are fine as named examples; their mechanism is `thermoregulation`.

**Required practical:** none.

**Calculations:** none.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "Negative feedback means the body did something bad." → It means the response opposes the change and brings the level back (4.5.3.6/4.5.3.7, HT).
- "Homeostasis keeps everything exactly the same." → Levels move slightly and are brought back to the optimum (4.5.1 "restore optimum levels").
- "The brain is the effector." → Effectors are muscles or glands; the brain is a coordination centre (4.5.1).
- "Homeostasis is just about temperature." → In humans it also controls blood glucose and water levels (4.5.1).
- "Control is always by nerves." → It may be nervous or chemical (4.5.1) (F4).

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** Everyone has sweated when hot or shivered when cold: when you get hot, does your body try to warm you up more or cool you down?

**Practice (rule 4):** 3 frozen items. q2 usable on all four routes. q1 usable on all four routes only if the base layer gives "negative feedback" as a name first, otherwise CH TH (F1). q3 is CH TH only: it is glucagon, HT (F1, F3). Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size.

**Diagrams:** `05-diagram-library/README.md` row 1 (nothing exists). Must draw: a general receptor → coordination centre → effector loop with the level line returning to normal. Higher copy: the same loop labelled "negative feedback".

**Examiner tip:** none approved.

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** A stimulus → effector chain where the pupil breaks one neurone or synapse and predicts which responses are lost.

**Route layers:** none. All base. The source's `higher` text (CH, TH) has no HT home and is off-spec; do not show it (F1).

**Required practical:** none (reaction-time RP is in `reaction-time`).

**Calculations:** none.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.

**Misconceptions to confront:**

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** Everyone has pulled their hand off something hot: does the message travel through your body as electricity in nerves, or in your blood?

### 4. Reaction Time
`reaction-time` · Biology / homeostasis · AQA 8464 4.5.2 (RP 6); 8461 4.5.2.1 (RP 7); cross-link 8464 6.5.4.3.2 / 8463 4.5.6.3.2 · CF CH TF TH

**Family:** REQUIRED PRACTICAL. The lesson is RP 6/7 itself: plan, run and process a ruler-drop investigation into one factor (BATCH-PLAN's suggestion kept).

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** A simulated ruler drop: the pupil picks a factor (caffeine or distraction), runs repeats, reads times from a conversion table, takes means and plots the bar chart.

**Route layers:** none. All base. No HT, no biology-only statement.

**Required practical:** Combined **RP 6** (8464 4.5.2; §10.2.6) = Biology **RP 7** (8461 4.5.2.1; §8.2.7): "plan and carry out an investigation into the effect of a factor on human reaction time." The same practical on all four routes (CF CH: RP 6; TF TH: RP 7). Ethics/safety (AT 4) and table → graph (MS 4a) must be added (F2).

**Calculations:**
- Mean of repeated reaction times (WS 3.7). Chains equations? no. Convert: none. Base.
- Distance → reaction time from a conversion table or graph (MS 4a). Chains equations? no. Convert: read the table's units (cm vs m, s vs ms). Base.
- Optional only, OFF-SPEC (F1): t = √(2d ÷ g) from d = ½ g t². Chains equations? no (one equation, rearranged). Convert: cm → m (the frozen FIFA: 20 cm = 0.20 m → 0.2 s). Not HT, not on any spec. If kept, it needs a second worked example with nothing to convert, before q1.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none on spec. If F1's optional calculation is kept: d = ½ g t², label **"Given in the question"** (it is on neither the 8464 nor the 8463 June 2026 sheet). Triangle: d on top; ½ g and t² on the bottom. It has a ½ and a square, so the extra steps are their own lines: cover t² → t² = 2d ÷ g, then a separate line t = √(2d ÷ g).

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "Repeating it makes it a fair test." → Repeats and a mean reduce random error; control variables make it fair (WS 3.7).
- "Further down the ruler means faster reactions." → A longer fall distance means a longer reaction time.
- "Caffeine slows you down." → Caffeine is a stimulant; alcohol, tiredness and distraction slow reactions (6.5.4.3.2 / 4.5.6.3.2).
- "Everyone's reaction time is 0.2 s." → Typical values range from 0.2 s to 0.9 s, and vary person to person (6.5.4.3.2 / 4.5.6.3.2).
- "Any number of people can be given as much caffeine as you like." → The investigation must be safe and ethical (RP AT 4).

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** Everyone has tried to catch something someone dropped: are you quicker to catch it when you're wide awake, or when you're tired?

**Practice (rule 4):** 2 frozen items. q2 usable on all four routes. q1 usable on all four routes only if the optional calculation (F1) is kept and taught first; otherwise not usable. No frozen item covers planning, variables, ethics or reading a conversion table. Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size.

**Diagrams:** `05-diagram-library/README.md` row 4: `figlib/charts.py line_graph()` for the results chart; nothing exists for the set-up. Must draw: the ruler-drop set-up (ruler vertical, 0 cm at the catcher's fingers, distance read at the top of the thumb), plus a mean-reaction-time-per-condition bar chart.

**Examiner tip:** none approved.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "To see close up, the ciliary muscles relax." → Near: ciliary muscles contract, ligaments loosen, lens thicker (4.5.2.3).

### 8. Thermoregulation
`thermoregulation` · Biology / homeostasis · AQA 8464 — (4.5.1 lists body temperature only); 8461 4.5.2.4 · TF TH

**Family:** SYSTEM — receptors → thermoregulatory centre → effectors (blood vessels, sweat glands, muscles), switching between two branches as temperature crosses 37 °C.

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** A body-temperature dial: push the body too hot or too cold and watch the skin's vessels, sweat glands and muscles respond, with the energy flow to the surroundings shown.

**Route layers:**
- Whole page: triple — 8461 4.5.2.4 (biology only).
- Explaining how each mechanism lowers or raises body temperature in a given context: triple-higher — 8461 4.5.2.4 (HT only). The source's `higher` is already TH-only; the theory's explanations and q3 also belong to TH (F2).

**Required practical:** none.

**Calculations:** none.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "When you're hot, your blood vessels move closer to the skin." → They widen (vasodilate); they do not move (4.5.2.4).
- "Vasoconstriction makes heat." → It reduces energy transfer from the skin; shivering is what releases more energy (4.5.2.4).
- "Sweat cools you because it's cold." → Water evaporating from the skin transfers energy to the environment (4.5.2.4).
- "When you're cold you sweat less, so you warm up." → Sweating stops so less energy is lost; it adds no heat (4.5.2.4).
- (TH) "Shivering warms you by friction." → Muscle contraction needs respiration, which releases energy (4.5.2.4 HT).

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** Everyone has gone red in the face after running: is your body trying to lose heat or keep it?

**Practice (rule 4):** 3 frozen items. q2 (vasodilation) usable TF TH. q1 (sweating) **not usable as written** — wx2 is wrong (F1); usable TF TH with the corrected wx2. q3 (shivering) TH only (F2). Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size; TF needs a "too cold" response item.

**Diagrams:** `05-diagram-library/README.md` #8 (nothing reusable). Must draw: skin cross-section, too hot vs too cold — arteriole dilated/constricted, sweat gland on/off, shivering muscle; arrows for energy to the environment. Hairs optional (off-spec, F4).

### 9. The Endocrine System
`endocrine-system` · Biology / Homeostasis · AQA 8464 4.5.3.1 (+4.5.3.6 HT); 8461 4.5.3.1 (+4.5.3.7 HT) (site's "4.5.3" is the whole topic) · true routes CF CH TF TH (base + higher)

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** a body map where a pupil fires a nerve impulse and a hormone at the same target and watches one arrive at once and fade, the other arrive late and last.

**Route layers:**

**Required practical:** none.

**Calculations:** none.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "Hormones travel along nerves." → Glands secrete them directly into the bloodstream.
- "Every organ responds to every hormone." → The blood reaches every organ; only the target organ responds.
- "Hormones are faster because they go everywhere at once." → Hormonal effects are slower but act for longer.
- "The brain controls hormones, so the pituitary isn't a gland." → The pituitary is a gland in the brain, the master gland whose hormones act on other glands.
- "Adrenaline makes you calm down after a fright." (HT) → It raises heart rate and boosts oxygen and glucose delivery to muscles and brain.

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** a fright makes your heart pound for minutes after the danger has gone — a guess between "a message along nerves" and "a chemical in your blood" stands on that.

### 10. Blood Glucose Control and Diabetes
`blood-glucose-diabetes` · Biology / Homeostasis · AQA 8464 4.5.3.2; 8461 4.5.3.2 (site's "4.5.3" is the whole topic) · true routes CF CH TF TH (base + higher)

**Family:** SYSTEM — a control loop with one arm (insulin) on every route and two arms (insulin + glucagon, negative feedback) on Higher; Type 1 vs Type 2 is what happens when the loop breaks (BATCH-PLAN's suggestion kept).

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** a blood-glucose level that a meal pushes up — the pupil releases insulin and watches glucose leave the blood into cells and liver glycogen; then break the pancreas (Type 1) or the cells' response (Type 2) and watch the level stay high.

**Route layers:**
- Higher: glucagon — too low → glycogen converted to glucose and released into the blood; glucagon and insulin interact in a negative feedback cycle — 8464/8461 4.5.3.2 (HT only). The words "negative feedback" are HT throughout.

**Required practical:** none.

**Calculations:** none. The spec's quantitative skill is reading blood-glucose-against-time graphs, with and without diabetes (MS 2c, base) — no equation (F2). The source's "FIFA" is a description, not a calculation; do not mount CFIFA for it (F5).

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "Insulin is sugar / insulin raises blood sugar." → Insulin lowers it: glucose moves from the blood into cells.
- "Glycogen and glucagon are the same thing." (HT) → Glycogen is the store in liver and muscle; glucagon is the hormone that converts it back to glucose.
- "Type 2 means you can't make insulin." → In Type 2 the body cells no longer respond to the insulin the pancreas makes.
- "All diabetics inject insulin." → Type 1 is treated with injections; Type 2 commonly with a carbohydrate-controlled diet and exercise.
- "Insulin could just be swallowed." → It is a protein; proteases would digest it.
- "Diabetes is caused by eating sugar." → Obesity is a risk factor for Type 2; Type 1 is the pancreas failing to make enough insulin.
