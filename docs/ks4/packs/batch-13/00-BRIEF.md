## The four lesson rules (Mide, 2 Oct 2026) — apply to every lesson here

1. **Start here is a two-option guess**, framed as a guess, answerable from everyday experience on every route; the reveal is encouraging either way and leads straight into the teaching.
2. **Equations are formula triangles you can cover** — in the equation block, the equation-sheet panel and CFIFA's Formula step. A square or a ½ gets its own extra line (square-root, ×2).
3. **Teach every step before you test it.** A calculation that chains two equations gets its own "Step 1 … Step 2 …" worked example before any pupil does one; CFIFA on each step.
4. **Practice is the same size in every lesson** — same number of questions at each rung of the end practice and the exam ladder. Set the number once (at least the content-standards floor) and keep it across the batch.

Each lesson below says which calculations chain, which equations need triangles, and how many verbatim quiz items are usable; the rest of each bank is yours to write to the fixed size.

### 1. Crude Oil, Hydrocarbons and Alkanes
`crude-oil-hydrocarbons` · Chemistry / organic · AQA 8464 5.7.1.1; 8462 4.7.1.1 · CF CH TF TH

- **Family:** CLASSIFY — the spec's skill is "recognise substances as alkanes given their formulae"; pupils sort formulae against CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ (BATCH-PLAN kept).
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** a sorter where pupils drop molecular and displayed formulae into "alkane / not an alkane" by testing H = 2n + 2.
- **Route layers:**
  - higher — moles from combustion equations, 8464 5.3.2.2 (HT only) / 8462 4.3.2.2 (HT only). Everything else is base.
- **Required practical:** none.
- **Calculations:**
  - Formula of an alkane from n: CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ — chains equations? no — no units — base.
  - Balancing a complete-combustion equation (methane, propane) — chains? no — no units — base (8464 5.7.1.3).
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none — CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ is a pattern and the rest are chemical equations; chemistry has no equation sheet (all "Learn it").
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "Crude oil is one chemical" — it is a mixture of a very large number of compounds (5.7.1.1).
  - "Hydrocarbons contain oxygen, they burn" — hydrogen and carbon only; oxygen comes from the air (5.7.1.1, 5.7.1.3).
  - "Alkanes go CₙH₂ₙ" — that is alkenes; alkanes are CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ (5.7.1.1).
  - "H–C–H is methane" — methane has four C–H bonds; a displayed formula shows every bond (5.7.1.1).
  - "Incomplete combustion just makes less CO₂" — it makes toxic CO and soot (5.9.3.1–2).
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** petrol, diesel, gas for a cooker — everyday fuels that all come from the same black liquid.
- **Practice (rule 4):** 2 verbatim quiz items usable on all four routes (q1, q2); none unusable. Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size.
- **Diagrams:** see `05-diagram-library/README.md` — displayed formulae methane–butane (`displayed_formula`); must draw: a "crude oil is a mixture" figure.
- **Examiner tip:** none approved.

### 2. Fractional Distillation of Crude Oil
`fractional-distillation` · Chemistry / organic · AQA 8464 5.7.1.2; 8462 4.7.1.2 · CF CH TF TH

- **Family:** PROCESS — the spec asks pupils to "explain how fractional distillation works in terms of evaporation and condensation", a sequence (BATCH-PLAN kept).
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** a column where pupils send one molecule up and watch it condense where the column first falls below its boiling point.
- **Route layers:** none — all base, no HT content.
- **Required practical:** none.
- **Calculations:** none.
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "Heavy/dense fractions sink to the bottom" — the level is set by boiling point against the column's temperature gradient, not density (5.7.1.2).
  - "Short chains collect at the bottom" — short chains have low boiling points and condense near the cool top (5.7.1.2).
  - "Fractional distillation is a chemical reaction" — it is evaporation and condensation; nothing new is made (5.7.1.2).
  - "Each fraction is one pure substance" — each fraction is molecules with a *similar number* of carbon atoms (5.7.1.2).
  - "More viscous means runnier" — more viscous means thicker, flows less easily (5.7.1.3).
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** steam from a pan condenses on a cold lid — gas turns back to liquid where it is cooler.
- **Practice (rule 4):** 1 verbatim quiz item usable on all four routes (q2); q1 not usable (FRACTIONAL-DISTILLATION-F2). Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size.
- **Diagrams:** see `05-diagram-library/README.md` — `fractional_distillation()` covers the column directly; no new figure needed. Label only the spec's fuels (LPG, petrol, kerosene, diesel oil, heavy fuel oil); no bp/chain-number ranges required.
- **Examiner tip:** none approved.

### 3. Properties of Hydrocarbons
`properties-of-hydrocarbons` · Chemistry / organic · AQA 8464 5.7.1.3; 8462 4.7.1.3 · CF CH TF TH

- **Family:** MODEL — one idea (bigger molecules, stronger forces between them) explains all three trends (BATCH-PLAN kept).
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** a chain-length slider that grows the molecule and moves three gauges — boiling point up, viscosity up, flammability down.
- **Route layers:** none — all base, no HT content. (The `higher` field's bromine-addition mechanism is chemistry-only 8462 4.7.2.2 and belongs in reactions-of-alkenes; not on this page.)
- **Required practical:** none ("Investigate the properties of different hydrocarbons" is a skills opportunity, not an RP).
- **Calculations:**
  - Balancing a complete-combustion equation for a hydrocarbon of given formula (e.g. octane: 2C₈H₁₈ + 25O₂ → 16CO₂ + 18H₂O) — chains equations? no — no units — base. Teach the C → H → O order, and the ×2 to clear a half (12.5O₂) as its own line.
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none — chemical equations only; chemistry has no equation sheet.
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "Long chains burn better because there's more fuel" — flammability decreases with molecular size (5.7.1.3).
  - "Boiling breaks the covalent bonds" — it overcomes the forces between molecules, which grow with size (5.2.2.4).
  - "Thick oil is more flammable" — viscous, long-chain fuels are the hardest to ignite (5.7.1.3).
  - "Combustion makes carbon and hydrogen disappear" — they are oxidised to CO₂ and H₂O (5.7.1.3).
  - "Above its boiling point means solid" — a solid needs its melting point above room temperature.
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** petrol catches fire easily; thick engine oil or candle wax doesn't.
- **Practice (rule 4):** 1 verbatim quiz item usable on all four routes (q1); q2 not usable (PROPERTIES-OF-HYDROCARBONS-F1). Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size.
- **Diagrams:** see `05-diagram-library/README.md` — must draw: a chain-length vs boiling-point trend chart with real data (methane −161 °C … hexadecane 287 °C), via `line_graph()`/`column_chart()`.
- **Examiner tip:** none approved.

### 4. Cracking and Alkenes
`cracking-alkenes` · Chemistry / organic · AQA 8464 5.7.1.4; 8462 4.7.1.4 · CF CH TF TH

- **Family:** PROCESS — conditions in, smaller alkane + alkene out, then the bromine-water test on the product (BATCH-PLAN kept).
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** a cracker where a long alkane goes in, pupils choose catalytic or steam conditions, and it splits into a shorter alkane and an alkene that then decolourises bromine water.
- **Route layers:**
  - triple — alkene names, formulae, CₙH₂ₙ: 8462 4.7.2.1 (chemistry only); 8464 5.7.1.4 "Students do not need to know the formulae or names of individual alkenes".
  - triple — addition reactions (H₂, Br₂, H₂O): 8462 4.7.2.2 (chemistry only). Taught fully in reactions-of-alkenes; at most a pointer here.
  - triple — addition polymerisation: 8462 4.7.3.1 (chemistry only).
  - triple-higher — addition vs condensation polymerisation: 8462 4.7.3.2 (HT only; chemistry only). Not needed on this page.
- **Required practical:** none.
- **Calculations:**
  - Balancing a cracking equation given the formulae (e.g. C₁₀H₂₂ → C₈H₁₈ + C₂H₄) — chains equations? no — no units — base. Teach: count C, then H, on each side.
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none — chemical equations only.
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "Cracking separates crude oil" — that is fractional distillation; cracking breaks molecules (5.7.1.4).
  - "Cracking only makes smaller alkanes" — the products include alkenes (5.7.1.4).
  - "Bromine water goes clear" — it goes from orange to **colourless** (5.7.1.4).
  - "Alkanes decolourise bromine water too, just slowly" — alkanes give no change (5.7.1.4).
  - "We crack because there's not enough crude oil" — we crack because demand for small-molecule fuels exceeds supply (5.7.1.4).
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** there is plenty of thick, heavy oil but drivers want petrol — could you break the big molecules into small ones?
- **Practice (rule 4):** 2 verbatim quiz items usable on all four routes (q1, q2); none unusable (q2 wx2 is weak, CRACKING-ALKENES-F5). Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size.
- **Diagrams:** see `05-diagram-library/README.md` — `cracking_apparatus()` and `displayed_formula("ethene")` cover it; must add steam-cracking conditions as text/label (no figure exists or is needed).
- **Examiner tip:** none approved.

### 5. Structure and Formulae of Alkenes
`structure-of-alkenes` · Chemistry / organic · AQA 8462 4.7.2.1 (no 8464 equivalent) · TF TH

- **Family:** CONTRAST — alkane vs alkene, side by side: same carbons, two fewer hydrogens, one C=C (BATCH-PLAN kept).
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** paired displayed formulae (ethane/ethene … pentane/pentene) where pupils remove two H atoms to make the C=C.
- **Route layers:**
  - triple — the whole page: 8462 4.7.2.1 (chemistry only). No HT content.
- **Required practical:** none.
- **Calculations:**
  - Formula from n: CₙH₂ₙ — chains equations? no — no units — triple.
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none — CₙH₂ₙ is a pattern, not a triangle.
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "Unsaturated means it has extra hydrogen" — it has two fewer H than the alkane (4.7.2.1).
  - "Alkenes are CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ like alkanes" — alkenes are CₙH₂ₙ (4.7.2.1).
  - "H₂C=CH₂ is the displayed formula" — a displayed formula draws every atom and every bond (4.7.2.1, 4.7.2.2).
  - "Butane and butene are the same molecule" — -ane single bonds, -ene a C=C (4.7.2.1).
  - "Both alkanes and alkenes decolourise bromine water" — only alkenes do (4.7.1.4).
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** "saturated" sponge — full, can't take any more; would a molecule with room for more atoms be more or less reactive?

### 6. Reactions of Alkenes
`reactions-of-alkenes` · Chemistry / organic · AQA 8462 4.7.2.2 (no 8464 equivalent) · TF TH

- **Family:** CLASSIFY — one rule (add across C=C) applied to each reagent; pupils match reagent → conditions → product (BATCH-PLAN kept).
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** an alkene (ethene–pentene) plus a chosen reagent (H₂, H₂O, Cl₂, Br₂, I₂): the C=C opens and the two atoms attach, one either side, giving the displayed product.
- **Route layers:**
  - triple — the whole page: 8462 4.7.2.2 (chemistry only); atom economy 8462 4.3.3.2 (chemistry only). No HT content.
- **Required practical:** none.
- **Calculations:**
  - Balancing addition equations (always 1 : 1 : 1) — chains equations? no — no units — triple.
  - Atom economy = 100% for addition (one product) — conceptual; no numerical calculation required here.
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none — chemical equations only.
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "Addition swaps an atom" — atoms add across the C=C, which becomes a single bond; nothing leaves (4.7.2.2).
  - "Ethene + water makes ethanoic acid" — it makes ethanol; acid needs oxidation (4.7.2.2, 4.7.2.3).
  - "Only bromine adds" — chlorine and iodine add the same way (4.7.2.2).
  - "100% atom economy means 100% yield" — it means one product, no by-product (4.3.3.2).
  - "Alkenes burn cleanly like alkanes" — they tend to burn with smoky flames from incomplete combustion (4.7.2.2).
  - "Bonds store energy that is released" — energy is released when bonds form (4.5.1.3).
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** two people holding both hands can let go of one hand to hold someone new — could a double bond do that?
- **Practice (rule 4):** 2 verbatim quiz items usable on TF and TH (q1, q2); none unusable. Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size.
- **Diagrams:** see `05-diagram-library/README.md` — `displayed_formula()` for reactants/products; must draw: an addition-reaction figure (C=C opening, two atoms adding) and a bromine-water test (orange → colourless).

### 7. Alcohols
`alcohols` · Chemistry / organic · AQA 8462 4.7.2.3 (chemistry only; + 4.7.2.2 for hydration) · TF TH (no HT layer)

- **Family:** CLASSIFY. The spec asks pupils to recognise alcohols from names/formulae and say what each of four reagents does; one –OH decides every answer (BATCH-PLAN kept).
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** a reagent picker: methanol…butanol × sodium / burn / water / oxidising agent; the pupil predicts the observation before it runs.
- **Route layers:** none. 4.7.2.3 carries no HT label; the source's `higher` field is not a layer (ALC-F1).
- **Required practical:** none.
- **Calculations:** none. Balancing the combustion equation of any of the first four alcohols is a required skill (the only alcohol equation pupils must balance); give it a worked example (ethanol: C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O) before pupils balance methanol/propanol/butanol.
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "Fermentation needs it hot, like hydration." Yeast at about 30 °C; high temperature kills it (4.7.2.3).
  - "Alcohols have OH so they're alkalis." They dissolve to give a neutral solution (4.7.2.3).
  - "Methanol is the alcohol in drinks." Ethanol; methanol is poisonous.
  - "Fermentation needs air." It must be anaerobic.
  - "Sodium reacts the same with alcohol as with water." It fizzes, but less vigorously.
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** wine, beer and hand gel are alcohol mixed into water — does alcohol mix with water or float on top like oil?
- **Practice (rule 4):** q1 and q2 usable verbatim on TF and TH (2 per route). None unusable. Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size (neutral solution, uses, sodium observation, oxidation product, balancing combustion).

### 9. Addition Polymerisation
`addition-polymerisation` · Chemistry / organic · AQA 8462 4.7.3.1 (chemistry only; + 4.3.3.2 atom economy) · TF TH (no HT layer)

- **Family:** PROCESS. The spec skill is a procedure run both ways: monomer → repeat unit → polymer, and repeat unit → monomer (BATCH-PLAN kept).
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** a stepper: open the C=C of a given monomer, link three units, box the repeat unit with bonds through the brackets and n; then run it backwards from a given repeat unit.
- **Route layers:** none. 4.7.3.1 carries no HT label; the source's `higher` field is base or another lesson's content (ADP-F1).
- **Required practical:** none.
- **Calculations:** none. (100% atom economy is taught as a concept: "no other molecule is formed".)
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "The polymer still has its double bonds." The C=C opens to C–C; the polymer is saturated.
  - "The repeat unit has fewer atoms than the monomer." Same atoms; only the bonding changes (4.7.3.1).
  - "Propene's repeat unit has three carbons in the chain." Two backbone carbons; CH₃ is a side group.
  - "Water is given off." Addition gives one product only — 100% atom economy.
  - "Any molecule can be a monomer for this." It needs a C=C.
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** a plastic carrier bag — is it made of lots of separate small molecules, or a few enormous chain molecules?
- **Practice (rule 4):** q2 usable verbatim on TF and TH (1 per route). q1 not usable (ADP-F2, F3). Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size (draw/identify the repeat unit from a given monomer, identify the monomer from a repeat unit, recognise addition polymers by C=C, 100% atom economy).

### 10. Condensation Polymerisation
`condensation-polymerisation` · Chemistry / organic · AQA 8462 4.7.3.2 (chemistry only, HT only) · TH only (whole page triple-higher)

- **Family:** CONTRAST. One discriminating difference from addition polymerisation (two functional groups and a small molecule lost vs C=C and one product), which both quiz items test (BATCH-PLAN kept).
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** the same "join the monomers" action run twice, addition vs condensation; each condensation join ejects an H₂O and an atom counter shows less than 100% of the atoms kept.
- **Route layers:**
  - triple-higher: the whole lesson. 8462 4.7.3.2 "Condensation polymerisation (HT only)", within 4.7.3 "(chemistry only)".
- **Required practical:** none.
- **Calculations:** none.
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "Condensation means steam turning to water." It means a small molecule (usually water) is lost at each join (4.7.3.2).
  - "Every polymer has 100% atom economy." Condensation loses water each join, so it is below 100%.
  - "Nylon is an addition polymer like poly(ethene)." Condensation, amide links (CDP-F1).
  - "One –OH on each monomer is enough." Each monomer needs two functional groups for the chain to grow.
  - "Ester and amide links are the same." Ester –COO– (diol + diacid); amide –CONH– (amino groups).
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** a polyester shirt and a polythene bag are both polymers — when the small molecules join, is anything left over, or does it all end up in the plastic?
- **Practice (rule 4):** q1 and q2 usable verbatim on TH (2). None unusable. Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size (identify functional groups, draw the repeat unit from the spec's ethanediol + hexanedioic acid, name the small molecule lost, addition vs condensation).
- **Diagrams:** `05-diagram-library/README.md` row 10, `figlib/chemistry.py box_monomers()` and `figlib/biochem.py box_repeat_unit()`. Must draw: ethanediol + hexanedioic acid → polyester repeat unit + water (spec's example, CDP-F2), in box notation and displayed.

### 11. Amino Acids
`amino-acids` · Chemistry / organic · AQA 8462 4.7.3.3 (chemistry only, HT only) · TH only (whole page triple-higher)

- **Family:** PROCESS. The spec content is one mechanism run step by step: two different functional groups → join, lose water → polypeptide → mixed chain = protein (BATCH-PLAN kept).
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** a chain builder: join glycine (H₂NCH₂COOH) units one at a time, a water leaving at each –CO–NH–, then swap in a different amino acid to make a mixed chain.
- **Route layers:**
  - triple-higher: the whole lesson. 8462 4.7.3.3 "Amino acids (HT only)", within 4.7.3 "(chemistry only)".
- **Required practical:** none.
- **Calculations:** none.
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "Amino acids join –COOH to –COOH." The –COOH of one reacts with the –NH₂ of the next (4.7.3.3).
  - "Nothing is lost when they join." Water is lost at each join — condensation (4.7.3.2–3).
  - "The peptide link is an ester link." It is –CO–NH– (amide), the same link as in nylon.
  - "A protein is made of one kind of amino acid." Different amino acids combine in the same chain (4.7.3.3).
  - "Proteins are addition polymers." Amino acids have no C=C.
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** your stomach breaks the protein in an egg into smaller pieces — is protein one giant molecule, or lots of small ones stuck together?

### 12. DNA and Other Naturally Occurring Polymers
`dna-naturally-occurring-polymers` · Chemistry / organic · AQA 8462 4.7.3.4 (chemistry only) · TF TH (no HT layer of its own)

- **Family:** CLASSIFY. The spec's one skill is naming the monomer of each natural polymer — a fast sort (BATCH-PLAN kept).

- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "DNA is made of amino acids." DNA's monomers are nucleotides; proteins' are amino acids (4.7.3.4).
  - "The monomers of DNA are A, T, C and G." Those are bases; the monomers are four different nucleotides.

  - "Only plastics are polymers." DNA, proteins, starch and cellulose are natural polymers.
