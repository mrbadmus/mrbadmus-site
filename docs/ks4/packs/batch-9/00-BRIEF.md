## The four lesson rules (Mide, 2 Oct 2026) — apply to every lesson here

1. **Start here is a two-option guess**, framed as a guess, answerable from everyday experience on every route; the reveal is encouraging either way and leads straight into the teaching.
2. **Equations are formula triangles you can cover** — in the equation block, the equation-sheet panel and CFIFA's Formula step. A square or a ½ gets its own extra line (square-root, ×2).
3. **Teach every step before you test it.** A calculation that chains two equations gets its own "Step 1 … Step 2 …" worked example before any pupil does one; CFIFA on each step.
4. **Practice is the same size in every lesson** — same number of questions at each rung of the end practice and the exam ladder. Set the number once (at least the content-standards floor) and keep it across the batch.

Each lesson below says which calculations chain, which equations need triangles, and how many verbatim quiz items are usable; the rest of each bank is yours to write to the fixed size.

### 1. DNA Structure
`dna-structure` · Biology / inheritance · AQA 8461 4.6.1.5 (biology only; not in 8464) · TF TH

**Family:** MODEL. Changed from BATCH-PLAN's PROCESS: protein synthesis is HT only, so on Triple Foundation the whole lesson is a structure (nucleotide → strand → three-base code) used to predict; TH adds the process on top.

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** Build a strand from nucleotide tiles, read it in threes to place amino acids; TH swaps one base and watches the chain and the folded shape change.

**Route layers:**
- Triple (whole page): nucleotides, four bases, alternating sugar–phosphate strands, three bases → one amino acid. 8461 4.6.1.5 (biology only).
- Triple Higher: complementary pairing C–G, T–A; protein synthesis on ribosomes with carrier molecules; folding → enzymes, hormones, collagen; mutations and their effect on the protein; non-coding DNA switching genes on and off. 8461 4.6.1.5 (biology only, HT only) (F1).

**Required practical:** none.

**Calculations:** none.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "A pairs with G." → C always links to G and T to A (4.6.1.5, HT).
- "DNA is made of amino acids." → DNA is a polymer of nucleotides; its base order codes for the order of amino acids (4.6.1.5).
- "One base codes for one amino acid." → A sequence of three bases codes for one (4.6.1.5).
- "Every mutation changes the protein." → Most do not alter it, or only slightly (4.6.1.5, HT).
- "All DNA is genes." → Non-coding parts can switch genes on and off (4.6.1.5, HT).

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** Everyone knows a recipe or a code: is a long message more likely written with lots of different symbols, or a few symbols in different orders?

**Practice (rule 4):** 2 frozen items, 0 usable as written. q1 has a self-contradicting distractor (F4) and is TH only even if corrected; q2 is off-spec (F2). Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size.

**Diagrams:** `05-diagram-library/README.md` row 1 (`dna_schematic()`, `dna_ladder()`). ### 2. Genetic Inheritance
`genetic-inheritance` · Biology / inheritance · AQA 8464 4.6.1.4; 8461 4.6.1.6 · CF CH TF TH

**Family:** QUANTITATIVE. The Punnett square is a calculation from genotypes to a probability or ratio (BATCH-PLAN's suggestion kept).

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** Drop parent alleles into a Punnett square and watch the offspring ratio and probability fill in; Foundation completes a given grid, Higher builds it from the parents.

**Route layers:**
- Higher: construct a genetic cross by Punnett square and use it to make predictions using the theory of probability. 8464 4.6.1.4 (HT only); 8461 4.6.1.6 (HT only) (F2).
- No triple layer. Codominance is not in either spec (F1).

**Required practical:** none.

**Calculations:**
- Probability / ratio of offspring genotypes and phenotypes from a single-gene cross (e.g. Bb × Bb → 1/4 recessive). No equation. **Chains equations? no.** Convert: nothing to convert (no units); pupils move between forms — fraction, %, "1 in 4" and ratio (a 3:1 ratio means 1 in 4 show the recessive phenotype, not 1 in 3). Completing the square = base; constructing it from the parents = HT.
- Proportion from a family tree (e.g. which individuals must be heterozygous). No equation. **Chains equations? no.** Base.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "Dominant means it's the most common allele." → Dominant only means it is expressed when one copy is present (4.6.1.4).
- "A 1 in 4 chance means one in every four children." → Each fertilisation is a separate chance; it is a probability (4.6.1.4, MS 2e).
- "Carriers have the condition a bit." → A recessive allele is only expressed if two copies are present (4.6.1.4).
- "Each gamete has both alleles." → A gamete carries one allele of each gene (4.6.1.4 gamete; 4.6.1.2).
- "Every feature is one gene, like eye colour." → Most characteristics are a result of multiple genes interacting (4.6.1.4).

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** Everyone has seen a child look like one parent more than the other: does a baby get its features from mum only, or from both parents?

**Practice (rule 4):** 3 frozen items, all usable on all four routes (q1 recall/apply, q2 carriers, q3 probability). The FIFA is not usable as written (F2, F3). Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size, including a family-tree item on every route.

**Diagrams:** `05-diagram-library/README.md` row 2 (`punnett_square()`; KS3 `_punnett()`). Must draw: Punnett square with gametes on the edges (base: gametes pre-placed; HT: built from the parents); a simple family tree with key.

**Examiner tip:** none approved.

### 3. Inherited Disorders

**Family:** CONTRAST. Polydactyly (dominant) against cystic fibrosis (recessive): the lesson is "why does one skip a generation and the other not?", then a judgement on embryo screening (BATCH-PLAN's suggestion kept).

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** Two family trees side by side, one dominant, one recessive; the pupil predicts who is affected in the next generation and sees why only CF can skip.

**Route layers:**
- Higher: constructing the Punnett square from the parents. 8464 4.6.1.4 (HT only); 8461 4.6.1.6 (HT only) (F3). Everything else, including embryo screening, is base (F1).
- No triple layer.

**Required practical:** none.

**Calculations:**
- Probability of an affected child: CF Ff × Ff → 1/4; polydactyly Dd × dd → 1/2. No equation. **Chains equations? no.** Convert: nothing to convert (no units); fraction ↔ % ↔ "1 in 4". Completing the square = base; constructing it = HT.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "Polydactyly is recessive because it's rare." → It is caused by a dominant allele (4.6.1.5).
- "Carriers have mild cystic fibrosis." → CF is caused by a recessive allele; one copy is not expressed (4.6.1.5; 4.6.1.4).
- "You can catch cystic fibrosis." → Inherited disorders are caused by the inheritance of certain alleles (4.6.1.5).
- "Healthy parents can't have a child with CF." → Two carriers have a 1/4 chance each time (4.6.1.4).
- "Embryo screening is just right or just wrong." → The spec asks for economic, social and ethical issues, judged from given information (4.6.1.5).

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** Everyone has noticed something that runs in a family: can two parents without a condition have a child who has it?

**Practice (rule 4):** 2 frozen items, both usable on all four routes. The FIFA is usable verbatim on CH TH only (F3). Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size, including an embryo-screening judgement item with given information on every route.

**Diagrams:** `05-diagram-library/README.md` row 3 (`punnett_square()`). Must draw: Ff × Ff and Dd × dd squares; two family trees (dominant vs recessive) with a key; a simple embryo-screening sequence (IVF embryos → cell tested → unaffected embryo implanted).

**Required practical:** none.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.

**Misconceptions to confront:**

- "50:50 means every family has equal boys and girls." → It is a probability; families vary (4.6.1.6).

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** Everyone knows families with all girls or all boys: is a baby's sex decided by the mother, or by the father?

### 5. Variation

**Family:** CLASSIFY. Pupils sort characteristics into genetic, environmental or both, then sort mutations by their effect on phenotype (BATCH-PLAN's suggestion kept).

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** Identical twins raised apart: drag each difference (scar, height, accent, blood group) into genetic / environmental / both, and see the twins' shared genes rule some out.

**Route layers:**
- None. 4.6.2.1 has no HT and no biology-only content on either spec; the `higher` field is off-spec (F2).

**Required practical:** none.

**Calculations:** none.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "All mutations are harmful." → Most have no effect on the phenotype; some influence it; very few determine it (4.6.2.1).
- "Only good mutations make variation." → All variants arise from mutations (4.6.2.1) (F1).
- "If I build muscles, my children will be stronger." → Environmental differences are not inherited (4.6.2.1).
- "Height is just genes" / "just food." → It is a combination of genes and the environment (4.6.2.1).
- "Organisms mutate because they need to." → Mutations occur continuously and at random; very rarely one gives a new phenotype that suits a change (4.6.2.1).

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** Everyone knows siblings who look different: is that difference more down to what they were born with, or how they've grown up?

- **Calculations:** none.
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.

### 12. Evidence for Evolution
`evidence-for-evolution` · Biology / inheritance · AQA 8464 4.6.3.1; 8461 4.6.3.4 · CF CH TF TH

- **Family:** INVESTIGATION — the spec's skill is WS 1.3, judging what the data shows; the content is three named strands of evidence (genes, fossils, antibiotic resistance). BATCH-PLAN kept.
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** an evidence board — the pupil sorts given observations onto the spec's three strands and says, for each, what it shows that Darwin could not show in 1859.
- **Route layers:** none. Every spec point is base on all four routes. The `higher` field is not HT (speciation is base 4.6.2.2, trees base 4.6.4, convergent evolution off-spec).
- **Required practical:** none.
- **Calculations:** none.
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "Gaps in the fossil record prove evolution is wrong." — most organisms never fossilise; the record is expected to be incomplete.
  - "Antibiotics cause the mutations." — mutations are random; the antibiotic kills the bacteria that are not resistant.
  - "Bacteria change their DNA on purpose." — variation comes from random mutation.
  - "Evolution only happens to simple things like bacteria." — bacteria show it fastest because they reproduce at a fast rate.
  - "Older fossils are more primitive." — older rock holds organisms less like today's; fossils show how much or how little organisms have changed.
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** antibiotics that worked last year don't always work now — has the medicine changed, or have the bacteria?

### 13. Fossils and Extinction
`fossils-extinction` · Biology / inheritance · AQA 8464 4.6.3.2–4.6.3.3; 8461 4.6.3.5–4.6.3.6 · CF CH TF TH

- **Family:** CLASSIFY — changed from BATCH-PLAN's PROCESS. The spec gives three parallel ways a fossil forms (decay conditions absent / minerals replace parts / traces) and a list of extinction factors; the demand is "which kind, and why", not one sequence.
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** a sorter — insect in amber, mammoth in ice, mineralised bone, footprint, bog body — pupil picks which of the three ways each formed and which decay condition was missing.
- **Route layers:** none. Every point is base on all four routes.
- **Required practical:** none.
- **Calculations:** none.
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "Fossils are always bones turned to stone." — the spec gives three ways, including unrotted remains and traces such as footprints and burrows.
  - "A frozen mammoth isn't a fossil." — parts that have not decayed because decay's conditions were absent count.
  - "Gaps in the fossil record mean evolution didn't happen." — early life was soft-bodied and traces were mainly destroyed by geological activity.
  - "Scientists know exactly how life began." — they cannot be certain, for exactly that reason.
  - "An extinct species could come back if conditions improve." — extinct means no individuals remain alive.
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** food left out goes mouldy but food in the freezer doesn't — so would a dead animal last longer in a warm swamp or in ice?

### 14. Resistant Bacteria
`resistant-bacteria` · Biology / inheritance · AQA 8464 4.6.3.4; 8461 4.6.3.7 · CF CH TF TH

- **Family:** PROCESS — the spec is one sequence: mutation → new strain → antibiotic kills the rest → resistant strain survives and reproduces → population rises → spreads between people. BATCH-PLAN kept.
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** a dish of bacteria the pupil doses with antibiotic, generation by generation, predicting the resistant share before each round — then stops the course early and sees what is left.
- **Route layers:** none. Every point is base on all four routes; the `higher` field is not HT.
- **Required practical:** none.
- **Calculations:** none.
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "The antibiotic makes the bacteria mutate." — mutations are random; the antibiotic kills the ones that are not resistant.
  - "The bacteria get used to the antibiotic." — individual bacteria do not adapt; the resistant strain survives and reproduces.
  - "I'm resistant to antibiotics." — bacteria become resistant, not people.
  - "Antibiotics will cure my cold." — they do not work on viruses; prescribing them for viral infections speeds resistance.
  - "We'll just make a new antibiotic." — development is costly and slow and unlikely to keep up.
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** people are told to finish a course of antibiotics even when they feel better — is that about you, or about the bacteria?
- **Practice (rule 4):** 3 verbatim items usable on all four routes (q1, q2, q3). None unusable. Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size.

### 15. Classification of Living Organisms
`classification-living-organisms` · Biology / inheritance · AQA 8464 4.6.4; 8461 4.6.4 · CF CH TF TH (site ships TF TH — moving under the route-flag PR)

- **Family:** CLASSIFY — place an organism in Linnaean ranks and a domain, and read relatedness off a tree. BATCH-PLAN kept.
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** a classifier — given an organism's features (and, second time round, its chemical/rRNA evidence), the pupil places it in a domain; one archaean looks like a bacterium until the chemistry is revealed.
- **Route layers:** none. Every point is base on all four routes; the `higher` field is not HT.
- **Required practical:** none.
- **Calculations:** none.
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "Things that look alike are the same species." — a species can interbreed to produce fertile offspring; horse × donkey gives a sterile mule.
  - "Archaea are just bacteria." — chemical analysis put them in their own domain.
  - "The genus name goes second / both names have capitals." — Genus first with a capital, species second in lower case.
  - "A more recent common ancestor means less related." — more recent ancestor means more closely related.
  - "Organisms higher on a tree are more evolved." — a tree shows relatedness, not progress.
  - "Classification never changes." — microscopes and biochemistry produced new models (the three-domain system).
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** a whale lives in the sea like a fish but breathes air like us — is it grouped by where it lives, or by what it is like inside?
- **Practice (rule 4):** 2 verbatim items usable on all four routes (q1, q2). None unusable. Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size, including "use information given" Linnaean items and tree reading.
- **Diagrams:** `05-diagram-library/README.md` row 15 — must draw the seven Linnaean ranks for one organism, the three domains, and an evolutionary tree with nodes marked as common ancestors.
