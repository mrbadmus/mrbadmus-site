## 5. For the lesson author
**Misconceptions seen in AQA marking**
- "White blood cells carry oxygen" / "red blood cells fight infection".
- "Platelets are cells" / "platelets fight germs".
- "Plasma is just water" — it carries dissolved glucose, CO₂, urea, hormones, antibodies.
- "Red blood cells have a nucleus" (drawn with one), or "no nucleus so they can squeeze through".
- "All white blood cells eat microbes" — lymphocytes make antibodies; phagocytes engulf.
- Antibodies and antigens swapped.

**Command words**: Identify (from a photomicrograph or diagram), Name, Describe, Explain (adaptation), Give.

**Typical questions** ⚑ examiner-drafted
- *[Photograph of a blood smear] Identify cell A. [1]* — red blood cell (biconcave, no nucleus) / white blood cell (large, has nucleus) / platelet (small fragment).
- *Explain one way a red blood cell is adapted to carry oxygen. [2]* — no nucleus (1) more room for haemoglobin (1) / biconcave (1) large surface area for diffusion (1).
- *Give three ways white blood cells defend the body against pathogens. [3]* — phagocytosis; antibody production; antitoxin production.
- *Describe the function of platelets. [1]* — help the blood to clot.
- *Name two substances transported in plasma. [2]* — e.g. carbon dioxide, urea, glucose, hormones.

**Required practical**: none. (AT 7 — observing and drawing blood cells under a microscope — is an apparatus technique, not a numbered RP.)

**Equations**: none.
