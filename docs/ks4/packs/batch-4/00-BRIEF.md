## The four lesson rules (Mide, 2 Oct 2026) — apply to every lesson here

1. **Start here is a two-option guess**, framed as a guess, answerable from everyday experience on every route; the reveal is encouraging either way and leads straight into the teaching.
2. **Equations are formula triangles you can cover** — in the equation block, the equation-sheet panel and CFIFA's Formula step. A square or a ½ gets its own extra line (square-root, ×2).
3. **Teach every step before you test it.** A calculation that chains two equations gets its own "Step 1 … Step 2 …" worked example before any pupil does one; CFIFA on each step.
4. **Practice is the same size in every lesson** — same number of questions at each rung of the end practice and the exam ladder. Set the number once (at least the content-standards floor) and keep it across the batch.

Each lesson below says which calculations chain, which equations need triangles, and how many verbatim quiz items are usable; the rest of each bank is yours to write to the fixed size.

### 1. The Heart and Blood Vessels
`heart-blood-vessels` · Biology / organisation · AQA refs (8464 4.2.2.2; 8461 4.2.2.2) · true routes CF CH TF TH

**Family:** SYSTEM — heart, lungs and three vessel types work as one circuit; the spec asks what each part does and what goes wrong when one fails (BATCH-PLAN's suggestion stands).

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** Follow one red blood cell round the double circulation, then break one part (leaky valve, blocked coronary artery, thin left ventricle) and predict where the oxygen stops arriving.

**Route layers:**
- None. Everything is base. The source's `higher` block (artificial pacemakers, valve replacement, artificial hearts, evaluating treatments) is base: pacemakers 8464 4.2.2.2; the rest 8464 4.2.2.4, taught in coronary-heart-disease.

**Required practical:** none.

**Calculations:**
- Rate of blood flow = volume of blood ÷ time. Chains equations? no. Convert: seconds ↔ minutes; cm³ ↔ dm³ (1 dm³ = 1000 cm³). Base (MS 1a, 1c).
- Heart rate (beats per minute) = number of beats ÷ time in minutes (from a pulse count, or 60 ÷ time for one beat in s). Chains equations? no. Convert: seconds → minutes. Base.
- Only if Design adds volume pumped per minute = volume per beat × heart rate (not a spec equation; AQA prints it in the question when used): chains equations? **yes** — Step 1 heart rate from a pulse count; Step 2 volume per minute. Needs its own Step 1 … Step 2 worked example first.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):**
- rate of blood flow = volume ÷ time — Learn it (no biology equation sheet). Triangle: volume on top; rate × time on the bottom.
- heart rate = beats ÷ time — Learn it. Triangle: beats on top; heart rate × time on the bottom.
- No squares or ½.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "Arteries carry oxygenated blood, veins carry deoxygenated." — The pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood; the rule is direction, away from or towards the heart.
- "The left ventricle is thicker because it holds more blood." — Both ventricles pump the same volume; the left pumps it all round the body at higher pressure.
- "Blood goes round the body once." — Double circulation: through the heart twice per circuit.
- "The heart gets its oxygen from the blood inside it." — Coronary arteries supply the heart muscle.
- "Capillaries have the lowest blood pressure." — Veins do; this is false in the frozen q4 too.
- "Thin capillary walls make blood flow faster." — They make the diffusion distance short.

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** You can feel your pulse in your wrist — guess whether that blood is moving away from the heart or back towards it.

**Practice (rule 4):** verbatim frozen quiz items usable on all four routes: 3 (q1, q2, q3). Not usable: q4 (HBV-F1, wrong wx1). Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size, including lungs, pacemaker and rate-calculation items.

**Diagrams:** see `05-diagram-library/README.md` (nothing exists). Must draw: heart section (four chambers, the five named vessels, valves unnamed, flow arrows, right/left as the patient's); artery / vein / capillary cross-sections side by side; lungs (trachea, bronchi, alveoli with capillary network).

- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** follow one drop from the sea to a cloud, onto a hill, through a tree and back, choosing each process at each arrow — and see the salt stay behind in the sea.
- **Route layers:** none — all base.
- **Required practical:** none.
- **Calculations:** none.
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "Clouds are water vapour / steam." — clouds are tiny liquid droplets formed by condensation.
  - "Rain is salty because it came from the sea." — evaporation leaves salts behind; the cycle "provides fresh water for plants and animals on land" (4.7.2.2).

  - "Living things use water up." — "Water is continuously evaporated and precipitated" (4.7.2.2); it is recycled.

### 4. Efficiency
`efficiency` · Physics / energy · AQA 8464 6.1.2.2; 8463 4.1.2.2 (supporting 6.1.2.1 / 4.1.2.1) · true routes CF CH TF TH

**Family:** QUANTITATIVE — the efficiency calculation carries the concept (useful share of the input); Sankey widths are the same ratio drawn.

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** A to-scale Sankey the pupil splits into useful and wasted, with efficiency read off as a decimal and a percentage, feeding CFIFA.

**Route layers:**
- Higher — "(HT only) Students should be able to describe ways to increase the efficiency of an intended energy transfer." 8464 6.1.2.2 (HT only); 8463 4.1.2.2 (HT only). CH TH only. (Lubrication and thermal insulation themselves are base, 8464 6.1.2.1.)

**Required practical:** none.

**Calculations:**
- Efficiency from energies or powers: efficiency = useful output energy transfer ÷ total input energy transfer; efficiency = useful power output ÷ total power input. Chains equations? no. Convert: input and output into the same unit (kJ→J, kW→W, MJ→J). Answer as decimal, then × 100 for %. Base.
- Rearranged — find the useful output (useful = efficiency × total input) or the total input (total = useful ÷ efficiency). Chains? no. Convert: a % efficiency → decimal (÷ 100) first. Base.
- Wasted energy from an efficiency (source q1). Chains? **yes** — Step 1 useful = efficiency × total input; Step 2 wasted = total − useful. Needs its own Step 1 … Step 2 worked example. Base.
- Comparing waste of two devices (source q2): wasted = total − useful, twice, then the difference. Chains? no (repeated subtraction). Base.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):**
- efficiency = useful output energy transfer ÷ total input energy transfer — On the sheet (8464 and 8463, June 2026). Top: useful output energy transfer; bottom: efficiency × total input energy transfer.
- efficiency = useful power output ÷ total power input — On the sheet (both). Top: useful power output; bottom: efficiency × total power input.
- No square or ½. The "× 100 for %" is a conversion line, not a triangle (EFFICIENCY-F3).

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "Efficiency = useful ÷ wasted." — the spec divides by total input; the answer can never exceed 1.
- "My answer is 1.4, fine." — over 1 means the division is upside down.
- "Efficiency is measured in joules." — it is a ratio, no unit.
- "Wasted energy is destroyed." — it is dissipated to the surroundings, stored in less useful ways (6.1.2.1).
- "Same light output means same waste." — waste = total − useful; totals differ (source q2).
- "35% goes in the equation as 35." — use 0.35.

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** An old filament bulb gets too hot to touch and an LED doesn't — which wastes more of its energy?

**Practice (rule 4):** q1, q2 usable verbatim on all four routes (2). None unusable. Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size; q1 needs the chained worked example taught first.

**Diagrams:** see `05-diagram-library/README.md` — existing `figlib/physics.py:924 sankey()` / `figlib/ks4phys.py:337 sankey_simple()`. Must draw: a to-scale Sankey (input, useful, wasted); the efficiency formula triangle.

**Examiner tip:** none approved.

### 5. Infrared Emission, Absorption and Black Bodies
`infrared-black-bodies` · Physics / waves · AQA 8463 4.6.3.1–4.6.3.2 (physics only); no 8464 equivalent · true routes TF TH

**Family:** MODEL — changed from BATCH-PLAN's CONTRAST. The matt-vs-shiny contrast is the base RP's result, which belongs on `properties-em-waves-1` (IRB-F6). **Route layers:**

**Required practical:** none on this page. The IR-surfaces RP — RP21 Combined (8464 6.6.2.2) / RP10 Physics (8463 4.6.2.2) — is base, on all four routes, and belongs on `properties-em-waves-1`. Here, recall its result only.

**Calculations:** none.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "Cold things don't give off radiation." — "All bodies (objects), no matter what temperature, emit and absorb infrared radiation."
- "A black body is black because it doesn't emit." — it absorbs everything and is the best possible emitter.
- "Hotter things give off short wavelengths instead of long ones." — more at every wavelength; the distribution shifts shorter.
- "White-hot means the peak is in the visible." — a glowing metal's peak is still in the infrared (IRB-F1/F2).

### 6. Diffusion, Osmosis and Active Transport
`transport-in-cells` · Biology / cell-biology · AQA 8464 4.1.3.1–4.1.3.3; 8461 4.1.3.1–4.1.3.3 · CF CH TF TH

- **Family:** CLASSIFY — the spec's closing demand is "explain the differences between the three processes"; every example sorts by direction (down/against the gradient), what moves (any particle / water only) and energy (none / from respiration). The RP and the two calculations sit inside it.
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** drop a particle at a membrane and decide — which way does it go, does it need energy, is it water — then name the process.
- **Route layers:** none — all base. The source's `higher` field (% change, isotonic point, SA:V) is base and must be on every route (TRANSPORT-IN-CELLS-F2).
- **Required practical:** Osmosis — effect of salt or sugar concentration on the mass of plant tissue. **Combined RP2 (8464 4.1.3.2) / Biology RP3 (8461 4.1.3.2).** On all four routes; label RP2 on CF/CH, RP3 on TF/TH (TRANSPORT-IN-CELLS-F3).
- **Calculations:**
  - % change in mass = (final mass − initial mass) ÷ initial mass × 100. Chains equations? no (one equation; the subtraction is inside it — keep the sign). Convert: masses into the same unit (g/mg). Base. Source FIFA ready (nothing to convert); second example needs a mg → g conversion.
  - Surface area to volume ratio: SA = 6 × L² (cube), V = L³, SA:V = SA ÷ V, written *n* : 1. Chains equations? **yes** — Step 1 surface area, Step 2 volume, Step 3 ratio; needs its own "Step 1 … Step 2 …" worked example (TRANSPORT-IN-CELLS-F7). Convert: all lengths into one unit (mm/cm). Base.
  - Rate of water uptake = change in mass ÷ time (g/min). Chains equations? no. Convert: time into one unit (min or s, as the question asks). Base.
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):**
  - % change in mass — Learn it (on no sheet) — top: change in mass; bottom: initial mass × (% change ÷ 100). The ×100 / ÷100 is its own line.
  - SA:V = surface area ÷ volume — Learn it — top: surface area; bottom: SA:V × volume.
  - Rate of water uptake = change in mass ÷ time — Learn it — top: change in mass; bottom: rate × time.
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "Osmosis is salt/sugar moving." — osmosis is "the diffusion of water … through a partially permeable membrane" (4.1.3.2).
  - "Water moves from concentrated to dilute." — dilute → concentrated (4.1.3.2).
  - "Diffusion needs energy." — passive; active transport alone "requires energy from respiration" (4.1.3.3).
  - "Active transport is just fast diffusion." — it goes against the gradient, from dilute to more concentrated (4.1.3.3).
  - "A plant cell in water bursts." — the cell wall stops it; it becomes turgid.
  - "Bigger organisms have a bigger SA:V." — SA:V falls as size rises; that is why multicellular organisms need exchange surfaces (4.1.3.1).
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** a smell spreading across a room, or a salted lettuce leaf going limp — does stuff move from where there's lots to where there's little, or the other way?
- **Practice (rule 4):** q1, q2, q4, q5 usable verbatim on all four routes (4). q3 not usable as written (TRANSPORT-IN-CELLS-F4). Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size, including SA:V, % change and RP-graph items.
- **Diagrams:** see `05-diagram-library/README.md` (no existing figure). Must draw: one membrane with diffusion / osmosis / active-transport panels; plant cell turgid vs plasmolysed and animal cell swollen/burst vs shrunk; cubes of increasing size for SA:V; the RP set-up and its % change vs concentration graph crossing zero; exchange surfaces incl. gills and leaf.

### 9. Thermal Conductivity and Reducing Unwanted Energy Transfers
`thermal-conductivity` · Physics / energy · AQA 8464 6.1.2.1; 8463 4.1.2.1 (spec field "6.1.3 (physics only)" is wrong — TC-F1) · true routes CF CH TF TH (RP2 layer TF TH)

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** A house wall the pupil changes (material, thickness), predicting then watching the rate of cooling change.

**Route layers:**
