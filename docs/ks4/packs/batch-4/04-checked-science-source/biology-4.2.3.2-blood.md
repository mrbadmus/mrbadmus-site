## common_mistake

Red blood cells carry oxygen — white blood cells do NOT carry oxygen. White blood cells fight infection. Platelets are NOT cells — they are cell fragments with no nucleus. They are involved in clotting, NOT in carrying oxygen or fighting infection.

## key_note

Red blood cells: O₂ transport, haemoglobin, no nucleus, biconcave. White blood cells: immune defence — phagocytes engulf, lymphocytes make antibodies. Platelets: clotting. Plasma: liquid transport medium.

## Spec core missing from the frozen data (written from the spec, examiner)

AQA 8464/8461 4.3.1.6, verbatim — the full list of white-blood-cell functions that 4.2.2.3's "know the functions of each of these blood components" requires: "White blood cells help to defend against pathogens by: phagocytosis, antibody production, antitoxin production."

Missing from the theory: **antitoxin production** — some white blood cells make antitoxins, which neutralise the toxins released by bacteria.

AQA 4.2.2.3, verbatim: "Students should be able to recognise different types of blood cells in a photograph or diagram, and explain how they are adapted to their functions." The source explains red-cell adaptations only; white cells: phagocytes have a flexible membrane to engulf pathogens; lymphocytes make antibodies. Recognition: red cell — biconcave, no nucleus, most numerous; white cell — larger, has a (often lobed) nucleus; platelets — very small fragments.
