## higher

Apply Newton's Second Law in the form F = ma to multi-step problems including objects on slopes and systems with multiple forces. Explain inertial mass: the ratio of force to acceleration (F/a) — a measure of how difficult it is to change an object's velocity. Distinguish inertial mass from gravitational mass.

## common_mistake

Newton's Third Law pairs act on DIFFERENT objects — they cannot cancel. When a horse pulls a cart (action), the cart pulls back on the horse (reaction). These don't cancel because they act on different things. Newton's First Law is about ONE object — don't confuse the two.

## key_note

N1: constant velocity (or stationary) if resultant = 0. N2: F = ma. N3: equal and opposite pairs on different objects. N2: bigger F → bigger a; bigger m → smaller a. N3 pairs: same type, same magnitude, opposite direction, different objects.

## fifas — CFIFA form (Convert step added; the four FIFA steps below are verbatim)

**Question:** A 900 kg car has a resultant forward force of 2700 N. Calculate its acceleration.

**Convert:** [NEW — examined ✓] Nothing to convert — mass is already in kilograms and force already in newtons, so a = F ÷ m gives an answer directly in m/s² with no unit change.

**F:** F = m × a, so a = F ÷ m

**I:** F = 2700 N, m = 900 kg

**F:** a = 2700 ÷ 900

**A:** a = 3 m/s²
