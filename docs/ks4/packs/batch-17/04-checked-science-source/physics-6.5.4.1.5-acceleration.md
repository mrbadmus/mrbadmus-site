## higher

Use the equations of motion quantitatively including v² = u² + 2as. Estimate the speed, acceleration or distance for situations including everyday transport. Interpret velocity-time graphs to find: acceleration from gradient, distance from area under the graph (trapezium or triangle calculation).

## common_mistake

Gradient of a v–t graph = ACCELERATION (not distance or speed). Area under a v–t graph = DISTANCE. This is the opposite of the d–t graph where gradient = speed. Don't confuse the two graphs.

## key_note

a = (v−u)/t. v² = u² + 2as. Units: m/s². Positive a = speeding up; negative a = decelerating. v–t graph: gradient = acceleration; area = distance. Horizontal v–t = constant velocity. Steep v–t = large acceleration.

## fifas — CFIFA form (Convert step added; the four FIFA steps below are verbatim)

**Question:** A train accelerates from rest to 50 m/s in 25 s. Calculate the acceleration.

**Convert:** [NEW — examined ✓] Nothing to convert — both velocities are already in m/s and the time already in seconds, so a = (v − u) ÷ t gives an answer directly in m/s² with no unit change.

**F:** a = (v − u) ÷ t

**I:** v = 50 m/s, u = 0 m/s, t = 25 s

**F:** a = (50 − 0) ÷ 25 = 50 ÷ 25

**A:** a = 2 m/s²
