## higher

Explain qualitatively that circular motion involves constant speed but changing velocity — the direction changes continuously, so the object is always accelerating towards the centre. This centripetal acceleration is caused by a centripetal force directed towards the centre of the circle.

## common_mistake

Speed is scalar (magnitude only). Velocity is vector (magnitude AND direction). A car travelling in a circle at constant SPEED has changing VELOCITY — direction is always changing. Average speed = total distance ÷ total time (not displacement).

## key_note

Distance: scalar, total path. Displacement: vector, start to finish with direction. Speed = d/t (scalar). Velocity = displacement/t (vector). Speed = |velocity| for straight-line motion. Constant speed in a circle = changing velocity. Average speed: total distance ÷ total time.
