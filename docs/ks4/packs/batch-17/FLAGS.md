## 1. Moments, Levers and Gears (`moments-levers-gears`)

# Flags — moments-levers-gears (examiner, 2 Oct 2026)

**MLG-F1** · WRONG · q1 wx1 "M = F ÷ d gives the force needed to produce a given moment at that distance — not the moment itself." · F ÷ d is not a physical quantity; the force needed for a given moment is M ÷ d. The explanation teaches a false rearrangement. (Key 20 N·m ✓; wx2, wx3 ✓.) · Moment = force × perpendicular distance; F = M ÷ d. · 8463 4.5.4 · **Do not use q1 as written.** If a corrected copy is allowed: wx1 "Dividing force by distance does not give a moment. Multiply: M = F × d = 40 × 0.5 = 20 N m." Then usable TF TH.

**MLG-F2** · WRONG · q2 wx1 "Small driving → large driven gear gives MORE SPEED not more force. Low gear = large driving sprocket (at pedals) → small sprocket (at wheel) in cycling, OR equivalently small driving → large driven output." · Contradicts the item's own key and th3. A small gear driving a large one makes the large one turn more slowly with a bigger moment; a large chainring driving a small sprocket is a high gear. Also the stem says the gears "mesh": bicycle sprockets are joined by a chain (and turn the same way). · Small driving large → slower, bigger turning effect. · 8463 4.5.4 · **Do not use q2 as written.** Corrected copy: stem "A small gear drives a larger gear. What is the effect on the larger gear?"; options and key unchanged except "back wheel"/"pedals" → "larger gear"/"small gear"; wx1 "A small gear driving a larger one makes the larger one turn MORE SLOWLY, not faster. Its teeth are pushed with the same force further from its axle, so its moment is bigger." Then TF TH.

**MLG-F3** · WRONG · th2 "LEVERS as FORCE MULTIPLIERS: … EXAMPLES: wheelbarrow, crowbar, scissors, tweezers, nutcracker, fishing rod." · Tweezers and a fishing rod have the effort between the pivot and the load: the output force is smaller than the input. They multiply movement, not force. · Force multipliers here: wheelbarrow, crowbar, nutcracker, scissors (and spanner, door handle). · 8463 4.5.4 · Theory is re-cut: drop tweezers and fishing rod from the force-multiplier list. If used at all, they are the contrast case ("the effort is nearer the pivot, so the force gets smaller").

**MLG-F4** · IMPRECISE · th3 "Output gear rotates SLOWER but with MORE FORCE (torque)"; "gears convert a small force over a large rotation into a large force over a small rotation"; key_note "large driven by small = more force, less speed"; matching row 4. · At the meshing teeth the two gears push on each other with equal force. "More force" is the wrong model; "torque" is not an AQA term. · Spec asks pupils to "explain how levers and gears transmit the rotational effects of forces": force passes tooth to tooth; moment = force × distance from the axle; bigger gear → bigger moment, fewer turns. · 8463 4.5.4 · Teach it as moments: "same force, bigger radius, bigger moment". Say "turning effect" or "moment", never "torque".

**MLG-F5** · ROUTE · `higher` (TH copy) "Calculate moments and apply the principle of moments to solve equilibrium problems. Solve problems involving multiple forces and pivots. Calculate gear ratios …". · 4.5.4 carries no HT label. Moment and principle-of-moments calculations are on Triple Foundation too (the TF copy is `null`, but the theory teaches them there). · The page is one layer: triple, on TF and TH. · 8463 4.5.4 · No Higher badge anywhere on this page. Gear ratio: see F6.

**MLG-F6** · OFF-SPEC · th2 "Different CLASSES of levers … Class 1 … Class 2 … Class 3"; th3 "GEAR RATIO: Gear ratio = number of teeth on driven gear ÷ number of teeth on driving gear"; key_note gear ratio; `higher` "Calculate gear ratios and predict output speed/force". · Correct physics, not in 8463. · — · 8463 4.5.4 · Context only, never tested; no gear-ratio calculation in practice.
