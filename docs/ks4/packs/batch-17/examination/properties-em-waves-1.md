## 5. For the lesson author
**Misconceptions seen in AQA marking**
- Angles of incidence/refraction measured from the surface, not the normal.
- "Light bends away from the normal going into glass."
- "Light bends because glass is denser" (no mention of speed) — HT answers need the speed change.
- "Frequency changes in glass" / "colour changes".
- "No change at all along the normal" — the speed still changes.
- RP21: not keeping the distance to the detector, the starting temperature or the volume of water the same; touching the hot cube.

**Command words**: Draw / Complete the ray diagram, Measure, Describe (RP method), Explain (HT: why the ray bends), Suggest (a control variable), Compare.

**Typical questions** ⚑ examiner-drafted
- *Complete the ray diagram to show the ray entering the glass block. [2]* — normal drawn (1); refracted ray bent towards the normal (1).
- *(HT) Explain why the light changes direction when it enters the glass. [2]* — light slows down in glass (1); one side of the wave front enters/slows first (1).
- *Describe how a Leslie cube can be used to compare the infrared radiated by four different surfaces. [6]* — fill with hot water; IR detector the same distance from each face; read each; same temperature water; repeat; matt black emits most, shiny silver least.

**Required practical**: Combined RP21 / Physics RP10 (8464 6.6.2.2; 8463 4.6.2.2), all four routes — IR absorbed or radiated by a surface (Leslie cube). Plus, TF TH only, the refraction half of Physics RP9 (8463 4.6.1.3, physics only).

**Equations**: none required. (λ = v/f appears in theory as reasoning; v = f λ is On the sheet.)

**Boundaries**: wave-front explanations → `wave-front-refraction` (CH TH). Black bodies, all bodies emit IR → `infrared-black-bodies` (TF TH), which recalls the RP21 result as a bridge only. Uses and HT "why suitable" → `uses-em-waves`.
