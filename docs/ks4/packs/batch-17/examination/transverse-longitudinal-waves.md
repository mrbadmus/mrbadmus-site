Spec statements (verbatim, 8464 = 8463): "Waves may be either transverse or longitudinal. The ripples on a water surface are an example of a transverse wave. Longitudinal waves show areas of compression and rarefaction. Sound waves travelling through air are longitudinal. Students should be able to describe the difference between longitudinal and transverse waves. Students should be able to describe evidence that, for both ripples on a water surface and sound waves in air, it is the wave and not the water or air itself that travels."

## 5. For the lesson author
**Misconceptions seen in AQA marking**
- "In a sound wave the air travels from the speaker to your ear."
- "Transverse = up and down, longitudinal = side to side" (orientation, not relative to travel).
- "All transverse waves can travel through space."
- Compression/rarefaction drawn on a transverse wave; peaks and troughs on a longitudinal one.
- Mixing "vibrate" (correct) with "move along" for particles.

**Command words**: Describe the difference, Describe evidence, Give an example, Label (compression, rarefaction), Explain.

**Typical questions** ⚑ examiner-drafted
- *Describe the difference between a transverse wave and a longitudinal wave. [2]* — transverse: oscillations perpendicular to direction of energy transfer (1); longitudinal: oscillations parallel to direction of energy transfer (1).
- *Describe evidence that it is the wave and not the water that travels. [1]* — a floating object bobs up and down but does not move along with the wave.
- *Give one example of a longitudinal wave. [1]* — sound (in air).

**Required practical**: none on this page.
