## summary

Use bond energies to calculate the energy change of a reaction.

## common_mistake

ΔH = energy IN (breaking) MINUS energy OUT (forming). Not the other way round. If you subtract the wrong way, you'll get the wrong sign. Remember: breaking bonds costs energy (positive contribution to ΔH); forming bonds releases energy (negative contribution). ΔH negative = exothermic.

## key_note

Bond breaking: endothermic (energy in). Bond forming: exothermic (energy out). ΔH = energy in − energy out. Negative ΔH = exothermic. Positive ΔH = endothermic. Use displayed formulae to count every bond broken and formed. Bond energies are averages — calculated values are approximate.

## fifas — CFIFA form (Convert step added; the four FIFA steps below are verbatim)

**Question:** Calculate ΔH for H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl. Bond energies: H-H=436, Cl-Cl=243, H-Cl=432 kJ/mol.

**Convert:** [NEW — examined ✓] Nothing to convert — all bond energies are already given in kJ/mol.

**F:** ΔH = energy in (bonds broken) − energy out (bonds formed)

**I:** Broken: 1×H-H(436) + 1×Cl-Cl(243) = 679 kJ. Formed: 2×H-Cl(432) = 864 kJ

**F:** ΔH = 679 − 864 = −185

**A:** ΔH = −185 kJ/mol (exothermic)
