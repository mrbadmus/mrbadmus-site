### 1. Using Electrolysis to Extract Metals
`electrolysis-extraction` · Chemistry / chemical-changes · AQA 8464 5.4.3.3; 8462 4.4.3.3 (+ HT 8464 5.4.3.1, 5.4.3.5; 8462 4.4.3.1, 4.4.3.5) · CF CH TF TH (HT layer on CH TH)

- **Family:** SYSTEM. The aluminium cell is one set-up whose parts (cryolite, carbon anode, current) each answer one of the spec's two "explain" statements (BATCH-PLAN kept).
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** the aluminium cell. The pupil removes the cryolite (the temperature jumps to ~2050 °C) and runs the cell (the anode shrinks as CO₂ forms).
- **Route layers:**
  - higher: Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al and 2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻ (th1; equations 1–2). 8464 5.4.3.1 (HT para), 5.4.3.5 (HT only). Currently on all four (EX-F4).

- **Required practical:** none.
- **Calculations:** none.
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "Just heat it with carbon like iron." Aluminium is more reactive than carbon (5.4.3.3).
  - "Cryolite is a reactant." It is a solvent that lowers the operating temperature. That is why a mixture is used (5.4.3.3).
  - "The anode wears out because it melts." Oxygen made at the anode reacts with the carbon to form CO₂ (5.4.3.3).
  - "Aluminium forms at the positive electrode." Al³⁺ goes to the cathode (5.4.3.1).

### 3. Half Equations for Electrode Reactions
`half-equations` · Chemistry / chemical-changes · AQA 8464 5.4.3.5 (HT only), 5.4.3.1 (HT para); 8462 4.4.3.5 (HT only), 4.4.3.1 (HT para) · CH TH

- **Family:** PROCESS. A fixed sequence (atoms → charge → electrons → combine) applied to any electrode (BATCH-PLAN kept).
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** a half-equation builder. The pupil sets coefficients, the charge tally on each side updates live, and electrons are added until the two sides match.
- **Route layers:**
  - higher (the whole lesson): 8464 5.4.3.5 (HT only) / 8462 4.4.3.5 (HT only). The page is CH TH, so there is no base layer.
- **Required practical:** none.
- **Calculations:** no numerical calculation. Two procedures are taught as CFIFA (Convert = "nothing to convert", already in the source):
  - Balance one half equation (e.g. Cl⁻ → Cl₂ ⇒ 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻). **Chains? no.** HT.
  - Combine two half equations into the overall equation. **Chains? yes.** Step 1: balance each half equation. Step 2: multiply so the electrons match, add, and cancel the electrons (e.g. 2Na⁺ + 2e⁻ → 2Na with 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻ gives 2NaCl → 2Na + Cl₂). It needs its own Step 1 / Step 2 worked example before q2 (rule 3). HT.
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none (half equations are not quantity relationships).
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "Electrons go on the right at the cathode." Positive ions gain electrons, so the electrons sit on the left (5.4.3.5).
  - "Cl⁻ → Cl + e⁻." Chlorine is diatomic: 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻.
  - "One electron per ion, always." The number equals the ion's charge (Al³⁺ needs 3e⁻; 2O²⁻ releases 4e⁻).
  - "The electrons stay in the overall equation." They cancel once matched.
  - "The anode equation must end in + e⁻." AQA also accepts 4OH⁻ − 4e⁻ → O₂ + 2H₂O (5.4.3.5; HE-F3).
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** a seesaw has to balance. If one side has two minus charges, does the other side need more minus or less?
- **Practice (rule 4):** q1 and q2 are usable on CH TH (2 per route). q2 depends on the combine worked example. Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size with complete-the-equation items (missing electrons, missing coefficient) for Al³⁺, O²⁻, Br⁻, OH⁻.
- **Diagrams:** `05-diagram-library/README.md` row 3, the shared `electrolysis()` cell (its cathode/anode tuples are these equations). Must draw: a step-by-step atom/charge tally beside the equation.
- **Examiner tip:** none approved.

### 4. Exothermic and Endothermic Reactions
`exothermic-endothermic` · Chemistry / energy-changes · AQA 8464 5.5.1.1; 8462 4.5.1.1 · CF CH TF TH (no HT or separate-science layer)

- **Family:** REQUIRED PRACTICAL (changed from BATCH-PLAN's CONTRAST). AQA limits this statement to "measurement of temperature change", so the exo/endo contrast is decided by the RP's own thermometer reading. With the calculation removed (EE-F1), the RP is the lesson's spine.
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** a polystyrene-cup bench. The pupil runs neutralisation, metal + acid, citric acid + NaHCO₃ and a displacement, reads start and end temperatures, and sorts each reaction by the sign of ΔT.
- **Route layers:**
  - None. All base. The `higher` content (molar ΔH) is off-spec on every route (EE-F4).
- **Required practical:** Combined **RP10** = Chemistry **RP4**, "investigate the variables that affect temperature changes in reacting solutions" (8464 5.5.1.1; 8462 4.5.1.1), on CF CH TF TH. The data's "RP5" is wrong (EE-F3).
- **Calculations:** temperature change = final temperature − initial temperature. **Chains? no.** Convert: none (°C). Base. **Not** Q = mcΔT, which is off-spec in chemistry (EE-F1; physics owns ΔE = m c Δθ).
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none (ΔT is a subtraction; Q = mcΔT is not taught here).
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "Endothermic means it gets hot." Endothermic takes energy in, so the surroundings get colder (5.5.1.1).
  - "The cold pack is making cold." It takes in energy from your skin (5.5.1.1).
  - "Exothermic reactions lose energy, so energy is destroyed." Energy is conserved. Products have less, the surroundings have more, by the same amount (5.5.1.1).
  - "All reactions that need heating are exothermic." Thermal decomposition needs heating and is endothermic (5.5.1.1).
  - "A bigger temperature change means a different type of reaction." The sign of the change decides the type, not its size.
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** a hand warmer and an instant ice pack both work by a reaction. Which one gives energy to your hand?
- **Practice (rule 4):** q1 is usable on CF CH TF TH (1 per route). q2 is not usable (EE-F1, EE-F2). Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size: classify from a temperature reading, the spec's examples, RP variables (independent / dependent / control), RP error (heat loss), and evaluate a use (self-heating can vs sports pack).
- **Diagrams:** `05-diagram-library/README.md` row 4, `ks3_art/c7.py r_temp_bench()`. Must draw: the polystyrene cup (lid, thermometer, beaker for support). The README's "labelled Q=mcΔT" is not wanted.
- **Examiner tip:** none approved.

### 5. Reaction Profiles
`reaction-profiles` · Chemistry / energy-changes · AQA 8464 5.5.1.2, 5.6.1.4; 8462 4.5.1.2, 4.6.1.4 · CF CH TF TH (no HT or separate-science layer)

- **Family:** MODEL. One diagram is the model, and every spec skill is drawing it or reading it (BATCH-PLAN kept).
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** a profile you drag. The pupil moves the products' level above or below the reactants (the badge flips exo/endo), drags the peak (Ea), and toggles a catalyst (the peak drops, ΔH stays).
- **Route layers:**
  - None. All base. The `higher` section is base and must reach CF TF (RPR-F1). Bond-energy calculation is HT 5.5.1.3, a separate lesson.
- **Required practical:** none.
- **Calculations:** (base; convert: none, all kJ or kJ/mol)
  - overall energy change ΔH = energy of products − energy of reactants. **Chains? no.**
  - activation energy Ea = energy at peak − energy of reactants. **Chains? no.**
  - q1 is an Apply item. Give one worked "read it off the diagram" example (CFIFA, Convert = nothing to convert) before it (rule 3).
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none. Both are differences read off a diagram, and a triangle does not apply.
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "Activation energy is the gap between reactants and products." Ea is reactants → peak (5.5.1.2).
  - "Exothermic reactions don't need activation energy." Every reaction does, because particles must collide with enough energy (5.5.1.2).
  - "A catalyst changes the energy given out." It lowers Ea only; reactant/product levels and the overall change are unchanged (5.6.1.4).
  - "A catalyst gives the particles more energy." It provides a different pathway with lower Ea (5.6.1.4).
  - "If the products are higher, energy was given out." Products higher means energy was taken in (endothermic) (5.5.1.2).
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** a match won't light until you strike it, then it burns by itself. Does burning need a push to get started?
- **Practice (rule 4):** q1 and q2 are usable on CF CH TF TH (2 per route). Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size: identify exo/endo from a profile, label Ea and the overall change, read Ea off a diagram, and sketch or choose the catalysed curve.
- **Diagrams:** `05-diagram-library/README.md` row 5, `figlib/chemistry.py reaction_profile(kind, with_catalyst)`. Must draw: exothermic and endothermic profiles with a curved line, Ea and overall-change arrows (Ea from the reactant level), and the catalysed overlay.
- **Examiner tip:** none approved.

### 6. Bond Energy Calculations
`bond-energy-calculations` · Chemistry / energy changes · AQA 8464 5.5.1.3; 8462 4.5.1.3 (HT only) · CH TH

- **Family:** QUANTITATIVE — the page is one calculation (bonds broken − bonds formed); BATCH-PLAN kept.
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** A two-pan energy ledger — bonds snapped from displayed formulae drop into "energy in", bonds made into "energy out"; the difference, with its sign, is the energy change.
- **Route layers:**
  - The whole page is higher: 8464 5.5.1.3 (HT only), 8462 4.5.1.3 (HT only).
  - "Bond energies are averages, so answers are approximate" (th3, key_note, q2) is enrichment beyond the spec statement.
- **Required practical:** none.
- **Calculations:**
  - Overall energy change = energy in (bonds broken) − energy out (bonds formed). Chains? **yes** — Step 1 sum each side (number of each bond × its bond energy, counted from displayed formulae); Step 2 in − out, and the sign gives exo/endo. Needs its own Step 1 / Step 2 worked example (th3 ex 2, CH₄ + 2O₂, is the counting one). Convert: none — AQA supplies bond energies in kJ/mol; no natural wrong-unit case exists here. HT.
  - Missing bond energy from a given overall change. Chains? **yes** — Step 1 sums of the known bonds; Step 2 rearrange in − out = ΔH. Not in the source; teach before testing. HT.
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none — "energy change = in − out" is a subtraction, not a product/quotient; no triangle. Learn it (chemistry has no equation sheet).
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "Making bonds takes energy." — energy is released when bonds in the products form (5.5.1.3).
  - "Subtract formed − broken." — in − out; the wrong order flips the sign (common_mistake).
  - "Negative means endothermic / the surroundings lose energy." — negative = exothermic; surroundings warm (q1 opt 3).
  - "Count each bond type once." — multiply by the number of bonds and by the equation's coefficients (CH₄: 4 C–H; 2O₂: 2 O=O).
  - "Add the two totals." — the energy change is their difference (q1 opt 2).
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** snapping a stick takes effort from you; does a reaction cost energy or give it out when things stick together?
- **Practice (rule 4):** q1, q2 usable on CH TH (2 per route); q2's explanations 2 and 3 are swapped (BEC-F1). Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size, including a counting item from displayed formulae and an endothermic result.
- **Diagrams:** `05-diagram-library/README.md` row 6 — nothing exists. Must draw: displayed formulae with bonds broken/formed marked; the energy-in vs energy-out comparison.
- **Examiner tip:** none approved.

### 9. Rate of Reaction and Calculations
`calculating-rates` · Chemistry / rates & equilibrium · AQA 8464 5.6.1.1; 8462 4.6.1.1 · CF CH TF TH

- **Family:** QUANTITATIVE — mean rate from data and rate from a graph's slope; BATCH-PLAN kept.
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** A gas syringe filling against a stopwatch, plotting volume–time live; lay a ruler on the curve as a tangent and watch the slope shrink as the reaction slows.
- **Route layers:**
  - Higher: rate in mol/s from moles — 8464 5.6.1.1 / 8462 4.6.1.1 ("For the Higher Tier…"); missing from the source (CR-F2).
  - Higher: calculate the gradient of a tangent at a specific time — 8464 5.6.1.1 bullet 4 (HT only).
  - Drawing a tangent and using its slope as a measure of rate is base, not HT (CR-F4).
- **Required practical:** the rate RP — Combined RP 11 (8464 5.6.1.2), Chemistry RP 5 (8462 4.6.1.2), all four routes. Its home lesson is `factors-affecting-rate`; here only its data processing (volume–time graph, 1/time). The source's "RP6" label is wrong (CR-F1).
- **Calculations:**
  - Mean rate = quantity ÷ time (g/s, cm³/s). Chains? no. Convert: minutes → s (q1: 4 min → 240 s) is the wrong-unit worked example; the FIFA (84 cm³ in 60 s) is the nothing-to-convert one. Base.
  - Mean rate in mol/s. Chains? **yes** — Step 1 n = m ÷ Mr; Step 2 rate = n ÷ t. Convert: min → s. HT.
  - Gradient of a tangent = change in quantity ÷ change in time, read from two points on the drawn tangent. Chains? no, but needs its own worked example (none in the source). HT.

### 13. Reversible Reactions and Equilibrium
`reversible-reactions-equilibrium` · Chemistry / rates-equilibrium · AQA 8464 5.6.2.1–5.6.2.3; 8462 4.6.2.1–4.6.2.3 · true routes CF CH TF TH
