### 1. Energy Stores and Systems
`energy-stores-systems` · physics/energy · AQA refs (8464 6.1.1.1; 8463 4.1.1.1) · CF CH TF TH

- **Family:** PROCESS — changed from the plan's CLASSIFY. The spec asks pupils to describe how the stores change as a system changes (before → pathway → after) in five named situations. Sorting store vs pathway is a drill inside that, not the whole job.
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** pick one of the five spec situations and trace it: the pupil names the store before, the pathway, and the store(s) after, with the total staying the same.
- **Route layers:** none. Everything is base.
- **Required practical:** none.
- **Calculations:** none. The spec's "calculate … on a common scale" is taught in changes-in-energy (6.1.1.2), energy-changes-in-systems (6.1.1.3) and power (6.1.1.4). Here only the idea of a common scale is needed: total before = total after, shared between stores. That is a sum, not an equation.
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none in this lesson. If Ek, Ep or Ee are mentioned, label Ek = ½mv² and Ep = mgh "On the sheet" and Ee = ½ke² base, "On the sheet". Link to changes-in-energy and do not teach them here.
- **Staying distinct:** changes-in-energy (batch 2) owns the Ek, Ep and Ee calculations. Power (batch 3) owns rate. energy-transfers-in-a-system (#3) owns conservation, dissipation and reducing waste. This lesson names stores and pathways and traces the five situations, with no numbers.
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "Heat is a type of energy store." The store is thermal; heating is a pathway.
  - "A kettle turns chemical energy into heat." Energy reaches the kettle electrically, then heats the water (spec situation 5).
  - "Light and sound are stores." They are pathways, and the energy ends up in the thermal store of the surroundings.
  - "When a car brakes, the kinetic energy disappears." It goes to the thermal store of the brakes, then the surroundings (spec situation 4).
  - "Energy is released when bonds break." Breaking bonds takes in energy; forming them releases it (8464 5.5.1.3).
  - "Energy gets used up." The total in a closed system does not change (6.1.2.1).
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** a ball thrown straight up slows down. Is it losing energy or moving it somewhere else? Every pupil has seen this.
- **Practice (rule 4):** 1 verbatim frozen item usable on all four routes (q1). q2 is not usable (flag F2: wx2 says energy is released when bonds break). Design must top the bank up to the batch's fixed size.
- **Diagrams:** see `05-diagram-library/README.md`. Must-draw: a store → pathway → store diagram for each of the five spec situations (`energy_transfer()`).
- **Examiner tip:** none approved.

### 2. Energy Changes in Systems
`energy-changes-in-systems` · physics/energy · AQA refs (8464 6.1.1.3; 8463 4.1.1.3) · CF CH TF TH

- **Family:** REQUIRED PRACTICAL. Agrees with the plan. The spec prints the SHC practical at this section, and the RP is what separates this lesson from temperature-changes-shc, which teaches the same equation.
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** run the heater-and-block practical in simulation, take E from P × t, measure Δθ, work out c, then see why heat loss makes c come out too high.
- **Route layers:** none. Everything is base.
- **Required practical:** Combined **RP14** = Physics **RP1**, "determine the specific heat capacity of one or more materials" (8464 6.1.1.3 / 8463 4.1.1.3). On all four routes. The badge must be route-aware: 14 on CF/CH, 1 on TF/TH (flag F1).
- **Calculations:**
  - ΔE = m c Δθ and its rearrangements (c, m, Δθ). **Chains equations? no.** Δθ = final − initial is part of Insert. Convert g → kg and kJ → J. Base.
  - RP data: E = P t, then c = ΔE ÷ (m Δθ). **Chains equations? yes.** Step 1: energy supplied E = P × t. Step 2: c = E ÷ (m × Δθ). Convert minutes → s and kW → W (Step 1); g → kg (Step 2). Base.
  - Heater warms a liquid: E = P t, then Δθ = ΔE ÷ (m c). **Chains equations? yes.** Step 1: E = P × t. Step 2: Δθ = E ÷ (m × c). Same conversions. Base.
  - CFIFA needs two worked examples: the frozen FIFA (nothing to convert) and a g → kg one (flag F3). Each chain needs its own Step 1 / Step 2 worked example before practice (flag F2).
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):**
  - ΔE = m c Δθ — On the sheet (8464 and 8463) — top ΔE / bottom m × c × Δθ. No square or ½.
  - E = P t — On the sheet (8464 and 8463) — top E / bottom P × t.
- **Staying distinct:** temperature-changes-shc (batch 3, 6.3.2.2) already teaches ΔE = mcΔθ in the particle-model framing (the rise depends on mass, material and energy input). This lesson is the energy framing and the RP's spec home. Lead with the practical and the two-step chain. Do not re-run batch 3's single-step drills (flag F6). Power (batch 3) owns P = E/t as a rate; here E = Pt is just Step 1.
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "Δθ is the final temperature." Δθ = final − initial (common_mistake).
  - "Grams are fine." The equation needs kg.
  - "Heat loss makes my c too low." Energy supplied is overcounted, so c comes out too high.
  - "High SHC means it heats up quickly." It means more energy per kg per °C, so it heats slowly.
  - "Same energy gives the same temperature rise in any material." c depends on the material.
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** on a sunny beach, the sand is too hot to walk on while the sea stays cool. Same sunshine, so why?
- **Practice (rule 4):** 2 verbatim frozen items usable on all four routes (q1, q2; minor imprecisions in F5, both usable). None unusable. Design must top the bank up to the batch's fixed size, including RP-data and two-step items once the chain has been taught.
- **Diagrams:** see `05-diagram-library/README.md`. Must-draw: the RP apparatus (`shc_apparatus()`: lagged block, heater, thermometer, joulemeter or power supply, balance).
- **Examiner tip:** none approved.

### 3. Energy Transfers in a System
`energy-transfers-in-a-system` · physics/energy · AQA refs (8464 6.1.2.1; 8463 4.1.2.1) · CF CH TF TH

- **Family:** MODEL. Agrees with the plan. One idea explains every case: the total is fixed, and what changes is how usefully the energy is stored. Dissipation and the ways of reducing it all follow from that.
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** a closed-system energy bar that stays the same height while the useful share drains into "thermal store of the surroundings". The pupil adds lubrication or streamlining and predicts which share shrinks.
- **Route layers:** none in this lesson. (The physics-only RP2, thermal insulators, 8463 4.1.2.1, lives in thermal-conductivity.)
- **Required practical:** none here. 8463 RP2 (physics only; no Combined equivalent) belongs to thermal-conductivity, TF TH.
- **Calculations:** none. "Wasted = total input − useful output" is a subtraction, not an equation. Efficiency is 6.1.2.2, a separate lesson.
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.
- **Staying distinct:** thermal-conductivity (batch 4) shares spec point 6.1.2.1 and owns conductivity, wall thickness, how insulation works, and RP2. Here, insulation is one example among lubrication and streamlining (flag F3). energy-stores-systems (#1) names the stores; this lesson is about the total staying fixed while energy spreads out. Do not re-teach the store list.
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "Energy gets used up." Energy "cannot be created or destroyed" (6.1.2.1).
  - "When the pendulum stops, its energy has gone." It is in the thermal store of the air and pivot.
  - "Wasted energy disappears." It is stored "in less useful ways", spread out in the surroundings.
  - "Insulation keeps the cold out" or "makes heat". It slows the rate of energy transfer out of the house.
  - "Oil removes friction." It reduces friction, so less energy is dissipated.
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** a swing left alone always stops. Has the energy been destroyed, or gone somewhere?
- **Practice (rule 4):** 2 verbatim frozen items usable on all four routes (q1, q2; F4 is minor). None unusable. Design must top the bank up to the batch's fixed size.

### 6. Current, Resistance and Potential Difference
`current-resistance-pd` · Physics / electricity · AQA 8464 6.2.1.3; 8463 4.2.1.3 · true routes CF CH TF TH (all base)

**Family:** REQUIRED PRACTICAL — the spec sites RP15/RP3 (factors affecting resistance) here and nowhere else; V = IR is the one equation the RP's analysis runs on. (BATCH-PLAN said QUANTITATIVE; changed because the RP is the lesson's unique content — V = IR alone is also used by both pilot neighbours.)

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** a wire on a metre rule with a sliding crocodile clip — move it, read V and I, watch R = V ÷ I build a straight R–length line.

**Route layers:** none — every point is base on all four routes.

**Required practical:** Combined RP15 = Physics RP3, "factors affecting the resistance of electrical circuits: the length of a wire at constant temperature; combinations of resistors in series and parallel" (8464 6.2.1.3; 8463 4.2.1.3). All four routes. ⚠ The frozen `rp` line is wrong (CRP-F2); use the spec text in the source file's "Spec core missing" section. Keep distinct from RP16/RP4 (I–V characteristics), which the pilot `resistors` owns.

**Calculations:**
- V = IR and both rearrangements (I = V ÷ R, R = V ÷ I). **Chains equations? no.** Convert: mA → A, kΩ → Ω. Base.
- RP analysis: R = V ÷ I at each length, then read the R–length graph. **Chains equations? no** (one equation, repeated). Convert: length in cm is a graph axis only, not in the equation. Base.
- Series example in theory 3 (12 V, 4 Ω + 8 Ω). **Chains equations? yes** — Step 1 R_total = R₁ + R₂; Step 2 I = V ÷ R_total; Step 3 V₁ = I R₁. This is the pilot `series-parallel-circuits`' content; if Design keeps it here, it needs its own "Step 1 … Step 2 …" worked example first. Base.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):**
- V = I R — **On the sheet** (8464 and 8463, June 2026). Triangle: V top / I × R bottom.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "R = V × I." — R = V ÷ I; the spec equation is V = IR.
- "A longer wire carries the same current, it's the same wire." — at the same pd, longer wire → more resistance → smaller current (6.2.1.3: greater R, smaller I).
- "The resistor uses up the current." — current is the same all round a single loop (6.2.1.2).
- "Leave it switched on, the readings don't change." — the wire heats; the RP needs constant temperature.
- "Resistors in parallel add up." — two resistors in parallel have less resistance than the smaller one (6.2.2).

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** a long, thin garden hose versus a short, wide one — which lets water through more easily — stands on everyday experience on every route.

**Practice (rule 4):** usable verbatim on all four routes: 1 (q2 — I–V gradient, though it is `resistors` content). Not usable as written: q1 (CRP-F1, option 4 working wrong). The bank must be topped up by Design to the batch's fixed size.

**Diagrams:** see `05-diagram-library/README.md`. Must-draw: the RP15/RP3 circuit (wire on a metre rule, crocodile clip, ammeter in series, voltmeter across the wire) and an R-against-length graph. ⚠ The README's suggested I–V characteristic graph is the pilot `resistors`' figure, not this lesson's.

### 7. Direct and Alternating Potential Difference
`direct-alternating-pd` · Physics / electricity · AQA 8464 6.2.3.1; 8463 4.2.3.1 · true routes CF CH TF TH (all base)

**Family:** CONTRAST — the spec asks for exactly one thing: explain the difference between direct and alternating pd. (BATCH-PLAN's suggestion kept.)

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** two oscilloscope screens side by side — a flat dc line and a 50 Hz ac wave — with an arrow showing which way the pd pushes.

**Route layers:** none — every point is base on all four routes.

**Required practical:** none.

**Calculations:**
- f = 1 ÷ T (period read from a trace). **Chains equations? no.** Convert: ms → s (20 ms = 0.020 s) — the frozen FIFA needs none, so a second worked example should carry it. Base.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):**
- T = 1 ÷ f (printed as "period = 1 / frequency") — **On the sheet** (8464 and 8463, June 2026). Triangle: 1 top / f × T bottom.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "dc is steady, ac changes size." — the difference that scores is direction: direct is always one way; alternating keeps reversing.
- "UK mains is 240 V / 110 V / 60 Hz." — UK mains is 50 Hz and about 230 V (6.2.3.1).
- "Batteries give ac." — cells and batteries give dc; mains is ac.
- "A steady pd can't make a current." — a cell's steady pd drives current all the time.

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** a torch runs on batteries, a kettle plugs into the wall — "are they the same kind of electricity?" stands on everyday experience on every route.

**Practice (rule 4):** usable verbatim on all four routes: 2 (q1, q2). None unusable. (q2's wx3 mentions "peak/rms" — true but beyond spec; DAP-F1.) The bank must be topped up by Design to the batch's fixed size.

### 9. Power
`power-electricity` · Physics / electricity · AQA 8464 6.2.4.1; 8463 4.2.4.1 · true routes CF CH TF TH (all base)

**Family:** QUANTITATIVE — two recall-and-apply equations and choosing between them is the whole spec point. (BATCH-PLAN's suggestion kept.)

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** one lamp, three meters (V, I, R) — the pupil picks which two readings they have and the lesson hands them P = VI or P = I²R.

**Route layers:** none — every point is base on all four routes (no HT).

**Required practical:** none.

**Calculations:**
- P = VI (and I = P ÷ V). **Chains equations? no.** Convert: mA → A, kW → W, kV → V. Base.
- P = I²R. **Chains equations? no.** Convert: mA → A, kΩ → Ω; square I as its own line. Base.
- P from V and R (theory 1's check runs the reverse). **Chains equations? yes** — Step 1 I = V ÷ R; Step 2 P = VI (or P = I²R). Needs its own "Step 1 … Step 2 …" worked example. Base.
- P from I and V, then energy over time. **Chains equations? yes** — Step 1 P = VI; Step 2 E = Pt (convert t to s). Teach in `energy-transfers-appliances` (#10); here only if Design keeps theory 2. Base.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):**
- P = V I — **On the sheet** (8464 and 8463). Triangle: P top / V × I bottom.
- P = I² R — **On the sheet** (8464 and 8463). Triangle: P top / I² × R bottom; ⚠ the square: "I = √(P ÷ R)" is its own line.
- E = P t, E = Q V — **On the sheet**; owned by #10 (link only).

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "P = I × R²." — only the current is squared: P = I²R (6.2.4.1).
- "P = I × R." — that gives volts (V = IR), not watts.
- "Power is the energy." — power is the rate of energy transfer (energy per second).
- "250 mA goes straight in as 250." — convert to 0.25 A first.
- "More resistance always means more power." — true at the same current (P = I²R), not at the same pd.

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** a kettle and a phone charger plug into the same socket — which transfers energy faster? — stands on everyday experience on every route.

**Practice (rule 4):** usable verbatim on all four routes: 1 (q1). Not usable: q2 (PE-F1 — fuse choice, beyond spec). The bank must be topped up by Design to the batch's fixed size.

**Diagrams:** see `05-diagram-library/README.md`. Must-draw: a lamp (or heater) in a circuit with V and I marked (`circuit()` supplies the engine).

**Examiner tip:** none approved.

### 10. Energy Transfers in Everyday Appliances
`energy-transfers-appliances` · Physics / electricity · AQA 8464 6.2.4.2; 8463 4.2.4.2 · true routes CF CH TF TH (all base)

**Family:** QUANTITATIVE — two recall-and-apply equations (E = Pt, E = QV), with the unit conversions the spec demands (t in s). (BATCH-PLAN's suggestion kept.)

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** a toaster and a fridge on one meter — power × time decides which transfers more energy in a day.

**Route layers:** none — every point is base on all four routes.

**Required practical:** none.

**Calculations:**
- E = Pt. **Chains equations? no.** Convert: kW → W, minutes/hours → s (the frozen FIFA needs both: 1.5 kW = 1500 W, 4 min = 240 s). Base.
- E = QV. **Chains equations? no.** Convert: kV → V, mC → C. Base. ⚠ Missing from the frozen data (ETA-F4) — worked example must be written fresh.
- Energy from pd, current and time. **Chains equations? yes** — Step 1 Q = It (or P = VI); Step 2 E = QV (or E = Pt). Needs its own "Step 1 … Step 2 …" worked example. Base.
- kWh and cost (source theory 2–3, q1). Beyond spec — not to be taught as a calculation (ETA-F1).

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):**
- E = P t — **On the sheet** (8464 and 8463). Triangle: E top / P × t bottom.
- E = Q V — **On the sheet** (8464 and 8463). Triangle: E top / Q × V bottom.
- "Energy (kWh) = Power (kW) × time (h)" — on neither sheet and not in either spec: no triangle.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "The more powerful appliance always uses more energy." — energy depends on power AND time (6.2.4.2).
- "Minutes go straight into E = Pt." — time must be in seconds for joules.
- "Energy gets used up." — it is transferred, by charge doing work, to stores such as a motor's kinetic store or a heater's thermal store.
- "Every appliance wastes energy as heat." — an electric heater's heating IS its useful job.
- "E = Q ÷ V." — E = QV: each coulomb transfers V joules.

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** "Which costs more to run for a day — the fridge or the kettle?" — stands on everyday experience on every route.

**Practice (rule 4):** usable verbatim on all four routes: 1 (q2). Not usable: q1 (ETA-F1 — kWh and cost, beyond spec). The bank must be topped up by Design to the batch's fixed size, including E = QV items.

**Diagrams:** see `05-diagram-library/README.md`. Must-draw: appliance energy-transfer diagrams (battery or mains → motor kinetic store; → heater thermal store) and a worked E = Pt figure.

### 11. The National Grid
`national-grid` · physics/electricity · AQA refs (8464 6.2.4.3; 8463 4.2.4.3, 4.7.3.4) · CF CH TF TH (base + HT layer; turns ratio is a TH layer)

- **Family:** SYSTEM — kept from the plan. The spec asks why the whole chain (station → step-up → cables → step-down → home) is efficient; each part only makes sense in relation to the others.
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** a grid strip where the pupil sets the transmission p.d. and watches the current in the cables and the I²R loss change, for the same power delivered.
- **Route layers:**
  - Higher (CH TH): VpIp = VsIs — "Higher tier only: … select and use the equation … as given on the equation sheet", 8464 6.2.4.3 (HT only); 8463 4.7.3.4 (physics only) (HT only).
  - Triple Higher (TH): turns ratio Vp/Vs = np/ns; step-up has more secondary turns; transformers need ac — 8463 4.7.3.4 (physics only) (HT only). The frozen FIFA and q2 belong here only. The site's TH `transformers` lesson also owns this.
- **Required practical:** none.
- **Calculations:**
  - Current in the cables, I = P ÷ V (from P = VI). Chains? no. Convert MW → W, kV → V. Base.
  - Power wasted in the cables, P = I²R, after finding I. **Chains? yes** — Step 1 I = P ÷ V, Step 2 P_lost = I²R. Convert MW → W, kV → V. Base.
  - Transformer currents, VpIp = VsIs. Chains? no. Convert kV → V, kW/MW → W, mA → A. HT (CH TH).
  - Turns ratio, Vs = Vp × ns ÷ np. Chains? no. Convert kV → V. TH.
  - Turns ratio then current. **Chains? yes** — Step 1 Vs from Vp/Vs = np/ns, Step 2 Is from VpIp = VsIs. TH.
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):**
  - P = VI — On the sheet (8464 and 8463). Top P; bottom V × I.
  - P = I²R — On the sheet (both). Top P; bottom I² × R. Square: finding I needs its own line, I = √(P ÷ R).
  - VpIp = VsIs — On the sheet (both), HT. Four quantities, no three-part triangle: show it as "power in = power out" and give the rearranged line (Is = VpIp ÷ Vs) as its own step.
  - Vp/Vs = np/ns — On the sheet (8463 only), HT, TH only. A ratio, no clean triangle: give Vs = Vp × ns ÷ np as its own line.
- **Staying distinct:** `power-electricity` (#9) owns P = VI and P = I²R as definitions; here they are used. `transformers` (TH) owns coil structure and induction. `mains-electricity` (#8) owns 230 V, 50 Hz.
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "High voltage means more current, so more gets through." For the same power, P = VI: higher V means lower I.
  - "Step-up transformers make more power." Power out is at most power in (VpIp = VsIs); only the p.d. and current trade.
  - "Halving the current halves the loss." Loss = I²R, so halving I quarters the loss.
  - "Homes get the high voltage too." Step-down transformers lower it to 230 V before it reaches homes.
  - "Transformers work on any supply." They need ac (TH layer only).
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** everyone has seen pylons with "Danger of death" signs: is the electricity in them at a higher or lower voltage than in a plug socket?
- **Practice (rule 4):** q1 usable on all four routes. q2 usable on TH only (turns ratio, NATIONAL-GRID-F1). No frozen item for VpIp = VsIs or the I²R chain. Design must top the bank up to the batch's fixed size.
- **Diagrams:** see `05-diagram-library/README.md`. Must-draw: the grid strip (station → step-up → pylons → step-down → home) and, for TH, a transformer schematic (primary coil, iron core, secondary coil). Both missing.

- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** rub two named materials and watch electrons move across one by one, leaving equal and opposite charges; then bring two charged objects together to see attraction or repulsion.
- **Route layers:** none beyond triple. The whole lesson is "4.2.5 Static electricity (physics only)", 8463 4.2.5.1, with no HT statement. The source's `higher` field is not HT (STATIC-CHARGE-F3).
- **Required practical:** none.
- **Calculations:** none.
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.
- **Staying distinct:** `electric-fields` (#13) explains WHY charges push and pull at a distance and why sparks happen. Here: how the charge gets there, and the evidence that forces exist.
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "The positive object gained protons." Only electrons move; the positive object lost electrons.

### 14. Density of Materials
`density-of-materials` · physics/particle-model · AQA refs (8464 6.3.1.1; 8463 4.3.1.1) · CF CH TF TH

- **Family:** REQUIRED PRACTICAL — kept from the plan. The RP (regular solid, irregular solid, liquid) is the spec's core activity; the particle-model explanation of density rides alongside it.
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** a virtual bench where the pupil measures a block with a ruler, a stone by displacement and a liquid on a balance, then works out each density.
- **Route layers:** none. Everything is base; 6.3.1.1 has no HT and no separate-science content. Floating/sinking is context only (formally 8463 4.5.5.1.2, physics only, HT only — DENSITY-OF-MATERIALS-F5).
- **Required practical:** Combined RP17 · Physics RP5 — "use appropriate apparatus to make and record the measurements needed to determine the densities of regular and irregular solid objects and liquids" (8464 6.3.1.1; 8463 4.3.1.1). All four routes. The source says only "RP17" (DENSITY-OF-MATERIALS-F2).
- **Calculations:**
  - ρ = m ÷ V and its rearrangements m = ρV, V = m ÷ ρ. Chains? no. Convert g → kg (÷ 1000), cm³ → m³ (× 10⁻⁶), or answer in g/cm³ (× 1000 → kg/m³). Base. The frozen FIFA converts nothing; a second worked example must convert.
  - RP regular solid. **Chains? yes** — Step 1 V = l × w × h (or πr²h), Step 2 ρ = m ÷ V. Convert mm/cm → m, g → kg (or stay in g/cm³ throughout). Base.
  - RP irregular solid. **Chains? yes** — Step 1 V = reading after − reading before (1 ml = 1 cm³), Step 2 ρ = m ÷ V. Base.
  - Mass conserved through a change of state ("apply this equation to changes where mass is conserved"). **Chains? yes** — Step 1 m = ρ₁ × V₁, Step 2 V₂ = m ÷ ρ₂ (e.g. water freezing to ice). Base. Not in the source.
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** ρ = m/V — On the sheet (8464 and 8463, June 2026). Top m; bottom ρ × V.
- **Staying distinct:** `changes-of-state` (#15) owns names of changes, mass conservation and physical-vs-chemical. The particle diagrams of solid/liquid/gas are taught here (6.3.1.1) and only reused there.
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "Heavy things are dense." Density is mass per volume; a big log is heavy and floats.
  - "Ice is lighter than water because it froze." Freezing keeps the mass; ice takes up more volume, so it is less dense.
  - "ρ = V ÷ m." Mass on top: kg per m³ says mass ÷ volume.
  - "2.7 g/cm³ is the same number in kg/m³." 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³.
  - "Gases are dense because their particles move fast." Gas particles are far apart, so there is little mass in each m³.
  - "The water level rise is the object's mass." The rise is its volume (displacement).
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** a bowling ball and a football are about the same size. Which has more stuff packed into it?
- **Practice (rule 4):** q1 usable on all four routes. q2 not usable as written (DENSITY-OF-MATERIALS-F1: a distractor is also right). Design must top the bank up to the batch's fixed size, including a conversion item, an RP-data item and a mass-conserved item, each after its worked example.
- **Diagrams:** see `05-diagram-library/README.md`. Must-draw: displacement (measuring cylinder or eureka can) for an irregular solid, and a regular solid with ruler/callipers. Both missing. `states_of_matter()` covers the particle picture.

### 15. Changes of State
`changes-of-state` · physics/particle-model · AQA refs (8464 6.3.1.2; 8463 4.3.1.2) · CF CH TF TH

- **Family:** MODEL — kept from the plan. The particle model explains both spec points: mass is conserved because the particles only rearrange, and the change is physical because they stay the same particles.
- **Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** a sealed box of particles on a balance; the pupil melts, boils, condenses and freezes it, and the arrangement changes while the reading never moves.
- **Route layers:** none. Everything is base; 6.3.1.2 has no HT and no separate-science content.
- **Required practical:** none.
- **Calculations:** none required by 6.3.1.2. The mass-conserved density chain (m = ρ₁V₁, then V₂ = m ÷ ρ₂) belongs to `density-of-materials` (#14); if linked here, it needs that worked example first.
- **Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.
- **Staying distinct:** `internal-energy` (batch 2, 6.3.2.1) owns "heating raises temperature or changes state" and KE + PE. `specific-latent-heat` (batch 3, 6.3.2.3, titled "Changes of State and Specific Latent Heat") owns the flat heating-curve plateau and E = mL. `density-of-materials` (#14) owns the solid/liquid/gas particle diagrams. This lesson: the six names, mass conservation, physical vs chemical. No heating curve, no E = mL.
- **Misconceptions to confront:**
  - "Ice weighs more than the water it came from, because it's bigger." Same particles, same mass; only the volume changes.
  - "Steam has no mass." The particles are still there, just far apart.
  - "Melting is chemical because the solid is gone." The material gets its original properties back if you reverse it — that makes it physical.
  - "Boiling and evaporation are the same." Evaporation is from the surface at any temperature; boiling is throughout the liquid at the boiling point.
  - "Particles get bigger when a substance melts or boils." They stay the same size; the spacing changes.
- **"Start here" access note (rule 1):** an ice cube in a sealed bag melts on the table. Does the bag now weigh the same, or less?
- **Practice (rule 4):** q1 and q2 usable on all four routes (q1's wx1 is muddled, CHANGES-OF-STATE-F4, but the key is right). Design must top the bank up to the batch's fixed size, including sublimation and condensation items.
- **Diagrams:** see `05-diagram-library/README.md`. Must-draw: the three states with arrows for the six changes (`states_of_matter()`); do not use `heating_curve()` here.
- **Examiner tip:** none approved.
