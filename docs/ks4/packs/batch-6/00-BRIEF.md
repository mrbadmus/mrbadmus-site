## The four lesson rules (Mide, 2 Oct 2026) — apply to every lesson here

1. **Start here is a two-option guess**, framed as a guess, answerable from everyday experience on every route; the reveal is encouraging either way and leads straight into the teaching.
2. **Equations are formula triangles you can cover** — in the equation block, the equation-sheet panel and CFIFA's Formula step. A square or a ½ gets its own extra line (square-root, ×2).
3. **Teach every step before you test it.** A calculation that chains two equations gets its own "Step 1 … Step 2 …" worked example before any pupil does one; CFIFA on each step.
4. **Practice is the same size in every lesson** — same number of questions at each rung of the end practice and the exam ladder. Set the number once (at least the content-standards floor) and keep it across the batch.

Each lesson below says which calculations chain, which equations need triangles, and how many verbatim quiz items are usable; the rest of each bank is yours to write to the fixed size.

### 3. Reactions of Acids with Metals and Bases

### 7. Culturing Microorganisms
`culturing-microorganisms` · Biology / cell biology · AQA 8461 4.1.1.6 (biology only); not in 8464 · TF TH

**Family:** REQUIRED PRACTICAL — Biology RP2 (zones of inhibition) is the page; aseptic technique and both calculations serve it (BATCH-PLAN suggestion kept).

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** Run the antibiotic-disc plate: set it up aseptically, incubate, measure each clear zone's diameter and turn it into an area.

**Route layers:**
- Whole lesson: "4.1.1.6 Culturing microorganisms (biology only)" → TF TH.
- Express the number of bacteria in standard form: 8461 4.1.1.6 "(HT only)" → TH only.

**Required practical:** Biology RP2 (8461 Required practical activity 2, biology only, §8.2.2): effect of antiseptics or antibiotics on bacterial growth, measuring zones of inhibition. No Combined number (not in 8464). TF TH. The source's "RP6" label is wrong (CULTURING-F1).

**Calculations:**
- Area of a clear zone or colony: A = π r². Chains equations? no — but halving the diameter to get r is its own line before squaring. Convert: cm → mm (or keep one unit throughout) so the area comes out in mm². Triple, both tiers. Frozen worked example: 18 mm → r = 9 mm → ≈ 254 mm² (nothing to convert). Second worked example must have a conversion.
- Number of bacteria after a time: chains equations? **yes** — Step 1: number of divisions = time ÷ mean division time; Step 2: bacteria = starting number × 2^(number of divisions). Convert: hours → minutes so time and division time share a unit. Triple, both tiers; giving the answer in standard form is HT only.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):**
- A = π r² — Learn it (biology has no equation sheet). Triangle: A on top; π × r² on the bottom. Has a square: covering r gives r² = A ÷ π, then square-root as its own line.
- number of divisions = time ÷ mean division time — Learn it. Triangle: time on top; number of divisions × mean division time on the bottom.
- bacteria = starting number × 2ⁿ — no triangle (exponent); teach as doubling, one line per step.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "Incubate at 37 °C — that's body temperature, they'll grow best." → school cultures are generally incubated at 25 °C to reduce the chance of growing human pathogens.
- "Tape the lid all the way round to keep it sealed." → a few strips only, so oxygen gets in and harmful anaerobic bacteria don't grow; store upside down.
- "A small clear zone means a strong antibiotic." → the bigger the zone, the more effective (same disc, same concentration).
- "Area = π × diameter²." → halve the diameter first.
- "Bacteria grow by adding the same number each time." → they double every division (binary fission).
- "You flame everything, even beakers." → loops are flamed; dishes, media and glassware are autoclaved.

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** Everyone has seen mould on old bread: "would germs grow faster somewhere warm or somewhere cold — warm or cold?"

**Practice (rule 4):** q1, q2, q3 all usable on TF and TH (3). None unusable. The frozen FIFA (inhibition-zone area) and equation are usable. The bank must be topped up by Design to the batch's fixed size, including bacterial-number items (TF and TH) and a standard-form item (TH only).

**Diagrams:** see `05-diagram-library/README.md` #7 — no drawing code exists. Must draw: labelled agar plate (lawn, antibiotic discs, control disc, measurable clear zones); taped lid with plate stored upside down; a doubling chart for binary fission.

**Examiner tip:** none approved.

**Misconceptions to confront:**

### 10. The Digestive System
`digestive-system` · Biology / organisation · AQA 8464 4.2.2.1 (+4.1.3.1); 8461 4.2.2.1 (+4.1.3.1) · true routes CF CH TF TH

**Family:** SYSTEM — organs and enzymes act in sequence along one tract; break one (no bile, no villi) and a named step fails. BATCH-PLAN's suggestion stands; the food-tests RP sits inside it.

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** a gut you follow a meal through, where each organ lights up the enzyme it makes and what it breaks down into what.

**Route layers:** none — all base on every route (the frozen `higher` is base content; DIGESTIVE-SYSTEM-F1).

**Required practical:** Food tests — **RP3 on Combined** (8464, CF CH), **RP4 on Biology** (8461, TF TH); qualitative reagents: Benedict's (sugars, heated), iodine (starch), Biuret (protein); lipid test by ethanol emulsion. On all four routes, numbered per route (F3, F4).

**Calculations:** None. (Rate calculations for enzyme reactions belong to `enzymes`.)

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none. Word equations only (starch → sugars; protein → amino acids; lipid → glycerol + fatty acids) — not triangles.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "Bile digests fat." — Bile emulsifies (physical) and neutralises; lipase digests (4.2.2.1).
- "All the enzymes come from the stomach / only the pancreas." — Sites: salivary glands, stomach, pancreas, small intestine (F2).
- "Bile is made in the gall bladder." — Made in the liver, stored in the gall bladder.
- "Protein digestion starts in the mouth." — Amylase in saliva acts on starch only; protein digestion starts in the stomach (pepsin).
- "Benedict's goes red straight away." — It must be heated, and the colour depends on how much sugar (F4).
- "Food is absorbed in the large intestine." — Digested food is absorbed in the small intestine; the large intestine absorbs water.

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** everyone has eaten bread and later felt hungry again — a guess whether the bread is "used as it is" or "broken down into smaller bits first" stands on that.

### 13. Transpiration
`transpiration` · Biology / organisation · AQA 8464 4.2.3.2; 8461 4.2.3.2 · true routes CF CH TF TH

**Family:** INVESTIGATION — the spec's two skills are explaining four factors and measuring rate by water uptake (potometer); BATCH-PLAN's suggestion stands.

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** a potometer whose bubble moves faster or slower as the pupil turns up heat, light, wind or humidity, with a stopwatch to read off a rate.

**Route layers:** none — all base on every route (the frozen `higher` is the base AT 3–5 skill; TRANSPIRATION-F1).

**Required practical:** none (the potometer is AT 3–5, not an RP, on either spec — F6).

**Calculations:**
- Rate of water uptake = distance moved by bubble ÷ time. Chains equations? no. Convert: s → min (or cm → mm) so units match the answer (mm/min). Base.
- Volume of water taken up = π r² × distance, then rate = volume ÷ time. Chains equations? **yes** — Step 1 volume from radius and distance, Step 2 rate from volume and time; needs its own "Step 1 … Step 2 …" worked example (rule 3). Convert: diameter → radius (not a unit), cm → mm. Base (MS 5c).
- Mean of repeat readings (MS 2a). Chains? no. Base.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** biology has no equation sheet on either route — all "Learn it".
- rate = distance ÷ time — Learn it — top: distance; bottom: rate × time.
- volume = π r² × distance — Learn it — top: volume; bottom: π r² × distance. Has a square: finding r is its own line (r = √(area ÷ π)).
- rate = volume ÷ time — Learn it — top: volume; bottom: rate × time.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "Humid air makes plants lose more water." — High humidity means a smaller gradient, so slower transpiration (4.2.3.2).
- "Transpiration is the plant sucking water up from the roots." — Water evaporates from the leaves and more is drawn up the xylem.
- "Wind cools the plant so it loses less water." — Wind removes water vapour near the stomata, so transpiration speeds up.
- "Plants transpire most at night." — In the dark guard cells close the stomata, so transpiration almost stops.
- "The potometer measures water lost." — It measures water uptake, which is close to, but not the same as, water lost.
- "Transpiration moves sugar." — That is translocation, in phloem.

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** washing on a line dries faster on a warm, windy day — a guess "will a plant lose water faster on a hot windy day or a cool still one?" stands on that.

**Practice (rule 4):** q1–q3 usable as written on all four routes (3); none unusable (q3 has a partly-true distractor — F5, usable). Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size, including rate-calculation Apply items once taught.

**Diagrams:** see `05-diagram-library/README.md` #13 — reuse `_leaf_section()`; must draw a labelled potometer (shoot, capillary, bubble, scale, reservoir) and a stoma with its two guard cells open and closed.

### 14. Translocation
`translocation` · Biology / organisation · AQA 8464 4.2.3.2; 8461 4.2.3.2 · true routes CF CH TF TH

**Family:** CONTRAST — xylem vs phloem (what, where, which way, dead vs living, structure); BATCH-PLAN's suggestion stands.

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** one stem cut open with xylem and phloem side by side — water rising in one, sugar flowing both ways in the other to a root, a fruit and a growing tip.

**Route layers:** none — all base on every route (the frozen `higher` is excluded mechanism; TRANSLOCATION-F2).

**Required practical:** none.

**Calculations:** None.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "Translocation and transpiration are the same." — Translocation is sugars in phloem; transpiration is water lost from leaves, drawn up xylem (4.2.3.2).
- "Phloem carries water up like xylem." — Phloem carries dissolved sugars from the leaves to the rest of the plant.
- "Sugar only goes down to the roots." — It goes wherever it is used or stored, up to growing tips and fruits too.
- "Phloem cells are dead like xylem." — Phloem is living, elongated cells with pores in the end walls; xylem is dead, hollow, lignified tubes.
- "Plants get their food from the soil." — Sugars are made in the leaves and moved by translocation.

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** potatoes and carrots store food underground but leaves are up top — a guess "is the food made in the leaves or in the roots?" stands on that.
