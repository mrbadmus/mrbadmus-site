## Spec core missing from the frozen data (written from the spec, examiner)
8464 5.1.2.5 / 8462 4.1.2.5: "Students should be able to describe the reactions of the first three alkali metals with oxygen, chlorine and water." The frozen data covers water fully; oxygen appears only as "tarnish" and chlorine not at all. Design needs (examiner-supplied, base, every route):
- **With oxygen:** the metals tarnish in air as a metal oxide forms (dull surface); they burn when heated. 4Li + O₂ → 2Li₂O; 4Na + O₂ → 2Na₂O; 4K + O₂ → 2K₂O. Reaction is faster down the group.
- **With chlorine:** the metals react vigorously when heated in chlorine, forming white solid metal chlorides (ionic, +1 metal ion). 2Li + Cl₂ → 2LiCl; 2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl; 2K + Cl₂ → 2KCl. More vigorous down the group.
- "Predict properties from given trends down the group" (WS 1.2), e.g. reactivity with water increases and melting point decreases (Li 181 °C, Na 98 °C, K 63 °C) → rubidium and caesium react even more violently and melt lower.

## higher

Quantitative explanation: increasing atomic radius down group → outer electron in higher shell → greater distance from nucleus → weaker electrostatic attraction → lower ionisation energy → more easily lost → more reactive. Predict properties of Rb and Cs.

## common_mistake

Reactivity INCREASES down Group 1 (opposite to Group 7). This is because the outer electron gets further from the nucleus and is more easily lost. Going down Group 1: more shells → outer electron further away → weaker attraction → MORE reactive. Going down Group 7: non-metals get LESS reactive (harder to attract an extra electron).

## key_note

Group 1: alkali metals, 1 outer electron, form +1 ions. Soft, low density, low melting point. React with water → metal hydroxide + hydrogen. Reactivity INCREASES down group (outer electron further from nucleus, less attracted, more easily lost).
