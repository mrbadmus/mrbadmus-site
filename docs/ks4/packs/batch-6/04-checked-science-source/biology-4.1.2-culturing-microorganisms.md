## common_mistake

Petri dishes in school are incubated at 25°C — NOT at 37°C (body temperature). This is deliberately to reduce the risk of growing dangerous human pathogens. A larger inhibition zone means MORE effective antibiotic — the bacteria cannot grow in that area. A disc with no clear zone = resistant bacteria.

## key_note

Culture medium: agar + nutrients in Petri dish. Aseptic technique: sterilise equipment, flame loops, work near Bunsen. Incubate at max 25°C in school (safety). Inhibition zone: clear area around antibiotic disc — larger = more effective.

## fifas — CFIFA form (Convert step added; the four FIFA steps below are verbatim)

**Question:** An antibiotic disc produces a clear zone with a diameter of 18 mm. Calculate the area of the inhibition zone.

**Convert:** [NEW — examined ✓] Nothing to convert — the diameter is given in millimetres (mm) and the radius and area are calculated in millimetres throughout, so no unit conversion is needed.

**F:** Area = π × r²

**I:** Diameter = 18 mm → radius = 9 mm. Area = π × 9²

**F:** Area = π × 81 = 254.47...

**A:** Area ≈ 254 mm²
