## summary

Calculate percentage yield and explain why the actual yield is often less than the theoretical yield.

## higher

Calculate theoretical yield using moles: convert reactant mass → moles → use molar ratio from equation → convert to product mass. Calculate percentage yield from these values. Evaluate the economic and environmental importance of maximising percentage yield. Identify the limiting reactant in a reaction and use it to calculate maximum theoretical yield.

## common_mistake

The theoretical yield must be calculated from the balanced equation — not just assumed. Use moles: find moles of limiting reactant → use molar ratio → calculate mass of product. Then divide actual mass by this calculated theoretical mass.

## key_note

% yield = (actual ÷ theoretical) × 100. Always less than 100% in practice. Reasons: reversible reactions, side reactions, practical losses, impurities. High yield = less waste, lower cost, more sustainable. Chemistry-only spec point.
