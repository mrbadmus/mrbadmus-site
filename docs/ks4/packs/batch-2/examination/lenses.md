Spec statements (verbatim, 8463 4.6.2.5): "A lens forms an image by refracting light. In a convex lens, parallel rays of light are brought to a focus at the principal focus. The distance from the lens to the principal focus is called the focal length. Ray diagrams are used to show the formation of images by convex and concave lenses. The image produced by a convex lens can be either real or virtual. The image produced by a concave lens is always virtual. Students should be able to construct ray diagrams to illustrate the similarities and differences between convex and concave lenses. The magnification produced by a lens can be calculated using the equation: magnification = image height / object height. Magnification is a ratio and so has no units. Image height and object height should both be measured in either mm or cm. In ray diagrams a convex lens will be represented by: [symbol] A concave lens will be represented by: [symbol]." ## 5. For the lesson author
**Misconceptions seen in AQA marking**
- "Concave lenses can form real images"; "convex lenses always make images bigger".
- Ray drawn through the centre of the lens bending; ray through F drawn emerging still diverging.
- Rays drawn without arrows, or refraction drawn at both surfaces inconsistently — AQA accepts refraction drawn at the centre line of the lens symbol.
- Image position marked where only one ray reaches; virtual image found without extending rays back as **dashed** lines.
- Magnification given a unit (cm, ×cm) or calculated as object ÷ image; heights in different units.
- "Real image = upright" (in AQA's single-lens cases, real images are inverted).
- Myopia corrected with a converging lens (swap).

**Command words**: Complete the ray diagram, Draw, Determine (image height/position from a scale drawing), Calculate (magnification), Describe (the image: real/virtual, upright/inverted, magnified/diminished), Compare (convex and concave), Explain.

**Typical questions** ⚑ examiner-drafted
- *Complete the ray diagram to show how the image is formed. Label the image. [3]* — ray parallel to axis refracted through F (1); ray through centre undeviated (1); image drawn where rays cross, as an arrow, labelled (1). (For a virtual image: rays extended back as dashed lines (1).)
- *Describe the image formed. [2]* — two of: real / virtual; inverted / upright; magnified / diminished — 1 mark each correct property, max 2 (or 3 if three asked).
- *An object 2.0 cm tall gives an image 7.0 mm tall. Calculate the magnification. [3]* — 2.0 cm = 20 mm (1); M = 7.0 ÷ 20 (1); = 0.35 (no unit) (1).
- *Compare the images formed by a convex lens and a concave lens. [4]* — convex can be real or virtual / concave always virtual (1); convex real image inverted / concave image upright (1); convex can magnify / concave always diminished (1); convex can be projected on a screen, concave cannot (1).
- 6-mark extended response is uncommon for 4.6.2.5 alone; the 6-mark eye question (accommodation and correcting myopia/hyperopia) is set on **Biology** papers (8461 4.5.2.3). Levels: L3 (5–6) both defects described (image in front of / behind retina, with cause) and correct lens with how it refracts; L2 (3–4) one defect fully or both partly; L1 (1–2) isolated facts. ⚑ examiner-drafted.

**Required practical**: none. 8463 has no lens RP (AT 4, 8 opportunity only: "Investigate the magnification produced by a range of convex lenses"). Not in 8464 at all.

**Equations**: magnification = image height ÷ object height — **on the sheet** (8463 Appendix A, sheet equation 7). Image distance ÷ object distance — not in spec.
