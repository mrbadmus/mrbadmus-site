## 5. For the lesson author
**Misconceptions seen in AQA marking**
- Enzymes are "killed" (never credited).
- "Denatured" used for low temperature (cold slows, does not denature).
- "The active site changes shape" without the consequence "so the substrate no longer fits / no enzyme–substrate complexes form" — the second mark is usually lost here.
- Enzyme and substrate "have the same shape" (they are complementary).
- Rate goes on rising with temperature; or "the optimum is always 37 °C" (pH optima differ by enzyme; plant/bacterial enzymes differ).
- Lock and key described with the enzyme as the key.
- RP: confusing the end point — the reaction is complete when iodine **stays orange-brown** (no blue-black); "iodine turns orange" is read as a positive test.
- Rate given as the time itself rather than 1/time (or 1000/time), with no unit (s⁻¹).

**Command words**: Describe, Explain, Suggest (a reason for an anomalous result / an improvement), Calculate (rate), Use the information, Plan / Describe a method (RP), Evaluate (method), Predict.

**Typical questions** ⚑ examiner-drafted
- *Explain why the rate of an enzyme reaction decreases above 40 °C. [3]* — enzyme denatured (1); active site changes shape (1); substrate no longer fits / fewer enzyme–substrate complexes (1).
- *Use the lock and key model to explain why protease cannot digest starch. [2]* — active site has a specific shape (1); starch is not complementary / does not fit, so no enzyme–substrate complex (1).
- *Starch at pH 6 was digested in 120 s. Calculate the rate. [2]* — 1 ÷ 120 (1) = 0.0083 s⁻¹ (1) — or 1000/120 = 8.3 (arbitrary units) if the question defines rate that way. Unit mark only if the answer line does not print it.
- *Describe a method to investigate the effect of pH on the rate of amylase. [6]* Levels of response — indicative content: add iodine to wells of a spotting tile; mix amylase and starch with a buffer of known pH; water bath at fixed temperature (e.g. 35 °C), solutions equilibrated first; start timer on mixing; every 30 s transfer a drop to iodine; record time when iodine stays orange-brown; repeat at a range of pH values (e.g. 4–9 using buffers); control volumes/concentrations of starch and amylase; repeat and calculate mean; calculate rate. L3 (5–6): logical, complete method that would give valid results, with control of variables; L2 (3–4): method with most steps, some control; L1 (1–2): some relevant steps, not in a workable order. ⚑ examiner-drafted.

**Required practical — 8464 RP4 / 8461 RP5: effect of pH on amylase**
- Method: one drop iodine per well on a spotting tile; 2 cm³ amylase + 1 cm³ pH buffer in a test tube, and 2 cm³ starch (1%) in another, both in a 35 °C water bath for ~5 min; mix and start the stopwatch; every 30 s put one drop of the mixture into a fresh iodine well; stop when the iodine no longer turns blue-black; repeat for each buffer pH (e.g. 5, 6, 7, 8). (Volumes are the handbook's typical values — the spec fixes only the 30 s sampling and the temperature control.)
- Variables: IV pH (buffer); DV time for starch to be completely digested (→ rate = 1/t); CV temperature, volume and concentration of amylase and starch, volume of buffer, sampling interval, drop size.
- Risks: iodine is an irritant and stains (eye protection, wipe spills); amylase can be an irritant/sensitiser (avoid skin and eye contact, do not inhale powder); hot water bath — scalds (keep ≤ 40 °C); glassware.
- Typical results: shortest time (fastest rate) around pH 6–7 for most school amylases; slower either side; at extreme pH starch may still be present after 10 minutes. Sources of error: judging the colour end-point; 30 s sampling resolution (end-point uncertain to ±30 s); mixture continues to react while sampling.

**Equations**: rate = 1 ÷ time (or amount ÷ time) — **learn it** (no biology equation sheet). CFIFA applies to the RP data processing (Convert step: minutes → seconds is the common one).
