## 5. For the lesson author
**Misconceptions seen in AQA marking**
- "Decomposers eat the dead material" with no mention of respiration / releasing CO₂.
- "Bacteria die in the cold" (cold slows them; freezing stops growth, does not sterilise).
- "Heat kills decomposers" used for every temperature rise — the rate rises up to an optimum first.
- Oxygen: "decomposers need oxygen to breathe" is weak; credited: "for (aerobic) respiration".
- Compost made "in a sealed bin" (that makes it anaerobic and slow, and produces methane).
- RP10: thinking milk "goes acidic because of bacteria" in the lipase version — the pH falls because **lipase breaks fat into fatty acids**; and saying the indicator turns *from colourless to pink* (it is pink → colourless).
- Rate reported as time; or rate calculated as time ÷ change.

**Command words**: Explain, Describe, Suggest (why the compost heap has holes / is turned), Calculate (rate of decay; rate change), Plot / Draw (graph), Plan (RP), Evaluate (e.g. composting kitchen waste vs landfill — a common AQA context; I did not find an "evaluate" statement in the 8461 v1.0 text of 4.7.2.3, so treat this as exam-practice convention, not a spec line).

**Typical questions** ⚑ examiner-drafted
- *(Combined, base) Explain the role of microorganisms in recycling materials. [3]* — microorganisms/decomposers break down dead organisms/waste (1); they respire releasing CO₂ to the atmosphere (1); mineral ions returned to the soil (1) [for plants to absorb].
- *(Triple) A gardener's compost heap has holes in the sides and is kept damp. Explain why. [4]* — holes let in oxygen (1) for aerobic respiration of decomposers (1); water needed by microorganisms / for enzyme reactions / survival (1); so decay is faster (1). Max 4.
- *(Triple) Mass of leaves fell from 80 g to 50 g in 6 weeks. Calculate the mean rate of decay. [2]* — 30 ÷ 6 (1) = 5 g per week (1).
- *(Triple) Describe how to investigate the effect of temperature on the rate of decay of milk. [6]* Levels of response — indicative content: milk + sodium carbonate solution + phenolphthalein in a test tube (pink); lipase in a separate tube; both in a water bath at the test temperature until equilibrated; mix, start timer; time until pink disappears (pH falls as fatty acids form); repeat at a range of temperatures (e.g. 20–60 °C); same volumes and concentrations; repeat and mean; rate = 1/time. L3 (5–6) coherent method that would give valid results with controls; L2 (3–4) most steps, some control; L1 (1–2) some relevant steps. ⚑ examiner-drafted.

**Required practical — 8461 RP10 (biology only; not in 8464)**
- Method (AQA handbook version, from memory — numbers indicative): 5 drops phenolphthalein + 5 cm³ full-fat milk + 7 cm³ sodium carbonate solution (0.05 mol/dm³) in a test tube → pink; 1 cm³ lipase solution (5%) in a second tube; both in a water bath at the chosen temperature ~5 min; add lipase, stir, start stopwatch; stop when the pink colour disappears; repeat at 5 temperatures (e.g. 20, 30, 40, 50, 60 °C). Alternative AQA-accepted version: a pH probe/data logger recording pH against time.
- Variables: IV temperature; DV time for indicator to go colourless (or rate of pH fall) → rate = 1/t; CV volumes and concentrations of milk, lipase, sodium carbonate, number of indicator drops, end-point colour judged against a white tile/standard, equilibration time.
- Risks: phenolphthalein is in ethanol — flammable, irritant (no flames, eye protection); sodium carbonate solution — irritant to eyes; lipase — enzyme sensitiser (avoid skin contact/inhalation); hot water — scalds; never taste the milk.
- Typical results: time falls (rate rises) from 20 °C to about 35–45 °C, then rises again as lipase denatures; at 60 °C+ the colour may not change within the time allowed. Errors: subjective end-point; temperature drift once tubes are mixed outside the bath.

**Equations**: rate of decay = change in mass (or other quantity) ÷ time; rate = 1/time for RP10 — **learn it** (no biology equation sheet). CFIFA applies on Triple routes (Convert step: days → weeks, minutes → seconds).
