Spec statements relied on (verbatim, 8461 = 8464):
- 4.1.2.1: "The nucleus of a cell contains chromosomes made of DNA molecules. Each chromosome carries a large number of genes. In body cells the chromosomes are normally found in pairs."
- 4.1.2.2: "Cells divide in a series of stages called the cell cycle. Students should be able to describe the stages of the cell cycle, including mitosis. During the cell cycle the genetic material is doubled and then divided into two identical cells. Before a cell can divide it needs to grow and increase the number of sub-cellular structures such as ribosomes and mitochondria. The DNA replicates to form two copies of each chromosome. In mitosis one set of chromosomes is pulled to each end of the cell and the nucleus divides. Finally the cytoplasm and cell membranes divide to form two identical cells. **Students need to understand the three overall stages of the cell cycle but do not need to know the different phases of the mitosis stage.** Cell division by mitosis is important in the growth and development of multicellular organisms. Students should be able to recognise and describe situations in given contexts where mitosis is occurring."
- 4.2.2.7: "describe cancer as the result of changes in cells that lead to uncontrolled growth and division. Benign tumours … contained in one area, usually within a membrane. They do not invade other parts of the body. Malignant tumour cells are cancers. They invade neighbouring tissues and spread to different parts of the body in the blood where they form secondary tumours. … lifestyle risk factors … genetic risk factors for some cancers."

## 5. For the lesson author
**Misconceptions seen in AQA marking**
- Mitosis produces four cells / cells with half the chromosomes (confusion with meiosis).
- "Chromosomes divide" or "cells split" with no DNA replication first.
- Counting: 46 chromosomes "become 92 chromosomes" (they become 46 chromosomes, each of two copies).
- The cell cycle = mitosis only (missing growth + replication and the division of cytoplasm).
- "Mitosis makes gametes"; "mitosis causes variation".
- Cancer is "caused by mitosis" (it is uncontrolled division caused by changes in the cell); benign "is not dangerous".
- Spelling/term slips: "meitosis"; "mitosis produces sex cells".

**Command words**: Describe (the stages / what happens), Explain (why DNA must replicate; why cells are identical), Give / Name, Suggest (where mitosis is happening in a given context), Compare (mitosis vs meiosis), Use the information / Determine (stage from a micrograph or graph of DNA mass).

**Typical questions** ⚑ examiner-drafted
- *Describe what happens to a cell during the cell cycle. [4]* — cell grows / number of sub-cellular structures (ribosomes, mitochondria) increases (1); DNA replicates to form two copies of each chromosome (1); one set of chromosomes pulled to each end of the cell (1); nucleus divides (1); cytoplasm and cell membrane divide (1); two genetically identical cells formed (1). Max 4. Correct order required for full marks.
- *A graph shows the mass of DNA in a cell over time (doubles, then halves). Explain the changes. [3]* — DNA doubles because it replicates / two copies of each chromosome made (1); stays constant while the cell prepares (1); halves because one set goes to each new cell / cell divides (1).
- *Compare mitosis and meiosis. [4]* — mitosis two cells, meiosis four (1); mitosis full chromosome number, meiosis half (1); mitosis identical, meiosis genetically different (1); mitosis growth/repair, meiosis gametes (1). Each point must be a comparison.
- 6-mark (levels of response), typically combined with stem cells or cancer: *Explain how a single fertilised egg develops into an adult, and why cancer can develop when the process goes wrong.* L3 (5–6): full cell-cycle sequence linked to growth and identical cells, plus cancer as uncontrolled division from changes in cells, benign vs malignant spread in blood to form secondary tumours; L2 (3–4): mostly correct cell cycle or cancer with some linking; L1 (1–2): isolated facts (e.g. "mitosis makes cells", "cancer is a tumour"). 0: nothing creditworthy. ⚑ examiner-drafted.
