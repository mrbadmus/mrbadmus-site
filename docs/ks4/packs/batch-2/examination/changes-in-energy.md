## 5. For the lesson author
**Misconceptions seen in AQA marking**
- Squaring after multiplying (½ × m × v, then squared) or squaring the ½mv product; "v² = 2v".
- Mass left in grams; speed in km/h; extension in cm (the commonest lost mark on Ee).
- Using weight (N) as m in mgh, or multiplying by g twice.
- Ep = mgh used with total height when the question asks for the *change* in height.
- Energy conservation answers that forget to equate Ep lost to Ek gained before rearranging for v (HT-style rearrangement v = √(2Ek/m) is examined on both tiers in AQA papers, with more support at Foundation).
- Writing "J/kg" or "N" as the energy unit.
- Elastic: confusing length with extension; applying Ee beyond the limit of proportionality.

**Command words**: Calculate, Write down the equation that links… (recall equations only — Ek and Ep; never Ee, which is given), Determine, Show that, Explain, Estimate.

**Typical questions** ⚑ examiner-drafted
- *Write down the equation that links kinetic energy, mass and speed. [1]* — Ek = ½ m v² (accept words). Asked only for Ek and Ep, never for Ee.
- *A 0.25 kg ball is dropped from 3.2 m. g = 9.8 N/kg. Calculate the speed just before it hits the ground. Ignore air resistance. [4]* — Ep = 0.25 × 9.8 × 3.2 = 7.84 J (1); Ek = Ep (1); 7.84 = ½ × 0.25 × v² (1); v = 7.9 m/s (1). Correct answer with no working = full marks (AQA convention).
- *A spring (k = 40 N/m) is stretched by 15 cm. Calculate the elastic potential energy. [3]* — e = 0.15 m (1); Ee = ½ × 40 × 0.15² (1); = 0.45 J (1).
- 6-mark extended responses on this content are rare; when set they describe energy transfers between stores (e.g. a bungee jump: Ep → Ek → Ee, dissipation). Levels: L3 (5–6) correct stores named at each stage, transfers linked to changes in height/speed/extension, dissipation to thermal store of surroundings; L2 (3–4) most stages correct, some links; L1 (1–2) some stores named. ⚑ examiner-drafted.

**Required practical**: none (AT 1 opportunity: "Investigate the transfer of energy from a gravitational potential energy store to a kinetic energy store" — not an RP).

**Equations**: Ek = ½mv² — **learn it**. Ep = mgh — **learn it** (g is always given). Ee = ½ke² — **on the sheet**. All three base on both tiers and both pathways.
