## 5. For the lesson author
**Misconceptions seen in AQA marking**
- "Bacteria have a nucleus" / "bacterial DNA is in the nucleus".
- Bacteria have a "cellulose cell wall"; or bacteria have no cell wall.
- Plasmids called "the nucleus" or "chromosomes"; plasmids in plant/animal cells.
- "Bacteria have mitochondria / chloroplasts".
- Viruses are cells or prokaryotes (they are neither).
- Unit slips: µm ↔ mm by 100 instead of 1000; 1 µm = 10⁻³ m.
- Order of magnitude read as "10 times" when the ratio is 100 (two orders) or answered as a ratio instead of a power of ten.
- Big cells need lungs (C15) — cell vs organism.

**Command words** typically used: Name, Give, Describe, Compare, Explain, Calculate, Estimate, Use (the scale bar), Complete (a table).

**Typical questions** ⚑ examiner-drafted
- *Compare the structure of a bacterial cell with an animal cell. [4]* — 1 mark each, max 4: bacterium has no nucleus / animal cell has a nucleus; bacterial DNA is a single loop (free in cytoplasm); bacterium may have plasmids; bacterium has a cell wall, animal cell does not; animal cell has mitochondria, bacterium does not; bacterium is much smaller (≈ 10× / one order of magnitude). Both cells: membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes = similarity (credit only if a comparison is made). A list about one cell only caps at 2.
- *A bacterial cell is 2 µm long; a plant cell is 0.1 mm long. How many orders of magnitude bigger is the plant cell? [3]* — convert 0.1 mm = 100 µm (1); ratio 100 ÷ 2 = 50 (1); ≈ 10¹·⁷ → "about 2 orders of magnitude" / 10² (1). Accept "one or two orders" only with working.
- *Calculate the real length of a bacterium shown 18 mm long at ×6000. Give the answer in µm and in standard form. [3]* — 18 ÷ 6000 = 0.003 mm (1); × 1000 = 3 µm (1); 3 × 10⁰ µm or 3 × 10⁻³ mm (1).
- 6-mark extended response is unusual on this content alone; when set, it is "Compare eukaryotic and prokaryotic cells and explain how bacteria can grow quickly" (Triple). Levels: L3 (5–6) detailed comparison of ≥ 4 features with size/order of magnitude **and** SA:V linked to rapid exchange (Triple: and division as often as every 20 min); L2 (3–4) several correct differences, some explanation; L1 (1–2) isolated features. ⚑ examiner-drafted.

**Required practical**: none for this lesson (RP1 microscopy — 8464 RP1 / 8461 RP1 — belongs to `microscopy`).

**Equations**: magnification = image size ÷ real size — **learn it** (GCSE Biology has no equation sheet). No physics sheet applies.
