## 5. For the lesson author
**Misconceptions seen in AQA marking**
- Plateau explained as "the plant has run out of chlorophyll / light / CO₂" instead of "another factor is limiting".
- "Enzymes are killed" at high temperature (credited answer: denatured; active site changes shape).
- Light described as giving the plant "more energy to grow" with no link to rate of photosynthesis.
- Bubble counting: ignoring that bubbles differ in size; no repeats; not controlling temperature (lamp heats water).
- HT: inverse square applied as "half the distance → double the intensity" (it is four times).
- HT: reading the limiting factor from the wrong section of a multi-line graph (on the rising part the x-axis factor limits; on the plateau something else does).

**Command words**: Describe (a trend from a graph), Explain, Calculate, Suggest, Plan / Describe a method (6-mark RP), Evaluate (HT greenhouse economics).

**Typical questions** ⚑ examiner-drafted
- *A pondweed produced 48 bubbles in 4 minutes. Calculate the rate of photosynthesis in bubbles per minute. [2]* — 48 ÷ 4 (1); 12 bubbles per minute (1).
- *A student collected 3.6 cm³ of oxygen in 90 s. Calculate the rate in cm³ per minute. [2]* — 90 s = 1.5 min (1); 3.6 ÷ 1.5 = 2.4 cm³/min (1).
- *Describe how to investigate the effect of light intensity on the rate of photosynthesis of pondweed. [6]* — level of response: lamp at measured distances; count bubbles / measure gas volume over fixed time; repeat at each distance and calculate mean; control temperature (heat shield / thermometer) and CO₂ (sodium hydrogencarbonate); same pondweed / same length; acclimatise before timing.
- *Explain why the rate stops increasing at high light intensity (one-factor graph). [2]* — light is no longer the limiting factor (1); another factor (e.g. CO₂ concentration / temperature) limits (1).
- *(HT) The lamp is moved from 10 cm to 20 cm. How many times less is the light intensity? [2]* — intensity ∝ 1/d² (1); 4 times less (1).
- *(HT) Graph with two CO₂ curves: which factor limits at point X? [1–2]* — on the rising section, light intensity; on the plateau of the low-CO₂ line, CO₂.
- *(HT) Suggest why a grower may not raise greenhouse temperature further. [2]* — another factor limits so no further gain (1); cost of heating would exceed the value of extra yield (1).

**Required practical**: Combined **RP5** (8464 4.4.1.2) = Biology **RP6** (8461 4.4.1.2). On all four routes; the inverse-square processing is HT.

**Equations**: rate = amount of oxygen (bubbles or cm³) ÷ time — learn it (no biology equation sheet). (HT) light intensity ∝ 1/distance² — learn it.
