## The four lesson rules (Mide, 2 Oct 2026) — apply to every lesson here

1. **Start here is a two-option guess**, framed as a guess, answerable from everyday experience on every route; the reveal is encouraging either way and leads straight into the teaching.
2. **Equations are formula triangles you can cover** — in the equation block, the equation-sheet panel and CFIFA's Formula step. A square or a ½ gets its own extra line (square-root, ×2).
3. **Teach every step before you test it.** A calculation that chains two equations gets its own "Step 1 … Step 2 …" worked example before any pupil does one; CFIFA on each step.
4. **Practice is the same size in every lesson** — same number of questions at each rung of the end practice and the exam ladder. Set the number once (at least the content-standards floor) and keep it across the batch.

Each lesson below says which calculations chain, which equations need triangles, and how many verbatim quiz items are usable; the rest of each bank is yours to write to the fixed size.

### 1. Communicable Diseases and Human Defence Systems
`communicable-diseases-defence` · Biology / infection-response · AQA 8464 4.3.1.1, 4.3.1.6; 8461 4.3.1.1, 4.3.1.6 · CF CH TF TH

**Family:** SYSTEM — layered defences (barriers, then white blood cells); the question is always "what gets through if this one fails?" (BATCH-PLAN's suggestion kept).

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** Send a pathogen in by one route (breath, food, cut); switch off one defence and predict where it now gets stopped.

**Route layers:** none. All base. The source's `higher` text (CH, TH) has no HT home and is not shown (F1).

**Required practical:** none.

**Calculations:** none.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "Phagocytes make antibodies." → Phagocytes engulf and digest; lymphocytes make antibodies (4.3.1.6).
- "Cilia kill germs." → Cilia sweep mucus with trapped pathogens out of the airways; stomach acid and white blood cells destroy them (4.3.1.6).
- "Antibiotics will sort out my flu." → Antibiotics cannot kill viral pathogens (4.3.1.8).
- "Antitoxins kill bacteria." → Antitoxins neutralise the toxins bacteria release (4.3.1.6).
- "The mosquito gives you malaria." → The mosquito is the vector; the protist is the pathogen (4.3.1.5).

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** Everyone has had a cold or a cut finger: does a germ get in more easily through skin or through a cut?

**Practice (rule 4):** 4 frozen items, all usable on all four routes (q1 phagocyte vs lymphocyte, q2 memory cells, q3 vector, q4 mucus). None unusable. Antitoxins (F2) and reducing spread (F3) have no frozen item. Design tops the bank up to the batch's fixed size.

**Diagrams:** `05-diagram-library/README.md` §1 (nothing reusable exists). Must draw: lines of defence — skin, nose, trachea and bronchi (mucus + cilia), stomach (acid), then phagocyte engulfing and lymphocyte making antibodies/antitoxins.

**Route layers:** none. All base.

**Required practical:** none.

**Calculations:** none.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.

### 3. Bacterial Diseases

**Family:** CONTRAST — changed in focus from BATCH-PLAN's "bacteria vs viruses": the spec content is two bacterial diseases, so the discriminating difference is how each spreads, and therefore how each is controlled (food → cook, hygiene, vaccinate poultry; sex → condoms, antibiotics). Bacteria vs viruses (antibiotics) stays as one supporting line.

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** Salmonella vs gonorrhoea A/B: predict the control measure from the route of spread, then explain why penicillin stopped working for one of them.

**Route layers:** none. All base.

**Required practical:** none.

**Calculations:** none.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "My body got used to the antibiotics." → The bacteria are resistant, not the patient (8464 4.6.3.4).
- "The antibiotic made the bacteria mutate." → Mutations are random; the antibiotic kills non-resistant bacteria and the resistant ones survive and reproduce (8464 4.6.3.4).
- "Gonorrhoea can't be treated." → It was easily treated with penicillin until many resistant strains appeared (4.3.1.3).
- "You catch Salmonella from other people coughing." → It is ingested in food or on food prepared in unhygienic conditions (4.3.1.3).
- "It's the bacteria that make you ill, full stop." → Symptoms are caused by the bacteria and the toxins they secrete (4.3.1.3).

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** Everyone knows undercooked chicken can make you ill: is it more likely to be the meat itself or something living on it?

### 5. Vaccination
`vaccination` · Biology / infection-response · AQA 8464 4.3.1.7; 8461 4.3.1.7 (not 4.3.2 as the file header says) · true routes: CF CH TF TH (all base)

**Family:** PROCESS — kept from BATCH-PLAN. One ordered sequence (dead/inactive pathogen in → white blood cells make antibodies → same pathogen returns → antibodies made quickly → no illness), then scaled up to a population (herd immunity).

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** Two timelines side by side — first meeting with a pathogen vs meeting it again after vaccination — with antibody level rising slowly (ill) vs fast (not ill).

**Route layers:**
- None. 4.3.1.7 carries no HT and no biology-only label. The source's `higher` box (threshold %, disease-incidence graphs) is base on all four routes (VACCINATION-F1).

**Required practical:** none.

**Calculations:** none required. (Reading a cases-per-year graph before and after a vaccine is introduced is data interpretation, WS 1.4, all routes.)

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "The vaccine has antibodies in it." → it has dead or inactive pathogen; your white blood cells make the antibodies (4.3.1.7).
- "A vaccine gives you a small dose of the illness." → dead or inactive forms cannot cause the disease; a sore arm is the immune response (F3).
- "A vaccine protects you from every disease." → the antibodies are specific; flu needs a new vaccine each year because its antigens change.
- "Herd immunity means everyone is vaccinated." → only a large proportion need to be immune to stop spread (4.3.1.7).
- "After vaccination the pathogen can't get in." → it gets in; the fast antibody response destroys it before you are ill.

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** Everyone has had (or seen) a jab at school or the GP and knows you catch chickenpox only once — "your body remembers, or the jab kills germs?" is a fair guess on every route.

### 9. Plant Disease Detection and Defence
`plant-disease-detection-defence` · Biology / infection-response · AQA 8461 4.3.3.1–4.3.3.2 (biology only); not in 8464 (not 4.3.5 as the file header says) · true routes: TF TH, with a Higher layer on TH

**Family:** CLASSIFY — kept from BATCH-PLAN. Both halves are sorting tasks: a symptom or a cause to its type (virus / fungus / insect / ion deficiency), and a defence to its spec category (physical / chemical / mechanical).

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** A plant doctor's bench: a sick plant shows one sign (mosaic leaves, black spots, aphids on the stem, stunted, yellow leaves) and the pupil diagnoses virus, fungus, insect, nitrate or magnesium deficiency.

**Route layers:**

- Higher (TH only): detecting disease by symptoms (stunted growth, leaf spots, rot, growths, malformed stems or leaves, discolouration, presence of pests) and identifying it (gardening manual or website, laboratory, testing kits with monoclonal antibodies) — 8461 4.3.3.1 "(HT only)", both lists. Currently unbadged (PLANT-DISEASE-F1).

**Required practical:** none.

**Calculations:** none.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "Plants fight disease with antibodies like we do." → no; their defences are physical, chemical and mechanical (4.3.3.2).
- "Yellow leaves always mean a germ." → magnesium deficiency causes chlorosis — less chlorophyll (4.3.3.1).
- "Nitrate is for making plants green." → nitrate is for protein synthesis and therefore growth; lack of it stunts the plant (4.3.3.1).
- "Thorns stop bacteria." → thorns and hairs deter animals (mechanical); walls, cuticle and bark resist microorganisms (physical) (4.3.3.2).
- "Bark is just a tough coat." → it is layers of dead cells that fall off, taking pathogens with them (4.3.3.2).

**Diagrams:** see `05-diagram-library/README.md` #9. ### 10. Photosynthesis
`photosynthesis` · Biology / bioenergetics · AQA 8464 4.4.1.1; 8461 4.4.1.1 · true routes: CF CH TF TH (all base)

**Family:** PROCESS (kept from BATCH-PLAN). One reaction runs in steps: raw materials arrive (CO₂ through stomata, water up the xylem), light energy is absorbed in the chloroplast, then glucose and oxygen come out.

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** Assemble the reaction in a leaf cell: route CO₂, water and light into a chloroplast, predict what comes out, then switch the light off and watch it stop while respiration carries on. Leave rate, lamp distance and graphs to the next lesson.

**Route layers:**
- None. 4.4.1.1 has no HT and no biology-only content, and the 8461 and 8464 text is identical.

**Required practical:** none here. The light-intensity RP (Combined RP5 / Biology RP6, 4.4.1.2) belongs to rate-of-photosynthesis (flag F1).

**Calculations:** none.

**Equations that need formula triangles (rule 2):** none. Chemical equation only: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen, with "light" over the arrow (must know). Symbols CO₂, H₂O, O₂, C₆H₁₂O₆ to recognise. The balanced form is enrichment.

**Misconceptions to confront:**
- "Plants get their food from the soil." → They make glucose from CO₂ and water using light (4.4.1.1).
- "Oxygen goes in, CO₂ comes out." → CO₂ and water are the reactants; oxygen is a product (4.4.1.1).
- "Plants photosynthesise instead of respiring." → Every living cell respires all the time; photosynthesis happens only in light, in cells with chloroplasts (4.4.1.1; 4.4.2.1).
- "Light is one of the ingredients." → Light is the energy source written over the arrow; the energy is transferred to the chloroplasts (4.4.1.1).
- "Photosynthesis gives out energy." → It is endothermic: it takes energy in from the environment (4.4.1.1).

**"Start here" access note (rule 1):** A houseplant left in a dark cupboard for weeks goes pale and droops. Guess: is that because it needs light, or because it needs fresh air?

**Practice (rule 4):** 3 frozen quiz items usable on all four routes (q1, q3, q4). q2 is off-spec (F3) and not usable. Design must top up the bank to the batch's fixed size, including an endothermic item and a symbols-recognition item.

**Diagrams:** `05-diagram-library/README.md` row 10 (nothing reusable) and "Figures still needed" item 10. Must draw: inputs → chloroplast → outputs (CO₂ via stomata, water via root and xylem, light energy, glucose and O₂ out), with the word equation beside it.

**Examiner tip:** none approved.

### 11. Rate of Photosynthesis
`rate-of-photosynthesis` · Biology / bioenergetics · AQA 8464 4.4.1.2; 8461 4.4.1.2 · true routes: CF CH TF TH (base + Higher layer on CH, TH)

**Family:** REQUIRED PRACTICAL (kept from BATCH-PLAN). This lesson owns the light-intensity RP. The limiting-factors theory is what you need to explain the practical's results.

**Flagship (a suggestion, not a spec):** Run the pondweed practical in simulation: move the lamp, collect O₂ over a fixed time, calculate the rate, plot it against distance, then watch the plateau appear when CO₂ runs short. HT pupils then replot against 1/d². This is a measurement bench, not the photosynthesis lesson's reaction stepper.
