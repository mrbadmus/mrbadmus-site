## 1. Poles of a Magnet and Permanent Magnetism (`poles-of-a-magnet`)

# Flags — poles-of-a-magnet (8464 6.7.1.1; 8463 4.7.1.1, base)

**POLES-OF-A-MAGNET-F1** · WRONG · quiz q2 "A fridge magnet and a compass are both magnets. What is the key difference between them regarding their magnetism?" — key "Fridge magnet: permanent — retains field indefinitely. Compass: also permanent…"; wx1 "…the question is testing understanding that both… are permanent" · The stem asks for a difference; the key says there is none, so a pupil answering the question as asked finds no correct option. "Indefinitely" overclaims (permanent magnets can be demagnetised). wx1 talks to the setter, not the pupil. · Both are permanent magnets: each produces its own field and keeps it when no other field is near. · 8464 6.7.1.1 · Do not use as written on any route. A replacement item on permanent vs induced is Design's to write.

**POLES-OF-A-MAGNET-F2** · IMPRECISE · theory 2 "INDUCED MAGNETS: … Made from SOFT magnetic materials — IRON"; common_mistake "Induced magnets (iron) lose it when the field is removed"; key_note "Induced magnets: temporary… (iron — soft)" · Treats "induced" as a material. Any magnetic material placed in a field becomes an induced magnet — the lesson's own q1 is a steel clip doing exactly that. · "An induced magnet is a material that becomes a magnet when it is placed in a magnetic field… When removed from the magnetic field an induced magnet loses most/all of its magnetism quickly." Soft iron loses almost all; steel can keep some (that is how steel is made into a permanent magnet). · 8464 6.7.1.1 · Teach induced as something that happens to iron, steel, nickel or cobalt in a field; use iron as the example, not the definition.

**POLES-OF-A-MAGNET-F3** · IMPRECISE (usable as written) · quiz q1 wx3 "Magnetic forces act on magnetic materials — they don't 'push' the clip forward." against option 3 "…pulling it forward" · wx3 misquotes the option ("push" for "pulling"). The option is incomplete (no mechanism) rather than false; the key is clearly the best answer. · Induced magnetism: the near end becomes the opposite pole, so the clip is attracted. · 8464 6.7.1.1 · Use q1 verbatim on all routes.

**POLES-OF-A-MAGNET-F4** · GAP · the frozen theory never says where a magnet's force is strongest · Spec core missing from this lesson (it appears only in `magnetic-fields`). · "The poles of a magnet are the places where the magnetic forces are strongest." · 8464 6.7.1.1 · Teach it here.

**POLES-OF-A-MAGNET-F5** · OFF-SPEC · theory 3 (magnetising by stroking/solenoid/hammering; Curie temperature; demagnetising by AC; magnetic domains) and key_note "Domains: aligned = magnetised, random = unmagnetised" · Not in AQA 6.7.1.1 / 4.7.1.1 on any route; never examined. Keep it out of Start here, the ladder and practice; no domain figure is required.

**POLES-OF-A-MAGNET-F6** · IMPRECISE · theory 1 "Only iron, steel, nickel and cobalt can become magnetised or be attracted to magnets." · True for GCSE, false as a universal claim. · The spec's magnetic materials are "iron, steel, cobalt and nickel". · 8464 6.7.1.2 · Say "The magnetic materials you need to know are iron, steel, nickel and cobalt."
