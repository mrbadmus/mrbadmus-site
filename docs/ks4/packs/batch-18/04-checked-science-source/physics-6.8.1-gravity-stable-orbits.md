## higher

HT only — explain qualitatively how gravity provides centripetal force for circular orbits. Explain why orbital speed must change if orbital radius changes for a stable orbit. Describe what happens when a satellite's speed changes.

## common_mistake

In circular motion at constant speed, the object is ACCELERATING (velocity direction changes). The centripetal force is always directed towards the CENTRE — it's provided by gravity for orbital motion. To move to a HIGHER orbit, a satellite must increase speed — but the final speed in the higher orbit is actually lower (paradoxically).

## key_note

Gravity = centripetal force for orbits. Lower orbit: stronger gravity, faster speed, shorter period. Geostationary: 36,000 km, 24 hrs, appears stationary. If speed↑ at same radius → spiral out to larger orbit. If speed↓ → fall to smaller orbit. LEO satellites decay due to atmospheric drag → re-enter.
