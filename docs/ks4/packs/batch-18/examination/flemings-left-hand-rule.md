Spec statements (verbatim, 8463 = 8464): "When a conductor carrying a current is placed in a magnetic field the magnet producing the field and the conductor exert a force on each other. This is called the motor effect. Students should be able to show that Fleming's left-hand rule represents the relative orientation of the force, the current in the conductor and the magnetic field. Students should be able to recall the factors that affect the size of the force on the conductor. For a conductor at right angles to a magnetic field and carrying a current: force = magnetic flux density × current × length, F = BIl … Students should be able to apply this equation which is given on the physics equation sheet. force, F, in newtons, N; magnetic flux density, B, in tesla, T; current, I, in amperes, A (amp is acceptable for ampere); length, l, in metres, m." ## 5. For the lesson author
**Misconceptions seen in AQA marking:** using the right hand; current drawn − to +; field drawn S→N; force taken as along the field or along the current; "a bigger current reverses the force"; length left in cm in F = BIl; B called "magnetic field strength" with the unit left off or given as "B".
**Command words:** Use Fleming's left-hand rule to determine…, Give the factors…, Calculate (F, or B/I/l rearranged), Explain why the wire moves.
**Typical calculation shapes (for Design's examples):** 8.0 cm wire, 2.5 A, 0.15 T → l = 0.080 m, F = 0.15 × 2.5 × 0.080 = 0.030 N; rearranged: F = 0.06 N, I = 3 A, l = 0.1 m → B = 0.06 ÷ (3 × 0.1) = 0.2 T.
**Required practical:** none.
**Equations:** F = BIl — On the sheet (8464 and 8463, June 2026, HT).
