**Which line was followed.** The scheme, row 15b, asks for the process of speciation. The scheme's own code (B38, extinction) does not match those words, and no NC line names speciation; NC B35 (differences between species) is the line whose words match what a species is. Both are taught.

**Gaps and mismatches.** The old page taught only what a species is. It never described how a new species forms. Speciation is now a card with a drawn four-stage figure: one population, a barrier splits it, different conditions on each side, changes build up over many generations until the two groups cannot breed to make fertile offspring. Natural selection is named in one line; Rainford 15a (Evolution) teaches it in full on the B11 lessons.

**Quiz notes.**
- Rung 1 (stored) stem: "Same species?" is now "Are the two parent birds the same species?". Options rewritten without dashes and to 13 words or fewer; key B stays B; options C and D are longer than the key.
- Rung 2 (stored) options C and D replaced. Old C "Because they have the same number of chromosomes" is true for dogs, so it was not definitely wrong; it is now "Because they have exactly the same DNA". Old D "Because they were bred from the same original animal" is also true; it is now "Because they live in the same homes and eat the same food". Key B "Because their offspring can themselves have offspring" is now "Because their puppies can grow up and have puppies".
- Rung 3 and Rung 4 stems reworded (render only); four ticks shortened. Rung 4 is now answerable from the new speciation card and Dive deeper's "Partway there".
- Feedback written for every wrong option of Rungs 1 and 2.

**Examiner fixes.** Rung 2 feedback for option C now answers it (dogs do not have identical DNA). The Easy mistake "A new species appears suddenly" (not definitely wrong: some plants do) is now "Two separated groups become new species straight away". Darwin's finches now say birds with suited beaks survived and bred more, not that beaks changed.

**Checked numbers.** Horse 64, donkey 62; gametes 32 and 31; mule 63, an odd number that cannot all pair. Over a dozen species of Galápagos finch.

**Left out because unsure.** Ring species (the gull example is disputed). Fertile female mules and ligers (the page says "almost never" and "male ligers"). Where the Galápagos finches came from.

**Removed or moved.** Removed: the species-cases bench (`same-species-or-not`, seven cases), the confrontation (`looks-alike-and-can-have-a-baby`), the "test, and where it runs out" rule cards and the summary note. Four of the bench's cases are now a reveal table. New: a drawn speciation figure. Moved to Dive deeper: groups partway to becoming two species; Darwin's finches.

**Easy mistakes rule.** GENE-09 and GENE-10 are tagged on the Easy mistakes card. Two untagged pairs added (separated groups do not become species straight away; organisms do not change on purpose).
