**Which line was followed.** NC C15/C18 and Rainford 17b agree. Rainford 20a and 20b have no page of their own and are taught here. The scheme cites NC C36* for acid rain (20b). C36 is about carbon dioxide from human activity, so C27 (non-metal oxides and acidity) is the closer science match. The scheme wins, so both codes are cited and both rows are ticked.

**Gaps and mismatches.** Acid rain (20b) has no page of its own: it is taught here as three blocks (How acid rain forms, What acid rain damages, Reducing acid rain and its damage). Uses of acids and alkalis (20a) also has no page: stomach acid, indigestion, heartburn and antacids are taught here. The old page named the uses only in its "four problems" bench (toothpaste, antacid, lime, factory waste); they are now cards. The old page never defined heartburn or described acid rain's causes. Universal indicator colours and bands are the same as in C6 lessons 1 and 2.

**Checked numbers.** Stomach acid is hydrochloric acid at about pH 2. Clean rain is about pH 5.6 (carbon dioxide dissolves in it); acid rain is lower. Tooth enamel dissolves below about pH 5.5 (not used here). The pH curve in the figure matches Rung 2: pH 3 after nine drops, 11 after ten.

**Left out because unsure.** Whether toothpaste is alkaline (many are only near neutral), so toothpaste is not named as a use. Whether calcium carbonate counts as a "base" (it neutralises acids but GCSE does not call it a base), so the page says antacids "contain substances that neutralise acid".
