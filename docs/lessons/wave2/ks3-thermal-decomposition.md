**Which line was followed.** NC C14 and Rainford 20a agree. The scheme's 20b "Testing for gases" lists no outcomes; the unit notes give the three tests (oxygen, hydrogen, carbon dioxide), and those were followed.

**Gaps and mismatches.** Rainford 20b (testing for gases) has no page of its own: it is taught here as a drawn figure (c5-gas-tests) and the reveal table The three gas tests. The wording matches Unit 3: "A lit splint at the mouth of the tube" gives "a squeaky pop" (acid-plus-metal), and limewater "turns from clear to milky" (acids-and-carbonates); oxygen "relights a glowing splint". The old page said limewater turns milky "for nothing else"; the page says only that it is the test for carbon dioxide.

**Checked numbers.** None on the page.

**Left out because unsure.** Zinc carbonate gives zinc oxide, which is yellow when hot and white when cold: colours are not given. Whether sodium hydrogencarbonate's products (sodium carbonate, water, carbon dioxide) should be named: Dive deeper names only the carbon dioxide.

**Examiner fixes.** None asked. The general equation now uses C4's left-to-right `c4-word-row` art, with heat over the arrow because heating must go on all the time.

**Removed or moved.** Removed: the test tube bench (`tube-heat`, with its cooling gate `cool-gate` and reveal `stage-4-reveal`), the five changes sort (`sort-five`), the confrontation (`think-commit-black`), the summary note. Moved to Dive deeper: baking soda and cakes; not every carbonate breaks down. Cut: airbags, the cement emissions percentage, quicklime with water.

**Easy mistakes rule.** REACT-12 and REACT-13 are tagged. Two untagged pairs: a gas that puts out a lit splint is carbon dioxide; the squeaky pop test uses a glowing splint.
