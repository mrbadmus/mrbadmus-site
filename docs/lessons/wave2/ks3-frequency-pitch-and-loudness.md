**Which line was followed.** Rainford 23a says pitch depends on "wavelength". The NC word is frequency (P30), so the page teaches pitch from frequency and adds one card: all sounds travel at the same speed in air, so a higher frequency sound has a shorter wavelength. Both readings of the scheme are then met.

**Gaps and mismatches.** The traces are displacement against time, so "more waves across the screen" is a higher frequency; the page says "time goes across each screen" and does not call that spacing a wavelength. Oscilloscopes are named once ("a microphone and an oscilloscope can show a sound as a trace"). The human hearing range (20 Hz to 20 000 Hz) is hearing-and-auditory-range's.

**Checked numbers.** 200 Hz × 3.0 s = 600 vibrations. 0.50 min × 60 = 30 s; 15 000 ÷ 30 = 500 Hz; 15 000 ÷ 0.50 = 30 000, sixty times too big. Rung 1: 256 × 4.0 = 1024. A 440 Hz string vibrates 440 times each second. Speed of sound in air about 340 m/s. 220, 440, 880 Hz are each an octave apart. Whisper about 30 dB, talking about 60 dB, road drill about 100 dB.
