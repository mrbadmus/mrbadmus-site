**Which line was followed.** NC C16 and Rainford 17a agree. Rainford's extra lessons 18c (making an indicator) and 19c (identify the pH) have no page of their own and are taught here.

**Gaps and mismatches.** The old page never showed how to make red cabbage indicator or how to find a pH step by step; both are added. The old page drew the fifteen-cell strip with pH 11 coloured dark blue while its bench called 11 to 14 "strongly alkaline"; the new drawn scale colours each band as the words name it. The bands are the same in C6 lessons 1 to 3: pH 0 to 2 red (strongly acidic), 3 to 6 orange or yellow (weakly acidic), 7 green (neutral), 8 to 10 blue (weakly alkaline), 11 to 14 purple (strongly alkaline).

**Checked numbers.** Universal indicator bands as above. Tooth enamel starts to dissolve below about pH 5.5. Red cabbage colours: red in strong acid, pink in weak acid, purple near neutral, blue, then green, then yellow as the solution gets more alkaline.

**Left out because unsure.** Exact pH values for each red cabbage colour (they vary with the cabbage and how it is made), so the figure labels the colours by acid, neutral and alkali only.
