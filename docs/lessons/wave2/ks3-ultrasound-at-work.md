**Which line was followed.** The Rainford scheme, row 24a, plus NC P34. The scheme cites P29 for 24a; P29 is water waves, so the NC line was matched by its words (P34 for the uses, P33 for the ranges).

**Gaps and mismatches.** The old page had nothing on infrasound. Rainford 24a needs the difference and the uses of both, so a reveal table compares them, with infrasound uses (detecting erupting volcanoes and far-off explosions; elephants and whales call each other with it). Earthquakes are left out on the commander's ruling: at GCSE they are detected by P and S waves through the ground.

**Checked numbers.** Rung 1: 0.060 ms = 0.00006 s; 5000 × 0.00006 = 0.30 m = 300 mm; ÷ 2 = 150 mm; halving twice gives 75 mm. Worked example: 0.040 ms = 0.00004 s; 5000 × 0.00004 = 0.2 m; ÷ 2 = 0.1 m = 100 mm. Cleaning baths about 40 000 Hz; scanners about 2 MHz to 15 MHz (old page; not used on the lean page).

**Left out because unsure.** The old page's frequency bands for physiotherapy and scanning are left out (not needed by the scheme or NC). "Unlike X-rays, ultrasound is not known to harm an unborn baby" (examiner's softer wording). Bats closing in "call much faster"; "higher" dropped, since many bats drop in pitch in the final buzz.
