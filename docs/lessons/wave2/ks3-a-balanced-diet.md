**Which line was followed.** NC B10 and Rainford 13a agree. Both are followed in full.

**Gaps and mismatches.** The old page had no "foods rich in each" list in its main text (only hints on the bench rows). The reveal table now gives foods for every nutrient. Deficiency diseases are named once here (scurvy, anaemia) and taught in full in when-diet-goes-wrong (Rainford 16b).

**Examiner fixes.** Fibre now gives "almost no energy" (lesson 8 teaches gut bacteria break some down; labels count 8 kJ/g), on the card and in Rung 1 feedback. "Protein can be respired for energy too" (blind pass). Vitamins and minerals are needed in "small" amounts; minerals are "several grams" a day. Start set-up: Plate B's eater "does not get that illness".

**Checked numbers.** Daily amounts for a 13-year-old, rounded: carbohydrate about 300 g, lipid about 70 g, protein about 45 g (UK reference about 42 g), fibre about 25 g (SACN 11 to 16 years: 25 g), water about 2 litres from food and drink, minerals a few grams in total, vitamins well under 1 g in total. One orange holds about 50 mg of vitamin C. 300 g ÷ 15 mg = 20 000.

**Left out because unsure.** Exact daily vitamin total (the old page said 0.2 g); the page says "less than 1 g", which is safe.

**Removed or moved.** Removed: the seven-band bench (`seven-bands`), the confrontation (`think-balanced`), the summary note. The "What each one is for / Without enough" comparison is now the reveal table (the "without enough" column moved to when-diet-goes-wrong). Moved to Dive deeper: vitamins A, D, E and K dissolve in fat; fats and oils are both lipids. Dropped: Takaki and beriberi.

**Easy mistakes rule.** DIET-01, DIET-02 and DIET-03 are tagged. One untagged pair: everyone needs protein, not only people who do sport.
