**Which line was followed.** NC C14 and Rainford 18b/19a agree. The scheme's content (fire triangle, putting out fires, complete and incomplete combustion) was followed. The old page had no fire triangle teaching beyond a reference block, so the triangle is now drawn and the five methods are a reveal table.

**Gaps and mismatches.** Rainford 19a (incomplete combustion) has no page of its own: it is taught here in the cards Complete combustion, Incomplete combustion, the reveal table and the Bunsen card. The old page's temperatures (about 1500 °C and 1000 °C) are dropped; the page says only that the blue flame is hotter.

**Quiz notes.**
- Rung 1 (stored): key D "Carbon dioxide and water" unchanged. The old set used "water" three times and "carbon dioxide" twice, so the key was the option most like the others. B "Carbon dioxide only" → "Carbon dioxide and soot"; C "Soot and water vapour" → "Soot and carbon monoxide". Now each of carbon dioxide, water, carbon monoxide and soot appears exactly twice. A is the longest.
- Rung 2 (stored): all four rewritten to one "X, so Y" frame, 15 words or fewer (A and B were 16 words). A "Turning the gas up makes the flame taller, so the colour follows the amount of fuel" → "The gas is running out, so the flame is dying down and yellow". B "A bigger, brighter flame is a hotter flame, so the yellow one is burning more fiercely" → "The flame is very hot, so it glows a bright yellow colour". Key C "Not enough air is getting in, so combustion is incomplete" → "Too little air is getting in, so the combustion is incomplete" (KEY TEXT CHANGED, same meaning). D "Yellow means the gas supply is contaminated, so the colour comes from the impurity" → "The gas is impure, so another substance is colouring the flame". D is the longest.
- Rungs 3 and 4 stems split into short sentences (render only). Ticks unchanged (no dashes).
- Feedback written for every wrong option of Rungs 1 and 2.

**Checked numbers.** None on the page.

**Left out because unsure.** Incomplete combustion often makes some carbon dioxide as well; the page follows the old page's equation (carbon monoxide + carbon + water) and does not say "only". Nothing now. The chip pan advice was ruled (UK fire service advice): see Examiner fixes.

**Examiner fixes.** Card "The fire triangle": "The candle has used up the oxygen in the jar." → "The oxygen in the jar runs low, so the flame goes out." Card "Complete combustion": "Methane is natural gas." → "Methane is the main gas in natural gas." Chip pan (ruled): card "When water is dangerous" no longer tells pupils to use a fire blanket on a pan fire; it says never throw water on a burning pan, do not try to put out a pan fire at home, turn off the heat if it is safe, then get out and call 999. The fire blanket stays in the reveal table as an example of cutting off oxygen. Both word equations now use C4's left-to-right `c4-word-row` art.

**Removed or moved.** Removed: the burner bench (`shut-hole-run`, with its prediction gate `gate-air-shut`), the three fuels cards (`three-fuels`), the confrontation (`think-commit-yellow`), the reference block (`three-things-a-fire-needs`, now the fire triangle card and reveal table), the summary note. Moved to Dive deeper: hydrogen as a fuel; charcoal. Cut: haemoglobin detail, where hydrogen is made.

**Easy mistakes rule.** REACT-10 and REACT-11 are tagged. Two untagged pairs: water puts out every fire; you would smell carbon monoxide.
