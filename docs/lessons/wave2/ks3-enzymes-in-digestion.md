**Which line was followed.** The scheme cites NC B13 and B14. B14 is gut bacteria, which this lesson does not teach (see `bacteria-in-the-gut`). B13 is cited. The scheme also asks how enzymes function, which goes past "simply as biological catalysts", so shape, temperature and pH are taught plainly. The words "active site" and "lock and key" are left for GCSE.

**Gaps and mismatches.** The old page had no figure ("the bench is the drawing"). Two phone-width figures are new: large molecules to small ones for each enzyme, and the three steps of one enzyme working and staying unchanged.

**Checked numbers.** Body temperature about 37 °C. Stomach about pH 2; mouth about pH 7; small intestine slightly alkaline (the old page said pH 8). Rung 2 uses 70 °C, well above the temperature where human amylase is denatured.

**Left out because unsure.** The exact temperature at which digestive enzymes are denatured. The old page said "above about 50 °C"; KS3 texts give 40 to 60 °C. The page says "too much heat" and no number.

**Removed or moved.** Removed: the enzyme bench (`the-bench`, enzyme-run instrument), the "three you must know" rule panel (now a reveal table), the confrontation (`two-wrong-ideas`), the key note, the convention note. Moved to Dive deeper: washing powder; pineapple and jelly (both from the old stretch explainer).

**Easy mistakes rule.** DIET-13 and DIET-14 tagged. Two untagged pairs: cold destroys enzymes; one enzyme digests any food.
