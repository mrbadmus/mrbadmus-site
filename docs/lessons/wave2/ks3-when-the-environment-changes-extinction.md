**Gaps and mismatches.** The old page taught the causes only through the bench and never defined extinction plainly or gave named examples. Now a definition card and a reveal table of six causes, each with a real example. "Endangered" added as a key word.

**Quiz notes.**
- Rung 2 (apply), all four options rewritten: dashes removed, each 15 words or fewer. Key B: "Yes — over 99% of all species that ever lived are extinct, mostly long before humans existed" → "Yes. Most species that have ever lived died out long before humans existed" (KEY TEXT CHANGED; the 99% figure was the only number).
- Rung 1 (recall) unchanged. Its key contains "one"; the examiner ruled this is not a tell.
- Rungs 3 and 4 stems rewritten in short sentences (render only); nine ticks shortened. Feedback written for every wrong option of Rungs 1 and 2.

**Checked numbers.** Five mass extinctions. Kakapo fell to 51 birds (1995); New Zealand's only native land mammals are bats. Dodo: found about 1598, gone within about 100 years, hunted by sailors and its eggs eaten by their rats and pigs. Hazel dormouse: usually one litter a year. Brown rat: up to five litters a year. Giant panda: one cub every two years at most; it is now Vulnerable, not Endangered (IUCN 2016), so the page names the tiger and elephants as endangered.

**Left out because unsure.** "Current extinction rates are tens to hundreds of times the background rate" (the page says "much faster than the natural rate"). "Over 99% of species are extinct" (page says "most").

**Removed or moved.** Removed: the pressure bench (`who-survives-what`), the risk band (now a card), the confrontation `extinction-is-natural-and-permanent`, the key note, the convention note. Moved to Dive deeper: the kakapo.

**Easy mistakes rule.** EVOL-05 and EVOL-06 tagged. Two untagged pairs: big animals are safe; endangered means extinct.
