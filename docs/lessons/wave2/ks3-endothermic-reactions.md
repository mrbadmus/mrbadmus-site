**Which line was followed.** NC C21 and Rainford 18a agree. The scheme has one lesson (18a) for both kinds; the site has this page for the endothermic half.

**Gaps and mismatches.** Rainford 19c (investigating exo and endothermic reactions) has no page of its own. The comparison is here, and the practical (method, results table, fair test) is on measuring-a-temperature-change. NC C21 says qualitative, so the page has no bond energies. "Surroundings" has the same definition as on exothermic-reactions. The old page said thermal decomposition, photosynthesis, melting and "many dissolvings" are endothermic; the page names ammonium nitrate only and does not claim most dissolving is endothermic.

**Examiner fixes.** Key word thermal decomposition: "Breaking a substance down by heating it. It takes in energy." → "Using heat to break one compound into two or more substances. It takes in energy." (matches C5 thermal-decomposition).

**Checked numbers.** Citric acid and sodium hydrogencarbonate 20 °C to 12 °C (fall of 8 °C): the old page's value, ruled in the 28 Aug 2026 chemistry audit, and the same on all three C7 pages. Instant cold pack: close to 0 °C.

**Left out because unsure.** The barium hydroxide and ammonium chloride demonstration (freezing a beaker to a wet board) is not used. Whether most dissolving is endothermic: not claimed.
