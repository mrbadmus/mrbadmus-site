**Which line was followed.** Rainford 21a and NC P29 agree on transverse water waves. Rainford 21a also asks for frequency, which the old page left out on purpose (it kept frequency for frequency-pitch-and-loudness). The scheme is followed: frequency is defined here as the number of waves passing a point each second, in hertz, with no calculation.

**Gaps and mismatches.** The page says "rest line" for the level of still water; the old page and Rung 1 said "still level". Rung 1's stem now says "rest line" too, so the page uses one word. The flashcard vocabulary still says "still level" (vocabulary is not edited). Longitudinal waves get one reveal row here; sound-is-longitudinal teaches them.

**Checked numbers.** Rung 1: crests 0.60 m apart, float 0.05 m above and below the rest line, so wavelength 0.60 m, amplitude 0.05 m, crest to trough 0.10 m. A float bobbing 3 times a second rides a 3 Hz wave. Tsunami: amplitude under a metre at sea, wavelength around 200 km (old page's figures).

**Left out because unsure.** The old page's "roughly 1 in 7" breaking steepness (it varies with depth and wind, and no scheme line needs it).
