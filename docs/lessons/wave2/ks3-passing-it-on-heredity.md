**Which line was followed (scheme over NC).** The Rainford scheme, rows 14b and 15c. NC B33 only asks for heredity as the passing on of genetic information. The scheme also asks for dominant and recessive characteristics and for calculating the probability of inheriting one, so both are taught: dominant and recessive in a card and a reveal table, and probability in two worked examples in CFIFA order on drawn Punnett grids.

**Gaps and mismatches.** The old page never used the words dominant or recessive, never said "mother and father", and never calculated a probability. All three are added. A second cross (Pp x pp, 1 in 2) gives Rainford 15c "More genetic crosses" a drawn example. The page says "version of a gene"; "allele" is named once, in Dive deeper.

**Quiz notes.**
- Rung 1 (stored) options rewritten without dashes; key D stays D. Old "23 — because half the chromosomes are lost when the sperm is made" was right number with an arguable reason; it is now "23, as each chromosome is cut in half to make the sperm", which is definitely wrong. Key "23 — one from each pair, so the fertilised egg has 46 again" is now "23, one from each pair, so the fertilised egg has 46", and option B is longer.
- Rung 2 (stored) options: A, B and C lengthened so the key (D, unchanged) is not the longest. B no longer says "the white parents" (the parents were purple).
- Rung 3 and Rung 4 stems split into short sentences (render only); six ticks shortened and their dashes removed.
- Feedback written for every wrong option of Rungs 1 and 2.

**Examiner fixes.** GENE-07 wrong line is now about pea plants (in humans a tall and a short parent can have a medium child, as height is many genes). "Two copies of most genes". "In a small batch of seeds". The reused Punnett figure now says "P is dominant over p" (was "P beats p") and its bracket labels lost their dashes.

**Checked numbers.** Pp x Pp: squares PP, Pp, Pp, pp; white 1/4 = 0.25 = 25% = 1 in 4; purple 3/4 = 75%. Pp x pp: squares Pp, pp, Pp, pp; white 2/4 = 1/2 = 0.5 = 50% = 1 in 2. The new drawer counts the squares itself and prints the fraction, percentage and "1 in" from that count. 23 + 23 = 46. Mendel published 1866, rediscovered 1900.

**Left out because unsure.** Human examples of dominant and recessive characteristics (most textbook ones, such as tongue rolling, are not single-gene). The page uses pea flower colour only, as the old page did; the Rung 4 dog coat is the scheme's transfer task.

**Removed or moved.** Removed: the pea-cross bench (`grow-seeds-and-count-them`), the confrontation (`blending-and-the-skipped-generation`), the "Halved, combined, then copied" rule cards (now the From parents to child card) and the summary note. Reused: the drawn Pp x Pp grid, now inside the first worked example. New: a drawn Pp x pp grid for the second. Moved to Dive deeper: the word allele (named once); Gregor Mendel.

**Easy mistakes rule.** GENE-07 and GENE-08 are tagged on the Easy mistakes card. Two untagged pairs added (1 in 4 is not exact; dominant does not mean common).
