**Unsure (left out until checked).**
- The spec names no apparatus or method. The record uses the standard method: ray box with a single slit, plane mirror, rectangular glass block, a clear rectangular plastic box of water as the second substance, crosses far apart, protractor from the normal. I believe this matches AQA's practical handbook, but did not see it in the spec text.
- Second substance: water in a thin clear plastic box; the Apparatus card also names an acrylic (Perspex) block (examiner, 14 Nov 2026), because AQA questions on this practical often use one. The made-up results stay glass against water. Acrylic (n ≈ 1.49) is too close to glass (n ≈ 1.5) for a pupil to see a difference; water (n ≈ 1.33) is not. The box walls are thin and parallel, so they do not change the ray's direction inside the water.
- Made-up results: computed from n(glass) = 1.5 and n(water) = 1.33 (sin r = sin i ÷ n), rounded to the nearest degree. Snell's law itself is not taught (not GCSE).
- "Different substances refract light by different amounts", and "glass bends the light more than water" as the reading of the example data: a result of the practical, not spec wording.
