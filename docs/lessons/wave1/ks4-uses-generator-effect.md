Rainford outcomes "how the output of alternators and dynamos can be increased" and "the period and peak output voltage from an oscilloscope trace" are covered by the reveal table and the graph card (peak p.d., time for one turn, period).

**Gaps and mismatches.** The old page had no p.d.–time graph and no graph interpretation, which the spec requires. It never said that turning faster also shortens the time for one cycle.

**Removed or moved.** Removed: back-emf in motors (not on the AQA spec), rotating-magnet generators, wind turbine gearboxes, hydroelectric efficiency figures, steam turbines, capacitor smoothing, and "bicycle dynamos" (many bicycle dynamos generate a.c., so the example misleads). Removed: "50 revolutions per second (3000 rpm)" (true only for a two-pole machine, and not on the spec). Removed: the matching activity. Moved to Dive deeper: power station generators are alternators; the link between a dynamo and a d.c. motor. Quiz: Q1 "Why does an alternator produce AC while a dynamo produces DC?": **question wording kept exactly, so its quiz ID is unchanged**; marked "fixed" because the options and feedback were rewritten (the credited option was 173 characters against at most 93, every option had dash tails, and the feedback said "emf"). Options now read "Alternator: slip rings. Dynamo: split-ring commutator" against three same-shape distractors. Q2 "What is 'back-emf' in an electric motor, and why is it important?" removed, ID dropped: back-emf is not on the spec and its right option ("making the motor efficient") was arguable. Replaced with a graph question, "What does the highest point on an alternator's p.d.–time graph show?" (the peak p.d.); a draft question on turning the coil faster was dropped because it repeated Rung 2 Q1. Q3, Q4 and Q5: new. The graph card now also says "The time for one cycle is the period" (Rainford outcome). Rung 4's dynamo point now says the graph "never goes below zero" (it touches zero twice a turn).
