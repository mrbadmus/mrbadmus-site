**Audit of the ladder and practice set (faults found and fixed).**
- Rung 2 item 2 (why copper compounds are coloured): credited option the longest (73 against 23 and 30). Key now "Copper is a transition metal"; same index credited.
- Rung 4 reject "Zinc is a typical transition metal": zinc is not one of the spec's six and its status is not settled at GCSE, so it cannot be offered as plainly wrong. Replaced by "Transition metals react vigorously with water."
- Practice Q1 (structural materials): credited option 149 characters, capitals and dashes. Now "They are strong, hard and react slowly with water"; distractors are cost, a false radioactivity claim and "lighter". Stem kept.
- Practice Q2 (Haber catalyst): credited option the longest (83). Options now single metals (iron, copper, platinum, nickel); stem and key kept.
- Practice Q3 (copper sulfate blue): credited option the longest (75). Now "Copper is a transition metal"; stem kept.
- Practice Q4 (FeCl₂ and FeCl₃): credited option the longest (57). Now "They form ions with different charges"; stem kept.
- Practice Q5 (iron in chlorine): credited option the longest (67). Now "It reacts less vigorously"; stem kept.
- Rungs otherwise checked on both routes; each chain has one valid order.

**Calculations (recomputed).** None to calculate. Data checked: melting points iron 1538 °C, sodium 98 °C; densities iron 7.9 g/cm³ (7.87), sodium 0.97 g/cm³, less than water's 1.00, so it floats.

**Unsure (left out until checked).**
- Iron in chlorine: hot iron wool does react strongly (it glows); "less vigorously than sodium" is the GCSE comparison the Design page made and is kept, but an examiner should confirm the wording.
- Iron(III) colour written "orange-brown" (as the Triple metal-hydroxides lesson writes Fe(OH)₃); solutions of iron(III) salts are often called yellow-brown. "Group 1 ions do not form coloured compounds": true of the ion; a Group 1 compound with a coloured anion (potassium manganate(VII)) is coloured because of the manganese, which the figure shows.
- Haber process: 8462 4.10.4.1 names an iron catalyst, so the example is on the Triple spec, but the pupil meets it later in the course.
