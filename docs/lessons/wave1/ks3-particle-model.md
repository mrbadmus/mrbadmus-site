**Gaps and mismatches.** Rainford has no lesson called "the particle model"; its Chem 1 starts at 5a Solids, liquids and gases. The site page is the groundwork for 5a.

**Question audit fixes (docs/b2c/QUESTION-AUDIT.md).** None listed for this lesson. Bank `c1-01-s01` (different sizes), `s02` (full gaps would add to 100 ml), `s03` (squashing shrinks the empty space) and `s04` (the last pieces are a few particles across) each have a card line.

**Quiz notes.** Rung 2 stem rewritten (render only): the stored stem asks "Why can they not make the twenty-fifth cut?", but the key says they can cut it. The new stem asks why they can never get a smaller piece of sugar. Rung 3 and 4 stems split into sentences of 15 words or fewer. Five ticks rewritten: two had spaced dashes, and Rung 4 tick 5 said each gap is "millions of times thinner than a hair". That is wrong: a gap between air particles is a few nanometres, tens of thousands of times thinner than a hair. It now says each gap is empty space, far too small to see. Feedback written for every wrong option of Rungs 1 and 2.

**Checked numbers.** 50 ml water + 50 ml ethanol gives about 96 to 97 ml. A 1 cm cube halved 24 times gives 0.6 nm, about one sucrose molecule (roughly 1 nm). A hair (about 0.1 mm) is about 100 000 times wider than 1 nm.
