Waves and sound · Model

Transverse waves, reflection and superposition

Start here

Drop two stones into still water at the same moment, a metre apart. Two sets of rings spread out and run into each other.

If you had to guess, where two ripples meet, do they add together, or bounce off each other?

Yes. Where they overlap, their heights add. Two lifts make a bigger lift.

Ripples pass through each other. Where they overlap, their heights add.

Key words

  • A wave where the material moves at right angles to the direction of travel.

  • How far the surface is above or below the rest line at one point.

  • A wave bouncing back off a barrier.

  • Adding the displacements of two waves where they overlap.

Key knowledge

In a transverse wave, the material moves at right angles to the direction of travel.

On water, the surface moves up and down. The wave moves across.

A rope shaken up and down carries a transverse wave too.

Light is also a transverse wave.

Displacement is how far the surface is from the rest line at one point.

Above the rest line counts as up. Below it counts as down.

At a crest, the displacement is the same as the amplitude.

A wave that hits a barrier bounces back. This is reflection.

The reflected wave travels back the other way.

It keeps the same wavelength.

Where two waves overlap, their displacements add. This is superposition.

Crest on crest makes a bigger crest. The waves add.

Crest on trough of the same size makes flat water. The waves cancel.

After they pass, both waves carry on as they were.

Two ripples meet crest on crest. One is 8 mm high and the other is 5 mm. Find the height of the water where they meet.

  • Convert: nothing. Both heights are in millimetres.
  • Formula: height where they meet = first height + second height
  • Insert: height = 8 mm + 5 mm
  • Fine-tune: 8 + 5 = 13
  • Answer: height = 13 mm

For crest on trough, take the smaller height away from the bigger one.

A crest 1.2 cm high meets a crest 7 mm high. Find the height where they meet.

  • Convert: 1.2 cm × 10 = 12 mm
  • Formula: height where they meet = first height + second height
  • Insert: height = 12 mm + 7 mm
  • Fine-tune: 12 + 7 = 19
  • Answer: height = 19 mm

Add 1.2 to 7 without converting, and the answer is wrong.

Add or cancel

RowWorkingWhere they meet
Crest 5 mm on crest 3 mm
Crest 5 mm on trough 5 mm
Crest 7 mm on trough 2 mm

  • Wrong: When two waves cancel, they destroy each other.

    Right: Both waves carry on past the overlap, unchanged.

  • Wrong: If the water is flat, the energy has gone.

    Right: The energy is still there. It carries on with both waves.

  • Wrong: Two waves meeting take the average of their heights.

    Right: Their displacements add. 8 mm on 5 mm makes 13 mm.

  • Wrong: The stronger wave wins, and the weaker one disappears.

    Right: Both waves add where they meet. Then both carry on.

  • Wrong: A 1.2 cm crest on a 7 mm crest makes 8.2 mm.

    Right: Change both to millimetres first. 12 mm + 7 mm = 19 mm.

Key fact

Where two waves overlap, their displacements add, so crest on crest makes a bigger crest. A crest on an equal trough cancels it, and both waves then carry on.

Rung 1

Rung 2

Two wave trains of amplitude 7 mm meet exactly crest on trough. A student says the two waves have destroyed each other. Which statement is right?

Rung 3

Two stones are dropped into a pond a metre apart at the same moment. Explain why some patches of the crossing pattern heave twice as far as either ripple on its own while other patches stay almost flat.

Rung 4

Two people send pulses along a rope from opposite ends. One sends a crest 3 cm high. The other sends a trough 3 cm deep. Explain what the rope looks like as they meet, and just after. Then say what changes if both send crests.

Superposition works for sound waves too.

Noise-cancelling headphones play a sound that cancels the noise outside.

Where the two waves meet, they add up to almost nothing.

Send waves down a tank with a wall at the far end.

The reflected waves run back through the waves still coming in.

Where they overlap, some points stay still and others move a lot.
