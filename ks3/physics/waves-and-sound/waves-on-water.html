Waves on water: what a wave is

Start here

Waves cross a pond for a whole minute. A gull sitting on the water ends up where it started.

If you had to guess, what crossed the pond: the water itself, or only its up-and-down movement?

The water stays put, just like the gull. Each patch only lifts and drops on the spot.

Yes. Each patch of water lifts and drops, then hands the movement on to the next.

Key words

  • The height of a crest above the rest line.

  • The distance from one crest to the next crest.

  • The number of waves passing a point each second, measured in hertz (Hz).

  • A wave where the material moves at right angles to the direction of travel.

Key knowledge

A wave is a disturbance that travels.

It carries energy from one place to another.

It does not carry the material with it.

On a pond, each patch of water moves up and down on the spot.

It passes the movement on to the next patch.

So the wave crosses the pond, and the water stays where it is.

The water moves up and down. The wave travels across the surface.

These two directions are at right angles.

A wave like this is called a transverse wave.

  • Crest: the highest point of the wave.
  • Trough: the lowest point of the wave.
  • Rest line: where the surface sits when the water is still.

The amplitude is the height of a crest above the rest line.

A trough is the same distance below the rest line.

So crest to trough is twice the amplitude.

The wavelength is the distance from one crest to the next crest.

Trough to trough gives the same wavelength.

Frequency is the number of waves passing a point each second.

It is measured in hertz, Hz.

1 Hz means one wave passes each second.

A float bobbing up and down 3 times a second rides a 3 Hz wave.

A wave can be tall, with its crests close together.

A wave can be low, with its crests far apart.

Two types of wave

Longitudinal

  • Wrong: The water travels along with the wave.

    Right: The water moves up and down on the spot. Only the energy travels across.

  • Wrong: A bigger wave is a longer wave.

    Right: Height is the amplitude and length is the wavelength. They change separately.

  • Wrong: The amplitude runs from the trough up to the crest.

    Right: The amplitude runs from the rest line up to the crest. That is half as far.

  • Wrong: If the water only goes up and down, the wave is standing still.

    Right: The wave travels across the pond while each patch of water moves up and down.

  • Wrong: The wavelength runs from a crest to the next trough.

    Right: The wavelength runs from a crest to the next crest.

Key fact

A wave transfers energy from place to place without moving the material along. Water waves are transverse: the water moves at right angles to the direction of travel.

Rung 1

Rung 4

A crowd in a stadium sends a wave right round the ground in 40 seconds, and nobody leaves their seat. Say what plays the part of the amplitude and what plays the part of the wavelength, then give one way this is not like a wave on water.

Out at sea, a tsunami can have an amplitude of under a metre.

Its wavelength can be around 200 km.

Ships ride over it and hardly notice.

Near the coast the water gets shallow and the wave slows down.

The same energy then piles up into a very tall wave.

Water in a wave does not move exactly straight up and down.

Each patch moves round a small circle, then ends up where it started.

Up and down is close enough to show what transverse means.
