Mass vs weight

Start here

An astronaut takes a hammer to the Moon, where gravity is weaker.

If you had to guess, is the hammer easier to swing on the Moon, or just as hard?

Key words

  • The amount of matter in an object. It is measured in kilograms, kg.

  • The force of gravity on an object. It is measured in newtons, N.

  • The pull of gravity on each kilogram. It is measured in N/kg.

  • The unit of force, written N. Weight is a force, so it is in newtons.

Key knowledge

Mass is the amount of matter in an object.

Mass is measured in kilograms, kg.

Weight is the force of gravity on an object.

Weight is a force, so it is measured in newtons, N.

In everyday speech, weight often means mass. In science they are different.

Gravitational field strength, g, is the pull of gravity on each kilogram.

It is measured in newtons per kilogram, N/kg.

On Earth, g is about 10 N/kg.

So 1 kg of matter weighs about 10 N on Earth.

Other places have a different g.

weight (W) = mass (m) × gravitational field strength (g)

Mass goes in as kilograms. Field strength goes in as N/kg.

Weight comes out in newtons.

To find the mass, divide: m = W ÷ g.

A tin of paint has a mass of 750 g. Find its weight on Earth.

  • Convert: 750 g ÷ 1000 = 0.75 kg
  • Formula: weight (W) = mass (m) × gravitational field strength (g)
  • Insert: W = 0.75 kg × 10 N/kg
  • Fine-tune: 0.75 × 10 = 7.5
  • Answer: W = 7.5 N

Put in 750 instead of 0.75, and the answer is a thousand times too big.

A spring balance on the Moon reads 32 N. The Moon's g is 1.6 N/kg. Find the mass.

  • Convert: nothing. N and N/kg are the right units already.
  • Formula: mass (m) = weight (W) ÷ gravitational field strength (g)
  • Insert: m = 32 N ÷ 1.6 N/kg
  • Fine-tune: 32 ÷ 1.6 = 20
  • Answer: m = 20 kg

The mass is 20 kg on Earth too.

Mass is the same everywhere.

A 60 kg astronaut has a mass of 60 kg on the Moon.

Weight changes with g. On Earth the astronaut weighs 600 N.

On the Moon, g is 1.6 N/kg, so the weight is 96 N.

The astronaut has the same matter, and the Moon pulls less.

Mass also shows how hard an object is to start or stop moving.

A hammer on the Moon is easier to lift, because its weight is smaller.

It is just as hard to swing. Swinging means starting and stopping it.

That depends on mass, and the mass has not changed.

A newtonmeter, or spring balance, measures weight in newtons.

The spring stretches, and the stretch shows the force.

A pan balance compares your object with known masses.

Gravity pulls on both sides equally, so the answer does not change.

A pan balance gives the same mass on the Moon.

A spring balance gives a smaller reading on the Moon.

  • Wrong: Astronauts float in orbit, so they have no mass.

    Right: They keep all their mass. A floating crate is still hard to stop.

  • Wrong: Weight is mass in different units, like metres and feet.

    Right: Mass is matter, in kg. Weight is a force, in N.

  • Wrong: On the Moon a hammer is easier to swing as well as to lift.

    Right: Only lifting is easier. Swinging depends on mass, which is unchanged.

  • Wrong: A spring balance reads mass, so it reads the same anywhere.

    Right: A spring balance reads weight, so its reading changes with g.

  • Wrong: Put 750 g straight into W = m × g.

    Right: Change grams to kilograms first.

Key fact

Mass is the amount of matter in an object. It is measured in kg and never changes. Weight is the force of gravity on that mass, in N. Weight = mass × g, and on Earth g is about 10 N/kg.
