Energy at home · Quantitative

Reading a fuel bill

Start here

An electricity bill charges for each unit of electricity you use.

If you had to guess, does a unit measure how fast, or how much?

How much. A unit is a kilowatt-hour, which is an amount of energy.

How much. A unit is a kilowatt-hour, which is an amount of energy.

Key words

  • On an electricity bill, one unit is one kilowatt-hour of energy.

  • The energy a 1 kW appliance transfers in 1 hour.

  • What the supplier charges for each kilowatt-hour you use.

  • A fixed amount paid each day for being connected. It does not depend on use.

Key knowledge

Bills charge for energy, not power.

One unit is one kilowatt-hour, kWh.

That is the energy a 1 kW appliance transfers in 1 hour.

So a 2 kW heater uses one unit in half an hour.

The meter counts the units you use.

Units used = new meter reading − old meter reading

For example, the old reading is 45 210. The new reading is 45 622.

Units used = 45 622 − 45 210 = 412 units.

cost = number of units × price per unit

Prices change, so the prices here are only examples.

A 2 kW heater runs for 3 hours. Each unit costs 27p.

  • Energy: 2 kW × 3 h = 6 kWh
  • Cost: 6 × 27p = 162p
  • Answer: £1.62

Most bills also have a standing charge.

It pays for the cables, meters and network.

You pay it every day, even if you use nothing.

So switching everything off does not make the bill zero.

A home uses 300 units in 30 days, at 27p a unit.

The standing charge is 50p a day.

  • Units: 300 × 27p = £81.00
  • Standing charge: 30 × 50p = £15.00
  • Total: £81.00 + £15.00 = £96.00

Multiply in each row first. Then add the rows.

Halving the units would not halve the bill. The standing charge stays the same.

  • Switch appliances off at the wall, not on standby.
  • Use LED lamps instead of old filament bulbs.
  • Insulate the loft and walls, and fit double glazing.
  • Turn the heating thermostat down by 1 °C.
  • Take shorter showers.
  • Wash clothes at a lower temperature.
  • Choose appliances with a good energy rating.

Appliances that heat things use the most energy, such as showers and ovens.

Lights use very little energy.

  • Wrong: A kilowatt-hour is a unit of power.

    Right: It is a unit of energy. Power is measured in kilowatts.

  • Wrong: Switch everything off and the bill is zero.

    Right: The standing charge is still paid every day.

  • Wrong: 45 minutes is 0.45 hours.

    Right: 45 minutes is 45 ÷ 60 = 0.75 hours.

Key fact

A unit on a bill is one kilowatt-hour of energy. Cost = number of units × price per unit. The standing charge is paid every day, whatever you use.

Cables, substations and meters cost money, even if you use nothing.
