  • How fast energy is transferred. It is measured in watts, W.

  • 1000 watts. Its symbol is kW.

  • A low-power mode. The appliance is not doing its job, but still transfers energy.

Key knowledge

Power is how fast energy is transferred.

It is measured in watts, W.

1 watt means 1 joule of energy transferred every second.

1 kilowatt, kW, is 1000 W.

So a 2000 W kettle transfers 2000 J every second.

power = energy transferred ÷ time

Power is in watts, W. Energy is in joules, J. Time is in seconds, s.

So energy transferred = power × time.

You may see this written as P = W ÷ t.

There, W stands for work done. Work done is the energy transferred.

Power depends on two things:

  • how much energy is transferred
  • how long it takes

More energy in the same time means more power.

The same energy in less time also means more power.

A motor transfers 9000 J in 30 s. Find its power.

  • Convert: joules and seconds already, so nothing changes.
  • Formula: power = energy ÷ time
  • Insert: power = 9000 J ÷ 30 s
  • Fine-tune: 9000 ÷ 30 = 300
  • Answer: 300 W

A kettle transfers 540 000 J in 3 minutes. Find its power.

  • Convert: 3 minutes × 60 = 180 s
  • Formula: power = energy ÷ time
  • Insert: power = 540 000 J ÷ 180 s
  • Fine-tune: 540 000 ÷ 180 = 3000
  • Answer: 3000 W, which is 3 kW

Always change minutes into seconds first.

Power tells you how fast. Energy tells you how much.

To find the energy, you also need the time.

The kettle has far more power than the router.

But the router is on far longer, so it uses more energy in a day.

More power means a job is done faster, such as boiling water.

The energy used is what you pay for on the bill.

A 1000 W kettle takes about twice as long to boil the same water.

So it transfers about the same energy as a 2000 W kettle.

It may waste slightly more, as it heats the kitchen for longer.

A lower rating saves energy only if the job takes no longer.

An LED lamp gives the same light as an old bulb, using fewer watts.

An appliance on standby is not fully off.

It still transfers a little energy every second.

Over a whole year, this adds up.

Switch it off at the wall to stop it.

  • Wrong: A watt is an amount of energy.

    Right: A watt is a rate. It is 1 joule every second.

  • Wrong: A higher power always means a bigger bill.

    Right: The bill depends on the power and the time it is on.

  • Wrong: Standby is the same as off.

    Right: Standby still uses some energy, all the time.

James Watt sold steam engines in the late 1700s.

His buyers were used to working with horses.

So he compared his engines with horses, using a unit called horsepower.

One horsepower is about 750 W. The watt is named after him.

At GCSE, you will also work out electrical power.

You will use the current and the potential difference.
