Moments: the turning effect

Start here

You push a door at the handle. Then you push just as hard by the hinge.

If you had to guess, does the door swing open just as easily near the hinge?

No, it hardly moves. Where you push matters, not just how hard.

No, it hardly moves. Where you push matters, not just how hard.

Key words

  • The point an object turns about, such as a hinge.

  • The turning effect of a force. Moment = force × distance from the pivot.

  • The unit of a moment, written N m.

  • An object is balanced when the clockwise moment equals the anticlockwise moment.

Key knowledge

A force can turn an object about a pivot.

A door turns about its hinges. A spanner turns about the nut.

The turning effect of a force is called its moment.

A bigger force gives a bigger moment.

A force further from the pivot gives a bigger moment.

moment (N m) = force (N) × distance from the pivot (m)

Measure the distance from the pivot, at right angles to the force.

Force is in newtons and distance is in metres.

So the unit of a moment is the newton metre, N m.

A moment is a force times a distance. It is not a force.

A force of 50 N acts 25 cm from a pivot. Find the moment.

  • Convert: 25 cm ÷ 100 = 0.25 m
  • Formula: moment = force × distance
  • Insert: moment = 50 N × 0.25 m
  • Fine-tune: 50 × 0.25 = 12.5
  • Answer: moment = 12.5 N m

Put in 25 instead of 0.25, and the answer is a hundred times too big.

A nut needs a moment of 60 N m. You pull with 150 N. Find how far from the nut you must pull.

  • Convert: nothing. N m and N are the right units.
  • Formula: distance = moment ÷ force
  • Insert: distance = 60 N m ÷ 150 N
  • Fine-tune: 60 ÷ 150 = 0.4
  • Answer: distance = 0.4 m

The same force gives a bigger moment further from the pivot.

A 50 N pull on a 0.10 m spanner gives 5 N m.

The same pull on a 0.40 m spanner gives 20 N m.

The pull is the same. The longer spanner gives four times the moment.

A door handle is fitted far from the hinges. A small push then turns the door.

A seesaw balances when the turning effects match.

The anticlockwise moment is 300 N × 2 m = 600 N m.

The clockwise moment is 400 N × 1.5 m = 600 N m.

The moments are equal, so the seesaw balances.

This is the principle of moments.

If an object is balanced, the clockwise moment equals the anticlockwise moment.

Ignore the weight of the seesaw itself.

This seesaw balances. Find how far from the pivot the 400 N child sits.

  • Convert: nothing. Newtons and metres are the right units.
  • Formula: clockwise moment = anticlockwise moment
  • Insert: 400 N × distance = 500 N × 1.2 m
  • Fine-tune: 500 × 1.2 = 600, and 600 ÷ 400 = 1.5
  • Answer: distance = 1.5 m

  • Wrong: A longer spanner means you are pulling harder.

    Right: The pull is the same. The longer spanner gives a bigger moment.

  • Wrong: Measure the distance from where you are standing.

    Right: Measure the distance from the pivot.

  • Wrong: A moment is a force, so it is in newtons.

    Right: A moment is in newton metres, N m.

  • Wrong: The distance only decides which way it turns.

    Right: The distance also decides how big the moment is. Double it, double the moment.

  • Wrong: Put 25 cm straight into the equation.

    Right: Change centimetres to metres first.

Key fact

A moment is the turning effect of a force about a pivot. Moment = force × distance from the pivot, in newton metres. An object is balanced when the clockwise moment equals the anticlockwise moment.
