Magnetism and electromagnetism · Investigation

Magnetic fields

Start here

Iron filings are sprinkled on paper over a bar magnet. They form curved lines.

If you had to guess, is the magnet's pull there before the filings arrive, or do the filings make it?

Yes. The field was there first. Each filing lines up with it.

Sweep the filings away and the pull is still there. They only show it up.

Key words

  • The space around a magnet where a magnetic material or another magnet feels a force.

  • A line showing which way a compass's N end points. Outside a magnet, it runs from N to S.

  • A small compass used to find the direction of a magnetic field at one point.

Key knowledge

A magnet has a magnetic field around it.

The field is the space where a magnetic material or another magnet feels a force.

The field is there even when nothing is in it.

It is strongest at the poles.

Iron filings line up with the field, so they show its shape.

Field lines show the shape and direction of a magnetic field.

Outside the magnet, the lines go from the north pole to the south pole.

Each line has an arrow pointing from N to S.

The lines are closest together at the poles, where the field is strongest.

Field lines never cross.

  • Put the magnet on paper and draw round it.
  • Put a plotting compass near the N pole.
  • Mark a dot where the needle's N end points.
  • Move the compass so its S end is over that dot. Mark again.
  • Keep going until you reach the S pole.
  • Join the dots into a smooth line, with an arrow from N to S.
  • Start again from other points near the N pole.

Each dot records the direction of the field at that point.

  • Wrong: There is no field between the lines.

    Right: The field fills all the space. The lines are only a drawing of it.

  • Wrong: The iron filings make the field.

    Right: The field is there first. The filings only show its shape.

  • Wrong: Field lines can cross where the field is strong.

    Right: Field lines never cross. A compass can only point one way.

  • Wrong: The magnet makes extra lines at its ends.

    Right: The same lines crowd closer together at the poles, where the field is strong.

  • Wrong: Field lines go from S to N.

    Right: Outside the magnet, field lines go from N to S.

Key fact

A magnetic field is the space around a magnet where magnetic materials feel a force. Field lines go from the north pole to the south pole. They are closest together where the field is strongest, and they never cross.

Rungs 3 and 4 you mark yourself.

Rung 1

On a field map of one bar magnet, the lines near the ends are packed close together and the lines out at the sides are far apart. What does that tell you?

Rung 2

A student draws a field map in which two lines meet and cross at a point. Why must the drawing be wrong?

Rung 3

Explain how you would plot the field around a bar magnet with a plotting compass and a pencil. Say what each pencil mark records.

Rung 4

Two bar magnets lie end to end with their N poles facing. Iron filings are scattered over them. A small bare patch is left half way between the magnets. Explain what is happening there. Say what a compass on that patch would do.

The lines from each N pole turn away from the gap.

Half way between the magnets, the two fields are equal and opposite.

They cancel, so the field there is zero. This is a neutral point.

The pulls of the two magnets on a compass there cancel.

Iron filings leave a bare patch there.

The field goes all the way round the magnet.

It is above and below the paper too.

Filings on paper only show one flat slice of it.
