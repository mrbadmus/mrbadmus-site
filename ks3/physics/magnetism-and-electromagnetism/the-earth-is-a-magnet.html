Magnetism and electromagnetism · System

The Earth is a magnet

Start here

A compass sits on a table, far from any magnet or metal. Spin it, and it always settles pointing the same way.

If you had to guess, is whatever turns the needle near the surface, or deep inside the Earth?

It is deep inside. The Earth's field is made in its core, far below you.

Yes. Moving liquid iron in the core makes a field that covers the whole Earth.

Key words

  • A small magnet that can turn freely. Its N end points north.

  • The direction of the geographic North Pole, at the top of the Earth's spin axis.

  • The direction a compass points. It is close to true north, and it moves slowly.

Key knowledge

A compass needle is a small magnet that can turn freely.

A magnet turns until it lines up with the magnetic field around it.

Far from any other magnet, a compass still points the same way.

So the Earth must have a magnetic field.

The needle lines up with the Earth's field, so its N end points north.

The Earth's field has the same shape as the field of a bar magnet.

It is as if a giant bar magnet were inside the Earth.

There is no real bar magnet. The core is too hot to stay magnetised.

The field is made by moving liquid iron in the Earth's core.

The N end of a compass needle points towards the Arctic.

Unlike poles attract.

So the magnetic pole near the geographic North Pole is a magnetic south pole.

That is why the magnet in the diagram has its S end at the top.

The ends of a needle were named for where they point.

A map is drawn with north at the top.

A compass shows magnetic north, which is a little different.

The angle between magnetic north and true north is called the declination.

It is different in different places.

It changes as the magnetic pole moves.

So a map prints the declination and the year it was measured.

Walkers use it to correct their compass bearings.

  • Wrong: The Earth's North Pole is a magnetic north pole.

    Right: There is a magnetic south pole near it. That is why a compass N end points there.

  • Wrong: A compass points exactly at the North Pole.

    Right: It points to magnetic north, which is some way from the North Pole.

  • Wrong: There is a bar of iron inside the Earth.

    Right: Moving liquid iron in the core makes the field. There is no bar magnet.

  • Wrong: A compass works best at the magnetic pole.

    Right: It works badly there, because the field points almost straight down.

  • Wrong: A compass needle is pulled north by gravity.

    Right: The needle is a magnet. It lines up with the Earth's magnetic field.

Key fact

A compass needle is a small magnet that lines up with the Earth's magnetic field. Its N end points north. So the magnetic pole near the geographic North Pole is a magnetic south pole.

Rung 4

Pilots on polar routes are trained not to trust a magnetic compass near the magnetic pole. Explain what goes wrong with a compass there. Suggest what they could use instead.

Near a magnetic pole, the Earth's field points steeply into the ground.

A compass needle can only turn flat, so it hardly settles.

Polar pilots and explorers use satellite navigation instead.

Magnetic north has moved more than 1000 km since 1900.

Over very long times, the Earth's field has flipped many times.

Rocks on the sea floor keep a record of these flips.

A map has a third north, called grid north.

It is the way the grid lines on the map run.

It is fixed by how the map is drawn, so it does not move.
