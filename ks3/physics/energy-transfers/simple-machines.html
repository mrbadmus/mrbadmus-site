Simple machines

A crowbar lets a small push lift a heavy paving slab.

If you had to guess, does your hand move further than the slab rises?

Much further. A smaller force has to move through a bigger distance.

Much further. A smaller force has to move through a bigger distance.

Key words

  • The energy transferred when a force moves an object. It is measured in joules.

  • A device, such as a lever or a ramp, that changes the force needed.

  • A rigid bar that turns about a fixed point.

  • The fixed point a lever turns about. It is also called the pivot.

Key knowledge

When a force moves an object, energy is transferred.

This energy is called the work done.

Work done depends on two things: the force, and the distance moved.

The distance is measured in the direction of the force.

work done = force × distance, or W = F × d

  • W is the work done, in joules (J).
  • F is the force, in newtons (N).
  • d is the distance moved, in metres (m).

To find the force: F = W ÷ d.

To find the distance: d = W ÷ F.

A bigger force or a bigger distance means more work done.

A force of 80 N pushes a crate 250 cm along the floor. Find the work done.

  • C, convert: 250 cm ÷ 100 = 2.5 m
  • F, formula: W = F × d
  • I, insert: W = 80 N × 2.5 m
  • F, fine-tune: 80 × 2.5 = 200
  • A, answer: W = 200 J

Forget to convert and you get 20 000 J. That is 100 times too big.

A lever lets a small force lift a heavy load.

This example is an ideal lever, with no friction.

You push down with 100 N. Your end moves 0.6 m.

Work done by you = 100 N × 0.6 m = 60 J.

The 600 N load rises 0.1 m.

Work done on the load = 600 N × 0.1 m = 60 J.

The force is 6 times smaller, so your end moves 6 times further.

A ramp lets you raise a load with less force. You push it further.

With no friction, push × ramp length = weight × height.

A pulley system lets you lift with less force. You pull more rope.

Gears can increase the turning force. The output then turns more slowly.

Some levers work the other way round.

A big force moving a short way makes the far end move a long way.

A fishing rod and your forearm work like this.

No simple machine gives out more energy than you put in.

Real machines have friction at the pivot, the axle or the ramp.

So you put in more energy than the load gains.

The extra energy is dissipated by heating.

So a real machine is less than 100% efficient.

Stretching a spring needs a force to move through a distance.

So work is done on the spring.

This work fills the spring's elastic potential store.

  • Wrong: A lever gives you extra energy.

    Right: A lever never gives out more energy than you put in. It changes the force and the distance.

  • Wrong: Put 250 cm straight into W = F × d.

    Right: Convert centimetres to metres first. Divide by 100.

  • Wrong: A longer lever needs less energy.

    Right: It needs less force. Your end moves further, so the energy is the same.

Key fact

Work done is the energy transferred when a force moves an object. Work done = force × distance, in joules. A simple machine trades force for distance. A smaller force must move further, and a bigger force moves less far. It never reduces the energy needed.

So the muscle needs a big force to lift a small load.
