Particles and their behaviour · Investigation

Testing the model: does it explain everything?

Start here

Ice floats on water. A lump of solid wax is dropped into melted wax.

If you had to guess, will the solid wax float or sink?

It sinks. Almost every solid sinks in its own liquid. Ice is a rare exception.

The simple model treats every particle as a plain sphere.

The particles of one substance are all identical, and not joined to each other.

Each failure needs more than that.

  • Diamond and graphite are carbon atoms joined in different patterns.
  • Rubber is made of particles joined in long, tangled chains.
  • In ice, water particles are held in an open pattern, with extra space.

Scientists test a model by checking its predictions against evidence.

They do not throw a model away when it fails.

They keep using it where it works, and note its limits.

The failures show where a better model is needed.

Ice floating matters. It is why lakes freeze from the top, and fish survive.

  • About 400 BC: Democritus argued matter is made of tiny pieces. He had no evidence.
  • 1803: Dalton said atoms are tiny solid spheres. This explained why mass is conserved.
  • 1897: Thomson found electrons, tiny parts inside atoms.
  • 1911: Rutherford found that an atom has a tiny nucleus at its centre.

Each new model kept what the old one explained, and fixed what it could not.

Dalton's explanation of conservation of mass is still used today.

A scientific idea can be changed by new evidence. That is how science works.

It does not mean nothing can be trusted.

Today's models have been tested more than any before them.

  • Wrong: If new evidence does not fit a model, the model was useless.

    Right: New evidence changing a model is how science works. Scientists improve the model and keep what still works.

  • Wrong: Once scientists agree, an idea never changes.

    Right: Ideas change when new evidence appears.

  • Wrong: Rubber is not made of particles.

    Right: It is. The simple model just cannot join them into chains.

Key fact

A model is judged by what it explains and where it fails. The particle model explains states, pressure and diffusion. It fails where particles are joined together or held in special patterns.
