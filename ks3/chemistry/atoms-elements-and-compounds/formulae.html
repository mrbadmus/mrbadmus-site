Key words

  • Symbols and numbers showing the elements in a substance and how many atoms of each.

  • Two or more atoms chemically joined together, as one separate particle.

  • A huge repeating pattern of joined particles, with no separate molecules.

  • How many of one thing there are for each of another.

Key knowledge

A formula shows which elements are in a substance.

It also shows how many atoms of each there are.

A small number after a symbol counts the atoms of that element.

No number means one atom. A 1 is never written.

  • H2O: two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom.
  • CO2: one carbon atom and two oxygen atoms.

A molecule is two or more atoms chemically joined together.

Some elements are made of molecules, like oxygen, O2.

Many compounds are too, like water, H2O, and carbon dioxide, CO2.

A small number after a symbol counts atoms inside one molecule.

A big number in front counts whole molecules.

2H2O is two molecules of water.

H2O2 is one molecule of hydrogen peroxide, a different substance.

Each hydrogen peroxide molecule has one more oxygen atom than a water molecule.

That one atom makes it a different substance.

Water is safe to drink. Hydrogen peroxide is not.

Changing the atoms in a molecule needs a chemical reaction.

Hydrogen peroxide slowly breaks down into water and oxygen. That is a chemical reaction.

Salt, sodium chloride, has no separate molecules.

It is a giant structure: a huge repeating pattern of sodium and chlorine.

Its formula, NaCl, gives the ratio. There is one sodium for every chlorine.

  • Wrong: The small number shows how much of the substance there is.

    Right: It counts atoms in one molecule. Changing it makes a different substance.

  • Wrong: 2H2O and H2O2 are the same thing.

    Right: 2H2O is two water molecules. H2O2 is one molecule of a different substance.

  • Wrong: NaCl is one molecule with one sodium and one chlorine.

    Right: Salt is a giant structure. NaCl gives the ratio, one to one.

Key fact

A formula shows which elements are in a substance and how many atoms of each. A small number counts atoms of the element just before it. A big number in front counts molecules.
