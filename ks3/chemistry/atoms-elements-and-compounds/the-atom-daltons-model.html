The atom: Dalton's model

Start here

For centuries, alchemists tried to turn lead into gold.

If you had to guess, could modern furnaces, acids and mixing turn lead into gold?

No, however good the kit. Chemical reactions only rearrange atoms. They cannot turn lead atoms into gold atoms.

Key words

  • The smallest particle of an element.

  • A substance made of only one kind of atom.

  • A simple picture of how something works. Scientists use it to explain and predict.

  • A change that rearranges atoms to make new substances.

Key knowledge

Everything is made of tiny particles called atoms.

An atom is the smallest particle of an element.

Each element has its own kind of atom.

Atoms are far too small to see, even with a light microscope.

John Dalton put forward his model of the atom over 200 years ago.

His model says:

  • Everything is made of atoms.
  • All atoms of one element are the same. They differ from the atoms of other elements.
  • Atoms cannot be split, made or destroyed.
  • Atoms join in simple whole-number ratios to make compounds.
  • A chemical reaction rearranges atoms. It never changes one kind into another.

  • Why mass stays the same in a reaction. The atoms are only rearranged.
  • Why a compound always has the same make-up. Pure water always has the same ratio of hydrogen to oxygen.
  • Why lead never turned into gold.

Alchemists worked with furnaces and acids.

They tried for centuries and always failed. That is evidence for the model.

If anyone made gold from lead by a chemical reaction, the model would be wrong.

Later scientists found that two of Dalton's claims were wrong:

  • Atoms can be split. They contain even smaller particles, such as electrons.
  • Atoms of one element can have different masses.

Radioactive atoms can change into atoms of another element. That is not a chemical reaction.

No chemical reaction changes one kind of atom into another.

A model is judged by what it explains.

Dalton's model still explains reactions, conservation of mass and fixed ratios.

It cannot explain electrons, radioactivity, or atoms of one element with different masses.

So the model is kept, with a known limit.

  • Wrong: A copper atom is orange, shiny and conducts electricity.

    Right: Those properties come from many atoms together. One atom does not have them.

  • Wrong: A model with one wrong part should be thrown away.

    Right: A model is kept while it explains what it is used for.

  • Wrong: Heating or mixing can turn one element into another.

    Right: Chemical reactions only rearrange atoms. The kinds of atom stay the same.

Key fact

An atom is the smallest particle of an element. Each element has its own kind of atom. Chemical reactions rearrange atoms, but never change one kind into another.
