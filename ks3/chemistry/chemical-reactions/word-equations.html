Chemical reactions · Model

Word equations

Start here

A pupil writes: “The magnesium burned with a bright white light. It left a white powder.”

If you had to guess, what would tell a chemist which reaction happened: how long it burned, or what the powder is?

Timing says nothing about what reacted. The chemist needs to know what the powder is.

Right. The powder is magnesium oxide. The magnesium joined with oxygen from the air.

Key words

  • A line naming the reactants and products of a reaction, with an arrow between.

  • A substance you start with in a reaction.

  • A substance a reaction makes.

  • Something a reaction needs that is not a substance, such as heat.

Key knowledge

A word equation sums up a reaction in one line.

It names every reactant and every product.

Reactants go on the left. Products go on the right.

An arrow points from the reactants to the products.

Magnesium burns in oxygen from the air. It makes magnesium oxide.

Read the arrow as makes, or turns into.

Read the plus sign as and. It does not mean add up.

So this one reads: magnesium and oxygen make magnesium oxide.

  • Name every reactant. Include any from the air, such as oxygen.
  • Name every product. Use its name, not what it looks like.
  • Write the reactants on the left, with + between them.
  • Draw an arrow. Write the products on the right, with + between them.

Marble chips are calcium carbonate. They fizz in hydrochloric acid.

The gas turns limewater milky, so it is carbon dioxide.

Calcium chloride and water are made too.

Only the substances that react, and the substances made, go in the equation.

Heat, light, energy and flames are not substances. Leave them out.

Heat given out is never written as a product either.

If a reaction needs heating all the time, heat can go above the arrow.

Leave out anything that was there but did not react, such as nitrogen in air.

Do not write bubbles. Name the gas, such as carbon dioxide.

The arrow is not an equals sign.

It shows which way the reaction goes.

Magnesium oxide does not turn back into magnesium and oxygen on its own.

So the equation cannot be written the other way round.

A compound of two elements is named after both. A metal's name always comes first.

The other element's name changes its ending to -ide:

  • oxygen becomes oxide, as in magnesium oxide
  • chlorine becomes chloride, as in sodium chloride
  • sulfur becomes sulfide, as in iron sulfide

Write the word equation

RowReactantsProducts
Iron and sulfur are heated. They make iron sulfide.
Sodium burns in chlorine gas.
Methane burns in air on a gas hob.
Zinc is put in copper sulfate solution. It makes copper and zinc sulfate.
Copper carbonate is heated. It breaks down into copper oxide and carbon dioxide.

A word equation names the substances. It does not show how many particles react.

Two hydrogen particles react with each oxygen particle. The word equation cannot show that.

Symbol equations use formulae and numbers to show it.

  • Wrong: The arrow is an equals sign, so the equation can be written either way round.

    Right: The arrow shows which way the reaction goes, from reactants to products.

  • Wrong: Heat can go in a word equation as a reactant.

    Right: Heat is not a substance. It is never a reactant or a product.

  • Wrong: The plus sign means add the substances up.

    Right: The plus sign means and. It sits between substances on the same side.

  • Wrong: Burning magnesium only needs magnesium on the left.

    Right: Oxygen from the air reacts too. It goes on the left as well.

Key fact

A word equation names the reactants on the left and the products on the right. The arrow means makes. Only substances go on either side of it.

Rung 1

In a word equation, what does the arrow mean?

Rung 2

A student writes: magnesium + oxygen + heat makes magnesium oxide. What is wrong with it?

Rung 3

Marble chips are calcium carbonate. Write the word equation for marble chips reacting with hydrochloric acid. Then explain how your equation shows that a gas was given off, and that a new substance was made.

Rung 4

A student says word equations are pointless because you could just describe the reaction in a sentence. Give two things a word equation forces you to say that a description can leave out, and one thing a word equation cannot say at all.

One reactant and two products means a substance has broken down.

Copper carbonate breaks down when heated. That is thermal decomposition.

Two reactants and one product means two substances have joined.

Iron and sulfur make a compound, iron sulfide. A magnet cannot pull iron out of it.

Iron oxide can mean more than one compound of iron and oxygen.

Formulae name each substance exactly.

You will use them in symbol equations.
