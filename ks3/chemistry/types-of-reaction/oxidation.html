Types of reaction · Process

Oxidation

Start here

A strip of magnesium burns with a bright white flame. It leaves a white powder.

If you had to guess, would all the powder left from burning magnesium weigh more than the metal did, or less?

Right. The magnesium joins with oxygen from the air. The powder is magnesium plus that oxygen.

Burning wood leaves less, because gases escape. Magnesium takes in oxygen and keeps it.

Key words

  • A reaction in which a substance gains oxygen.

  • A compound of a metal with oxygen, made when the metal is oxidised.

  • Hydrated iron oxide. Iron and steel rust when they meet oxygen and water.

  • A metal being slowly worn away by reacting with substances around it.

Key knowledge

Oxidation is a reaction in which a substance gains oxygen.

A metal reacts with oxygen to make a metal oxide.

The oxygen usually comes from the air.

Magnesium burns with a bright white flame.

It leaves a white powder, which is magnesium oxide.

The powder weighs more than the magnesium did.

The extra mass is the oxygen it gained from the air.

Metals reacting with oxygen

RowProductWhat you see
magnesium + oxygen
copper + oxygen
iron + oxygen

When a fuel burns, it gains oxygen.

So combustion is an oxidation reaction. It is fast and gives out heat and light.

Rusting is an oxidation reaction too. It is slow and can take years.

Rusting is the oxidation of iron.

Iron needs both oxygen and water to rust.

Only iron and steel rust. Other metals corrode.

Rust is weak and flaky. It falls off, so the iron underneath keeps rusting.

  • Tube 1 has air and water. The nail rusts.
  • Tube 2 has air but no water. A drying agent takes the water out.
  • Tube 3 has water but no air. Boiling took the air out of the water, and oil keeps air out.
  • Tube 4 has air and salt water. The nail rusts fastest.

One tube on its own cannot show what rusting needs.

Only tube 1 rusts. Tubes 2 and 3 do not.

So rusting needs both oxygen and water.

Tube 4 shows that salt makes rusting faster.

Aluminium reacts with oxygen in the air straight away.

A thin layer of aluminium oxide forms on its surface.

The layer sticks tightly and stops air reaching the metal underneath.

So aluminium window frames last for many years outdoors.

  • Paint, oil or grease keeps oxygen and water away from the iron.
  • Galvanising coats the iron with a layer of zinc.
  • Blocks of zinc are bolted to a ship's steel hull.

Zinc is more reactive than iron, so the zinc corrodes instead of the iron.

This is called sacrificial protection.

The zinc blocks slowly wear away, so they must be checked and replaced.

  • Wrong: Aluminium does not react with air, so it never corrodes.

    Right: Aluminium oxidises straight away. Its oxide layer protects the metal underneath.

  • Wrong: Rusting needs only water, or only air.

    Right: Rusting needs oxygen and water together.

  • Wrong: Rusting makes iron lighter.

    Right: Rust weighs more than the iron it came from. Oxygen and water have joined it.

  • Wrong: Every metal rusts.

    Right: Only iron and steel rust. Other metals corrode.

  • Wrong: Salt is needed for rusting.

    Right: Salt is not needed. It makes rusting happen faster.

Key fact

Oxidation is a reaction in which a substance gains oxygen, so the product weighs more. Iron rusts only when it has both oxygen and water.

Respiration is an oxidation reaction.

Glucose reacts with oxygen to make carbon dioxide and water.

It happens in small steps, so your cells can use the energy.

The opposite of oxidation is reduction.

In reduction, a substance loses oxygen.

Many metals are extracted from their ores by reduction.
