Acids and alkalis · Process

Acid + metal

Start here

A strip of magnesium is dropped into dilute hydrochloric acid. It fizzes, and the magnesium disappears.

If you had to guess, did the gas come from the metal, or from the acid?

The metal has dissolved into the liquid as a salt. The gas is hydrogen from the acid.

Right. Every acid contains hydrogen. The metal takes its place, and the hydrogen bubbles off.

Key words

  • The compound made when a metal takes the place of the hydrogen in an acid.

  • The gas given off when a metal reacts with an acid.

  • Reacts easily. The more reactive a metal is, the faster it reacts.

  • The test for hydrogen. A lit splint at the mouth of a tube of it makes a squeaky pop.

Key knowledge

Drop a piece of magnesium into dilute hydrochloric acid.

  • Bubbles of gas fizz off the metal.
  • The metal gets smaller, then disappears.
  • The tube gets warm.

The gas is hydrogen.

Reactive metals all react with acids in the same way.

The salt is made from the metal and part of the acid.

The hydrogen comes from the acid.

The metal does not turn into a gas.

It dissolves in the liquid as part of the salt.

Evaporate the water, and the salt is left behind as crystals.

Magnesium and hydrochloric acid leave white crystals of magnesium chloride.

The first word of the salt's name is the metal.

The second word comes from the acid.

  • Hydrochloric acid makes chlorides.
  • Sulfuric acid makes sulfates.

So zinc and sulfuric acid make zinc sulfate and hydrogen.

Word equations with hydrochloric acid

In dilute hydrochloric acid, the more reactive the metal, the faster it fizzes.

  • Magnesium fizzes fast, and the tube gets warm.
  • Zinc fizzes steadily.
  • Iron fizzes slowly.
  • Copper does not react at all.

Copper is below hydrogen in the reactivity series.

So it cannot push hydrogen out of a dilute acid.

  • Hold an empty test tube upside down over the reaction.
  • Hydrogen is less dense than air, so it rises into the tube.
  • Close the tube with a bung. Move it away from the reaction.
  • Take out the bung and hold a lit splint at the mouth.

A squeaky pop shows the gas is hydrogen.

The pop is hydrogen burning quickly with oxygen in the air. It makes water.

  • Wrong: The bubbles are the metal turning into a gas.

    Right: The bubbles are hydrogen from the acid. The metal dissolves as a salt.

  • Wrong: Every metal reacts with dilute acids.

    Right: Copper does not react with dilute hydrochloric or sulfuric acid.

  • Wrong: A metal and an acid make a salt and water.

    Right: A metal and an acid make a salt and hydrogen.

  • Wrong: Hydrochloric acid makes sulfates.

    Right: Hydrochloric acid makes chlorides. Sulfuric acid makes sulfates.

Key fact

A metal reacts with an acid to make a salt and hydrogen. The hydrogen comes from the acid. A lit splint at the mouth of a tube of hydrogen gives a squeaky pop.

Metals can be put in order, from most reactive to least reactive.

This order is the reactivity series. Hydrogen is often put in it too.

A metal above hydrogen can take the place of hydrogen in an acid.

Copper, silver and gold are below hydrogen.

So they do not react with dilute hydrochloric or sulfuric acid.

Sodium and potassium react with acids violently.

The hydrogen can catch fire. So they are never added to acid in school.

Acids are kept in glass or plastic bottles.

Many metals would react with the acid and give off hydrogen.

Hydrogen mixed with air can explode if it meets a flame.
