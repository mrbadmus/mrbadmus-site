Energy changes in reactions · Process

Exothermic reactions

Start here

A reusable hand warmer is a sealed pouch at room temperature. You snap a metal disc inside, and the pouch heats up to about 50 °C.

If you had to guess, does the warmth come from your body heat in the pocket, or from inside the pouch?

Your body is about 37 °C. The pouch gets hotter than that, so the heat comes from inside it.

Right. The liquid inside turns to crystals and gives out energy. That is a change of state, not a reaction.

Key words

  • A reaction that transfers energy to the surroundings, so their temperature rises.

  • Everything around the reacting chemicals, such as the water, the container and the air.

  • Energy stored in substances. A reaction can release it.

  • A reaction in which a fuel reacts with oxygen and gives out energy.

Key knowledge

In an exothermic reaction, energy is transferred to the surroundings.

The surroundings are everything around the reacting chemicals.

That includes the water they are in, the container and the air.

The surroundings gain energy, so their temperature rises.

Exo means out. The energy goes out of the chemicals.

Before the reaction, the energy is in the chemical store of the chemicals.

The reaction transfers some of it to the thermal store of the surroundings.

No energy is made. Energy cannot be created or destroyed.

It only moves from one store to another.

Put a thermometer in the reaction mixture.

Read it before you mix, and again at the highest reading.

Here an acid and an alkali are mixed. The temperature rises from 20 °C to 27 °C.

The temperature rises, so the reaction is exothermic.

When the reaction stops, the warm mixture slowly loses energy to the room.

It cools back down to room temperature.

Exothermic or not: use the data

RowWhat the data shows
Acid and alkali: 20 °C at the start, 27 °C at the end

Citric acid and sodium hydrogencarbonate: 20 °C at the start, 12 °C at the end

Methane needs a spark to light. Magnesium needs a flame.

This energy only gets the reaction going.

Once it starts, it gives out far more energy than the spark put in.

So it is still exothermic.

That is why a gas flame keeps burning after the spark has gone.

  • Wrong: A reaction that needs heating to start cannot be exothermic.

    Right: It is exothermic if it gives out more energy than went in to start it.

  • Wrong: Chemical reactions create energy.

    Right: Energy is never created. It was stored in the chemicals.

  • Wrong: In an exothermic reaction, the surroundings get colder.

    Right: In an exothermic reaction, the surroundings get warmer.

  • Wrong: Exothermic reactions take energy in from the surroundings.

    Right: Exothermic reactions transfer energy to the surroundings.

Key fact

An exothermic reaction transfers energy to the surroundings, so their temperature rises. The energy was stored in the chemicals before the reaction.

The iron reacts with oxygen in the air and gives out energy.

A self-heating can of coffee uses calcium oxide reacting with water.

Burning fuels heats homes. Respiration keeps your body warm.

The hand warmer in Start here holds a solution that turns into solid crystals.

That is a physical change, so it is not a reaction.

It still gives energy out, so the change is exothermic.

Boiling the pouch puts the energy back in and resets it.

Bacteria in a compost heap respire as they break down grass cuttings.

Respiration is exothermic. The middle of a big heap can reach 60 °C.

A badly stored pile of damp hay can even catch fire.
