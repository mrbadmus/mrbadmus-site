Life processes and what living things are made of

Start here

A candle flame moves and grows. It takes in oxygen and gives out waste gases.

If you had to guess, is a candle flame alive?

Key words

  • A single living thing. It can be one cell or many cells.

  • The smallest living unit. Every living thing is made of one or more cells.

  • The seven things every living thing does. MRS GREN helps you remember them.

  • Getting rid of waste made by the body's own cells.

Key knowledge

MRS GREN: the seven life processes

RowWhat it means
M: Movement
R: Respiration
S: Sensitivity
G: Growth
R: Reproduction
E: Excretion
N: Nutrition

Living things carry out these processes across their lives.

They do not have to do them all at once.

An acorn in a drawer is not growing or moving. It is still alive.

Its cells are respiring very slowly.

A cell is the smallest living unit.

Every living thing is made of one or more cells.

Yeast is just one cell. You are made of about thirty trillion.

Some things seem to do life processes but have no cells. They are not alive.

To check, look at a sample under a microscope.

Cells show up as many small separate units, each with an edge around it.

Alive or not

RowWhat it doesMade of cellsAlive
Candle flame
Robot vacuum
Acorn in a drawer
Yeast in bread dough

  • Burning is not respiration. Respiration is a set of reactions inside living cells.
  • Emptying out dust is not excretion. Excretion removes waste made by the body's own cells.
  • Charging a battery is not nutrition. No food goes in.

Living, once living, never living

RowWhich groupWhy
Moss on a wall
A wooden spoon
A leather boot
A lump of coal
A granite pebble
A candle flame

  • Wrong: If it moves, it is alive.

    Right: A flame moves but is not alive. A tree stays in one place and is alive.

  • Wrong: If something grows on its own, it is alive.

    Right: Fire and crystals both grow, but neither is alive. Living things do all seven life processes and are made of cells.

  • Wrong: A seed in a packet is dead.

    Right: Its cells are alive and respiring very slowly.

  • Wrong: Every living thing takes in oxygen.

    Right: Some cells respire without oxygen. All living things are made of cells.

Key fact

All living things carry out seven life processes, remembered as MRS GREN. Every living thing is made of one or more cells. A thing with no cells, like a flame, is not alive.

A virus is not made of cells.

It can only copy itself inside a living cell.

Many biologists say viruses are not alive. Others disagree.

In 1665, Robert Hooke looked at a thin slice of cork under a microscope.

He saw tiny boxes and called them cells.

They were the walls of dead cork cells.
