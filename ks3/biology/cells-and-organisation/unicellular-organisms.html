Unicellular organisms

Start here

A Paramecium is one cell, about a quarter of a millimetre long. It swims, feeds and splits in two.

If you had to guess, what makes the Paramecium a whole organism?

It does every life process itself. Nothing else feeds it or cleans up after it.

It does every life process itself. Nothing else feeds it or cleans up after it.

Key words

  • Made of just one cell. That one cell is the whole organism.

  • Made of many cells, which are usually specialised.

  • Tiny hair-like parts on a cell's surface. They beat together, like oars.

  • A long tail like a whip, which a cell beats to swim.

Key knowledge

A unicellular organism is made of one cell.

That one cell carries out all seven life processes by itself.

A cell in your body does one job. The rest of your body does the others for it.

At ×400 you can see a Paramecium's cilia and its contractile vacuole.

Water keeps moving into a Paramecium from the pond.

Its contractile vacuole fills with the extra water and squeezes it out.

Your body keeps the fluid around your cells steady, so they need no such vacuole.

A bacterium is a single cell with no nucleus.

Its DNA is a loose loop in the cytoplasm. Its instructions are still there.

So a bacterium can divide, sometimes every twenty minutes.

A red blood cell lost its nucleus and its DNA. It can never divide.

A bacterium is about 0.002 mm long. At ×400 it still looks like a dot.

Moving, size and shape do not decide it. Sperm cells swim, and they are part of a body.

Check whether the cell does every life process for itself.

A whole organism finds or makes its own food, and reproduces on its own.

A cell fed or held in place by a body is part of an organism.

A cell wall does not prove it is a plant. Yeast and bacteria have walls too.

No chloroplasts does not rule out a plant cell. Root cells have none.

  • Wrong: A single cell cannot be a whole living thing.

    Right: A unicellular organism is one cell that does all seven life processes.

  • Wrong: No nucleus means no instructions, so a bacterium cannot divide.

    Right: Its DNA is loose in the cytoplasm, so it can divide.

  • Wrong: A single-celled organism is simpler than one of your cells.

    Right: It often has more parts, like cilia, because it must do every job itself.

  • Wrong: If a cell swims, it must be a whole organism.

    Right: Sperm cells swim, and they are part of an organism.

Key fact

A unicellular organism is a single cell that carries out all seven life processes itself. Adaptations such as cilia, a flagellum and a contractile vacuole help it survive alone.

Rungs 3 and 4 you mark yourself.

Rung 1

A single cell moves on its own. It has mitochondria and is about 0.05 mm long. Which fact would best show whether it is an organism or part of one?

Rung 2

A red blood cell has no nucleus and can never divide. A bacterium has no nucleus and can divide every twenty minutes. What explains the difference?

Rung 3

A Paramecium and your cheek cell are both single cells. Explain why the Paramecium needs cilia, an oral groove and a contractile vacuole. Explain why your cheek cell needs none of them.

Rung 4

A student finds a single cell in water from a plant pot. It has a wall. It has no chloroplasts. It buds off smaller copies of itself. The student says it must be part of a plant, because it has a wall. Are they right? Which fact would you use instead? What would you still want to check?

Euglena makes food from light, like a plant.

Single-celled life does not sort neatly into plants and animals.

Cells lining your airways have cilia too.

On a Paramecium, cilia move the whole organism.

In your airways, the cells stay still. The cilia sweep mucus and dust along.
