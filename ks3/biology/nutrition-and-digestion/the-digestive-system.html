Nutrition and digestion · System

The digestive system

Start here

A cheese sandwich is blended for two minutes. It becomes a smooth liquid with no lumps.

If you had to guess, could that smooth liquid go straight into your blood?

Blending only makes the pieces smaller. The starch molecules in it are still too big.

Right. The starch is still in big molecules. Enzymes must break them into small ones.

Key words

  • Breaking large food molecules into small, soluble molecules.

  • Small food molecules passing from the gut into the blood.

  • Waves of muscle squeezing that push food along the gut.

  • Getting rid of food that was never absorbed, as faeces.

Key knowledge

Food contains large molecules, such as starch, protein and fat.

They are too big to pass into the blood.

Digestion breaks them down into small molecules that dissolve.

These small, soluble molecules can be absorbed into the blood.

Food travels along one long tube, from the mouth to the anus.

The liver, gall bladder and pancreas sit beside the tube.

Food never passes through them. They send juices into the small intestine.

Why it helps

The wall of the gut is made of muscle.

The muscle squeezes behind the food, in waves. This pushes the food along.

These waves are called peristalsis.

They work even if you swallow upside down. Gravity is not needed.

The liver makes bile. The gall bladder stores it.

Bile flows into the small intestine.

It breaks fat into many small droplets.

This gives the fat-digesting enzyme, lipase, more surface to work on.

Bile is not an enzyme. It does not break down any molecules itself.

  • Wrong: Digestion is only food being squashed into smaller pieces.

    Right: That is mechanical digestion. Enzymes must also break large molecules into small ones.

  • Wrong: Food stays in the stomach until it is digested.

    Right: Food leaves the stomach only part digested. Most digestion happens in the small intestine.

  • Wrong: Food passes through the liver and the pancreas.

    Right: Food never passes through them. They send juices into the small intestine.

  • Wrong: Bile is an enzyme that digests fat.

    Right: Bile breaks fat into droplets. The enzyme lipase digests the fat.

Key fact

Digestion breaks large food molecules into small, soluble molecules. These are absorbed into the blood, mostly in the small intestine.

Rung 4

A person has part of their small intestine removed after an injury. Predict how their digestion and absorption will change. Explain why the stomach cannot take over. Name one thing that will not change.

  • Mouth: about a minute.
  • Stomach: about 2 to 4 hours.
  • Small intestine: about 4 hours.
  • Large intestine: about a day, or more.

Food stays longest in the large intestine. Almost all digestion is already done.

These are typical times. They vary a lot between people and meals.

Egestion gets rid of food that was never absorbed. It leaves as faeces.

Excretion gets rid of waste made by your own cells.

Carbon dioxide and urea are examples of excretion.
