Absorption and the small intestine

Start here

A garden hose and your small intestine are about the same length and width.

If you had to guess, how does the intestine get sixty times the surface of a hose?

Coiling a tube does not add any inside surface. The wall itself is folded.

Yes. The wall is folded, and the folds are covered in tiny villi.

Key words

  • Small food molecules passing from the gut into the blood.

  • Tiny finger-like parts that cover the inside wall of the small intestine.

  • Particles spreading from a high concentration to a low concentration.

  • The total area of a surface. A bigger area means faster absorption.

Key knowledge

Starch, protein and fat molecules are large. They cannot pass through the gut wall.

Digestion breaks them into small, soluble molecules.

Only these small molecules can pass into the blood.

Absorption is small food molecules passing from the gut into the blood.

Almost all of it happens in the small intestine.

The molecules cross the wall by diffusion.

They move from high concentration in the gut to low concentration in the blood.

The blood then carries them around the body, to every cell.

The inside wall of the small intestine is covered in millions of villi.

Each villus is a tiny, finger-like part of the wall.

The inside wall of the small intestine is folded.

The folds are covered in villi.

The cells on each villus have even smaller parts, called microvilli.

Together they give about 30 m² of surface to absorb food.

That is about 60 times the inside of a smooth tube the same size.

Visking tubing has tiny holes. Small molecules can pass through. Large molecules cannot.

Glucose is small, so it passes through the tubing. Starch is too big, so it stays in.

In the same way, starch cannot pass through the gut wall.

It must be digested into sugars first. Then it can be absorbed.

  • Wrong: Villi make the intestine longer.

    Right: Villi add surface area. The length stays the same.

  • Wrong: The muscles push the food through the gut wall into the blood.

    Right: Food molecules cross the wall by diffusion. Muscles only move food along the gut.

  • Wrong: Starch can pass through the gut wall.

    Right: Starch molecules are too big. Starch must be digested into sugars first.

  • Wrong: Most food is absorbed in the stomach.

    Right: Most food is absorbed in the small intestine.

Key fact

Digested food is absorbed into the blood in the small intestine, by diffusion. Villi give a large surface area, a thin wall and a good blood supply.

The tubing is not alive. It has no villi and no folds.

In the body, blood flows past and carries the glucose away.

In the model, the water stays still. Glucose builds up in it.

So in the model, glucose moves out more and more slowly.

Lungs and the gills of fish work the same way.

Each has a large surface area, a thin wall and a good blood supply.
