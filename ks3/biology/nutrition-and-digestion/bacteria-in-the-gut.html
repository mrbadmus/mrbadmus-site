Nutrition and digestion · System

Bacteria in the gut

Start here

A mouse is raised in a sealed bubble. It never has any bacteria in or on it.

If you had to guess, does a mouse with no bacteria need more food than a normal mouse, or less?

Yes, about 30% more. Gut bacteria release energy from fibre the mouse cannot digest.

It needs about 30% more. Without gut bacteria, the energy in fibre is lost.

Key words

  • The trillions of bacteria that live in your gut, mostly in the large intestine.

  • Plant material, such as cellulose, that your own enzymes cannot digest.

  • A vitamin your blood needs to clot. Gut bacteria make some of it.

  • A medicine that kills bacteria, helpful ones as well as harmful ones.

Key knowledge

Trillions of bacteria live in your gut. Most live in the large intestine.

Few live in the stomach. Its acid kills most bacteria that you swallow.

Most gut bacteria are harmless or helpful. A few kinds can cause disease.

Immune system

The bacteria get a warm place to live, at about 37 °C.

They get a steady supply of food from what you eat.

You get the jobs they do. Both sides gain.

A bacterium is a single cell.

But bacteria can make enzymes that your own cells cannot make.

That is how gut bacteria can break down fibre that you cannot digest.

Antibiotics are medicines that kill bacteria.

They kill helpful gut bacteria as well as harmful ones.

This leaves space and food in the gut.

A harmful bacterium, such as C. difficile, can then grow fast.

It can cause severe diarrhoea.

Most kinds of E. coli live harmlessly in the large intestine.

If E. coli gets into the bladder or the blood, it can make you ill.

So whether a bacterium harms you can depend on where it is.

  • Wrong: Bacteria are germs. Having bacteria inside you means you are ill.

    Right: Everyone has trillions of gut bacteria. Most of them are helpful.

  • Wrong: A bacterium is just a simpler version of one of our cells.

    Right: Bacteria can do things our cells cannot, such as break down fibre.

  • Wrong: Antibiotics only kill harmful bacteria.

    Right: Antibiotics kill helpful gut bacteria too.

  • Wrong: Most gut bacteria live in the stomach.

    Right: Most live in the large intestine. Stomach acid kills most bacteria.

Key fact

Most gut bacteria are harmless or helpful. Some break down fibre we cannot digest. Some make vitamins, and they crowd out harmful bacteria.

Rung 3

A germ-free mouse needs about 30% more food than a normal mouse. It also catches infections more easily. Explain both, using at least three things gut bacteria do.

Rung 4

Cows digest grass, which is mostly cellulose. Cows make no enzyme that breaks down cellulose. Explain how a cow can digest grass. Suggest how a cow's gut compares in size with ours.

Scientists can raise mice with no bacteria at all.

These mice need about 30% more food than normal mice.

Their gut wall is thinner, and they catch infections more easily.

Newborn babies have very little vitamin K.

Little of it crosses from the mother before birth.

Their gut has almost no bacteria yet to make it.

In the UK, babies are offered vitamin K soon after birth.
