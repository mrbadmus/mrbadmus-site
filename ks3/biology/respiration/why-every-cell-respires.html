Respiration · System

Why every cell respires

Start here

You can live for weeks without food, but only minutes without oxygen.

If you had to guess, how much spare oxygen does the body store?

Only a few minutes' worth. The body stores weeks of fuel as fat.

Key words

  • A reaction in every living cell. It releases energy from glucose.

  • A substance spreading from a high to a low concentration. It needs no energy.

  • Moving a substance from a low to a high concentration. It needs energy.

  • Tiny parts of a cell where aerobic respiration happens.

Key knowledge

Every living cell respires, all the time.

This includes plant cells in the roots, stems and leaves.

Plants respire in the light and in the dark.

You respire fastest when you exercise. But respiration never stops, even when you sleep.

What the energy is used for

RowWhat it meansExample
Movement
Building large molecules
Active transport
Keeping warm

Diffusion moves substances from a high to a low concentration. It needs no energy.

Active transport moves substances from a low to a high concentration.

This needs energy from respiration.

Root hair cells take in minerals from the soil by active transport.

Water moves into the root without needing energy.

Muscle cells, sperm cells and root hair cells all need a lot of energy.

So they have many mitochondria.

Root cells respire, so they need oxygen.

They get it from air spaces between soil particles.

In waterlogged soil, water fills these air spaces.

The roots cannot respire aerobically, so they start to die.

Dead roots cannot take up water.

The body stores enough fat to last for weeks.

It stores only a few minutes' worth of oxygen.

Without oxygen, brain cells start to die within a few minutes.

  • Wrong: Plants photosynthesise and animals respire.

    Right: Plants do both. Every living cell respires.

  • Wrong: Plants only respire at night.

    Right: Plants respire day and night. Photosynthesis only happens in the light.

  • Wrong: You only respire when you exercise.

    Right: You respire all the time. Exercise makes it faster.

Key fact

Every living cell respires all the time, in plants and in animals. Respiration releases the energy for movement, building larger molecules, active transport and keeping warm.

In winter, a hibernating dormouse lets its body cool down.

Its respiration slows right down.

So its store of fat lasts until spring.

A lizard does not use energy from respiration to keep warm.

It warms itself in the sun instead.

So it needs far less food than a mammal of the same size.
