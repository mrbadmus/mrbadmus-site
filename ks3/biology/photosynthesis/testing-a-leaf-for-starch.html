Testing a leaf for starch

Start here

In the starch test, the leaf is boiled in ethanol, an alcohol.

If you had to guess, does the alcohol remove the green, or clean the leaf?

To remove its green. Ethanol dissolves the chlorophyll, so the colour change can be seen.

To remove its green. Ethanol dissolves the chlorophyll, so the colour change can be seen.

Key words

  • The store of glucose in a plant. It does not dissolve in water.

  • An orange-brown liquid. It turns blue-black when starch is there.

  • Keep a plant in the dark so it uses up its stored starch.

  • A kind of alcohol. Hot ethanol dissolves chlorophyll out of a leaf.

Key knowledge

Photosynthesis makes glucose. Glucose dissolves and is used up quickly.

The plant joins glucose together to make starch, and stores it.

Starch does not dissolve, so it stays in the leaf.

So starch in a destarched leaf shows it photosynthesised during the experiment.

The method, step by step

RowHowWhy
1. Destarch the plant
2. Boil the leaf in water
3. Boil it in ethanol
4. Dip it in hot water
5. Add iodine solution

Ethanol is highly flammable.

Never heat it over a Bunsen flame.

Turn the Bunsen off. Then stand the tube of ethanol in a beaker of hot water.

Wear eye protection.

Testing what photosynthesis needs

RowHow you take it awayResult
Light
Carbon dioxide
Chlorophyll

Start with destarched plants.

Take away one thing only. Keep everything else the same.

Compare with a leaf, or part of a leaf, that still has it.

For carbon dioxide, seal a second plant in a bag with no soda lime.

Keep both in the same light for the same time.

Only the leaf from the plant with carbon dioxide turns blue-black.

  • Wrong: Iodine turns blue-black because it reacts with chlorophyll.

    Right: Iodine tests for starch. The ethanol has already removed the chlorophyll.

  • Wrong: You can test any leaf straight from the plant.

    Right: Destarch the plant first. Otherwise old starch spoils the result.

Key fact

Iodine solution turns from orange-brown to blue-black when starch is present. Starch in a destarched leaf shows it photosynthesised. Boil the leaf in ethanol, in a water bath, to remove the chlorophyll.

In the dark, a plant cannot photosynthesise.

It still respires, so it breaks down its stored starch.

After two days, the leaves have almost no starch left.

Iodine solution turns bread and potato blue-black too.

They contain starch that a plant made and stored.

In a real laboratory: ethanol is highly flammable and is heated only in a water bath with no naked flame in the room; iodine solution stains and irritates skin and eyes; boiling water and hot apparatus need eye protection and a teacher present.
