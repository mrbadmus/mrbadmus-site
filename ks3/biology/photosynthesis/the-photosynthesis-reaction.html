Photosynthesis · Process

The photosynthesis reaction

Start here

Van Helmont grew a willow tree in a pot of soil for five years.

If you had to guess, was the soil much lighter, or about the same?

Almost the same. The soil lost about 57 g, but the tree gained about 74 kg.

Almost the same. The soil lost about 57 g, but the tree gained about 74 kg.

Key words

  • How plants make glucose from carbon dioxide and water, using light energy.

  • A substance used up in a reaction. It goes on the left of the arrow.

  • A substance made in a reaction. It goes on the right of the arrow.

  • The green pigment in chloroplasts. It absorbs light energy.

Key knowledge

Carbon dioxide and water are the reactants.

Glucose and oxygen are the products.

Light is not a reactant. It gives the energy, so it goes above the arrow.

Photosynthesis happens mainly in the leaves, inside chloroplasts.

Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll, a green pigment.

Chlorophyll absorbs the light energy the reaction needs.

Glucose is a sugar. It is a store of energy.

  • It is used in respiration to release energy.
  • It is turned into starch and stored.
  • It is used to make cellulose for new cell walls.

Roots take in water and mineral nutrients from the soil.

Minerals are needed in small amounts to keep the plant healthy.

For example, a plant needs magnesium to make chlorophyll.

Minerals are not food. A plant makes its own food by photosynthesis.

Van Helmont's tree gained about 74 kg. The soil lost only about 57 g.

So the tree was not built from the soil.

Dry mass is the mass left when all the water is removed.

Most of a plant's dry mass is built from carbon dioxide from the air.

A plant stores the glucose it makes as starch.

Iodine solution turns from orange-brown to blue-black when starch is there.

First, destarch a plant by keeping it in the dark for two days.

Cover part of one leaf with foil. Leave the plant in the light for a day.

The uncovered part turns blue-black with iodine. It made starch.

The covered part stays orange-brown. It made none.

So light is needed for photosynthesis.

A variegated leaf is green in some parts and white in others.

The white parts have no chlorophyll.

Destarch the plant. Then leave it in the light for a day.

Only the green parts turn blue-black with iodine.

Both parts are on one leaf. They get the same light, water and carbon dioxide.

So chlorophyll is needed for photosynthesis.

  • Wrong: Plants get their food from the soil.

    Right: Plants make their own food, glucose, by photosynthesis.

  • Wrong: Light is a reactant.

    Right: Light gives the energy. It is written above the arrow.

  • Wrong: Photosynthesis makes energy.

    Right: Energy cannot be made. Energy from light is stored in glucose.

Key fact

Photosynthesis happens in the chloroplasts of leaves. Carbon dioxide and water react to make glucose and oxygen. Chlorophyll absorbs the light energy the reaction needs.

Rung 4

A grower has tomatoes in a greenhouse. It is June, so it is bright and warm. The plants have good soil and plenty of water. But the crop has stopped growing any faster. She can buy one upgrade. Her choices are mineral feed, extra lights, carbon dioxide or bigger pots. Which would you advise, and why? How would you test your advice first?

Air is only about 0.04% carbon dioxide.

In a bright, warm, well-watered greenhouse, this often holds growth back.

So growers pump extra carbon dioxide into their greenhouses.

At GCSE, you will call this a limiting factor.

Plants respire all the time, day and night.

In bright light, photosynthesis is faster than respiration.

So in bright light a plant gives out more oxygen than it takes in.

Van Helmont thought his tree was made from the water he gave it.

He did not know that carbon dioxide in the air was involved.

Later scientists showed that the carbon came from the air.
