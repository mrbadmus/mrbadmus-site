Health and drugs · System

What drugs do to the body

Caffeine, paracetamol, nicotine and alcohol are all drugs. That is not a judgement about any of them — it is a description of what the molecules do once they are in your blood.

Start here

A mug of coffee contains a drug.

Around 90 milligrams of caffeine, bought in a café, legal at any age, no prescription. It changes how your nerve cells behave, and that is why you drink it. So the word drug cannot mean what most people think it means.

What makes a substance a drug?

A drug is any substance that changes the way the body works. That is the whole definition. Whether it is legal, whether a doctor prescribed it, and whether it is addictive are three separate questions, and the answers do not line up the way you would expect.

Where else the same dose went

  • Heart

    Beats faster. Two mugs is enough to measure the difference.

  • Kidneys

    Produce more urine, so more water leaves the body than you drank in the coffee.

  • Stomach

    Releases more acid, which is why coffee on an empty stomach can hurt.

None of those three organs has anything to do with feeling awake. They were simply on the route.

Where else the same dose went

  • Liver

    Breaks the drug down, at a fixed rate it cannot exceed. Too much in one day damages liver cells, and that damage can be permanent even though the person feels fine for a day or two.

  • Kidneys

    Filter the broken-down drug out into the urine. This is how the dose eventually leaves you.

  • Every other organ

    Received the drug and had no use for it.

The tablet did not travel to your head. It went everywhere, and your head is where there was something for it to act on.

Where else the same dose went

  • Heart

    Beats faster, and blood vessels narrow, so blood pressure rises.

  • Blood vessels in the skin

    Narrow too, reducing blood flow to the surface — one reason wounds heal more slowly in smokers.

  • Airways and lungs

    Damaged by tar and by the other substances in smoke rather than by nicotine itself. Which substance does which damage is the subject of b4-04.

Nicotine is the reason people keep smoking. The substances that cause most of the harm are different molecules travelling in the same smoke.

Where else the same dose went

  • Liver

    Breaks it down at roughly one unit an hour, and nothing raises that rate. Years of heavy drinking scar the liver permanently.

  • Kidneys

    Produce more urine, so heavy drinking dehydrates you.

  • Stomach

    Lining irritated directly, which is why heavy drinking causes sickness.

A depressant does not make you sad — it slows nerve signals down. That is a statement about signalling speed, not about mood.

Sorted by what they do, not by what they are called

Three classes, and all three are about nerve signals.

  • Stimulants · Speeds signals up

    Nerve signals pass more readily, so heart rate and alertness rise. Caffeine, nicotine. Prescribed stimulants exist too, and they are prescribed precisely because the effect is useful at a controlled dose.

  • Depressants · Slows signals down

    Signals between nerve cells pass less readily, so reactions, coordination and judgement all worsen. Alcohol, and the sedatives used in anaesthetics. Slower is not safer: most alcohol harm comes from what a slowed nervous system then does.

  • Painkillers · Blocks the message

    The pain signal is reduced or blocked on its way to the brain. The cause of the pain is untouched. Paracetamol, ibuprofen, and morphine at the strong end. Pain that stops being felt has not stopped being a warning.

Key fact

A drug is a substance that changes the way the body works. The blood carries it to every organ, so it acts wherever it fits — which is why a drug taken for one job also does other things you did not ask for.

!

Think again

“Drugs are illegal substances.”

Legality is a decision made by a parliament. It differs between countries, it has changed several times within living memory, and the molecule is unaffected by any of it. Caffeine is a drug and it is sold to children. Paracetamol is a drug and it is in most kitchen drawers. Alcohol and nicotine are drugs, they are legal for adults in the UK, and between them they cause more illness and more deaths here than every illegal drug combined. Meanwhile morphine is a controlled drug that is also given in hospitals every day. So legal and safe are not the same word, illegal and harmful are not the same word, and drug and medicine describe overlapping sets rather than opposite ones: a medicine is simply a drug used to treat something.

“A painkiller goes to the part that hurts.”

Nothing in a tablet knows where your head is. It dissolves, crosses the gut wall into the blood, and the blood takes it round the entire body in under a minute — liver, kidneys, skin, feet, all of it, whether they need it or not. The tablet works on your headache because the pain pathways carrying that particular signal are something it can act on, not because it travelled there. Everything else it touched on the way is what we call a side effect, and side effects are not a manufacturing fault. They are the unavoidable consequence of delivering a drug through a system that goes everywhere. It also explains why the number on the box matters: your liver breaks the drug down at a fixed rate, and a second dose taken early does not get a second liver.

Key note

A drug is any substance that changes how the body works. Stimulants speed nerve signals up, depressants slow them down, painkillers block the pain signal without touching its cause. Every drug is carried everywhere by the blood, acts where it fits, and is broken down mainly by the liver at a rate that cannot be hurried. Dose decides whether a drug treats you or harms you.

Going further

Digoxin comes from the foxglove in the hedgerow. At the right dose it steadies a failing heart and it has been prescribed for two hundred years; a little above that dose it stops the heart, which is why foxglove is one of the most dangerous plants in Britain. Same molecule, same body, different amount — so poison is not a category of substance at all, it is a statement about quantity. Even water follows the rule: drink several litres in an hour and you can dilute your blood dangerously. This is why pharmacists talk about doses rather than about safe and unsafe substances, and why it is natural, so it must be gentle is a claim about where a molecule came from rather than about what it does.
