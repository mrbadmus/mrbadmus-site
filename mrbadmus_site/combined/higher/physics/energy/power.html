Power

Start here

Two kettles, one cup of tea.

A 3000 W kettle and a 1500 W kettle each heat 0.5 kg of water from 20 °C to 100 °C. The 3000 W kettle finishes first.

Commit first

Which kettle transfers more energy to the water?

Power is the rate at which energy is transferred, or the rate at which work is done. Its unit is the watt (W): a transfer of 1 joule every second. So the 3000 W kettle transfers 3000 J each second, and the 1500 W kettle 1500 J. When a motor lifts a crate, the work it does goes into the crate’s gravitational potential store, Ep = m g h. On the bench, two motors lift crates side by side. Call the result before they run.

Equations

On the sheet

P = E ÷ t

power (W) = energy transferred (J) ÷ time (s)

E = P × t · t = E ÷ P

On the sheet

P = W ÷ t

power (W) = work done (J) ÷ time (s). Here W on the right is work done, not watts.

W = P × t · t = W ÷ P

On the sheet

Ep = m g h

gravitational potential energy (J) = mass (kg) × gravitational field strength (N/kg) × height (m): the work done lifting a load.

Units first

Joules and seconds go in; watts come out. Convert anything else before the equation.

1 kW = 1000 W · 1 MW = 1 000 000 W · 1 kJ = 1000 J · 1 MJ = 1 000 000 J · 1 minute = 60 s · 1 hour = 3600 s

Key fact

Power is how fast energy is transferred: 1 watt is 1 joule every second. The same work done in less time means more power.

Mixing up power and energy, or stuck on a unit?

The lift test uses g = 9.8 N/kg and treats each motor as lifting at a steady speed with nothing dissipated, so all the work done goes into the crate’s gravitational potential store. In the hook, each kettle is treated as transferring all of its energy to the water.
