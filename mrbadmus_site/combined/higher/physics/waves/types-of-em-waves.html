Types of electromagnetic waves

Start here

Your phone is sending a wave to the nearest mast right now. You cannot see it.

The phone signal, the warmth you feel from a fire, the light from this screen, the ultraviolet that tans skin and the X-rays that photograph a broken bone are one family of waves: electromagnetic waves. Your eyes respond to just one group of them.

Commit first

How much of the whole electromagnetic spectrum can your eyes detect?

Every group in the spectrum is the same kind of wave. Electromagnetic waves are transverse: the oscillations are at right angles to the direction the energy travels. They transfer energy from a source to an absorber, and they need no material to travel through, so they cross the vacuum of space. Together they form one continuous spectrum, grouped by wavelength (the distance from a point on one wave to the same point on the next wave) and frequency (the number of waves passing a point each second). You know where visible light sits. Can you place the other six?

Equations

The bench showed it: at one fixed speed, shorter waves mean more waves every second. The wave equation says exactly that.

On the sheet

v = f λ

wave speed (v, metres per second) = frequency (f, hertz) × wavelength (λ, metres)

f = v ÷ λ · λ = v ÷ f

The speed

Any electromagnetic wave in a vacuum or air: v = 3 × 10⁸ m/s, which is 300 000 000 m/s. The question gives you this value.

Units first

Frequency in Hz: kHz × 1000, MHz × 1 000 000, GHz × 1 000 000 000. Wavelength in m: km × 1000, cm ÷ 100.

Key fact

From radio waves to gamma rays the wavelength gets shorter and the frequency gets higher. The speed in a vacuum never changes.

Key note · AQA 6.6.2.1 (8464)

Types of electromagnetic waves

  1. 01EM spectrum (long→short λ): radio, microwave, infrared, visible, UV, X-ray, gamma.
  2. 02All travel at 3×10⁸ m/s in vacuum.
  3. 03All transverse.
  4. 04Higher frequency = shorter λ.
  5. 05Visible: red (longest) to violet (shortest).
  6. 06Our eyes detect only visible light.

    Not sure which way wavelength and frequency change across the spectrum?

    The waves on the bench are drawn to show which is longer, not to scale: real wavelengths run from kilometres for radio waves to far smaller than an atom for gamma rays. On screen every wave moves at the same slow speed, as all electromagnetic waves share one speed in a vacuum; the real speed is 3 × 10⁸ m/s. The groups have no sharp edges: neighbouring groups merge into each other.
