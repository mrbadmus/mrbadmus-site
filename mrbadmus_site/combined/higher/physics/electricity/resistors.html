Required practical · the AQA method

Build the circuit. Change the p.d. Measure the current.

  1. Connect the component in series with an ammeter, a variable resistor and the battery. Connect a voltmeter in parallel across the component.
  2. Use the variable resistor to set the p.d. Record the p.d. and the current.
  3. Repeat for a range of p.d. values, switching off between readings so that the resistor stays at a constant temperature.
  4. Reverse the connections to the battery and repeat, to get negative values.
  5. Plot current (y-axis) against p.d. (x-axis) for each component.

Risks. A filament lamp gets hot enough to burn: do not touch it while it is on, and let it cool. A resistor carrying a large current also warms up, which changes its resistance: keep currents low and switch off between readings.

Equation · processing the data

Equation sheet

V = I R

R = V ÷ I

p.d. in volts (V), current in amperes (A), resistance in ohms (Ω).

Work out R from one reading at a time. On a curved I–V graph, R is different at every point: never use the gradient to find it.

Key fact

A resistor at constant temperature is ohmic: current is directly proportional to p.d. A filament lamp's resistance rises as it heats, and a diode lets current flow in one direction only.

Key note · AQA 6.2.1.4 · RP

Resistors and I–V characteristics

  1. 01Required practical: component in series with an ammeter and variable resistor; voltmeter in parallel across it.
  2. 02Change the p.d. with the variable resistor; reverse the connections for negative values; plot I against V.
  3. 03Resistor at constant temperature: straight line through the origin. Ohmic: I is directly proportional to V.
  4. 04Filament lamp: curve. Resistance increases as the filament gets hotter.
  5. 05Diode: current flows in one direction only; very high resistance in reverse.
  6. 06Thermistor: R falls as temperature rises. LDR: R falls as light intensity rises. R = V ÷ I, current in amperes.
