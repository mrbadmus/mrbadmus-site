No sample of the early atmosphere survives: evidence is limited because of the 4.6-billion-year time scale. So scientists build the theory from indirect evidence, such as the minerals in the oldest rocks, the gases volcanoes give out today and the atmospheres of other planets. Theories about the early atmosphere have changed over time as new evidence arrived. In the exam you may be given evidence and asked to evaluate a theory: what supports it, what weakens it, then a judgement.

Key note · AQA 5.9.1.2–5.9.1.4 (8464)

The Earth's early atmosphere and how it changed

  1. 01Early atmosphere (one theory): gases released by volcanoes; mainly carbon dioxide, with water vapour and some nitrogen, little or no oxygen, perhaps some methane and ammonia.
  2. 02Oceans formed when the Earth cooled and water vapour condensed; carbon dioxide dissolved and carbonates formed sediments.
  3. 03Oxygen increased from about 2.7 billion years ago, by photosynthesis by algae and later plants, to a level that let animals evolve.
  4. 04Carbon dioxide decreased: dissolved in the oceans, photosynthesis, and locked in sedimentary rocks (limestone) and fossil fuels (coal, crude oil, natural gas).
  5. 05Nitrogen built up: released by volcanoes and unreactive, so little was removed.
  6. 06Evidence is limited because of the 4.6-billion-year time scale.
