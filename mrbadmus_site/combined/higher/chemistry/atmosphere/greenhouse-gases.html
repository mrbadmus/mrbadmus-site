Greenhouse gases and climate change

The same few gases keep Earth warm enough for life, and are now warming it further. How do they hold on to heat, and why is cutting them so hard?

Start here

Take away three gases and Earth would average about −18 °C.

Today the Earth's surface averages about 15 °C. Same Sun, same distance, same ground. The difference is made by water vapour, carbon dioxide and methane in the air.

Commit first

How do those gases keep the surface warmer?

The Sun is very hot, and most of its radiation is short wavelength: visible light, with some ultraviolet. It reaches the Earth and warms the surface. The warmed surface then gives out radiation of its own, but at long wavelengths: infrared. What happens to that infrared on its way out decides how warm the surface stays.

Radiation bench

No greenhouse gases

Sunlight warms the ground, and the ground gives out infrared. Where does the infrared go?

Human activities add more of two greenhouse gases. Carbon dioxide comes from burning any fuel that contains carbon, and from deforestation: fewer trees take it in, and felled trees release it when burned or left to rot. Methane is given off when some living things digest food or rot with little air, and natural gas is mainly methane.

An increase in average global temperature is a major cause of climate change. Its effects reach the sea, the weather, food and wildlife. Scientists weigh each effect by its scale, its risk and what it does to the environment.

Exam trap

Describe

A student lists four effects of global climate change. Which one would not earn a mark as an effect of climate change?

Based on peer-reviewed evidence, many scientists believe human activities will raise the temperature of the atmosphere at the Earth's surface, causing global climate change. Peer review means other experts check the work before it is published. But the climate is a complex system and hard to model, so models are simplified and predictions are uncertain. Media reports may use only part of the evidence, and may be biased. That is why results must be communicated clearly, to everyone.

The carbon footprint is the total amount of carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases emitted over the full life cycle of a product, service or event. It is reduced by cutting emissions of carbon dioxide and methane: renewable energy, saving energy with insulation and efficient vehicles, capturing and storing carbon dioxide, carbon taxes, offsetting such as tree planting, and less waste sent to landfill. Actions may be limited: they cost money, some technology is not ready, people are reluctant to change their lifestyles, not everyone is informed, some doubt the evidence, and countries find it hard to agree.

Key note · AQA 5.9.2.1–5.9.2.4 (8464)

Greenhouse gases and climate change

  1. 01Greenhouse gases (water vapour, carbon dioxide and methane) keep the Earth warm enough to support life.
  2. 02Short-wavelength radiation from the Sun passes through the atmosphere and warms the surface; the surface emits long-wavelength (infrared) radiation; greenhouse gases absorb it and re-emit it in all directions, including back to Earth.
  3. 03Human activities: carbon dioxide from burning fossil fuels and deforestation; methane from cattle farming and landfill.
  4. 04Effects of climate change: rising sea levels, more severe storms, changed rainfall, changes to food production and to where species live.
  5. 05Carbon footprint: the total carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases emitted over the full life cycle of a product, service or event.
  6. 06Cutting emissions may be limited by cost, technology, lifestyle, lack of information and countries failing to agree.
