Changes in energy

Start here

Twice as fast. How much harder to stop?

To stop a car, its brakes must transfer away all of its kinetic energy, and the brakes get hot. Now the same car is driven at twice the speed.

If you had to guess, do the brakes now have to transfer twice as much energy, or more than twice as much?

Three energy stores each have their own equation. A moving object has energy in its kinetic store. An object raised above the ground has energy in its gravitational potential store. A stretched or compressed spring has energy in its elastic potential store. On the bench, change one quantity at a time and call the factor before the store fills.

What happens to the energy in the gravitational potential store?

Every equation here works in joules, kilograms, metres, metres per second and newtons per metre. Exam questions often give a quantity in another unit on purpose. Converting it is the first line of your working, before the equation.

Equations

Equation sheet

Ek = ½ m v²

kinetic energy (J) = 0.5 × mass (kg) × speed² (m/s)

m = 2Ek ÷ v² · v = √(2Ek ÷ m)

Equation sheet

Ep = m g h

gravitational potential energy (J) = mass (kg) × gravitational field strength (N/kg) × height (m). The question always gives g.

h = Ep ÷ (m g)

Equation sheet

Ee = ½ k e²

elastic potential energy (J) = 0.5 × spring constant (N/m) × extension² (m). e can be a compression. Valid up to the limit of proportionality.

k = 2Ee ÷ e²

!

Think again · weight is not mass

“The box weighs 20 N, so m = 20.”

Spot the flaw

Identify

A box of weight 20 N is lifted 3.0 m. A student writes Ep = 20 × 9.8 × 3.0 = 588 J. What has gone wrong?

Key note · AQA 6.1.1.2 (8464)

Changes in energy

  1. 01Ek = ½mv² (kinetic).
  2. 02Ep = mgh (gravitational PE, g = 9.8 N/kg Earth).
  3. 03Ee = ½ke² (elastic PE).
  4. 04Doubling speed → ×4 kinetic energy.
  5. 05Doubling extension → ×4 elastic PE.
  6. 06All three equations need mass in kg and distance in m.
