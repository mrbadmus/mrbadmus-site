Key knowledge

Photosynthetic organisms are the producers of biomass for life on Earth.

All food chains begin with a producer which synthesises molecules. This is usually a green plant or alga which makes glucose by photosynthesis.

Feeding relationships within a community can be represented by food chains.

Producers are eaten by primary consumers, which in turn may be eaten by secondary consumers and then tertiary consumers.

Each arrow points from the food to the eater. It shows the direction biomass is transferred.

Consumers that kill and eat other animals are predators, and those eaten are prey.

In the pond chain the heron is a predator and the small fish are its prey.

Most animals eat more than one kind of food, so food chains join up into a food web.

The organisms in a web depend on each other. Change one population and the populations linked to it change too.

In this web, the foxes die out. Follow the arrows one step at a time.

  • Foxes ate rabbits, so more rabbits survive. More rabbits eat more grass, so the grass falls.
  • Foxes also ate shrews, so more shrews survive. Owls eat shrews, so the owls have more food.

Key fact

All food chains begin with a producer, usually a green plant or alga that makes glucose by photosynthesis. The arrows point from the food to the eater, and in a food web a change in one population changes the populations linked to it.

  • Draw: a food chain with the producer first and arrows from the food to the eater.
  • Name: the producer and the primary, secondary and tertiary consumers.
  • Explain: say what happens to one population when another changes, and why.
