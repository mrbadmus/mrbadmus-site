Conservation of mass and balanced equations

If you had to guess, once the flask has cooled, does the reading go down or stay the same?

Key knowledge

The law of conservation of mass states that no atoms are lost or made during a chemical reaction, so the mass of the products equals the mass of the reactants.

In a reaction, bonds break and new bonds form. The same atoms are only rearranged into new substances.

total mass of reactants = total mass of products

24 g of magnesium reacts with 16 g of oxygen. 24 + 16 = 40 g of magnesium oxide forms.

If you know every mass but one, add up the side you know. Then subtract to find the missing mass.

Because no atoms are lost or made, a symbol equation must be balanced.

A balanced equation has the same number of atoms of each element on both sides.

  • A big number in front of a formula multiplies the whole formula. 2H2O is 4 H atoms and 2 O atoms.
  • A small number written low in a formula counts the atom just before it. H2O has 2 H atoms and 1 O atom.
  • A small number after a bracket multiplies everything inside the bracket. Ca(OH)2 has 1 Ca, 2 O and 2 H.
  • To balance an equation, change only the big numbers. Never change a formula.

  • Write the correct formulae: H2 + O2 → H2O
  • Count the atoms. Left: 2 H and 2 O. Right: 2 H and 1 O. The oxygen does not balance.
  • Put a 2 in front of H2O: H2 + O2 → 2H2O. The right is now 4 H and 2 O.
  • Put a 2 in front of H2: 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O. Both sides now have 4 H and 2 O. Balanced.

A word equation names the substances:

magnesium + hydrochloric acid → magnesium chloride + hydrogen

A symbol equation uses their formulae, and is balanced:

Mg + 2HCl → MgCl2 + H2

State symbols show the state of each substance: (s) solid, (l) liquid, (g) gas and (aq) aqueous, which means dissolved in water.

Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl2(aq) + H2(g)

  • Wrong: Balance H2 + O2 → H2O by writing H2O2.

    Right: Never change a formula. H2O2 is a different substance. Change only the big numbers.

  • Wrong: 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O has three molecules on the left and two on the right, so the mass goes down.

    Right: Mass depends on the atoms. There are 4 H and 2 O on each side, so the mass stays the same.

  • Wrong: 2H2O has 2 hydrogen atoms.

    Right: The 2 in front multiplies the whole formula: 4 H and 2 O.

Key fact

No atoms are lost or made during a chemical reaction, so the mass of the products equals the mass of the reactants. A balanced symbol equation shows the same number of atoms of each element on both sides.

Lead nitrate solution and potassium iodide solution are mixed in a flask on a balance. A yellow solid forms, but the reading does not change.

Pb(NO3)2(aq) + 2KI(aq) → PbI2(s) + 2KNO3(aq)

No gas is made, so nothing leaves the flask.

  • Balance: put numbers in front of formulae until every element has the same number of atoms on each side.
  • Write: correct formulae, balancing numbers, and state symbols when the question asks for them.
  • Calculate: show each line of working and give the unit.
  • Explain: give each reason as a step that leads to the next.
