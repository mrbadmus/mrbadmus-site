Atoms, elements and compounds

Sodium is a metal that reacts with water. Chlorine is a toxic gas. Together they make table salt.

If you had to guess, are the sodium and chlorine in salt just mixed together, or joined into something new?

Key knowledge

All substances are made of atoms. An atom is the smallest part of an element that can exist.

An element contains only one type of atom. There are about 100 different elements, and they are shown in the periodic table.

Atoms of each element are represented by a chemical symbol: O represents an atom of oxygen, Na represents an atom of sodium, Fe represents an atom of iron.

Compounds are formed from elements by chemical reactions.

A compound contains two or more elements chemically combined in fixed proportions. Water, H2O, always has two hydrogen atoms for every oxygen atom.

A compound has different properties from the elements it was made from.

A compound can only be separated into its elements by chemical reactions.

A chemical reaction always makes one or more new substances.

It often involves a detectable energy change, such as the mixture getting hot.

  • Element: only one type of atom. Two atoms of the same element joined together, such as O2, are still an element.
  • Compound: two or more elements chemically combined, in the same fixed ratio all the way through.
  • Mixture: two or more elements or compounds that are not chemically combined.

Example

A formula shows the symbols of the elements in a compound and the number of atoms of each. The small number counts the atom just before it.

  • H2O: 2 hydrogen atoms and 1 oxygen atom.
  • CO2: 1 carbon atom and 2 oxygen atoms.
  • H2SO4: 2 hydrogen atoms, 1 sulfur atom and 4 oxygen atoms.

  • A metal with one non-metal: the non-metal ending changes to -ide. NaCl is sodium chloride, MgO is magnesium oxide.
  • A name ending in -ate means oxygen is in the compound too. CaCO3 is calcium carbonate.
  • Two non-metals: CO2 is carbon dioxide (di means two).

Chemical reactions can be represented by word equations or by equations using symbols and formulae.

  • Word equation: sodium + chlorine → sodium chloride
  • Symbol equation: 2Na + Cl2 → 2NaCl

Atoms are not made or destroyed in a reaction. So a balanced equation has the same number of each type of atom on both sides.

Hydrogen + oxygen → water.

  • Step 1: H2 + O2 → H2O. Left: 2 H and 2 O. Right: 2 H and 1 O. Oxygen does not balance.
  • Step 2: H2 + O2 → 2H2O. Right: 4 H and 2 O. Now hydrogen does not balance.
  • Step 3: 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O. 4 H and 2 O on each side. Balanced.

Only change the big numbers in front of a formula. Never change a formula.

  • Wrong: O2 is a compound because it has two atoms.

    Right: O2 is an element. A compound needs two or more different elements.

  • Wrong: H2 + O2 → H2O2 balances the equation for water.

    Right: H2O2 is a different substance. Put numbers in front: 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O.

  • Wrong: Bronze, a metal with a metal, is a compound.

    Right: Bronze is copper and tin, not chemically combined. An alloy is a mixture.

  • Wrong: A compound can be separated by filtering or distilling.

    Right: Only a chemical reaction separates a compound into its elements.

Key fact

A compound contains two or more elements chemically combined in fixed proportions, and it can only be separated into its elements by a chemical reaction. A balanced equation has the same number of each type of atom on both sides, because atoms are not made or destroyed in a reaction.

About 100 elements is the number to learn. 118 are known today, but some have only ever been made in a laboratory, a few atoms at a time.

Learn the names and symbols of the first 20 elements and the elements in Groups 1 and 7. Some symbols come from Latin names: Na (sodium), K (potassium), Fe (iron), Cu (copper).

  • Identify: pick out the answer from a diagram, a formula or a list.
  • Complete: fill the gaps, such as the numbers that balance an equation.
  • Explain: give the reason, such as chemically combined or not, fixed proportions or not.
