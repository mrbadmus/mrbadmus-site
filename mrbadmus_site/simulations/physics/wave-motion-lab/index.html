Transverse waves

Meet the particles

Definitions

Transverse wave

Work it out from the simulation first.
Copy this down

The oscillations (vibrations) are perpendicular, at 90°, to the direction of energy transfer.

  • Examples: ripples on water, all electromagnetic waves (including light), S-waves in an earthquake.
  • Has peaks (crests) and troughs.

Longitudinal wave

Work it out from the simulation first.
Copy this down

The oscillations (vibrations) are parallel to the direction of energy transfer. It has compressions and rarefactions.

  • Examples: sound waves in air, P-waves in an earthquake, a slinky pushed and pulled.
  • Compression: particles squashed close together. Rarefaction: particles spread out.

Frequency and period

Work it out from the time graph first.
Copy this down

Frequency (f) is the number of waves passing a point each second, in hertz (Hz). The period (T) is the time for one complete wave to pass, in seconds (s).

T = 1 / f T in s · f in Hz
  • Higher frequency: more waves each second, so each one takes less time.
  • Example: f = 4 Hz, so T = 1 ÷ 4 = 0.25 s.

In both waves

Copy this down

A wave transfers energy from one place to another. The particles of the material are NOT transferred. They only vibrate about a fixed rest position.

Evidence: a duck on a pond bobs up and down as ripples pass, but stays where it is. When a speaker plays, the air in front of it is not blown across the room.
