AQA Chemistry 5.9.3.1–5.9.3.2

Atmospheric pollutants from fuels

Burn a fuel and you get more than carbon dioxide and water. Predict what comes out of the exhaust from what is in the fuel and how it burns, then match each pollutant to the harm it does.

Start here

Standing by a busy road.

You can smell the traffic, and an old van pulling away leaves a puff of black smoke. The fuels are mostly carbon and hydrogen.

If you had to guess, does burning them put out only carbon dioxide and water, or other substances too?

Predict the products

What is in the fuel, and how does it burn?

Carbon and hydrogen

With plenty of oxygen: carbon dioxide and water. With too little oxygen, some carbon forms carbon monoxide, or solid carbon (soot), and some fuel stays unburned.

Sulfur in the fuel

Some fuels contain sulfur as an impurity. It burns to sulfur dioxide.

S + O2SO2

High temperature

In a hot engine, nitrogen and oxygen from the air react to make oxides of nitrogen. The nitrogen comes from the air, not the fuel.

N2 + O22NO

Worked example

Diesel containing a little sulfur, burned in a hot engine with plenty of air. Carbon and hydrogen with plenty of oxygen: carbon dioxide and water. Sulfur in the fuel: sulfur dioxide. Hot engine: oxides of nitrogen. Plenty of air, so no carbon monoxide or soot expected.

What each pollutant does

From source, to pollutant, to harm.

Carbon monoxide

Toxic. It is colourless and odourless, so it is not easily detected. This is why homes with gas boilers need a carbon monoxide detector.

Sulfur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen

Cause respiratory problems in humans, and cause acid rain. Acid rain has a lower pH than normal rain.

Particulates

Solid particles (soot) and unburned hydrocarbons. They cause global dimming, less sunlight reaching the Earth's surface, and health problems.

Key fact

Carbon monoxide is toxic and not easily detected. Sulfur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen cause respiratory problems and acid rain. Particulates cause global dimming and health problems.

Key note · AQA 5.9.3.1–5.9.3.2

Atmospheric pollutants from fuels

  1. 01Fuels are mainly carbon and hydrogen: complete combustion gives carbon dioxide and water.
  2. 02Too little oxygen: carbon monoxide, soot and unburned hydrocarbons (particulates).
  3. 03Sulfur impurities in the fuel burn to sulfur dioxide.
  4. 04At high temperatures in engines, nitrogen and oxygen from the air form oxides of nitrogen.
  5. 05Carbon monoxide: toxic, colourless, odourless, not easily detected.
  6. 06Sulfur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen: respiratory problems and acid rain. Particulates: global dimming and health problems.
