Party balloons.

Party balloons that float are filled with helium. At birthday parties they are often tied up right beside the cake and its lit candles.

If you had to guess, does helium react easily, or hardly at all?

Full outer shells

Stable atoms, so unreactive and single.

The noble gases are unreactive and do not easily form molecules, because their atoms have stable arrangements of electrons. They have eight electrons in their outer shell, except helium, which has only two. So they exist as single atoms: they are monatomic. Pick an atom.

Helium: 2 electrons. Its only shell is full. No tendency to gain, lose or share electrons, so it exists as single atoms and hardly reacts.

When atoms of other elements react, they gain, lose or share electrons and end up with the electron arrangement of a noble gas. That is in the metals and non-metals and bonding lessons.

Why it rises

Going down the group, the atoms get bigger and their relative atomic mass increases. The forces between the atoms get stronger, so more energy is needed to overcome them: the boiling point rises. Boiling separates the atoms; it does not break anything inside them. Density also increases down the group.

Key fact

Key note · AQA 5.1.2.4

Group 0: the noble gases

  1. 01Group 0 elements are the noble gases. They are unreactive and do not easily form molecules.
  2. 02Their atoms have stable arrangements of electrons: 8 in the outer shell, or 2 for helium.
  3. 03They exist as single atoms (monatomic).
  4. 04Boiling points increase with increasing relative atomic mass, going down the group.
  5. 05Bigger atoms, stronger forces between atoms, more energy needed to overcome them.
  6. 06You can predict an unknown value, such as radon’s boiling point, from the trend.
