Using moles — calculations and limiting reactants

Magnesium fizzes in acid until something runs out. Which one runs out, how much hydrogen do you get, and how do the masses tell you the balanced equation in the first place?

Triple Science · Higher tier
  • Combined Science · Higher tier

Start here

The fizzing stops. Something ran out.

Magnesium fizzes in hydrochloric acid, giving off hydrogen gas: Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂. A student mixes 2.4 g of magnesium with 3.65 g of hydrogen chloride dissolved in water. After a while the fizzing stops.

Commit first

Which reactant runs out first?

When two substances react, the one that is completely used up is the limiting reactant: it limits the amount of product. The other is in excess, and some of it is left over. Chemists often use an excess of one reactant so that all of the other one reacts. To find the limiting reactant, convert each mass to moles (n = mass ÷ Mr). Then compare those moles with the ratio in the balanced equation, not with each other.

The masses in a reaction also tell you its balanced equation. Convert each mass in grams to moles, then divide every amount by the smallest one. That gives the simplest whole-number ratio, and those numbers are the balancing numbers. Use each substance’s formula as written: hydrogen gas is H₂, so its Mr is 2, not 1; oxygen gas is O₂, Mr 32.

“The ratio came out as 1 : 1.5 : 1, so I rounded the 1.5 up to 2.”

Spot the flaw

Balance

Iron reacts with chlorine: Fe + Cl₂ → FeCl₃. From the masses, the moles are Fe 0.10, Cl₂ 0.15 and FeCl₃ 0.10, a ratio of 1 : 1.5 : 1. What is the balanced equation?

Triple

Moles in solution · AQA 4.3.4

Many reactions happen in solution, where amounts are measured as concentrations in mol/dm³. concentration (mol/dm³) = amount (mol) ÷ volume (dm³), so amount (mol) = concentration × volume. Convert cm³ to dm³ first: 100 cm³ of 2 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid holds 2 × 0.1 = 0.2 mol.

Key fact

The limiting reactant is the one completely used up, and it fixes the amount of product. Find it from moles and the equation ratio, never from the masses alone.
