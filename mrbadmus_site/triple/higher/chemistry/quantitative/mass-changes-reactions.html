Mass changes in reactions

Start here

Marble chips react with hydrochloric acid in an open flask on a balance. The mixture fizzes.

If you had to guess, does the reading go down or stay the same?

Key words

  • non-enclosed system
  • thermal decomposition
  • particle model

Key knowledge

Some reactions may appear to involve a change in mass but this can usually be explained because a reactant or product is a gas and its mass has not been taken into account.

No atoms are lost or made. A gas has left the container, or joined the product, without being weighed.

A product is a gas, and it escapes from an open container.

In the thermal decomposition of a metal carbonate, carbon dioxide is produced and escapes into the atmosphere. The metal oxide is the only solid product.

CaCO3(s) → CaO(s) + CO2(g)

Marble chips and acid in an open flask lose mass the same way:

CaCO3(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CaCl2(aq) + H2O(l) + CO2(g)

A metal reacts with oxygen from the air.

The mass of the oxide produced is greater than the mass of the metal, because it now includes the oxygen.

2Mg(s) + O2(g) → 2MgO(s)

In a sealed flask, no gas can get in or out.

Everything that was weighed at the start is still weighed at the end, so the reading does not change.

Falls, rises or stays the same

RowReadingWhy
Marble chips and acid in an open flask
Zinc and dilute sulfuric acid in an open tube
Magnesium heated in an open crucible
Calcium carbonate heated in a sealed tube

  • Reading falls: gas particles move about randomly and spread out of the open container. They take their mass with them, so the balance no longer weighs them.
  • Reading rises: oxygen particles from the air bond to the metal atoms and become part of the solid oxide. Mass that was not on the balance now is.

The state symbols tell you which substances are gases.

CuCO3(s) → CuO(s) + CO2(g)

The carbon dioxide is a gas. Heated in an open tube, it escapes, so the mass of solid goes down.

In 2Mg(s) + O2(g) → 2MgO(s) the gas is a reactant. The oxygen comes from the air, so it was not weighed at the start. It joins the solid product, so the mass of solid goes up.

mass of gas lost = mass before − mass after

mass of gas gained = mass after − mass before

Both readings must be in the same unit before you subtract.

  • Wrong: Burning destroys some of the magnesium, so the ash weighs less.

    Right: Oxygen joins the magnesium, so the magnesium oxide weighs more.

  • Wrong: The reading falls because mass is lost in the reaction.

    Right: No atoms are lost. A gas escaped and is no longer being weighed.

  • Wrong: A sealed flask loses mass when a gas is made.

    Right: The gas cannot leave a sealed flask, so the reading stays the same.

Key fact

Some reactions may appear to involve a change in mass because a reactant or product is a gas and its mass has not been taken into account. A gas that escapes from an open container lowers the reading, and oxygen from the air that joins a metal raises it.

On Higher you can work out the mass a metal gains from the balanced equation, using moles. Step 1 finds the moles of metal. Step 2 uses the ratio in the equation. Step 3 turns moles of oxygen back into a mass.

  • Predict: say whether the reading rises, falls or stays the same.
  • Explain: name the gas, say whether it left or joined, and use particles.
  • Calculate: subtract the readings, in the same unit, and give the unit.
