Key knowledge

Chemical amounts are measured in moles. The symbol for the unit mole is mol.

The mass of one mole of a substance in grams is numerically equal to its relative formula mass (Mr). For an element, use its relative atomic mass (Ar).

  • One mole of carbon, C (Ar 12), has a mass of 12 g.
  • One mole of water, H2O (Mr 18), has a mass of 18 g.
  • One mole of sodium chloride, NaCl (Mr 58.5), has a mass of 58.5 g.

One mole of a substance contains the same number of the stated particles, atoms, molecules or ions as one mole of any other substance.

The number of atoms, molecules or ions in a mole of a given substance is the Avogadro constant. The value of the Avogadro constant is 6.02 × 10²³ per mole.

Moles can count atoms, molecules, ions, electrons, formulae and equations. In one mole of carbon (C) the number of atoms is the same as the number of molecules in one mole of carbon dioxide (CO2).

One mole of water contains 6.02 × 10²³ molecules.

Each water molecule has three atoms: two hydrogen and one oxygen.

So one mole of water contains 3 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.806 × 10²⁴ atoms.

n = m ÷ Mr

The mass must be in grams. Convert kilograms first: 1 kg = 1000 g.

You need to remember this equation.

N = n × 6.02 × 10²³

The Avogadro constant is a fixed number, so it is never the thing you find.

6.02 × 10²³ is 602 followed by 21 zeros.

To multiply, multiply the front number: 2 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 12.04 × 10²³.

Then keep the front number between 1 and 10: 12.04 × 10²³ = 1.204 × 10²⁴.

To divide two numbers with the same power of ten, divide the front numbers: 3.01 × 10²³ ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ = 0.5.

Always go through moles.

  • Particles to mass: n = particles ÷ 6.02 × 10²³, then m = n × Mr.
  • Mass to particles: n = m ÷ Mr, then particles = n × 6.02 × 10²³.

  • Wrong: A mole of anything has the same mass.

    Right: A mole of anything has the same number of particles. Its mass in grams is its Mr.

  • Wrong: 2.7 kg of aluminium is 2.7 ÷ 27 = 0.1 mol.

    Right: Convert to grams first: 2700 ÷ 27 = 100 mol.

  • Wrong: n = Mr ÷ mass.

    Right: n = mass ÷ Mr.

  • Wrong: One mole of water contains 6.02 × 10²³ atoms.

    Right: It contains 6.02 × 10²³ molecules, which is 3 × 6.02 × 10²³ atoms.

Key fact

Chemical amounts are measured in moles (mol). One mole of any substance contains 6.02 × 10²³ particles (the Avogadro constant), and its mass in grams equals its relative formula mass, so number of moles = mass (g) ÷ Mr.

Rearrange the equation: Mr = mass ÷ number of moles.

0.5 mol of a substance has a mass of 20 g, so its Mr is 20 ÷ 0.5 = 40.

Percentage by mass of an element in a compound is in Relative formula mass.

Using moles to work out reacting masses is in Amounts of substances in equations.

  • Calculate: show each line, with the mass in grams.
  • Give your answer in standard form: front number between 1 and 10.
