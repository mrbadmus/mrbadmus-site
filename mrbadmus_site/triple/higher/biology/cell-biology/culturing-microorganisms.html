Key words

Key knowledge

Bacteria multiply by simple cell division, called binary fission. This can happen as often as once every 20 minutes if they have enough nutrients and a suitable temperature. Each division doubles the number of bacteria.

Bacteria can be grown in a nutrient broth solution, or as colonies on an agar gel plate.

Uncontaminated cultures of microorganisms are needed to investigate the action of disinfectants and antibiotics. Aseptic technique keeps a culture uncontaminated.

Aseptic technique

RowWhy
Sterilise Petri dishes and culture media before use
Pass the inoculating loop through a flame
Secure the lid with a few strips of tape
Store the dish upside down
Incubate at 25 °C in school

  • Use aseptic technique to spread bacteria evenly over an agar plate.
  • Place paper discs soaked in different antibiotics or antiseptics on the agar, and a control disc soaked in sterile water.
  • Keep the disc size, the concentration and the incubation time the same. Tape the lid, store the dish upside down and incubate at 25 °C.
  • Measure the diameter of the clear zone around each disc. This is the zone of inhibition, where the bacteria have not grown.

The bigger the clear zone, the more effective the antibiotic or antiseptic. In the drawing, B is the most effective, and C did not stop these bacteria growing.

Measure the diameter, then halve it to get the radius r. Keep every length in mm, so the area is in mm²: cm × 10 gives mm.

  • Step 1: number of divisions = time ÷ mean division time. Put both times in minutes first.
  • Step 2: double the starting number once for each division. This is the same as starting number × 2ⁿ, where n is the number of divisions.

One bacterium dividing every 20 minutes for 3 hours: 180 ÷ 20 = 9 divisions, and 1 × 2⁹ = 512 bacteria.

Give a large number of bacteria in standard form. Move the decimal point until one digit is in front of it, and count the places moved.

  • 512 = 5.12 × 10²
  • 2 560 000 = 2.56 × 10⁶

Key fact

Bacteria multiply by binary fission, as often as once every 20 minutes, so each division doubles the number. Aseptic technique keeps a culture uncontaminated, and the bigger the clear zone around a disc, the more effective the antibiotic or antiseptic.

The control disc is the same paper, soaked in sterile water, with only the antibiotic left out. If the bacteria grow right up to its edge, the paper and the water are ruled out, so a clear zone round another disc is down to the antibiotic on it.

Antibiotics, and bacteria that are resistant to them, are in Antibiotics and painkillers.

  • Explain why for each aseptic step: the step, then what it prevents.
  • Calculate an area: halve, then πr². A number of bacteria: divide, then double.
  • Compare antibiotics: the sizes of the clear zones, in a fair test, against the control.
