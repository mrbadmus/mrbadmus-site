The carbon cycle

The carbon in your body was once carbon dioxide in the air, and one day it will be again. What carries it all the way round?

Start here

A fallen leaf rots away. Its carbon does not.

A leaf that drops in autumn has mostly vanished a year later. Yet the amount of carbon on Earth never changes: carbon atoms are not made or destroyed, only moved.

Commit first

So where did the leaf's carbon go?

Because the total amount of carbon is fixed, it has to be recycled. It sits in stores: the air, as carbon dioxide; living things, in carbohydrates, proteins, fats and DNA; dead matter and waste; and fossil fuels deep underground. Processes move it from store to store. Here, follow one atom and call each process before it happens.

For thousands of years before people burned fossil fuels on a large scale, carbon dioxide was taken out of the air about as fast as it was put back. Humans are now adding it faster than natural processes can remove it. Burning coal, oil and gas releases carbon that was locked away for millions of years. Deforestation does double damage: fewer trees are left to photosynthesise, and the felled trees are burned or rot, releasing more carbon dioxide. Cattle and rice fields add methane. Rising levels of carbon dioxide and methane contribute to global warming.

Key fact

Photosynthesis takes carbon dioxide out of the air. Feeding passes carbon along food chains. Respiration by every organism, including decomposers, and combustion return it to the air.

Key note · AQA 4.7.2.2 (8461)

The carbon cycle

  1. 01Photosynthesis takes carbon dioxide out of the air.
  2. 02Feeding passes carbon compounds along food chains.
  3. 03Respiration by plants, animals and decomposers returns carbon dioxide.
  4. 04Combustion of fossil fuels returns carbon locked away for millions of years.
  5. 05Deforestation and burning add carbon dioxide faster than it is removed.
