The carbon cycle

Key words

  • carbon cycle
  • photosynthesis
  • respiration
  • combustion

Key knowledge

Many different materials cycle through the living (biotic) and non-living (abiotic) parts of an ecosystem.

All materials in the living world are recycled to provide the building blocks for future organisms.

Carbon is one of these materials. The carbon cycle shows how it moves.

Plants and algae take in carbon dioxide from the atmosphere for photosynthesis.

The carbon becomes part of glucose. The plant uses the glucose to make its other carbon compounds, such as starch, cellulose, proteins and fats.

When an animal eats a plant, the carbon compounds in the plant pass into the animal.

When that animal is eaten, the carbon passes on again.

Three processes return carbon to the atmosphere as carbon dioxide.

  • Respiration by plants, animals and microorganisms.
  • Decay: microorganisms feed on dead plants, dead animals and animal waste. As they respire, they release the carbon as carbon dioxide.
  • Combustion: burning wood or fossil fuels.

Some dead organisms did not decay, because the conditions needed for decay were missing.

Over millions of years their remains became fossil fuels: coal, oil and gas.

Burning fossil fuels releases this carbon into the atmosphere as carbon dioxide.

The carbon cycle returns carbon from organisms to the atmosphere as carbon dioxide, to be used by plants in photosynthesis.

Plants need that carbon dioxide to make glucose. Every animal depends on the food that plants make.

Without decay, the carbon would stay locked in dead organisms.

  • Wrong: Plants take in carbon dioxide but never give it out.

    Right: Plants respire all the time, day and night, so they also release carbon dioxide.

  • Wrong: Decay takes carbon dioxide out of the air.

    Right: Decay releases carbon dioxide, because the microorganisms respire.

  • Wrong: Plants get their carbon from the soil.

    Right: Plants get their carbon from carbon dioxide in the air.

  • Wrong: Carbon is used up when an organism dies.

    Right: Carbon is never used up. It is recycled.

Key fact

The carbon cycle returns carbon from organisms to the atmosphere as carbon dioxide, by respiration, decay and combustion. Plants take in the carbon dioxide for photosynthesis, so the same carbon is used again and again.

Burning fossil fuels releases carbon that was locked away for millions of years.

Cutting down and burning forests releases carbon dioxide, and leaves fewer trees to take it in.

Both raise the level of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere. The Global warming lesson explains why that matters.

  • Describe: say how the carbon moves, naming each process.
  • Explain: say what happens and why, for example that decay releases carbon dioxide because microorganisms respire.
  • Interpret a diagram: name the process on each arrow and say which way the carbon moves.
