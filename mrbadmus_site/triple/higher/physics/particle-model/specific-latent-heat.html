Changes of state and specific latent heat

Steam and boiling water are both at 100 °C, yet steam does far more damage. Where is the extra energy, and how do you calculate it?

Start here

Same temperature, worse burn.

A splash of boiling water on your hand hurts. A puff of steam from a kettle spout, at the same 100 °C, can leave a much worse burn.

Commit first

Why is the steam worse?

When a substance changes state, the energy supplied changes its internal energy but not its temperature. The energy needed to change state is called latent heat. The specific latent heat of a substance is the energy needed to change the state of 1 kg of it with no change in temperature. Melting, solid to liquid, takes the specific latent heat of fusion. Boiling, liquid to vapour, takes the specific latent heat of vaporisation. Freezing and condensing give the same energy back. For water, fusion takes 334 000 J/kg and vaporisation 2 260 000 J/kg.

Equations · choose by what is changing

On the sheet

E = m L

State changing, temperature constant. Energy for a change of state (J) = mass (kg) × specific latent heat (J/kg).

m = E ÷ L · L = E ÷ m

On the sheet

ΔE = m c Δθ

Temperature changing, state constant. c is the specific heat capacity (J/kg °C).

On the sheet

E = P t

Energy transferred at a steady rate: power (W) × time (s).

On a temperature–time graph, a sloping section means the temperature is changing: ΔE = m c Δθ. A flat section means the state is changing: E = m L. When energy goes in or out at a steady rate, the length of a flat section measures the energy, through E = P t.

Key fact

State changing at constant temperature: E = m L. Temperature changing: ΔE = m c Δθ. Specific latent heat is the energy needed to change the state of 1 kg with no change in temperature.

Key note · AQA 4.3.2.3 (8463)

Changes of state and specific latent heat

  1. 01E = mL.
  2. 02No temperature change during state change — energy goes into PE (breaking bonds).
  3. 03Fusion: solid ↔ liquid.
  4. 04Vaporisation: liquid ↔ gas.
  5. 05Lv > Lf (more energy to boil than melt).
  6. 06Water Lf = 334 kJ/kg; Lv = 2260 kJ/kg.
  7. 07Steam burns worse than boiling water — releases latent heat on condensing.
