Waves for detection and exploration

Triple Science · Higher tier
    View the full equation sheetAQA · GCSE Physics (8463) · June 2026 · PDF

    Start here

    Nobody has ever drilled down to the Earth's core.

    If you had to guess, what gives us evidence about what the core is like?

    Key words

    • ultrasound
    • echo
    • P-wave
    • S-wave

    Key knowledge

    The differences in velocity, absorption and reflection between different types of wave in solids and liquids can be used both for detection and exploration of structures which are hidden from direct observation.

    Ultrasound waves have a frequency higher than the upper limit of hearing for humans, above 20 kHz.

    • Ultrasound waves are partially reflected when they meet a boundary between two different media. The rest travels on.
    • The time taken for the reflections to reach a detector can be used to determine how far away such a boundary is.
    • This allows ultrasound waves to be used for both medical and industrial imaging, such as scanning an unborn baby or finding a crack inside a metal beam.

    The deeper the boundary, the later its echo arrives.

    distance travelled = speed × time, s = v t

    • s: distance travelled, in metres (m)
    • v: speed, in metres per second (m/s)
    • t: time, in seconds (s)

    The echo travels to the boundary and back, so the depth is half of the distance travelled.

    Echo sounding, using high frequency sound waves, is used to detect objects in deep water and measure water depth.

    A ship sends a pulse down and times the echo from the seabed or from a shoal of fish.

    • Seismometers around the world record the P-waves and S-waves from an earthquake.
    • No S-waves are detected on the far side of the Earth. S-waves cannot travel through a liquid, so part of the core must be liquid: the outer core.
    • P-waves change speed as they enter and leave the core, so they refract. This leaves a band of the surface where no P-waves arrive.
    • The size of these regions, called shadow zones, shows how big the core is.

    In the figure, blue paths are P-waves and gold paths are S-waves. A filled letter at a seismometer means that wave was recorded there; a dashed one means it was not.

    The study of seismic waves provided new evidence that led to discoveries about parts of the Earth which are not directly observable.

    Key fact

    Ultrasound is partially reflected at a boundary between two different media, and the time for the echo to return gives the distance to the boundary. P-waves are longitudinal and travel through solids and liquids, but S-waves are transverse and cannot travel through a liquid, so seismic waves give evidence for the structure and size of the Earth's core.

    Seismic waves travel on curved paths through the Earth. Their speed changes gradually with depth, so they refract all the way.

    Ultrasound is used instead of X-rays to scan an unborn baby. X-rays are ionising radiation, which can cause the mutation of genes.

    • Calculate a depth: s = v t, then halve it for the trip there and back.
    • Explain the evidence: name the wave, say what it can or cannot travel through, then say what that shows about the Earth.
