The classroom demonstration

Molten lead bromide.

Solid lead bromide in a crucible with two graphite electrodes: no current. Heat it until it melts and the circuit completes. A grey bead of lead collects at the cathode; red-brown bromine vapour comes off the anode. Bromine is toxic, so this is a teacher demonstration in a fume cupboard (anhydrous zinc chloride is a safer alternative). It is not a required practical: the electrolysis practical uses aqueous solutions.

Key fact

When a simple ionic compound is electrolysed in the molten state using inert electrodes, the metal is produced at the cathode and the non-metal at the anode.

Key note · AQA 5.4.3.2 (+ 5.4.3.5 HT) · Combined and Triple

Electrolysis of molten ionic compounds

  1. 01When a simple ionic compound (such as lead bromide) is electrolysed in the molten state using inert electrodes, the metal is produced at the cathode and the non-metal is produced at the anode.
  2. 02Molten: only the compound’s own ions are present, so prediction is simple. NaCl gives sodium and chlorine; PbBr2 gives lead and bromine; Al2O3 gives aluminium and oxygen.
  3. 03The solid does not conduct because its ions are fixed; melting frees them. Overall equations: 2NaCl(l) → 2Na(l) + Cl2(g); PbBr2(l) → Pb(l) + Br2(g).
  4. 04Lead bromide is a teacher demonstration in a fume cupboard (bromine is toxic; zinc chloride is a safer alternative). It is not a required practical: the electrolysis practical uses aqueous solutions.
