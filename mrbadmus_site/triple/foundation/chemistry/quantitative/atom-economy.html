AQA Chemistry (8462) 4.3.3.2 · Quantitative

Atom economy

Triple Science · Foundation tier
  • Triple Science · Higher tier

Start here

Factory A makes ethanol by fermenting sugar: C6H12O6 → 2C2H5OH + 2CO2. Factory B adds steam to ethene: C2H4 + H2O → C2H5OH.

If you had to guess, which factory turns more of its starting mass into ethanol?

Key words

  • atom economy
  • desired product
  • by-product
  • sustainable development

Key knowledge

The atom economy (atom utilisation) is a measure of the amount of starting materials that end up as useful products.

It comes from the balanced equation, so you can work it out before the reaction is ever run.

% atom economy = (relative formula mass of desired product from equation ÷ sum of relative formula masses of all reactants from equation) × 100

  • Use the balancing numbers: 2Fe counts as 2 × 56 = 112.
  • Add up every reactant on the bottom, not just one.
  • Mass is conserved, so the sum of the Mr of the reactants equals the sum of the Mr of the products.

You need to remember this equation.

Three reactions (Mr or Ar in brackets)

RowWorkingAtom economy
Making ethanol: C2H4 (28) + H2O (18) → C2H5OH (46)
Making calcium oxide: CaCO3 (100) → CaO (56) + CO2 (44)
Making iron: Fe2O3 (160) + 3CO (28) → 2Fe (56) + 3CO2 (44)

When a reaction makes only one product, such as an addition reaction, every atom in the reactants ends up in the desired product.

Its atom economy is 100%.

It is important for sustainable development and for economic reasons to use reactions with high atom economy.

  • Less of the starting materials is wasted, so raw materials last longer.
  • There is less waste to deal with.
  • It costs less to make the same mass of product.

  • Wrong: Divide by the mass of the waste product.

    Right: Divide by the sum of the Mr of all the reactants.

  • Wrong: 2NaCl → 2Na + Cl2, so the top is 23.

    Right: The equation makes 2Na, so the top is 2 × 23 = 46.

  • Wrong: Atom economy is how much product you collect.

    Right: That is percentage yield. Atom economy comes from the equation.

  • Wrong: An atom economy of more than 100%.

    Right: The desired product can never have more mass than all the reactants.

Key fact

% atom economy = (Mr of the desired product from the equation ÷ sum of the Mr of all the reactants) × 100. A high atom economy means less waste, which matters for sustainable development and for economic reasons.

Electrolysing brine: 2NaCl + 2H2O → 2NaOH + Cl2 + H2.

The reactants add up to 2 × 58.5 + 2 × 18 = 153.

  • Sodium hydroxide: 80 ÷ 153 × 100 = 52.3%
  • Chlorine: 71 ÷ 153 × 100 = 46.4%
  • Hydrogen: 2 ÷ 153 × 100 = 1.3%

All three products are sold, so very little of the mass is wasted.

  • Calculate: show the working from the equation and give the answer as a percentage, usually to 3 significant figures.
  • Give: one short answer.
  • Explain: give the reason as linked steps.
  • Evaluate (Higher): use the data for both sides, weigh them, and end with a justified conclusion.
