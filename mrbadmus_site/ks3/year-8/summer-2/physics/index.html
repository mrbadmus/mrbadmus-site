Electric circuits

Put an ammeter either side of a bulb and both read the same. Follow that one fact from a torch to a junction, a voltmeter, an ohm and a cable that is copper inside and plastic outside.

  1. 03Current at a junctionSystemThree ammeters round one split in the wire. Two of the readings always add up to the third — whatever you put in the branches, and however lopsided the split.Open this lesson
  2. 04Potential differenceModelA voltmeter never goes in a circuit. It goes across a part of one, and it answers a different question from an ammeter: not how much is flowing, but how hard it was pushed through there.Open this lesson
  3. 05ResistanceQuantitativeResistance is not something you measure directly. You measure the push, you measure the flow, and you divide one by the other. That ratio is the number.Open this lesson
  4. 06Conductors and insulatorsClassifyConductor and insulator are not two kinds of thing. They are the two ends of one number, and that number runs across more than a million million.Open this lesson
  5. 07Building and measuring a circuitInvestigationA meter in the wrong place does not give you a wrong reading. It gives you a different circuit, and then a perfectly correct reading of that.Open this lesson
