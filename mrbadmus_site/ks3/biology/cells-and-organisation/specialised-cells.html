Specialised cells

Start here

A red blood cell starts out with a nucleus. As it matures, it pushes the nucleus out.

If you had to guess, what does the cell gain by losing its nucleus?

More room. The space fills with haemoglobin, which carries oxygen.

Key words

  • A cell with a shape or parts that suit it to one job.

  • A feature that helps a cell or an organism do its job.

  • The spreading out of particles from a high concentration to a low concentration.

  • The area of the outside surface of something.

Key knowledge

Most cells in a plant or animal are specialised. Each is suited to one job.

It has the same basic parts as other cells.

It may have more of some parts, fewer of others, or a special shape.

Some have extra parts too, such as a tail or cilia.

Without a nucleus, a red blood cell cannot repair itself or divide.

It lasts about 120 days. Bone marrow keeps making new ones.

Substances get into a cell through its surface.

More surface lets substances get in faster.

A root hair's long thin shape gives a much larger surface than a round cell.

The hair also reaches between soil particles, where the water is.

The plant needs water to stay firm, and minerals to grow.

A red blood cell's disc shape gives more surface than a ball of equal volume.

Particles spread out from where there are many to where there are few.

This is diffusion. It goes from a high concentration to a low concentration.

Diffusion needs no energy from the cell.

Cells use oxygen for respiration, so the concentration inside stays low.

So oxygen diffuses into cells through the cell membrane.

Respiration makes carbon dioxide. It diffuses out of the cell.

In the lungs, oxygen diffuses into red blood cells.

Mitochondria release energy by respiration.

Cells that work hard all the time have many of them.

Muscle cells, sperm cells, ciliated cells and root hair cells all do.

A root hair cell uses this energy to take in minerals.

Water moves in without the cell using energy.

  • Wrong: Specialised cells are made of different parts.

    Right: They have the same basic parts. Some add extras, like a tail.

  • Wrong: A red blood cell is not a real cell, as it has no nucleus.

    Right: It is a cell. It pushed out its nucleus to make room for haemoglobin.

  • Wrong: Every cell in your body has a nucleus.

    Right: Red blood cells have none.

  • Wrong: Diffusion needs energy.

    Right: Diffusion needs no energy. Particles spread out from high to low concentration.

Key fact

Specialised cells have adaptations that suit them to one job. Examples are a special shape or many mitochondria. Oxygen and carbon dioxide move in and out of cells by diffusion. Diffusion is from a high concentration to a low concentration.

Rung 1

A student says a red blood cell is not really a cell, because it has no nucleus. What is the best reply?

Rung 4

Design a cell to line your windpipe and stop dust reaching your lungs. Say which parts it would have more of. Say what would go wrong in the body if it stopped working. You may invent a structure, but say which problem it solves.

The nerve cell from your spine to your toe can be over a metre long.

Each jump from one cell to the next slows a signal down.

One long cell carries the signal faster than a chain of short ones.
