Life processes and what living things are made of

A candle flame moves, grows, feeds and makes waste. So what stops it being alive?

Start here

Watch a candle for a minute

It moves. Feed the wick and it grows. It takes in oxygen and gives out carbon dioxide, water and soot. Open a door and it leans away. Touch a second wick to it and there are two flames.

Motion

Is a candle flame alive?

It is not alive — and the reason is not on that list of behaviours. A flame does nearly all of them. What settles it is what a flame is made of.

Biologists describe living things by seven life processes. Their initials spell MRS GREN.

  • MMovement
  • RRespiration
  • SSensitivity
  • GGrowth
  • RReproduction
  • EExcretion
  • NNutrition

Key fact

The seven processes describe what living things do. They do not decide whether something is alive.

“If it moves and grows, it must be alive.”

What settles it

Every living thing is made of cells.

  • A candle flame

    Hot glowing gas. No cells, so not alive — whatever it does.

  • An acorn

    Cells, packed with a food store and ticking over slowly.

  • You

    Around thirty trillion cells, all working at once.

One cell is enough — yeast manages on one. You are somewhere near thirty trillion. A flame has none, so however it behaves, it never joins the club.

Your turn · sort all eight

Living, once living, never living

Three boxes, not two. Something can be made of cells that stopped working a long time ago.

  • Moss on a wall

    Living — Green, growing, made of cells that are working right now.

  • Yeast in bread dough

    Living — Single cells, feeding on sugar and budding into new ones.

  • A woodlouse under a stone

    Living — Moves, feeds, breeds — and it is built from cells.

  • A wooden spoon

    Once living — Wood is the leftover walls of tree cells. The cells themselves died when the tree was cut.

  • A leather boot

    Once living — Skin is a tissue. Tanning preserves it long after the animal has gone.

  • A lump of coal

    Once living — Squashed plants from around three hundred million years ago. No cells left, but it was part of living things.

  • A granite pebble

    Never living — Crystals that grew from cooling molten rock. No cells were ever involved.

  • A candle flame

    Never living — Hot glowing gas doing six of the seven processes and made of no cells at all.

Key note

The seven life processes — MRS GREN — describe what living things do. Being made of one or more cells is what makes a thing living.

Going further

A virus is not made of cells, and on its own it does none of the seven — it cannot even copy itself without getting inside a cell. So most biologists do not count viruses as living. A few argue the line is in the wrong place, and that argument is still open.
