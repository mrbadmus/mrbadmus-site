Animal and plant cells

Start here

Cells can have seven parts. Think of a cheek cell and an oak leaf cell.

If you had to guess, does a cheek cell have anything a leaf cell lacks?

Of the seven parts, all of a cheek cell's are in a leaf cell. The leaf cell has three more.

Key words

  • Contains the genetic material that controls what the cell does.

  • A thin layer around every cell. It controls what goes in and out.

  • A strong layer outside the membrane of a plant cell. It is made of cellulose.

  • The part of a plant cell where light is absorbed to make food.

Key knowledge

Plant cells also have a cell wall and a large vacuole.

Most cells in the green parts of a plant have chloroplasts.

A plant cell has both. The wall is on the outside, with the membrane just inside it.

Water and dissolved substances pass straight through the wall.

The membrane controls what gets into the cell and what leaves it.

In pure water, cells take in water and swell.

An animal cell can burst. The wall stops a plant cell bursting.

The vacuole is full of cell sap. It pushes out against the cell wall.

This keeps the cell firm and helps hold the plant up.

When a plant is short of water, its vacuoles shrink.

The cells go soft and the plant wilts.

Chloroplasts are only made in cells that get light.

Root cells have no chloroplasts, because no light reaches them.

Onion bulb cells have none either.

A plant cell with no chloroplasts is still a plant cell.

Plants are not the only living things with cell walls.

Fungi, such as yeast, have cell walls. So do bacteria.

Their walls are not made of cellulose.

So a wall on its own does not prove a cell came from a plant.

At ×400 you can see a leaf cell's wall, nucleus, vacuole and chloroplasts.

You cannot see its mitochondria or its membrane. They are still there.

A chloroplast is several times wider than a mitochondrion.

Mitochondria are too small to see clearly with a school microscope.

The membrane is only a few molecules thick. It is pressed against the wall.

In a cheek cell, the edge you see marks where the membrane is.

A light microscope cannot show detail below a certain size.

This limit is called its resolution.

Turning up the magnification then only gives a bigger, blurrier image.

Most cell parts are almost see-through, so stains are added.

Methylene blue stains a cheek cell. Its nucleus becomes the darkest thing you see.

Iodine solution is used to stain onion cells.

To get cheek cells, rub a clean cotton bud inside your cheek. Smear it on a slide.

  • Wrong: A plant cell has a wall instead of a membrane.

    Right: A plant cell has both. The membrane is just inside the wall.

  • Wrong: Every plant cell has chloroplasts.

    Right: Only cells that get light have them. Root cells have none.

  • Wrong: If you cannot see mitochondria, the cell has none.

    Right: They are there. They are too small to see with a school microscope.

  • Wrong: Only plant cells have walls.

    Right: Fungi and bacteria have cell walls too.

Key fact

Animal and plant cells both have a nucleus, cytoplasm, a cell membrane and mitochondria. Plant cells also have a cell wall, a large vacuole and chloroplasts. Only plant cells that get light have chloroplasts.

Rungs 3 and 4 you mark yourself.

Rung 1

A cell has a membrane, cytoplasm, a nucleus and mitochondria. It has no wall, large vacuole or chloroplasts. Which of these could it be?

Rung 2

A student says a root hair cell cannot be a plant cell, because it is not green. Which answer best explains what is wrong with the reasoning?

Rung 3

Down a school microscope you can see a leaf cell’s wall, nucleus, vacuole and chloroplasts. You cannot see its mitochondria or its membrane. Explain why not.

Rung 4

A single cell is found in pond water. It has a cell wall, a large vacuole, cytoplasm, a nucleus and mitochondria. It has no chloroplasts. Can you say it came from a plant? Say what you can conclude and what you cannot.

Cells have other tiny parts too, such as ribosomes.

An electron microscope can show these, and the membrane, clearly.

Celery is stringy because of cellulose in its cell walls.

Paper is made mostly of cellulose from wood.
