Food tests

Start here

Two tubes hold the same milk and Benedict's solution, in one hot water bath. Tube 1 is heated for five minutes and turns brick red. Tube 2 is heated for thirty seconds and stays blue.

If you had to guess, what does tube 2 on its own tell you about the milk?

Tube 1 shows the milk does contain sugar. Tube 2 was not heated for long enough.

Right. Thirty seconds is too short for the test to work. So tube 2 proves nothing.

Key words

  • A test that shows whether a food contains a particular nutrient.

  • A chemical you add in a test. It changes colour if the substance is there.

  • The colour change that shows the nutrient is there.

  • A cloudy mixture of tiny drops of one liquid spread through another.

Key knowledge

A food test shows whether a nutrient is in a food.

You add a reagent to the food. If the nutrient is there, the colour changes.

That colour change is a positive result.

Each test finds one nutrient only.

Crush a solid food with a little water, using a pestle and mortar.

Let the bits settle, or filter it. Test the liquid.

Use a clean test tube and a fresh sample for each test.

The four food tests

Add a few drops of iodine solution to the food.

Iodine solution is orange-brown.

If starch is there, it turns blue-black. No heating is needed.

Add Benedict's solution to the food solution. It is blue.

Heat the tube in a hot water bath for about five minutes.

If sugar is there, it turns green, yellow, orange or brick red.

Without heating, Benedict's solution stays blue.

Add Biuret solution to the food solution. It is blue.

If protein is there, it turns purple. Some books call this lilac.

No heating is needed.

Shake the food with ethanol. Let it settle.

Pour the ethanol into a tube of water.

If fat is there, a cloudy white layer forms. This is an emulsion.

If there is no fat, the water stays clear.

No colour change means the test did not detect the nutrient.

A small amount may still be there. It can be too little to show.

Potato is about 2% protein, but Biuret solution usually stays blue.

So write ‘protein was not detected’, and not ‘there is no protein’.

Chewed bread can test positive for sugar.

Saliva has broken some of the starch down into sugar.

A food test shows whether a nutrient is there.

Benedict's colours give a rough idea of the amount of sugar.

Green means a little sugar. Brick red means a lot.

This only works if every tube is heated for the same time.

No food test measures exactly how much is there.

  • Wrong: A darker colour always means more of the nutrient.

    Right: The colour also depends on heating time and how much reagent you add.

  • Wrong: No colour change proves the nutrient is not there.

    Right: A small amount can be too little for the test to detect.

  • Wrong: Benedict's solution works without heating.

    Right: Benedict's solution must be heated in a hot water bath.

  • Wrong: Iodine solution turns blue-black with sugar.

    Right: Iodine solution turns blue-black with starch. Benedict's is the sugar test.

Key fact

Starch turns iodine solution blue-black. Sugar turns heated Benedict's solution from blue to green, yellow, orange or brick red. Protein turns Biuret solution purple. Fat gives a cloudy white emulsion with ethanol and water.

Rung 1

A student shakes a food sample with ethanol, then pours the ethanol into water. A cloudy white layer forms. What may they write down?

Rung 2

Bread turns iodine blue-black and leaves Benedict’s blue. A student chews some for a few minutes. Now iodine gives a weaker colour, and Benedict’s turns orange. What happened?

Rung 3

A group tests potato with Biuret solution. It stays blue. They write ‘potato contains no protein’. Potato is about 2% protein. Explain what is wrong with their conclusion. Write a better one. Say what you would change to detect the protein.

Rung 4

A cereal bar says it is ‘protein-packed and sugar-free’. You have iodine, Benedict's, ethanol and Biuret. Say which tests you would run. Say what result would support each claim. Say which claim these tests could never fully check.

Benedict's solution finds sugars such as glucose.

Table sugar does not change its colour, even when heated.

Boil table sugar with a little acid first. It splits into glucose and another sugar.

Neutralise the acid, then add Benedict's solution.

Now it gives a positive result.

To compare the sugar in two foods, keep everything else the same.

Use the same volumes, the same water bath and the same heating time.

Compare the colours with tubes that hold known amounts of sugar.

Ask MrBadmus

Unsure what a negative result lets you write down?

Wear eye protection. Biuret solution contains sodium hydroxide, which can harm your eyes and skin. Ethanol catches fire easily, so keep it away from flames. Never taste anything in the lab.
