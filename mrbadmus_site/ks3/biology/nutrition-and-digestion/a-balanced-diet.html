Nutrition and digestion · Classify

A balanced diet

Start here

Plate A is white rice, chicken, oil, water and salt. Plate B is the same food, plus one orange. Someone who eats only Plate A for months becomes very ill. Someone who eats Plate B does not get that illness.

If you had to guess, what in the orange saves the person on Plate B?

Yes. One orange has about 50 mg of vitamin C. Plate A has almost none.

The rice and oil already give plenty of energy. Plate A lacks vitamin C.

Key words

  • A substance in food that the body needs to stay alive and healthy.

  • A diet with every nutrient, each in the right amount.

  • The scientific word for fats and oils.

  • Plant material the body cannot digest. It keeps food moving through the gut.

Key knowledge

A healthy diet contains seven groups of nutrients:

  • carbohydrates
  • lipids (fats and oils)
  • proteins
  • vitamins
  • minerals
  • dietary fibre
  • water

The body needs every one of them.

Carbohydrates and lipids are the body's main sources of energy.

Lipids hold about twice as much energy per gram as carbohydrates.

Protein can be respired for energy too. The body mainly uses it for growth and repair.

Vitamins, minerals and water release no energy.

Your body cannot digest fibre, so you get almost no energy from it.

A balanced diet has each nutrient in the right amount.

The right amounts are very different. A 13-year-old needs roughly this much a day:

  • water: about 2 litres, from food and drink
  • carbohydrate: about 300 g
  • lipid: about 70 g
  • protein: about 45 g
  • dietary fibre: about 25 g
  • minerals: several grams in total
  • vitamins: less than 1 g in total

Too much or too little of any nutrient causes health problems.

Vitamins and minerals are needed in small amounts. Some are needed in milligrams.

They release no energy. Each one has its own job.

Iron is needed to make haemoglobin. Haemoglobin carries oxygen in red blood cells.

Too little iron causes anaemia. Too little vitamin C causes scurvy.

An illness caused by a missing nutrient is a deficiency disease.

Your body cannot digest fibre, so it is not absorbed into the blood.

It gives the muscles of the gut something to push against.

This keeps food moving and prevents constipation.

You lose water all day, in urine, sweat and breath.

So you need to take in about 2 litres a day.

  • Wrong: A balanced diet means equal amounts of each nutrient.

    Right: It means the right amount of each. You need far more water than vitamins.

  • Wrong: Vitamins give you energy.

    Right: Vitamins release no energy. Carbohydrates, lipids and proteins do.

  • Wrong: A healthy diet has no fat in it.

    Right: Lipids are a nutrient. You need some for energy, warmth and cell membranes.

  • Wrong: Only people who do sport need protein.

    Right: Everyone needs protein for the growth and repair of cells.

Key fact

A balanced diet has all seven nutrients, each in the right amount. They are carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, vitamins, minerals, dietary fibre and water.

Vitamins A, D, E and K dissolve in fat.

The gut absorbs them along with the fat in a meal.

So a diet with no fat at all can leave you short of them.

Fats are solid at room temperature, like butter.

Oils are liquid at room temperature, like olive oil.

Both are lipids.
