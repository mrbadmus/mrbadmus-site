Nutrition and digestion · Process

Enzymes in digestion

Start here

Mix a teaspoon of amylase with a kilogram of starch. Wait, and the starch is all gone.

If you had to guess, is the amylase still there once the starch has gone?

Yes. An enzyme is a catalyst. It speeds up the reaction without being used up.

All of it is still there. An enzyme is not used up, so it can work again.

Key words

  • A protein, made by living cells, that speeds up a reaction.

  • Something that speeds up a reaction without being used up.

  • The substance an enzyme works on.

  • Describes an enzyme whose shape has changed for good, so it no longer works.

Key knowledge

A catalyst speeds up a reaction. It is not used up or changed by the reaction.

Enzymes are catalysts made by living cells. They are proteins.

So enzymes are called biological catalysts.

One enzyme molecule can be used again and again.

So a small amount of enzyme can digest a lot of food.

Large food molecules are too big to be absorbed into the blood.

Digestive enzymes break them into small molecules that can be absorbed.

Without enzymes, this would happen far too slowly.

The three digestive enzymes

RowBreaks downInto
Carbohydrase (amylase)
Protease
Lipase

  • Carbohydrase: the salivary glands, the pancreas and the small intestine.
  • Protease: the stomach, the pancreas and the small intestine.
  • Lipase: the pancreas and the small intestine.

All three work in the small intestine. That is where digestion is finished.

Each enzyme has a special shape.

Only one kind of substance fits that shape. This is its substrate.

So each enzyme works on one substrate. Amylase breaks down starch, but not protein.

The products leave, and the enzyme is ready to work again.

Enzymes in the body work fastest at body temperature, about 37 °C.

In the cold they work slowly. Warm them up and they work again.

Too much heat changes the enzyme's shape for good. It is denatured.

The substrate no longer fits, so the enzyme stops working.

Cooling it down again does not bring the shape back.

Each enzyme works best at one pH.

  • Mouth: about pH 7, neutral. Amylase in saliva works well here.
  • Stomach: about pH 2, very acidic. The protease made here works best in acid. Amylase stops working.
  • Small intestine: slightly alkaline. Amylase, protease and lipase from the pancreas work here.

The pancreas also releases an alkali. Bile helps too.

Together they neutralise the stomach acid in the small intestine.

  • Wrong: Enzymes are killed by heat.

    Right: Enzymes were never alive. Heat changes their shape, so they are denatured.

  • Wrong: The enzyme gets used up as the food is digested.

    Right: Enzymes are not used up. Each one works again and again.

  • Wrong: Cold destroys enzymes.

    Right: Cold only slows enzymes down. Warm them up and they work again.

  • Wrong: One enzyme can digest any food.

    Right: Each enzyme works on one substrate. Lipase digests fats, but not starch.

Key fact

Enzymes are biological catalysts. They speed up the breakdown of large food molecules into small ones. They are not used up.

Rung 1

After digesting a large amount of starch, how much of the original amylase is left?

Rung 2

Amylase at 70 °C digests no starch. It is cooled back to 37 °C and still digests no starch. Why not?

Rung 3

Amylase from saliva works in the mouth but stops in the stomach. Starch digestion starts again in the small intestine. Explain all three stages. Say what the pancreas adds besides enzymes.

Rung 4

A biological washing powder claims to work at any temperature. Predict what its enzymes do at 20 °C, at 40 °C and at 90 °C. Then plan a fair test of the claim.

Biological washing powder contains protease and lipase.

They digest stains made of protein, such as blood, and grease.

That is why they are used in a cool wash, not a very hot one.

A very hot wash would denature the enzymes.

Fresh pineapple contains a protease.

Jelly is set by a protein called gelatin. The protease breaks it down.

So jelly made with fresh pineapple does not set.

Tinned pineapple has been heated, which denatures the protease. Its jelly sets.
