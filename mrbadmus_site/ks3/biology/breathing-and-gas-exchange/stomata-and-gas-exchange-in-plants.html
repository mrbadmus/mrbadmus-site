Breathing and gas exchange · Contrast

Stomata and gas exchange in plants

Start here

A plant sealed in a jar shows a steady carbon dioxide reading at dawn.

If you had to guess, during those steady minutes, is the plant making or using any carbon dioxide at all?

Yes, it is doing both. Photosynthesis uses carbon dioxide as fast as respiration makes it.

Key words

  • stomata
  • guard cells

  • The spreading of a substance from where it is more concentrated to where it is less concentrated, with nothing pushing it.

Key knowledge

Stomata are tiny pores in a leaf. Most are on the underside.

In the light, carbon dioxide diffuses in through the stomata.

Oxygen and water vapour diffuse out.

A pair of guard cells sits around each stoma.

Water moves into the guard cells and they swell. They bend apart and the stoma opens.

Water moves out and they go limp. The stoma closes.

In bright light, the rate of photosynthesis is higher than the rate of respiration.

So overall the leaf takes in carbon dioxide.

In the dark, only respiration happens. So overall the leaf gives out carbon dioxide.

Respiration happens continuously. That means all the time.

Net means overall.

  • Air spaces inside the leaf give a large surface for diffusion.
  • The leaf is thin, so gases only travel a short distance.
  • Stomata let gases in and out.

Open stomata let water vapour escape.

A plant short of water closes its stomata.

This saves water, but it also stops carbon dioxide getting in.

  • Wrong: Plants breathe.

    Right: Plants have no lungs or breathing muscles. Gases move by diffusion.

  • Wrong: Plants only respire at night.

    Right: Plants respire all the time, day and night.

  • Wrong: Plants only ever take in carbon dioxide.

    Right: In the dark, a plant gives out carbon dioxide overall.

Key fact

Gases diffuse through stomata, which guard cells open and close. Plants respire all the time and photosynthesise only in light.

Want to check what the flat line at dawn actually means?

Rates on the bench are relative units chosen to make the two processes comparable, not measured values. The light level at which the two balance differs widely between species and conditions. B7 Photosynthesis owns the reaction itself; this lesson covers only the movement of gases.
