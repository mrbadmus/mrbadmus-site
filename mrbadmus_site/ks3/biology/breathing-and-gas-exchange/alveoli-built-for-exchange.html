Breathing and gas exchange · Model

Alveoli: built for exchange

Start here

If you had to guess, how big is the inside surface of your lungs?

About half a tennis court. Millions of tiny air sacs make a huge surface.

About half a tennis court. Millions of tiny air sacs make a huge surface.

Key words

  • One of the roughly 500 million tiny air sacs where gas crosses between the lungs and the blood.

  • The net movement of particles from where they are more concentrated to where they are less concentrated, caused by their random motion.

  • The smallest blood vessel, with a wall one cell thick, carrying blood past every alveolus.

  • concentration

Key knowledge

Air in the alveoli has a high concentration of oxygen.

Blood arriving at the lungs has a low concentration of oxygen.

So oxygen diffuses from the alveoli into the blood.

Carbon dioxide diffuses the other way, from the blood into the alveoli.

How alveoli are adapted

RowThe featureWhy it helps
Large surface area
Thin walls
Good blood supply
Moist lining

Breathing brings fresh air into the alveoli.

Blood flow carries oxygen away from them.

Together they keep the concentration difference high.

If either one stops, oxygen stops entering the blood overall.

  • Wrong: Oxygen is pumped into the blood.

    Right: Oxygen moves by diffusion. No pump is involved.

  • Wrong: Oxygen moves because it wants to spread out.

    Right: Particles move randomly. More cross from the high concentration side.

  • Wrong: Gases only cross in one direction.

    Right: Particles cross both ways. The overall movement follows the concentration difference.

Key fact

Alveoli have a large surface area, thin walls and a good blood supply. Oxygen diffuses into the blood and carbon dioxide diffuses out.

Rung 1

Rung 3

Explain why gas exchange needs both breathing and blood flow, and what happens if either one stops. Use the words diffusion and concentration difference, and do not use the words pump, suck or want.

Rung 4

At the top of a high mountain the air still contains 21% oxygen, yet climbers become short of oxygen. Using what you have learnt, explain why — and why breathing faster helps a little but does not solve it.

The small intestine absorbs food in the same way.

It also has a large surface area, thin walls and a good blood supply.

Air on a mountain is still 21% oxygen.

But the air is less dense, so each breath holds less oxygen.

The concentration difference is smaller, so less oxygen diffuses into the blood.

Breathing faster helps a little, but it cannot make the air denser.
