Respiration · Process

Anaerobic respiration in humans

Start here

A 100 m sprinter takes only a few breaths during the race.

If you had to guess, do the muscles stop using oxygen, or keep using it?

They keep using it too. They also break down extra glucose without oxygen.

They keep using it too. They also break down extra glucose without oxygen.

Key words

  • Respiration without oxygen. It releases much less energy from each glucose.

  • The product of anaerobic respiration in human muscles.

  • The extra oxygen needed after exercise to break down lactic acid.

Key knowledge

In a sprint, your muscles need energy very fast.

Your heart and lungs cannot bring oxygen fast enough.

Aerobic respiration carries on as fast as the oxygen allows.

Anaerobic respiration makes up the rest. It does not use oxygen.

Anaerobic means without oxygen.

The glucose is only partly broken down.

So it releases much less energy than aerobic respiration.

It makes no carbon dioxide and no water.

Lactic acid builds up in the muscles.

During long, hard exercise, the muscles get tired and stop contracting efficiently.

After hard exercise, you keep breathing hard and your heart keeps beating fast.

This brings extra oxygen to break down the lactic acid.

The oxygen needed to remove the lactic acid is called the oxygen debt.

Training makes the heart stronger. It pumps more blood with each beat.

More oxygen reaches the muscles during exercise.

So a fit person respires less anaerobically and makes less lactic acid.

Their oxygen debt is smaller, so they recover faster.

  • Wrong: In a sprint, muscles switch from aerobic to anaerobic respiration.

    Right: Aerobic respiration carries on. Anaerobic respiration adds to it.

  • Wrong: Anaerobic respiration in humans makes carbon dioxide.

    Right: In human muscles, it makes lactic acid only.

  • Wrong: Lactic acid makes your legs ache two days later.

    Right: That ache comes from tiny damage to muscle fibres. The lactic acid is long gone.

Key fact

When muscles cannot get enough oxygen, they also respire anaerobically. Glucose is broken down to lactic acid. This releases much less energy than aerobic respiration. The extra oxygen needed afterwards to break down the lactic acid is the oxygen debt.
