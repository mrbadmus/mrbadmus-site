Leaves built for the job

Start here

A leaf has tiny holes that let gases in and out.

If you had to guess, are most of these holes on top, or underneath?

On the underside. It is shaded and cooler there, so less water evaporates.

Key words

  • A feature that helps an organism survive where it lives.

  • Tall cells packed with chloroplasts, just under the top surface of a leaf.

  • Tiny holes in a leaf that let gases in and out. One is a stoma.

  • The waxy layer on the outside of a leaf. It reduces water loss.

Key knowledge

Carbon dioxide enters through a stoma on the underside.

It diffuses through the air spaces to the palisade cells.

Water arrives through the veins.

Carbon dioxide diffuses from the stomata to every cell.

In a thin leaf, this distance is short, so it arrives quickly.

Light also gets dimmer as it passes down through the leaf.

So extra cells deep in a thick leaf would get little light.

Adding more cells does not add much photosynthesis.

Stomata let carbon dioxide in. But water vapour escapes through them too.

The underside is shaded and cooler, so less water evaporates there.

Guard cells open the stomata in the light. They close them at night.

They also close them when the plant is short of water.

Features that help photosynthesis can also make a leaf lose more water.

So the best leaf depends on where the plant lives.

  • Dark forest floor: large, thin, dark green leaves catch more light. Water is plentiful, so losing water matters less.
  • Hot, dry places: small leaves, a thick cuticle and few stomata.
  • A cactus has spines instead of leaves. Its green stem photosynthesises.

Leaves that save water photosynthesise more slowly. So these plants grow slowly.

Chlorophyll absorbs mostly red and blue light.

It reflects much of the green light, so leaves look green.

So under green light alone, a plant photosynthesises slowly.

  • Wrong: Stomata only let oxygen out.

    Right: They let carbon dioxide in, and oxygen and water vapour out.

  • Wrong: Bigger leaves are always better.

    Right: Big leaves lose more water. The best leaf depends on the habitat.

  • Wrong: Carbon dioxide gets in through the cuticle.

    Right: It gets in through the stomata. The waxy cuticle lets very little gas through.

Key fact

A leaf is adapted for photosynthesis. It is broad to absorb light and thin so gases diffuse quickly. Palisade cells full of chloroplasts sit near the top surface. Stomata on the underside let carbon dioxide in.

Rung 1

Why are the palisade cells at the top of the leaf rather than the bottom?

Rung 2

Pondweed is put under a green filter, then a red filter. Both let through the same amount of light energy. It gives off far fewer bubbles under green. Which is the best explanation?

Rung 3

A leaf is usually less than half a millimetre thick. Explain why being thin helps. Then explain why a leaf twice as thick would not photosynthesise twice as fast.

Rung 4

Design a leaf for a plant on a dark rainforest floor, where water is plentiful. Then design one for a plant on a hot, dry hillside. Give three features of each. Explain each one using light and water loss. Say what each leaf gives up.

In autumn, many trees break down their chlorophyll.

Yellow and orange pigments were in the leaf all summer.

Without the green, these pigments now show.

Pine needles are small, tough and waxy.

They lose very little water, even in winter.

So a pine tree can keep its leaves all year.
