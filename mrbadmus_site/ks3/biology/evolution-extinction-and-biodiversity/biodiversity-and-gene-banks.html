Biodiversity and gene banks

Start here

A clone is an exact genetic copy. Cavendish bananas are grown from shoots of a parent plant, so every plant is identical.

If you had to guess, why could one disease wipe out a whole field of these bananas?

They all share one weak spot. Each plant is healthy, but every plant is the same.

Yes. Every plant is the same, so a disease that kills one can kill them all.

Key words

  • The variety of living things: different species, and the variation within each species.

  • An organism that is genetically identical to the one it came from.

  • A store of seeds, sperm, eggs or tissue, kept to save hereditary material.

  • Protecting species and their habitats so they are not lost.

Key knowledge

Biodiversity is the variety of living things.

It includes how many different species there are in an area.

It also includes the variation within each species.

Species depend on each other in food webs. Lose one, and others are affected.

Variation within a species helps it survive a change, such as a new disease.

People use many species for food, medicines and materials.

Each species lost takes genes with it that may have been useful.

In the 1840s, most potatoes in Ireland were one variety.

A disease called potato blight destroyed the crop, year after year.

About a million people died in the famine that followed.

In the 1950s, a fungus wiped out the Gros Michel banana on farms.

The same kind of fungus now attacks the Cavendish banana.

  • Protect habitats in nature reserves and national parks.
  • Breed endangered animals in zoos, and plants in botanic gardens.
  • Make laws against hunting and trading endangered species.
  • Plant hedgerows and leave field edges wild for other species.
  • Store hereditary material in gene banks.

A gene bank stores hereditary material, so variation is not lost.

Seed banks store dried seeds in freezers at about −18 °C.

Sperm, eggs and tissue from animals can be frozen too.

If a wild population is lost, stored material can grow new plants or animals.

The Svalbard Global Seed Vault holds over a million seed samples.

A seed can be stored. Its pollinators, soil fungi and habitat cannot.

Stored seeds stop changing, while the world outside keeps changing.

So a gene bank is a backup. Protecting wild habitats still matters.

  • Wrong: Biodiversity just means how many species there are.

    Right: It also includes the variation within each species.

  • Wrong: We have gene banks, so losing species in the wild does not matter.

    Right: A gene bank is a backup. It cannot store a species' habitat or pollinators.

  • Wrong: A field of a thousand plants has high biodiversity.

    Right: Not if they are clones. Identical plants have no variation.

  • Wrong: Gene banks store whole animals.

    Right: They store seeds, sperm, eggs and tissue.

Key fact

Biodiversity is the variety of living things. It includes the number of species and the variation within each one. Variation helps a species survive change. Gene banks store seeds, sperm, eggs and tissue, so hereditary material is not lost.
