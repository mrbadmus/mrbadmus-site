When the environment changes: extinction

Start here

Half of a wood is cut down. Brown rats and hazel dormice both live there.

If you had to guess, what helps an animal most when its home changes?

Yes. Fast breeders soon replace the animals they lose. Unfussy eaters still find food.

Breeding fast and eating anything helps most. Strength does not bring back lost food or habitat.

Key words

  • When a species dies out completely. No individuals are left alive anywhere.

  • At risk of becoming extinct.

  • The place where an organism lives.

  • Differences in genes between individuals of the same species.

Key knowledge

A species is extinct when no individuals of it are left alive anywhere.

Extinction is permanent. The species cannot come back.

The dodo and the dinosaurs are extinct.

A species at risk of extinction is endangered, such as the tiger.

Each species is adapted to its environment.

When the environment changes, some individuals may no longer be well adapted.

They cannot compete well, so fewer of them survive and reproduce.

If some have variations that suit the new conditions, the species may survive.

If none can survive and reproduce, the species becomes extinct.

After half the wood is cleared

Most species that have ever lived are now extinct.

Most of them died out long before there were humans.

There have been five mass extinctions, when most species died out in a short time.

Today species are dying out much faster than the natural rate.

Habitat destruction, hunting, introduced species and climate change are the main causes.

After a mass extinction, the number of species took millions of years to recover.

Meanwhile, the food web has lost a link, so other species are affected.

Each species also has its own mix of genes. Once lost, that mix is gone.

  • Protect its habitat, or make its reserve bigger.
  • Link cut-off habitats, for example with hedgerows. Then groups can meet and breed, which keeps genetic variation.
  • Breed it in zoos and release the young into the wild.
  • Ban hunting and trade in the species.

Captive breeding raises numbers. The animals still need a habitat to return to.

  • Wrong: Extinction only happens because of humans.

    Right: Most extinct species died out before humans existed. Humans are speeding it up now.

  • Wrong: If a species goes extinct, another one just takes its place.

    Right: Replacement can take millions of years. Meanwhile the food web loses a link.

  • Wrong: Big, strong animals are safe from extinction.

    Right: Size does not protect a species. Tigers and elephants are both endangered.

  • Wrong: Endangered means extinct.

    Right: Endangered means at risk. Extinct means none are left alive.

Key fact

A species becomes extinct when no individuals are left alive. This happens when the environment changes and too few individuals can survive and reproduce. Causes include new predators, new diseases, competitors, habitat destruction and climate change.

The kakapo is a large parrot from New Zealand that cannot fly.

New Zealand had no land mammals except bats. Birds that could not fly still survived.

When threatened, it freezes. That fools an eagle, but not a stoat that hunts by smell.

It breeds only in years when certain trees fruit heavily.

People brought rats, cats and stoats. The kakapo fell to 51 birds.

Every kakapo is now named and tracked. Numbers are slowly rising.
