Inheritance and DNA · Model

Chromosomes, genes and DNA

Start here

Uncoil the DNA from one of your cells, and it is about two metres long.

If you had to guess, how does two metres of DNA fit inside one tiny nucleus?

Yes. DNA is very thin, and it is coiled up tightly into chromosomes.

All two metres are inside the nucleus. DNA is very thin and tightly coiled.

Key words

  • The chemical that carries the genetic code. It is found in the nucleus.

  • The shape of DNA: two strands twisted round each other.

  • One long DNA molecule, coiled up tightly. It is found in the nucleus.

  • A section of DNA that carries the code for one characteristic.

Key knowledge

DNA is a chemical found in the nucleus of a cell.

It carries the instructions for building and running an organism.

These instructions are called the genetic code.

All living organisms have DNA.

A DNA molecule is made of two long strands.

The strands twist round each other. This shape is called a double helix.

Pairs of bases join the two strands, like the rungs of a ladder.

There are four bases, called A, T, C and G.

A always pairs with T. C always pairs with G.

The order of the bases along the DNA is the code.

The nucleus holds the chromosomes.

Each chromosome is one long DNA molecule, coiled up tightly.

A gene is a section of the DNA in a chromosome.

Each gene carries the code for one characteristic, such as blood group.

A chromosome is like a book. A gene is a chapter. The bases are the letters.

A human body cell has 46 chromosomes, in 23 pairs.

One chromosome of each pair came from your mother.

The other came from your father.

So you have two copies of most of your genes.

Every body cell with a nucleus has a full set of your chromosomes.

So a cell in your foot has the genes for eye colour too.

Each cell only uses the genes it needs. The others are switched off.

Red blood cells have no nucleus, so they have no DNA.

Apart from identical twins, everyone has a different order of bases.

So one cell with a nucleus can identify a person.

A sample can be damaged, or mixed with someone else's DNA.

  • Wrong: Chromosomes, genes and DNA are three different things in the nucleus.

    Right: A chromosome is coiled DNA. A gene is a section of that DNA.

  • Wrong: Only the cells that need a gene contain it.

    Right: Every body cell with a nucleus has every gene. Only some are switched on.

  • Wrong: A gene is bigger than a chromosome.

    Right: A chromosome carries many genes.

  • Wrong: DNA is a single strand.

    Right: DNA has two strands, twisted into a double helix.

Key fact

DNA is a chemical with a double helix shape that carries the genetic code. It is coiled into chromosomes in the nucleus. A gene is a section of DNA that codes for one characteristic.
