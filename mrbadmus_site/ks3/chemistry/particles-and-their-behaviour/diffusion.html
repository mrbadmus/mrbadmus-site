Diffusion

How does a smell get across the room with no wind?

Start here

Perfume at the front of the lab

A bottle is opened at the front of a still room. Hands go up row by row as the smell reaches each one.

Nothing is blowing it. So what is moving it?

Particles in a gas or a liquid never stop moving. They move in random directions. Where particles are crowded, more of them drift outwards than inwards. That is only because there are more of them there to start with. Nothing pushes them.

Think again

Demand: make the randomness visible, and the net effect countable

Count particles crossing the middle line, each way, for ten seconds.

Particles cross in BOTH directions. More cross from the crowded side only because more start there. The spreading is a result, not a goal.

Words to know

diffusion
The spreading out of particles from where there are many to where there are few, caused by their own random movement.
concentration
How many particles of a substance there are in a given space.
random
With no pattern and no set direction.

In science 'random' does not mean 'strange'. It means genuinely unpredictable, direction by direction.

Key note

Particles move randomly all the time. Where they are crowded, more happen to move outwards than inwards — so the substance spreads. Warmer means faster; liquids are much slower than gases. Nothing pushes and nothing intends it.

Going further

Diffusion is much slower in a liquid than in a gas. Liquid particles already touch. So each one is knocked off course at once. It takes far longer to get anywhere.
