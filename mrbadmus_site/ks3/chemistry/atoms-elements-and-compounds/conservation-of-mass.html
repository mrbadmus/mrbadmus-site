Conservation of mass

Start here

A candle burns all night. By morning, the wax has gone.

If you had to guess, where did the wax go?

Into gas in the air. The wax reacts with oxygen and leaves as invisible gases.

Key words

  • The total mass before a reaction equals the total mass after it.

  • A substance you start with in a reaction.

  • A substance a reaction makes.

  • A sealed container. No substance can get in or out.

Key knowledge

In a chemical reaction, atoms are rearranged into new substances.

No atoms are made or destroyed.

So the total mass stays the same.

The total mass of the reactants equals the total mass of the products.

This is called conservation of mass. It is true for every chemical reaction.

It is also true when a substance changes state.

50 g of ice melts to make 50 g of water.

2.40 g of magnesium burns in air. It makes 4.00 g of magnesium oxide.

  • mass of magnesium + mass of oxygen = mass of magnesium oxide
  • 2.40 g + mass of oxygen = 4.00 g
  • mass of oxygen = 4.00 g − 2.40 g = 1.60 g

The oxide is heavier, because oxygen from the air joined the magnesium.

Marble chips react with acid and give off carbon dioxide gas.

In an open flask, the gas escapes. The balance reading goes down.

In a sealed container, the gas cannot escape. The reading stays the same.

A sealed container is a closed system.

When the mass seems to change

RowBalance readingWhy
Marble chips and acid, open flask

Candle wax reacts with oxygen from the air.

It makes carbon dioxide and water vapour. Both are gases, and they escape.

So the candle loses mass.

The gases weigh more than the wax that burnt. They include the oxygen.

Burn a candle in a sealed container, and the total mass stays the same.

Heat and light are energy. They are not made of atoms.

Iron reacts with oxygen and water to make rust.

The extra mass is oxygen and water from the air, joined to the iron.

Most of that extra mass is oxygen.

So a rusty nail is heavier than the nail was.

Seal a nail in a jar of air. The total mass stays the same.

The oxygen and water it uses were already inside the jar.

  • Wrong: When something burns, its mass is destroyed.

    Right: Its atoms leave as gases. Their mass is still there.

  • Wrong: Heat and light carry the mass away.

    Right: Heat and light are energy. They have no mass to weigh.

  • Wrong: A rusty nail is heavier, so mass was made.

    Right: Oxygen and water from the air joined the iron. No mass was made.

Key fact

In a chemical reaction, atoms are rearranged, never made or destroyed. So the total mass of the reactants equals the total mass of the products. If the mass seems to change, a gas has left or come in.
