Acids and carbonates

Start here

Marble is calcium carbonate. A marble chip fizzes in dilute hydrochloric acid and gives off carbon dioxide.

If you had to guess, does the gas come out of the rock, or out of the acid?

Right. Carbon dioxide contains carbon. Only the calcium carbonate has carbon in it.

Hydrochloric acid has no carbon in it. The carbon and oxygen come from the carbonate.

Key words

  • A compound of a metal with carbon and oxygen, such as calcium carbonate.

  • The gas given off when a metal carbonate reacts with an acid.

  • A clear solution that turns milky when carbon dioxide bubbles through it.

  • A tube that carries a gas from a reaction into another tube.

Key knowledge

Add dilute hydrochloric acid to marble chips.

  • The chips fizz as bubbles of gas form.
  • The chips get smaller.
  • The gas is carbon dioxide.

Chalk, limestone and marble are mainly calcium carbonate.

They all fizz with acid in the same way.

Every metal carbonate reacts with an acid in the same way.

There are three products.

The carbonate uses up the acid, so the acid is neutralised.

The carbon dioxide comes from the carbonate.

The salt's first word is the metal from the carbonate.

Its second word comes from the acid.

  • Hydrochloric acid makes chlorides.
  • Sulfuric acid makes sulfates.
  • Nitric acid makes nitrates.

So calcium carbonate and nitric acid make calcium nitrate, water and carbon dioxide.

  • Put the carbonate and the acid in a boiling tube.
  • Fit a bung and delivery tube straight away.
  • Put the end of the delivery tube under the surface of the limewater.
  • Watch the limewater.

Carbon dioxide turns limewater from clear to milky.

This is the test for carbon dioxide.

Carbon dioxide puts out a lit splint.

So do many other gases, such as nitrogen.

So a splint going out does not show the gas is carbon dioxide.

Use limewater. If it turns milky, the gas is carbon dioxide.

Test for the gas

  • Wrong: A gas that puts out a lit splint is carbon dioxide.

    Right: Many gases put out a splint. Limewater turning milky is the test for carbon dioxide.

  • Wrong: A carbonate and an acid make a salt and hydrogen.

    Right: A carbonate and an acid make a salt, water and carbon dioxide.

  • Wrong: Copper carbonate does not react with dilute acid.

    Right: Copper carbonate fizzes with acid. It is copper metal that does not react.

Key fact

A metal carbonate reacts with an acid to make a salt, water and carbon dioxide. Carbon dioxide turns limewater milky.

Limewater is calcium hydroxide dissolved in water.

Carbon dioxide reacts with it to make calcium carbonate.

Calcium carbonate does not dissolve. Its tiny white specks make the limewater milky.

Keep bubbling carbon dioxide through, and milky limewater goes clear again.

The extra carbon dioxide makes the white specks dissolve.

So the result is the change to milky. Watch for it as it happens.

Rain is slightly acidic. Acid rain is more acidic.

Limestone and marble are mainly calcium carbonate, so the acid slowly reacts with them.

Over many years, statues and buildings made of them wear away.
