Chemical reactions · Contrast

Chemical change vs physical change

Start here

Both changes were caused by heat. Melting chocolate is a physical change. Frying an egg is a chemical change.

If you had to guess, what makes frying an egg a chemical change: the change of colour, or that something new is made?

Colour is only a clue. Food colouring in water changes colour, but nothing new is made.

Right. Cooked egg white is a new substance. It is firm and white, not runny and clear.

Key words

  • One kind of material with its own properties, such as water, iron or salt.

  • A change that makes no new substance. The same substances are still there.

  • A change that makes at least one new substance.

  • A change that rearranges atoms to make new substances. Another name for a chemical change.

Key knowledge

In a physical change, no new substance is made.

The same substances are still there. They have changed state, shape or place, or been mixed.

  • Melted ice is still water, now a liquid.
  • Sugar dissolving in tea is still sugar, spread through the water.
  • A smashed glass is still glass, in pieces.

Melting, freezing, boiling, dissolving and breaking are all physical changes.

In a chemical change, at least one new substance is made.

The new substance has different properties from what you started with.

A chemical change is also called a chemical reaction.

  • Wax burning makes carbon dioxide and water vapour.
  • An iron nail rusting makes rust, a new substance.
  • Frying an egg makes cooked egg white, which stays firm.
  • Marble chips fizzing in acid make carbon dioxide gas.

When ice melts, the water particles move around more. They are still water particles.

When hydrogen burns, its atoms join up with oxygen atoms in a new way.

They make new particles of water, a new substance.

These signs show that a chemical change may have happened:

  • Bubbles of gas are given off, so it fizzes.
  • The colour changes.
  • It gets hot or cold.
  • Light is given out, or a flame.
  • There is a new smell.
  • A solid appears when two solutions are mixed.

Each sign is only a clue. Some physical changes show them too.

Boiling water bubbles. Mixing two paints changes colour.

The test is whether a new substance has been made.

Most physical changes are easy to reverse. Cool melted wax and it sets again.

Most chemical changes are hard to reverse.

But undoing is only a clue. It does not decide the kind of change.

A smashed glass cannot be put back together. It is still a physical change.

Rusting is a chemical change. Yet a blast furnace can get iron back from iron oxide.

Salt stirred into water and marble chips in acid both seem to vanish.

Salt dissolving is a physical change. Evaporate the water and the salt comes back.

Marble chips in acid is a chemical change. The chips fizz and make carbon dioxide.

Evaporate that liquid and you get a different white solid, not marble.

Examples

  • Wrong: If a change cannot be undone, it must be chemical.

    Right: A smashed glass cannot be put back, but it is still glass. It is physical.

  • Wrong: Anything that vanishes into a liquid is the same kind of change.

    Right: Salt in water is physical. Marble fizzing away in acid is chemical.

  • Wrong: A colour change always means a chemical change.

    Right: Food colouring in water changes colour, but nothing new is made.

  • Wrong: Bubbles always mean a new gas has been made.

    Right: Boiling water bubbles too. Those bubbles are water as a gas.

Key fact

A chemical change makes at least one new substance. A physical change makes no new substance, so the same substances are still there.

Rung 3

A student heats sugar in a pan. First it melts to a clear syrup. Then it turns brown and smells of caramel. They say it was all one change. Explain how many changes happened, and which kind each was. Give your evidence.

Rung 4

A cook says cooking is just heating food up. Pick one physical change and one chemical change from a kitchen. Use them to explain why the cook is wrong. Say why the idea is easy to believe.

Cooking uses heat, but not every change in a pan is chemical.

Melting butter in a pan is physical. It sets again in the fridge.

Toasting bread is chemical. New substances give toast its brown colour and smell.

Whipping cream is physical. Whip it too far and the fat clumps into butter.

Nothing new is made in the butter. The fat was in the cream all along.

To compare two changes, link them with whereas.

Melting wax is physical as nothing new forms, whereas burning wax makes new substances.
