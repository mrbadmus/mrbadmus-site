Types of reaction · Process

Thermal decomposition

Start here

A green powder is heated in a test tube. It turns black. A gas comes off that turns limewater milky.

If you had to guess, once the heating is over, does what is left in the tube weigh more than before, or less?

Right. Carbon dioxide gas escapes from the tube. The black solid left behind weighs less.

Nothing joins the powder. Part of it leaves as a gas, so the solid gets lighter.

Key words

  • Using heat to break down one compound into two or more substances.

  • A compound of a metal with carbon and oxygen, such as copper carbonate.

  • A compound of a metal with oxygen, such as copper oxide.

  • A clear solution that turns milky when carbon dioxide bubbles through it.

Key knowledge

Thermal means heat. Decomposition means breaking down.

In thermal decomposition, heat breaks one compound into two or more substances.

There is only one reactant. Nothing else is added.

It needs heat all the time. Take the flame away and it stops.

  • The green powder turns black.
  • A gas comes off and turns the limewater milky.
  • So the gas is carbon dioxide.
  • The black solid left in the tube is copper oxide.

Many metal carbonates break down like this when they are heated.

The metal oxide stays in the tube. The carbon dioxide escapes.

Weigh the tube of powder before and after heating.

The solid left behind weighs less.

No mass is destroyed. The carbon dioxide has escaped into the air.

Collect and weigh the gas too, and the total mass stays the same.

Burning needs oxygen from the air. Thermal decomposition does not.

Copper carbonate breaks down even in a tube with no air in it.

When a metal burns, it gains oxygen and gets heavier.

Here the solid gets lighter.

The black solid is copper oxide. It is not soot.

Limestone is mainly calcium carbonate.

Cement is made by heating limestone with clay in a very hot kiln.

In the kiln, the calcium carbonate breaks down and gives off carbon dioxide.

This carbon dioxide comes from the limestone itself.

So changing the fuel that heats the kiln cannot stop it.

Each test shows that one gas is there.

The three gas tests

RowTestPositive result
Oxygen
Hydrogen
Carbon dioxide

  • Wrong: The powder went black, so it burnt.

    Right: Nothing burnt. The black solid is copper oxide, made with no oxygen from the air.

  • Wrong: When the tube cools, the copper oxide turns back into copper carbonate.

    Right: The copper oxide stays black. The carbon dioxide has gone, so the change does not reverse.

  • Wrong: A gas that puts out a lit splint is carbon dioxide.

    Right: Many gases put out a splint. Limewater turning milky is the test for carbon dioxide.

  • Wrong: The squeaky pop test uses a glowing splint.

    Right: The squeaky pop test uses a lit splint. A glowing splint is the oxygen test.

Key fact

Thermal decomposition uses heat to break one compound into two or more substances. Heating a metal carbonate gives a metal oxide and carbon dioxide.

Baking soda is sodium hydrogencarbonate.

It breaks down in a hot oven and gives off carbon dioxide.

The gas makes bubbles in the mixture, so the cake rises.

Sodium carbonate and potassium carbonate do not break down in a Bunsen flame.

Calcium carbonate needs a very hot flame for a long time.

Copper carbonate breaks down easily, so it is used in school.

Ask MrBadmus

Still not sure why nothing burnt in the tube?

Wear eye protection. Take the delivery tube out of the limewater before you stop heating. If you do not, cold limewater is sucked back and the hot tube can crack.
