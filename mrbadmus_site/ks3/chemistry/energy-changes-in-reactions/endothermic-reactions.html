Energy changes in reactions · Contrast

Endothermic reactions

Start here

Citric acid and sodium hydrogencarbonate are stirred into water at 20 °C. The thermometer falls to 12 °C. Nothing was put in a fridge.

If you had to guess, what happened to the energy that left the water?

Right. The reaction took in energy from its surroundings, so they got colder.

Energy is never destroyed. The reaction took it in from the water, the beaker and the air.

Key words

  • A reaction that takes in energy from the surroundings, so their temperature falls.

  • Everything around the reacting chemicals, such as the water, the container and the air.

  • Using heat to break one compound into two or more substances. It takes in energy.

  • Energy cannot be created or destroyed. It can only be transferred.

Key knowledge

In an endothermic reaction, energy is taken in from the surroundings.

The surroundings are everything around the reacting chemicals.

That includes the water they are in, the container and the air.

The surroundings lose energy, so their temperature falls.

Endo means in. The energy goes into the chemicals.

The reaction does not make cold. Cold is not a substance.

The reaction takes energy from the water, the beaker and the air.

They are left with less energy, so they are colder.

The energy is not destroyed. It is stored in the products.

How common

Both kinds of reaction obey conservation of energy.

Exothermic reactions move energy out of the chemicals.

Endothermic reactions move energy into the chemicals.

Melting is endothermic. The ice takes in energy from a drink.

Freezing is exothermic. The water gives out energy as it turns to ice.

Freezing a mass of water gives out the energy that melting it took in.

Photosynthesis is endothermic and respiration is exothermic.

Run a change backwards, and the energy goes the other way.

  • Wrong: An endothermic reaction makes cold.

    Right: It takes in energy from the surroundings, so they get colder.

  • Wrong: Melting and freezing both take energy in.

    Right: Melting takes energy in. Freezing gives energy out.

  • Wrong: Energy is destroyed in an endothermic reaction.

    Right: The energy is stored in the products. None is destroyed.

  • Wrong: In an endothermic reaction, the surroundings get warmer.

    Right: In an endothermic reaction, the surroundings get colder.

Key fact

An endothermic reaction takes in energy from the surroundings, so their temperature falls. The energy is stored in the products.

Rung 4

A designer wants a cold pack that can be reused indefinitely. Explain what the chemistry would have to allow, and why the ammonium nitrate pack cannot do it.

A cold pack holds ammonium nitrate and a small pouch of water.

Squeeze it, and the pouch bursts. The ammonium nitrate dissolves.

This takes in energy, and the pack gets close to 0 °C.

It works once. The dissolving cannot be undone inside the bag.

A fuel is burned to give energy out.

An endothermic reaction takes energy in.

You would have to keep supplying energy, and get none back.

Endothermic reactions are useful for cooling things down.

Photosynthesis takes in energy from light. It stores it in glucose.

Respiration, burning and digestion release that stored energy later.
