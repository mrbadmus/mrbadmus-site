Energy in food

Start here

A 50 g bag of crisps says 229 kcal and also 958 kJ.

If you had to guess, do they measure one thing or two different things?

The same thing. Both give the energy in the crisps, in two different units.

The same thing. Both give the energy in the crisps, in two different units.

Key words

  • Energy stored in food, fuels and batteries. Chemical reactions release it.

  • 1000 joules. Its symbol is kJ.

  • Another unit of energy on food labels. 1 kcal is about 4.18 kJ.

  • Apparatus that measures the energy released when a sample burns.

Key knowledge

Food is a chemical store of energy.

Your body releases this energy by respiration.

Energy is measured in joules, J. 1 kilojoule, kJ, is 1000 J.

Food labels give the energy in kJ, usually per 100 g.

Labels give the energy in kilojoules, kJ, and in kilocalories, kcal.

These are two units for the same energy.

1 kcal is about 4.18 kJ.

So 229 kcal is about 958 kJ. It is the same energy.

Energy per 100 g lets you compare foods fairly.

Energy per portion tells you how much you actually eat.

Cheese has far more energy in 100 g than apple.

Portions are different sizes, so check both numbers.

Energy in a portion = energy per gram × mass

Bread holds about 10 kJ per gram. Find the energy in a 40 g slice.

  • Convert: both use grams, so nothing changes.
  • Formula: energy = energy per gram × mass
  • Insert: energy = 10 kJ/g × 40 g
  • Fine-tune: 10 × 40 = 400
  • Answer: 400 kJ

Fat holds about 37 kJ in each gram.

Carbohydrate and protein hold about 17 kJ in each gram.

So fat holds about twice as much energy per gram.

Water and fibre add mass, but little or no energy.

The energy figure does not tell you about vitamins, minerals, protein or fibre.

It does not show whether the energy comes from fat or carbohydrate.

Two foods with the same energy can be very different for your health.

Burn a piece of food under a tube of water.

Measure how much the water's temperature rises.

With the same volume of water, a bigger rise means more energy.

Your result will be lower than the label. Here is why:

  • Some energy heats the air, not the water.
  • Some energy heats the glass tube.
  • The food may not burn completely.
  • The warm water loses some energy to the air.

Each of these makes the reading too low, never too high.

Repeating the test does not fix this. Every repeat is low in the same way.

An error like this is called a systematic error.

Energy is never used up. It is transferred.

When you move, some energy goes to a kinetic store.

Most of it ends up in the thermal store of you and the room.

That is why exercise makes you warm.

  • Wrong: Energy from food is used up and gone.

    Right: It is not destroyed. Most of it ends up warming you and the room.

  • Wrong: kcal and kJ measure different things.

    Right: They are two units for the same energy. 1 kcal is about 4.18 kJ.

  • Wrong: Every gram of food holds the same energy.

    Right: Fat holds about twice as much energy per gram as carbohydrate or protein.

Key fact

Food is a chemical store of energy. Labels give it in kilojoules, kJ. 1 kcal is about 4.18 kJ. Energy in a portion = energy per gram × mass. Fat holds about twice as much energy per gram as carbohydrate or protein.

A label gives the energy your body can release from the food.

Some food passes through you without being digested.

So a perfect burn, with no energy lost, would release more than the label.

Burning glucose and respiring it release the same energy.
