Calculating energy transferred

Start here

A 9000 W shower runs for 8 minutes. A 50 W lamp is left on for 24 hours.

If you had to guess, how does the shower's energy compare with the lamp's?

About the same. The shower has far more power, but the lamp is on far longer.

About the same. The shower has far more power, but the lamp is on far longer.

Key words

  • The unit of energy. Its symbol is J.

  • 1000 joules. Its symbol is kJ.

  • The energy a 1 kW appliance transfers in 1 hour. It is 3 600 000 J.

  • To change a quantity into different units, such as minutes into seconds.

Key knowledge

energy transferred = power × time

Use power in watts and time in seconds. This gives energy in joules.

Or use power in kilowatts and time in hours. This gives kilowatt-hours.

Never put minutes straight into the equation.

  • 1 minute = 60 seconds
  • 1 hour = 60 minutes = 3600 seconds
  • 30 minutes = 0.5 hours
  • 45 minutes = 0.75 hours

Convert the time before you put it into the equation.

A 1500 W hairdryer runs for 4 minutes. Find the energy it transfers.

  • Convert: 4 minutes × 60 = 240 s
  • Formula: energy = power × time
  • Insert: energy = 1500 W × 240 s
  • Fine-tune: 1500 × 240 = 360 000
  • Answer: 360 000 J, which is 360 kJ

A 3 kW heater runs for 30 minutes. Find the energy it transfers.

  • Convert: 30 minutes = 0.5 h
  • Formula: energy = power × time
  • Insert: energy = 3 kW × 0.5 h
  • Fine-tune: 3 × 0.5 = 1.5
  • Answer: 1.5 kWh

Power, time and energy

RowWorkingEnergy
Kettle: 2 kW for 6 minutes
TV: 100 W for 4 hours
LED lamp: 10 W for 5 hours

The TV has far less power than the kettle.

But it is on for much longer, so it transfers more energy.

You cannot judge energy from the power rating alone.

A joule is a small amount of energy.

Boiling a full kettle takes hundreds of thousands of joules.

If your answer for a kettle is only a few thousand joules, check the time.

  • Wrong: 2000 W for 3 minutes is 2000 × 3 = 6000 J.

    Right: Convert first. 3 minutes is 180 s, so it is 360 000 J.

  • Wrong: A kilowatt-hour is a unit of power.

    Right: It is a unit of energy: 1 kW for 1 hour.

  • Wrong: A few thousand joules will boil a kettle.

    Right: Boiling a kettle takes hundreds of thousands of joules.

Key fact

Energy transferred = power × time. Use watts with seconds to get joules. Use kilowatts with hours to get kilowatt-hours. Always convert the time first.
