Waves and sound · System

Hearing and auditory range

Start here

A dog whistle is blown hard in a quiet room. No person hears it, but the dog does.

If you had to guess, is the whistle too quiet for people to hear, or too high?

A microphone shows the sound is loud. It is too high for human ears.

Right. Its frequency is above 20 000 Hz, the top of the human range.

Key words

  • A coiled tube of liquid in the inner ear. It turns vibrations into electrical impulses.

  • The range of frequencies an ear can hear, from the lowest to the highest.

  • Sound below about 20 Hz. It is too low for humans to hear.

  • Sound above about 20 000 Hz. It is too high for humans to hear.

Key knowledge

Sound waves travel into the ear and make the eardrum vibrate.

Each part passes the vibration on to the next.

In the cochlea, the vibrations are turned into electrical impulses.

The auditory nerve carries the impulses to the brain.

The brain turns the impulses into the sound you hear.

Wear ear defenders or earplugs near loud machines and at concerts.

Keep the volume on your headphones low.

A hearing aid makes sounds louder for someone with hearing loss.

Your auditory range is the range of frequencies you can hear.

A healthy young person hears from about 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz.

Sound below 20 Hz is infrasound. Sound above 20 000 Hz is ultrasound.

Both are real sound waves. Human ears cannot detect them.

As you get older, the top of your range comes down.

A dog hears up to about 45 000 Hz, so it hears a dog whistle.

A bat hears up to about 110 000 Hz.

An elephant hears down to about 16 Hz. It only hears up to about 12 000 Hz.

So no animal hears every frequency. Each range sits in a different place.

Bats find insects in the dark using echoes.

A higher frequency sound has a shorter wavelength.

Short wavelengths reflect well off small things, like a moth.

So bats call with ultrasound, and their ears can hear the echoes.

  • Wrong: A dog whistle makes no sound.

    Right: It makes a loud sound. Its frequency is too high for human ears.

  • Wrong: Losing the top of your range just makes everything quieter.

    Right: You stop hearing high frequencies, so some speech sounds are hard to tell apart.

  • Wrong: Ultrasound is a different kind of wave from ordinary sound.

    Right: Ultrasound is ordinary sound, at a frequency too high for humans to hear.

  • Wrong: Animals hear better than people do.

    Right: Animals hear different ranges. An elephant hears lower than us, but not as high.

  • Wrong: Everyone can hear up to 20 000 Hz.

    Right: That is for young people. The top of the range falls with age.

Key fact

In the cochlea, the ear turns vibrations into electrical impulses. The auditory nerve carries them to the brain. Young people hear from about 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz. Sound below 20 Hz is infrasound, and sound above 20 000 Hz is ultrasound.

Hair cells in the cochlea do not grow back in humans.

Loud noise damages the ones that detect high frequencies first.

Ringing in your ears after a concert, called tinnitus, is a warning sign.

Some people have hair cells that are too damaged for a hearing aid to help.

A cochlear implant sends electrical signals straight to the auditory nerve.

The brain learns to turn these signals into sounds.
