Static electricity · Process

Charging by rubbing

Start here

A polythene rod is rubbed with a woollen duster. The rod ends up negatively charged.

If you had to guess, what charge does the duster have now?

It is positive. The rod's extra electrons came off the duster.

Yes. The duster lost the electrons the rod gained, so it is positive.

Key words

  • A tiny particle with a negative charge. Electrons are on the outside of every atom.

  • Having no overall charge. The positive and negative charges are equal.

  • Protons and electrons carry charge. An object is charged when it gains or loses electrons.

  • A material that charge cannot flow through, such as plastic, wool or glass.

Key knowledge

There are two types of charge: positive and negative.

Every atom has protons in its nucleus. Protons have a positive charge.

Electrons are around the outside of the atom. Electrons have a negative charge.

Most objects have equal numbers of protons and electrons.

The charges cancel out, so the object is neutral.

Rubbing two insulators together moves electrons from one to the other.

Only electrons move. Protons are held in the nucleus and cannot move.

The object that gains electrons has more electrons than protons.

So it becomes negatively charged.

The object that loses electrons has fewer electrons than protons.

So it becomes positively charged.

Rubbing does not make charge. It only moves electrons.

Every electron the rod gains comes off the duster.

So the rod and the duster get equal and opposite charges.

Add the two charges together and the total is still zero.

Some materials hold on to their electrons more tightly than others.

When two are rubbed, the one that holds electrons more tightly gains them.

So the same material can end up positive or negative.

A cloth gives electrons to polythene, so the cloth becomes positive.

A cloth takes electrons from acetate, so the cloth becomes negative.

In an insulator, electrons cannot flow. They stay where they were rubbed on or off.

A metal is a conductor. Electrons flow through it easily.

Rub a metal rod held in your hand, and charge flows through you.

So the metal rod does not stay charged.

  • Wrong: Rubbing makes new charge.

    Right: Rubbing only moves electrons from one object to the other.

  • Wrong: A positive object has gained positive charge.

    Right: A positive object has lost electrons. Protons never move.

  • Wrong: Only the rubbed rod becomes charged.

    Right: Both objects become charged, with equal and opposite charges.

  • Wrong: A material always gets the same charge, whatever rubs it.

    Right: It depends on the pair. A cloth can end up positive or negative.

  • Wrong: A metal rod held in your hand stays charged after rubbing.

    Right: Charge flows through your hand, so the rod does not stay charged.

Key fact

There are two types of charge, positive and negative. Rubbing two insulators moves electrons from one to the other. The object that gains electrons becomes negative. The object that loses electrons becomes positive, by the same amount.

Rung 2

A student rubs a balloon on a jumper, finds the balloon negative, and says the friction created the negative charge. What is right?

Rung 3

A polythene rod is rubbed with a woollen duster. The rod ends up negatively charged. Explain what has happened. Name what moves and what does not. Say what charge the duster has now.

Rung 4

A fuel tanker is joined to a metal spike in the ground before any fuel is pumped. Pumping stops if the wire comes off. Explain what this prevents. Explain why doing the same to a plastic bottle of water would be pointless.

Clothes from a tumble dryer rub together, so they cling.

Walk across a nylon carpet and you can pick up a charge.

Touch a metal door handle, and the charge jumps across as a small spark.

Fuel flowing through a pipe rubs against it, so charge builds up.

A spark near fuel vapour could start a fire.

So a fuel tanker is joined to the ground by a metal wire first.

The charge flows away through the wire instead of building up.

This is called earthing.
