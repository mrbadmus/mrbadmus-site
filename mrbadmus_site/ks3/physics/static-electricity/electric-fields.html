Static electricity · Model

Electric fields

Start here

A comb pulled through dry hair is held near a thin stream of tap water. The stream bends towards the comb without touching it.

If you had to guess, does the comb need the air between it and the water to bend the stream?

No. The comb's field acts across the gap. It does not need air.

Right. The comb's electric field fills the space, with or without air.

Key words

  • The space around a charged object, where another charge feels a force.

  • A force that acts between objects that are not touching.

  • An arrow showing which way a small positive charge would be pushed at that point.

Key knowledge

A charged object has an electric field around it.

The field is the space where another charged object feels a force.

The field is there even when nothing is in it.

Put a charged object in the field, and it is pushed or pulled straight away.

The field is how a force acts across the space between objects.

So charged objects push and pull without touching.

Nothing has to travel across the gap, and no air is needed.

It works just the same in a vacuum.

Arrows show the field. Each one shows the push on a small positive charge.

Around a positive charge, the arrows point away from it.

Around a negative charge, the arrows point towards it.

The field is strongest close to the charge.

A negative charge is pushed the opposite way to the arrows.

Half way between two equal positive charges, a test charge is pushed both ways.

The two pushes are equal and opposite, so they cancel.

So the field at that one point is zero.

Move a little closer to one charge, and that charge's push wins.

  • Wrong: The air in the gap carries the force.

    Right: A negative charge is pushed the opposite way to the arrows.

    Right: A field arrow shows the push on a small positive charge.

Key fact

A charged object has an electric field around it. Any other charged object in that field feels a force. So the force acts across the gap, without the objects touching.

Rungs 3 and 4 you mark yourself.

Rung 1

The electric field around a small charged object points outwards, away from it, in every direction. What can you say about the object, and about a small negative charge released nearby?

Rung 2

A student says the field between two equal positive charges must be at its strongest half-way between them, because that point is close to both. What is right?

Rung 3

A comb is charged by rubbing it on a jumper. It is held 1 cm above small pieces of paper. They jump up to it without being touched. Explain what is happening, using the word field. Say what would change if this were done in a vacuum.

Rung 4

People are safe from lightning inside a car, and the rubber tyres are not the reason. Explain why, using the idea of a field and what you know about conductors.

A charged comb bends a thin stream of tap water.

The comb's field acts on the water from a few centimetres away.

Nothing touches the water, and no air is needed.

A car's metal body is a conductor.

If lightning strikes, the charge flows round the outside of the metal.

The field inside the car is almost zero, so the people inside are safe.

The metal body protects you. The rubber tyres do not.
