What a force is

Start here

You stand on a skateboard and push against a wall. You roll away from it.

If you had to guess, as you roll away, are you still carrying the wall's push with you?

Key words

  • A push or a pull on an object, caused by a second object.

  • The unit of force. Its symbol is N.

  • A force that acts only while two objects touch, such as friction.

  • A force that acts across a gap, such as gravity or magnetism.

Key knowledge

A force is a push or a pull.

Every force acts between two objects.

To describe a force, name both objects. For example, the tow bar pulls the caravan.

A force lasts only while the two objects act on each other.

An object cannot store a force or carry one with it.

When you push a wall, the wall pushes you back.

The two forces are the same size and point in opposite directions.

They act on different objects, one on each.

The wall's push on you is what makes you roll away.

A swimmer pushes water backwards. The water pushes the swimmer forwards.

  • Make an object speed up or slow down.
  • Change the direction an object moves in.
  • Change an object's shape, such as squashing a sponge.
  • Make an object turn, such as a door swinging open.

An apple resting on your hand presses down with about 1 N.

A newtonmeter measures force. It has a spring inside.

A bigger pull stretches the spring further, so the pointer moves further down the scale.

Check it reads zero first. Read the scale at eye level.

Nothing has to fill the gap between the objects.

A magnet pulls a paperclip just as hard with all the air pumped away.

  • Wrong: A thrown ball carries a force that slowly runs out.

    Right: Nothing carries a force. Air resistance and gravity act on the ball as it flies.

  • Wrong: A table does nothing to a book resting on it.

    Right: The table pushes up on the book. That is why the book does not fall through.

  • Wrong: The force is in the ball's movement.

    Right: Movement is not a force. A force is one object pushing or pulling another.

Key fact

A force is a push or a pull between two objects, measured in newtons (N). Contact forces act only while the objects touch. Non-contact forces, such as gravity and magnetism, act across a gap.
