Light travels

Start here

Lightning hits a hill two kilometres away. The flash and the thunder start at the same moment. You hear the thunder about six seconds after you see the flash.

If you had to guess, how long does the flash take to reach you?

Light is very fast, but it does take time. Sunlight takes about 8 minutes to reach us.

Right. Light covers two kilometres in about seven millionths of a second.

Key words

  • A straight line with an arrow, showing the path light takes.

  • A wave whose vibrations are at right angles to the direction it travels.

  • A space with no particles in it at all.

  • 300 000 000 m/s in a vacuum. Nothing travels faster.

Key knowledge

Light is a wave. It carries energy from a light source, such as the Sun or a lamp.

Light is a transverse wave, like a wave on water.

Its vibrations are at right angles to the direction it travels.

Like other waves, light can be reflected and absorbed.

Light travels in straight lines.

We draw its path as a ray: a straight line with an arrow.

An object that light cannot pass through blocks the rays that hit it.

This leaves a shadow behind the object. A small lamp gives a shadow with sharp edges.

Sound needs particles to pass it on, so it cannot travel through a vacuum.

Light needs no particles. It can travel through a vacuum.

This is how light from the Sun crosses empty space to reach the Earth.

In a vacuum, light travels at 300 000 000 m/s.

Sound in air travels at about 340 m/s.

So light is nearly a million times faster than sound in air.

Light is fastest in a vacuum. It slows down in water and glass.

Light is very fast, but it is not instant.

Light from the Sun takes about 8 minutes to reach the Earth.

distance (s) = speed of light (c) × time (t)

time (t) = distance (s) ÷ speed of light (c)

Distance is in metres, m. Time is in seconds, s.

The speed of light, c, is 300 000 000 m/s.

Light takes 8.0 minutes to travel from the Sun to the Earth. Find the distance.

  • Convert: 8.0 minutes × 60 = 480 s
  • Formula: distance = speed of light × time
  • Insert: distance = 300 000 000 m/s × 480 s
  • Fine-tune: 300 000 000 × 480 = 144 000 000 000
  • Answer: distance = 144 000 000 000 m

Put in 8.0 instead of 480, and the answer is sixty times too small.

A laser beam travels 36 000 000 m from the Earth to a satellite. Find the time.

  • Convert: nothing. The distance is in m and the speed is in m/s.
  • Formula: time = distance ÷ speed of light
  • Insert: time = 36 000 000 m ÷ 300 000 000 m/s
  • Fine-tune: 36 000 000 ÷ 300 000 000 = 0.12
  • Answer: time = 0.12 s

  • Wrong: Light is instant. It takes no time at all.

    Right: Light is very fast, but it takes time. Sunlight takes about 8 minutes to reach us.

  • Wrong: Space is empty, so light travels slowly through it.

    Right: Light needs no material. It is fastest in a vacuum.

  • Wrong: Light is longitudinal, like sound.

    Right: Light is a transverse wave. Sound is longitudinal.

  • Wrong: The thunder is made after the flash.

    Right: The flash and the thunder are made together. The sound arrives later.

  • Wrong: Put 8.0 minutes straight into the equation.

    Right: Change minutes to seconds first: 8.0 × 60 = 480 s.

Key fact

Light is a transverse wave that needs no material, so it can cross a vacuum. In a vacuum, it travels in straight lines at 300 000 000 m/s.

Light from the nearest star after the Sun takes about four years to reach us.

So we see that star as it was about four years ago.

Big telescopes see distant galaxies as they were millions of years ago.

A light-year is the distance light travels in one year.

It is a unit of distance, used for the huge distances in space.

The speed of light in a vacuum is 299 792 458 m/s.

We round it to 300 000 000 m/s.
