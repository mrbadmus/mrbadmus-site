Refraction

Start here

A straw stands in a glass of water. Seen from above at a slant, it looks bent at the water surface.

If you had to guess, what really bends: the straw, or the light coming from it?

Lift the straw out and it is straight. The light from it bends at the water surface.

Right. Light from the straw changes direction as it leaves the water.

Key words

  • The change in direction of light as it crosses from one material into another.

  • A line drawn at right angles to a surface, where a ray meets it.

  • The surface where two materials meet, such as air and glass.

Key knowledge

Light travels fastest in a vacuum, and almost as fast in air.

It slows down when it goes into water, perspex or glass.

In glass, light travels at about two thirds of its speed in air.

It speeds up again when it comes back out into the air.

Crossing a boundary at an angle, light changes speed and so changes direction.

This is refraction.

Going into glass, light slows down and bends towards the normal.

Coming out into air, light speeds up and bends away from the normal.

A block with parallel sides sends the ray out in its starting direction, shifted sideways.

A ray that meets the boundary along the normal does not bend.

It still slows down inside the glass.

So light can change speed without changing direction.

An example

Light from the bottom of the straw travels up through the water.

At the surface, it speeds up and bends away from the normal.

Your brain assumes that light has come in a straight line.

So it traces the ray straight back, to a point higher than the real end.

The straw is straight. The light from it is what bent.

  • Wrong: The straw really bends in water.

    Right: The straw stays straight. The light from it bends at the surface.

  • Wrong: Water is thicker, so it pushes light sideways.

    Right: Nothing pushes the light. It bends because its speed changes.

  • Wrong: Light speeds up when it goes into glass.

    Right: Light slows down in glass. It is fastest in a vacuum.

  • Wrong: A ray that goes straight through has not changed speed.

    Right: Along the normal, light slows down but does not bend.

  • Wrong: Going into glass, light bends away from the normal.

    Right: Going into glass, light bends towards the normal.

Key fact

Refraction is light changing direction as it crosses into a new material at an angle. It bends towards the normal when it slows down, and away when it speeds up.

Light travels at about 226 000 000 m/s in water.

It travels at about 200 000 000 m/s in glass.

Glass slows light more than water does.

So at the same angle, glass bends a ray more than water does.

Light inside glass can meet the surface at a very large angle to the normal.

Then none of it escapes. It all reflects back inside the glass.

This is total internal reflection.

Optical fibres use it to carry light along thin glass threads, even round bends.

Each colour in white light is refracted by a slightly different amount.

A prism uses this to split white light into a spectrum.
