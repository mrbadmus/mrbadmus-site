Colour and the spectrum

Start here

Sunlight shines through a clear glass prism. A band of colours appears on the wall.

If you had to guess, were the colours already in the sunlight, or did the glass add them?

Right. White light is a mixture of colours. The prism spreads them out.

Clear glass has no colour to add. It spreads out the colours already in sunlight.

Key words

  • The band of colours that white light splits into, from red to violet.

  • The splitting of white light into colours, because each colour refracts by a different amount.

  • The number of waves passing a point each second. For light, it decides the colour.

  • The family of waves that light belongs to, from radio waves to gamma rays.

Key knowledge

White light, such as sunlight, is a mixture of colours.

A prism splits white light into a band of colours called a spectrum.

The colours are red, orange, yellow, green, blue and violet.

The colours change smoothly from one to the next. There are no lines between them.

Each colour is light of a different frequency.

Red light has the lowest frequency. Violet light has the highest.

All the colours slow down and refract when they go into the glass.

Higher frequencies refract more. So violet bends the most, and red bends the least.

The colours fan out. This is dispersion.

A second prism, the other way up, bends each colour back again.

The colours join up, and white light comes out.

So the colours were in the white light all along.

Send red light alone into a prism, and only red light comes out.

Visible light is one small part of a family of waves.

This family is called the electromagnetic spectrum.

All of these waves are transverse, and all can travel through a vacuum.

In a vacuum, they all travel at the same speed, 300 000 000 m/s.

They differ in frequency. Higher frequency waves have shorter wavelengths.

In order of frequency, lowest first:

  • radio waves
  • microwaves
  • infrared
  • visible light
  • ultraviolet
  • X-rays
  • gamma rays

  • Wrong: The prism adds the colours to the light.

    Right: The colours were already in the white light. The prism separates them.

  • Wrong: A rainbow is six separate colours, with lines between them.

    Right: The colours change smoothly. The six names are labels along one band.

  • Wrong: High frequency light is bent the least.

    Right: High frequency light is bent the most. Violet bends more than red.

  • Wrong: The colours come from the coloured edges of the prism.

    Right: The prism is clear glass. The colours come from the white light.

  • Wrong: X-rays travel faster than radio waves.

    Right: In a vacuum, every electromagnetic wave travels at the same speed.

Key fact

White light is a mixture of colours, which a prism splits into a spectrum. Violet has the highest frequency and is refracted most, and red is refracted least.

Rung 4

A beam of white light passes through a prism onto a screen. The colours land in order from red to violet. Explain why they spread out in that order. Then say why a beam of only green light would not spread out.

Some books also name indigo, between blue and violet, as a seventh colour.

Infrared lies just below red in frequency. You feel it as warmth.

Ultraviolet lies just above violet. Bees can see it, but people cannot.

Higher frequency electromagnetic waves carry more energy.

Ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays can damage the cells they pass through.

This is why X-ray staff stand behind a screen.

Ask MrBadmus

Want to check which colour a prism bends furthest, and why?

Never look directly at the Sun, with your eyes, through a lens or through a pinhole camera. A lens gathers sunlight to a point hot enough to scorch paper. It will do the same to the back of your eye. The damage does not hurt while it happens, and it does not heal.
