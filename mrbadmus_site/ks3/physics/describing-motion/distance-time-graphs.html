Distance–time graphs

Start here

A walker goes 6 m along a corridor, waits at a door, then runs on. A sensor draws the journey.

If you had to guess, while the walker waits, does the line go flat, or drop to the bottom?

It goes flat. The walker is still 6 m from the start. So the line stays at that height.

Key words

  • A graph of how far an object is from its start, against time.

  • How steep a line is. On a distance-time graph, the gradient is the speed.

  • Not moving. The distance from the start does not change.

  • The distance an object travels each second.

Key knowledge

A walker goes 6 m, waits at a door, then runs 12 m.

Time goes along the bottom. Distance from the start goes up the side.

The line shows how far the walker is from the start at each time.

The graph shows the journey. It does not show the route.

The gradient is how steep the line is.

A steeper line means a faster speed.

A less steep line means a slower speed.

Gradient = change in distance ÷ change in time.

That is the speed equation again: speed = distance ÷ time.

In the graph, the walk is 6 m in 6 s. That is 1 m/s.

The run is 12 m in 4 s. That is 3 m/s.

A flat line means the distance is not changing.

The object is stationary. Its speed is 0 m/s.

A steady speed is a straight line that slopes up.

A curved line means the speed is changing.

Where the line gets steeper, the object speeds up.

Where it gets less steep, the object slows down.

A line goes from 10 m at 2 s to 40 m at 12 s. Find the speed.

  • Convert: nothing. Distance is in m and time is in s.
  • Distance moved: 40 m minus 10 m = 30 m
  • Time taken: 12 s minus 2 s = 10 s
  • Formula: speed (v) = distance (s) ÷ time (t)
  • Insert: speed = 30 m ÷ 10 s
  • Answer: speed = 3 m/s

A line sloping down means the object is coming back.

Its distance from the start is getting smaller.

The steepness still shows the speed.

Distance travelled keeps adding up.

A lift goes 30 m up and 30 m down. It has travelled 60 m.

Its distance from the start is 0 m.

  • Wrong: A distance-time graph is a picture of the route, so a rising line means uphill.

    Right: The line shows distance from the start. It never shows the route.

  • Wrong: A flat line means a steady speed.

    Right: A flat line means stopped. A steady speed slopes up.

  • Wrong: A curved line means the object goes round a bend.

    Right: A curve means the speed is changing. It does not show the route.

  • Wrong: A steeper line means the object went further.

    Right: Steeper means faster. Read how far from the distance axis.

Key fact

A distance-time graph shows distance from the start against time. The gradient is the speed: steeper means faster, and a flat line means stopped. A curve means the speed is changing.

Two lines can share the same axes.

Where the lines cross, the objects are at the same place at the same time.

The steeper line belongs to the faster object.

On a curve, the gradient changes along the line.

At GCSE you draw a tangent to a curve.

The gradient of the tangent is the speed at that moment.
