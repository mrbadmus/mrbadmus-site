Speed

Start here

A fly 30 cm away crosses your view in half a second. A plane 10 km up takes a minute.

If you had to guess, which one is really going faster?

The plane. It looks slow because it is far away. It covers about 250 m every second.

The plane. It looks slow because it is far away. It covers about 250 m every second.

Key words

  • How far an object travels in a certain time.

  • The total distance of a journey divided by the total time.

  • A unit of speed, written m/s. It is the metres travelled each second.

  • The time between something happening and you responding to it.

Key knowledge

speed = distance ÷ time

Distance in metres and time in seconds give speed in metres per second, m/s.

Distance in kilometres and time in hours give speed in kilometres per hour, km/h.

To find a distance: distance = speed × time.

To find a time: time = distance ÷ speed.

A car covers 1.8 km of motorway in 90 s. Find its speed in m/s.

  • Convert: 1.8 km × 1000 = 1800 m
  • Formula: speed = distance ÷ time
  • Insert: speed = 1800 m ÷ 90 s
  • Fine-tune: 1800 ÷ 90 = 20
  • Answer: speed = 20 m/s

Put in 1.8 instead of 1800, and the answer is a thousand times too small.

A cyclist rides at 5 m/s for 2 minutes. Find the distance travelled.

  • Convert: 2 minutes × 60 = 120 s
  • Formula: distance = speed × time
  • Insert: distance = 5 m/s × 120 s
  • Fine-tune: 5 × 120 = 600
  • Answer: distance = 600 m

To change km/h into m/s, divide by 3.6.

To change m/s into km/h, multiply by 3.6.

This works because 1 km is 1000 m and 1 hour is 3600 s.

So 36 km/h ÷ 3.6 = 10 m/s.

Speed often changes during a journey.

Average speed = total distance ÷ total time.

Walk 100 m in 100 s, then run 100 m in 20 s.

Average speed = 200 m ÷ 120 s = 1.67 m/s.

That is close to walking pace, because most of the time was spent walking.

The speed at one moment is the instantaneous speed.

A speedometer shows it.

Measure a distance with a tape measure. Mark a start and a finish.

Time the object between the two marks with a stopwatch.

Your reaction time is the biggest error when you time by hand.

A typical reaction time is about 0.2 s.

Time over a longer distance, so the reaction time matters less.

Light gates time automatically, so there is no reaction time.

Repeat timings usually differ a little. That is normal.

So do the run three times and work out the mean time.

To find the mean, add the times and divide by how many there are.

Then use the mean time in speed = distance ÷ time.

  • Wrong: Whoever finishes first is fastest.

    Right: Not if the distances or start times differ. Work out distance ÷ time for each.

  • Wrong: Something far away that looks slow is slow.

    Right: How far away it is changes how fast it looks, not its speed.

  • Wrong: Average speed is the average of the speeds.

    Right: Slow parts take longer, so they count for more. Use the totals.

  • Wrong: Divide the two numbers in the order you are given them.

    Right: Distance always goes on top. Divide the distance by the time.

Key fact

Speed = distance ÷ time. Distance in metres and time in seconds give speed in metres per second (m/s). Average speed is the total distance ÷ the total time.
