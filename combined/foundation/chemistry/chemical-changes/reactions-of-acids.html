What does the acid meet?

Acids produce hydrogen ions, H+, in water. Metal oxides and hydroxides are bases: they neutralise acids. An alkali is a base that dissolves in water. Choose an acid and a reactant, then predict the products.

Predict the products.

The four reactions

The salt comes from the metal and the acid.

Hydrochloric acid makes chlorides, sulfuric acid makes sulfates, nitric acid makes nitrates. The reactions with oxides, hydroxides and carbonates are all neutralisation. Only metals above hydrogen in the reactivity series react with dilute acids: copper doesn’t.

Formulae of salts

Balance the charges.

A salt has no overall charge. Take enough of each ion for the charges to cancel. Mg2+ needs two Cl−, so magnesium chloride is MgCl2. Two Na+ balance one SO42−, so sodium sulfate is Na2SO4. Now you.

Key fact

Acid + metal gives a salt and hydrogen. Acid + metal oxide or hydroxide gives a salt and water. Acid + carbonate gives a salt, water and carbon dioxide.

Key note · AQA 5.4.2.1–5.4.2.2

Reactions of acids

  1. 01Acids produce H⁺ ions in water. Bases (metal oxides and hydroxides) neutralise acids; alkalis are soluble bases.
  2. 02Acid + metal → salt + hydrogen (metals above hydrogen only; copper doesn’t react). Test: lit splint, squeaky pop.
  3. 03Acid + metal oxide or hydroxide → salt + water. Acid + carbonate → salt + water + carbon dioxide. Test: limewater milky. All three are neutralisation.
  4. 04Hydrochloric → chlorides; sulfuric → sulfates; nitric → nitrates.
  5. 05Salt formulae: balance the ion charges, e.g. Mg²⁺ + 2Cl⁻ → MgCl2.
