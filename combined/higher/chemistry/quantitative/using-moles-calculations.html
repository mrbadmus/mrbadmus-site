Using moles — calculations and limiting reactants

Combined Science · Higher tier
  • Triple Science · Higher tier

Start here

Magnesium reacts with hydrochloric acid: Mg + 2HCl → MgCl2 + H2. A student mixes 2.4 g of magnesium with 3.65 g of hydrogen chloride.

If you had to guess, which one runs out first?

Key words

  • balancing numbers
  • limiting reactant
  • excess
  • whole number ratio

Key knowledge

The balancing numbers in a symbol equation can be calculated from the masses of reactants and products by converting the masses in grams to amounts in moles and converting the numbers of moles to simple whole number ratios.

  • Convert each mass in grams to moles: n = m ÷ Mr.
  • Divide every number of moles by the smallest one.
  • If you get a .5, double every number.
  • The whole numbers are the balancing numbers.

Use each formula as written: hydrogen gas is H2 (Mr 2), oxygen gas is O2 (Mr 32), chlorine gas is Cl2 (Mr 71).

11.2 g of iron reacts with 21.3 g of chlorine to make 32.5 g of iron chloride. Fe + Cl2 → FeCl3

In a chemical reaction involving two reactants, it is common to use an excess of one of the reactants to ensure that all of the other reactant is used.

The reactant that is completely used up is called the limiting reactant because it limits the amount of products.

The other reactant is in excess. Some of it is left over.

  • Convert each reactant's mass to moles.
  • Use the balancing numbers to work out how many moles of the second reactant the first one needs.
  • If there is less than that, the second reactant is limiting. If there is more, the first reactant is limiting.

Mg + 2HCl → MgCl2 + H2. 2.4 g of Mg is 0.1 mol and 3.65 g of HCl is 0.1 mol.

0.1 mol of Mg needs 0.2 mol of HCl. There is only 0.1 mol, so HCl is the limiting reactant and some magnesium is left over.

Work out the product from the limiting reactant only.

0.1 mol of HCl makes 0.1 × 1 ÷ 2 = 0.05 mol of H2, which is 0.05 × 2 = 0.1 g of hydrogen.

Adding more of the reactant in excess makes no more product.

Doubling the limiting reactant, with the other still in excess, doubles the amount of product.

  • Wrong: The reactant with the smaller mass is the limiting reactant.

    Right: Convert to moles and compare with the ratio in the equation.

  • Wrong: The reactant with fewer moles is the limiting reactant.

    Right: 0.05 mol of Mg needs 0.10 mol of HCl. With 0.08 mol of HCl, the HCl runs out first.

  • Wrong: A ratio of 1 : 1.5 : 1 rounds to 1 : 2 : 1.

    Right: Double it to 2 : 3 : 2. Balancing numbers are whole numbers.

  • Wrong: Hydrogen gas has Mr 1.

    Right: Hydrogen gas is H2, so Mr = 2.

Key fact

To balance an equation from masses, convert each mass in grams to moles and turn the moles into the simplest whole number ratio. The limiting reactant is the reactant that is completely used up, so it limits the amount of products.

When you make a salt from an insoluble metal oxide and an acid, you add the oxide in excess.

Then all of the acid reacts. The leftover oxide is filtered off.

  • Balance: find the moles of every substance, divide by the smallest, and put the whole numbers into the equation.
  • Determine: use the data to decide which reactant runs out.
  • Calculate: show the moles, the ratio, the moles of product, then the mass with its unit.
  • Explain: link the limiting reactant to the amount of product.
