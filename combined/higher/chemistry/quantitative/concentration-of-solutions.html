Concentration of solutions

The same 10 g of sugar can make a drink that is sickly sweet or barely sweet. Which number tells you how strong a solution is, and how do you calculate it?

Start here

60 g of sugar in every litre.

A sports drink label reads: carbohydrate 60 g/dm³. That number is a concentration: the mass of sugar dissolved in each dm³ of drink. One dm³ is one litre, or 1000 cm³. You pour yourself a 250 cm³ glass.

Commit first

How much sugar is in your glass?

Concentration is the mass of solute dissolved in each dm³ of solution, measured in g/dm³. A concentrated solution has a lot of solute in a given volume; a dilute solution has a little. To calculate it, divide the mass of solute by the volume of the solution: concentration (g/dm³) = mass (g) ÷ volume (dm³). The volume must be in dm³. Lab volumes usually come in cm³, so divide by 1000 first: 500 cm³ = 0.500 dm³.

Equations

Learn it · no chemistry equation sheet

c = m ÷ V

concentration (g/dm³) = mass (g) ÷ volume (dm³)

m = c × V

V = m ÷ c

mass (g) = concentration (g/dm³) × volume (dm³); volume (dm³) = mass (g) ÷ concentration (g/dm³)

cm³ ÷ 1000 = dm³

Key fact

Concentration = mass of solute ÷ volume of solution. With the mass in g and the volume in dm³, the answer is in g/dm³.

Key note · AQA 5.3.2.5 (8464)

Concentration of solutions

  1. 01Concentration (g/dm³) = mass (g) ÷ volume (dm³).
  2. 02Convert cm³ to dm³: ÷ 1000.
  3. 03Concentrated = lots of solute per volume. Dilute = little solute per volume.
  4. 04mass (g) = concentration (g/dm³) × volume (dm³). volume (dm³) = mass (g) ÷ concentration (g/dm³).
  5. 05Dilution: same mass, larger volume, lower concentration.

    Not sure when to divide by 1000?

    The bench draws the solution in a measuring cylinder with one dot for every 0.5 g of dissolved solute, so the number of dots in a given space stands for the concentration. Real dissolved particles are far too small to see, and an accurate solution is made up in a volumetric flask rather than a measuring cylinder.
