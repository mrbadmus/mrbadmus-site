Think again · the most common error

“Bacteria have a nucleus. It is just smaller.”

Size is not the difference. A bacterium has no nucleus at all: its genetic material is not enclosed in a membrane. Having a nucleus is exactly what makes a cell eukaryotic, so “bacterial DNA is in the nucleus” loses the mark. Write: the DNA is a single loop, free in the cytoplasm.
!

Think again · which wall?

“Bacteria have a cellulose cell wall, like plants.”

Cellulose is the material of plant and algal cell walls. A bacterium does have a cell wall around its membrane, but it is not made of cellulose. In an exam, “cell wall” on its own earns the mark; “cellulose cell wall” for a bacterium is wrong.

Cells are measured in tiny units. Milli means a thousandth, micro a millionth and nano a thousand-millionth of a metre, so 1 mm = 1000 µm and 1 µm = 1000 nm (and centi, a hundredth: 1 cm = 10 mm). An order of magnitude is a factor of ten. If one thing is about 10ⁿ times bigger than another, it is n orders of magnitude bigger. Put both sizes in the same unit before you divide.

Key fact

Eukaryotic cells keep their genetic material in a nucleus. Prokaryotic cells are about an order of magnitude smaller, with a single DNA loop, possibly plasmids, and a cell wall around the membrane.
