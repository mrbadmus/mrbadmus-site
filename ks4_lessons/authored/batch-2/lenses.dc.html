Draft — not yet science-reviewed.

A lens forms an image by refracting light. A convex (converging) lens brings parallel rays together at the principal focus, F; the distance from the lens to F is the focal length. A concave (diverging) lens spreads parallel rays out, as if they came from F on the near side. To find an image, follow two rays from the top of the object: one parallel to the axis, which a convex lens bends through F, and one through the centre of the lens, which goes straight on. Where they meet is the top of the image. If the rays really meet, the image is real and a screen can catch it. If they only seem to come from a point, it is virtual.

Think again

“A convex lens always forms a real image.”

Only when the object is beyond F. Inside the focal length the rays leave the lens still spreading apart, so they never meet. Your eye traces them back to a point on the object's side: a virtual, upright, magnified image that no screen can catch. That is how a magnifying glass works. A concave lens always forms a virtual image.

Lenses in spectacles · AQA Biology 4.5.2.3

In myopia (short sight) the eyeball is too long or the eye lens too strongly curved, so light from distant objects focuses in front of the retina. In hyperopia (long sight) the eyeball is too short or the lens not curved enough, so light from near objects would focus behind it.

Equations

On the sheet

magnification = image height ÷ object height

image height = magnification × object height

Units first

Both heights in the same unit, mm or cm: 1 cm = 10 mm. Magnification is a ratio, so it has no unit. Bigger than 1: magnified. Less than 1: diminished.

Command words in this lesson

Key fact

Convex lens: object beyond F, real and inverted image; object inside F, virtual, upright and magnified; object at F, no image. Concave lens: always virtual, upright and diminished.
