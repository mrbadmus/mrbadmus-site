Concentration of solutions

The same 10 g of sugar can make a drink that is sickly sweet or barely sweet. Which number tells you how strong a solution is, and how do you calculate it?

Concentration is the mass of solute dissolved in each dm³ of solution, measured in g/dm³. A concentrated solution has a lot of solute in a given volume; a dilute solution has a little. To calculate it, divide the mass of solute by the volume of the solution: concentration (g/dm³) = mass (g) ÷ volume (dm³). The volume must be in dm³. Lab volumes usually come in cm³, so divide by 1000 first: 500 cm³ = 0.500 dm³.

Concentration in mol/dm³ · AQA 4.3.4

Concentration can also be measured in mol/dm³: the amount of solute, in moles, in each dm³ of solution. concentration (mol/dm³) = amount (mol) ÷ volume (dm³). Mass and concentration in mol/dm³ are linked by Mr: turn grams into moles first (n = mass ÷ Mr), then divide by the volume in dm³.

Key fact

Concentration = mass of solute ÷ volume of solution. With the mass in g and the volume in dm³, the answer is in g/dm³.
