Start here

One drop turns the whole flask.

A conical flask holds 25.00 cm³ of sodium hydroxide solution and a few drops of phenolphthalein, so it is pink. Acid runs in from a burette. Near the end, each splash makes a colourless patch that vanishes when the flask is swirled. Then, after one more drop, the pink is gone for good.

Required practical 2 (Chemistry) · the AQA method

Pipette the alkali. Burette the acid. Stop on one drop.

AQA titrations use strong acids only (sulfuric, hydrochloric, nitric) and strong alkalis such as sodium hydroxide.

{{ rigFig }}
  1. Use a pipette and pipette filler to put 25.00 cm³ of sodium hydroxide solution into a conical flask on a white tile.
  2. Add a few drops of a suitable indicator, such as phenolphthalein or methyl orange.
  3. Rinse the burette with the acid, then fill it through a funnel, below eye level. Remove the funnel. Record the initial reading to the nearest 0.05 cm³, at the bottom of the meniscus.
  4. Do a rough titration first. Then repeat, swirling the flask and adding acid drop by drop near the end point, until one drop gives a permanent colour change.
  5. Record the final reading. Titre = final reading − initial reading.
  6. Repeat until at least two titres are concordant (within 0.10 cm³ of each other). Calculate the mean of the concordant titres.
Measured
The titre, in cm³.
Kept the same
25.00 cm³ of alkali, the acid's concentration, the indicator and how many drops.
Risks
Wear eye protection: sodium hydroxide harms eyes. Never pipette by mouth. Indicators in ethanol are flammable: keep them away from flames.

Equations · processing the data

Learn it · chemistry has no equation sheet

Titre = final burette reading − initial burette reading

Mean titre = sum of concordant titres ÷ number of concordant titres

Never the rough run, never an anomalous titre. Give the mean to 2 decimal places.

From titre to concentration

On the Higher paper the mean titre starts a calculation. Find the moles in the solution whose concentration you know, with n = c × V and V in dm³. Use the ratio in the balanced equation to get the moles of the other substance, then c = n ÷ V. For g/dm³, multiply the concentration in mol/dm³ by Mr.

n = c × V

moles (mol) = concentration (mol/dm³) × volume (dm³). cm³ ÷ 1000 = dm³.

g/dm³ = mol/dm³ × Mr
