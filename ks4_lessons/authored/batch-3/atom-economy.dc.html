Start here

Two factories. One product.

Both factories make ethanol, C₂H₅OH. Factory A ferments sugar: C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂. Factory B adds steam to ethene: C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH. Both run perfectly: every molecule of starting material reacts.

That share is the atom economy (also called atom utilisation): a measure of the amount of starting materials that end up as useful products. It comes from the balanced equation and relative formula masses, so a chemist can work it out before the reaction is ever run.

Equations

Learn it · no chemistry equation sheet

% atom economy = Mr of desired product ÷ sum of Mr of all reactants × 100

Both from the balanced equation.

sum of Mr of reactants = sum of Mr of products

Mass is conserved, so dividing by the products’ total gives the same answer.

All three products are sold, so little of the mass is really wasted. That is why the usefulness of by-products counts when a route is chosen.

A high atom economy matters for sustainable development and for economic reasons: fewer raw materials are wasted, and there is less waste to dispose of. A reaction with only one product, such as an addition reaction, has an atom economy of 100%.

Atom economy is not percentage yield. Atom economy belongs to the balanced equation, so it is the same every time the reaction runs. Percentage yield depends on how the reaction is carried out: how much product is actually collected.
