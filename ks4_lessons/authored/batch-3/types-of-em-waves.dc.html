Every group in the spectrum is the same kind of wave. Electromagnetic waves are transverse: the oscillations are at right angles to the direction the energy travels. They transfer energy from a source to an absorber, and they need no material to travel through, so they cross the vacuum of space. Together they form one continuous spectrum, grouped by wavelength (the distance from a point on one wave to the same point on the next wave) and frequency (the number of waves passing a point each second). You know where visible light sits. Can you place the other six?

Think again

“Violet light has a higher frequency, so it must travel faster.”

Frequency counts how many waves pass a point each second. It says nothing about how fast each wave moves: the two crests keep pace. Every electromagnetic wave travels at the same speed through a vacuum or air: 3 × 10⁸ m/s.

Equations

The bench showed it: at one fixed speed, shorter waves mean more waves every second. The wave equation says exactly that.

On the sheet

v = f λ

wave speed (v, metres per second) = frequency (f, hertz) × wavelength (λ, metres)

f = v ÷ λ · λ = v ÷ f

The speed

Any electromagnetic wave in a vacuum or air: v = 3 × 10⁸ m/s, which is 300 000 000 m/s. The question gives you this value.

Units first

Frequency in Hz: kHz × 1000, MHz × 1 000 000, GHz × 1 000 000 000. Wavelength in m: km × 1000, cm ÷ 100.

Key fact

From radio waves to gamma rays the wavelength gets shorter and the frequency gets higher. The speed in a vacuum never changes.
